Full wall washing

Full wall washing cleans entire wall surfaces top to bottom, typically on durable finishes like semi-gloss paint, epoxy, FRP or tile, where dust film or overspray covers whole walls.

Also called wall wash

Why it matters

Some spaces, such as kitchens, restrooms, healthcare rooms and back-of-house areas, need walls cleaned completely, not just spot-cleaned, before occupancy.

How we approach it

Crews work bottom-up with cleaning solution to prevent streaking and top-down for rinsing, using two-bucket systems and changing water often.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Wall areas and finishes
  • Paint cure time
  • Cleaning solution compatibility
  • Protection of floors and outlets

Watch-outs

  • Not all paints are washable; check the paint specification.
  • Water near outlets and switches must be controlled.

Questions about full wall washing

Can all painted walls be washed?

No. Only washable paints that have fully cured should be washed; flat paints may only tolerate spot cleaning.

Why wash walls from the bottom up?

Applying cleaner from the bottom prevents dirty drips from streaking dry areas below. Rinsing is done top down.

Which spaces need full wall washing after construction?

Kitchens, restrooms, healthcare rooms and back-of-house areas with washable finishes.

How long before new paint can be washed?

It depends on the product; many paints need weeks to fully cure.

What else is full wall washing called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "wall wash". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
