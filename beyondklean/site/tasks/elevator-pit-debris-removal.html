Elevator pit debris removal

Elevator pit debris removal clears construction debris, trash and dust from the bottom of elevator hoistways, done only under the elevator contractor's lockout, supervision and fall protection procedures.

Also called elevator pit cleaning

Why it matters

Elevator inspectors require clean pits. Debris in a pit is a fire hazard and can interfere with equipment.

How we approach it

Crews enter only with the elevator contractor's lockout, fall protection and supervision, remove debris and vacuum dust.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Pit count
  • Lockout and supervision
  • Debris type
  • Water in pit

Watch-outs

  • Pit entry requires lockout and trained supervision.
  • Water in pits must be addressed by the GC.

Questions about elevator pit debris removal

Who is allowed to enter an elevator pit?

Only authorized people working under the elevator contractor's lockout and supervision. Pits have fall, crushing and electrical hazards.

Why must elevator pits be clean for inspection?

Debris in a pit is a fire hazard and can interfere with buffers and equipment, so elevator inspectors require pits to be clean.

What is typically found in elevator pits on construction projects?

Construction debris, trash, dropped tools and materials, and dust that falls down the hoistway from every floor.

What if there is water in the elevator pit?

Water must be addressed by the GC before inspection. It can indicate a waterproofing problem and damages equipment.

What else is elevator pit debris removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "elevator pit cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
