Dryer vent debris check

A dryer vent debris check looks for construction debris, drywall dust, insulation and blockages in dryer vent connections and runs before turnover, and reports anything that restricts airflow.

Why it matters

Dryer vents installed during construction can collect drywall dust, debris or crushed sections. Restricted vents are a leading cause of dryer fires and resident complaints.

How we do it

Crews inspect accessible connections and terminations, clear loose debris at the connection and exterior hood, and report blockages to the GC for specialist cleaning.

Our capture-first method

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Vent runs per unit
  • Accessible connections
  • Exterior terminations
  • Reporting

Watch-outs

  • Clogged dryer vents are a fire hazard.
  • Long or concealed runs need specialist equipment.

Common questions

Why check dryer vents in new construction?

Construction debris and drywall dust can block vents, which reduces airflow and creates a fire risk.

Who cleans blocked dryer vents?

Dryer vent specialists with rotary brush equipment, coordinated by the GC.

What is checked during a dryer vent check?

The connection behind the dryer, the exterior termination and any accessible sections for debris.

How common is construction debris in dryer vents?

Common enough that a check before turnover is good practice.

What else is dryer vent debris check called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "dryer vent cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
