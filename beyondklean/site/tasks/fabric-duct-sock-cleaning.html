Fabric duct sock cleaning

Fabric duct sock cleaning removes construction dust from the outside of textile air dispersion ducts, and coordinates removal and laundering when the manufacturer recommends it.

Also called textile duct cleaning

Why it matters

Fabric ducts are porous by design, and construction dust can clog them or discolor them. Many manufacturers recommend laundering, and the wrong cleaning method can damage the fabric or its coating.

How we approach it

Surface dust is removed with low-suction HEPA vacuuming. If the fabric is dirty, crews follow the manufacturer's procedure, which often means taking sections down and sending them for laundering.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Fabric duct length, diameter and suspension type
  • Manufacturer and cleaning instructions
  • Whether takedown and reinstallation are included
  • Laundering vendor and turnaround time

Watch-outs

  • Do not spray cleaners on fabric duct; follow the manufacturer's laundering instructions.
  • Takedown and rehanging may need the mechanical contractor.
Keep it clean after turnover. Overhead dust comes back. Recurring high dusting for this surface is handled through our partner program at AboveEyeLevelCleaning.com.

Questions about fabric duct sock cleaning

Can fabric ductwork be washed?

Many fabric duct systems can be removed and laundered according to the manufacturer's instructions. Check the product's care guide first.

Why is fabric duct used?

It distributes air evenly and quietly, and is common in gyms, warehouses, food plants and open offices.

Does construction dust hurt fabric duct?

It can clog pores, reduce airflow performance and discolor the fabric if it is not removed.

Who reinstalls fabric duct after laundering?

Usually the mechanical contractor or the cleaning crew following the manufacturer's suspension instructions.

What else is fabric duct sock cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "textile duct cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
