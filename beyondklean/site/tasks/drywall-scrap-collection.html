Drywall scrap collection

Drywall scrap collection gathers gypsum board offcuts, broken pieces and dust from hanging and finishing work so floors stay clear and clean gypsum can be recycled where accepted.

Why it matters

Drywall scrap is heavy, bulky and produces dust when stepped on. Clean gypsum is recyclable in many regions, but only if it is kept separate and dry.

How we do it

Crews collect scraps as hanging progresses, keep clean gypsum separate from painted or contaminated pieces, and bag dusty fragments rather than sweeping them dry.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Volume of board being hung and schedule
  • Whether clean gypsum is recycled locally
  • Container type for drywall
  • Dust control requirements

Watch-outs

  • Wet drywall scrap gets very heavy and can be rejected by recyclers.
  • Crushed drywall underfoot creates fine dust; collect pieces before they are walked on.

Common questions

Can drywall scrap be recycled?

Clean, unpainted gypsum can be recycled in many areas. It must be kept dry and free of other debris.

Who cleans up drywall scraps?

The drywall contractor is often responsible, but GCs commonly hire cleanup crews to keep up with large installs.

Why does drywall scrap make so much dust?

Gypsum crumbles easily. When scraps are walked on or swept, they break down into fine dust.

Should drywall scrap go in a separate dumpster?

If the project is recycling gypsum, yes. Otherwise it goes in mixed debris.

What else is drywall scrap collection called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "gypsum scrap removal". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
