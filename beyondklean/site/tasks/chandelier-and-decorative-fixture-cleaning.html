Chandelier and decorative fixture cleaning

Chandelier and decorative fixture cleaning removes dust and fingerprints from crystal, glass, metal and specialty decorative fixtures in lobbies, ballrooms, restaurants and hospitality spaces.

Also called chandelier cleaning

Why it matters

Decorative fixtures are often the most expensive light fixtures in a building and the focal point of the room. Careless cleaning can break crystals, tarnish finishes or loosen parts.

How we approach it

Specialists clean in place from lifts or scaffolding, piece by piece with cotton gloves and approved cleaners, or follow the manufacturer's disassembly procedure for heavily soiled fixtures.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Fixture size, weight, height and materials
  • Manufacturer care instructions
  • Access method and floor protection
  • Whether disassembly is permitted

Watch-outs

  • Crystal and glass parts break easily; never spray cleaner directly onto electrical components.
  • Some finishes, like antique brass, should not be polished.
Keep it clean after turnover. Overhead dust comes back. Recurring high dusting for this surface is handled through our partner program at AboveEyeLevelCleaning.com.

Questions about chandelier and decorative fixture cleaning

How do you clean a crystal chandelier?

Piece by piece with cotton gloves and an appropriate glass cleaner, or by removing crystals for washing when the manufacturer allows.

Should a chandelier be turned off before cleaning?

Yes. It should be off and cool, and liquid is never sprayed into sockets.

Who cleans chandeliers in hotels and lobbies?

Specialists with lift access and experience with decorative fixtures.

How long does it take to clean a large chandelier?

Large crystal fixtures can take several hours each, depending on size and detail.

What else is chandelier and decorative fixture cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "chandelier cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
