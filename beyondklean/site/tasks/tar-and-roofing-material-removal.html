Tar and roofing material removal

Tar and roofing material removal cleans asphalt, roofing cement, mastic, sealant and tar from walls, walkways, windows, fixtures and floors near roofing and waterproofing work, including tar tracked inside on boots.

Also called tar removal

Why it matters

Roofing tar is sticky, black and spreads easily. It is tracked from roofs and waterproofing areas onto walkways and new floors, where it stains and is hard to remove.

How we approach it

Crews remove bulk material mechanically and use tar removers tested on the surface.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Surfaces
  • Material type
  • Removers
  • Protection

Watch-outs

  • Solvent-based tar removers can damage paint, plastics and resilient flooring.
  • Tar tracked onto new floors spreads quickly; stop the source with mats and boot cleaning.

Questions about tar and roofing material removal

How is roofing tar removed from concrete walkways?

Bulk material is scraped off when cool and hard, then a tar remover suited to concrete is applied, agitated and rinsed with the water recovered.

Can tar be removed from windows and frames?

Usually, with careful scraping on glass and tar removers that are safe for the frame finish, tested first.

How does tar get onto new floors inside a building?

Workers track it on boots from roofs and waterproofing areas. Walk-off mats and boot cleaning stations reduce it.

Who removes roofing tar at the end of a project?

The roofer is often responsible for tar outside their work area, with the cleaning crew handling remaining spots.

What else is tar and roofing material removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "tar removal". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
