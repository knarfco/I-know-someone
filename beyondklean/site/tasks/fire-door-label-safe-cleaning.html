Fire door label-safe cleaning

Fire door label-safe cleaning cleans fire-rated doors and frames, including faces, edges, glass and hardware, without removing, painting over or damaging the fire rating labels that inspectors check.

Also called fire door cleaning

Why it matters

Fire inspectors check that doors and frames have legible rating labels. A removed or painted label can mean replacing the door or getting it re-labeled.

How we approach it

Crews locate labels before cleaning, clean around them and report any labels that are missing, painted or damaged.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Fire-rated door locations
  • Label locations on doors and frames
  • Paint on labels
  • Reporting procedure

Watch-outs

  • Never remove or scrape fire labels.
  • Painted labels must be reported, not cleaned aggressively.

Questions about fire door label-safe cleaning

Where is the fire label on a door?

Usually on the hinge edge of the door and on the frame's hinge jamb.

What if a fire door label is painted over?

Report it to the GC. A painted or missing label may need relabeling by a certified service.

Can fire-rated doors be cleaned normally?

Yes. Fire-rated doors are cleaned with normal methods, as long as the fire rating labels on the door edge and frame are protected.

Why do fire door labels matter?

They prove the door and frame are rated for fire resistance, and inspectors check them.

What else is fire door label-safe cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "fire door cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
