Government historic preservation project cleaning

Government historic preservation project cleaning is gentle construction cleaning of historic courthouses, capitols, post offices and landmark public buildings, using methods that protect original materials during and after restoration.

Why it matters

Historic plaster, stone, wood and decorative metal can be damaged permanently by harsh chemicals, abrasion or water. Preservation rules on public buildings often require reviewed, documented methods.

How we do it

Our crews test methods on small areas first, use HEPA vacuums, soft brushes and pH-neutral cleaners, and follow the preservation specification and the architect's guidance for each material.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Historic materials and finishes in scope
  • Preservation specification and approved methods
  • Test panels and mockups
  • Protection of original features during cleaning
  • Documentation for the agency's preservation review

Watch-outs

  • Acidic or abrasive cleaners can etch marble and historic stone permanently.
  • Excess water can stain plaster and swell historic wood floors.
  • Older buildings may contain lead paint, so disturbance must be avoided.

Common questions

How do you clean a historic courthouse after restoration?

Crews start with HEPA vacuuming and dry methods, test any wet cleaning on a hidden area, and use pH-neutral products and soft tools approved for each historic material.

What cleaning products are safe for historic marble?

Generally pH-neutral stone cleaners and plain water in small amounts. Acids, vinegar and abrasive powders can etch marble, so methods are confirmed with the project's preservation specification.

Why are test areas used in historic building cleaning?

A test shows how a material reacts before the whole surface is cleaned. It lets the architect or preservation reviewer approve the method and avoid permanent damage.

Can construction dust harm historic plaster and woodwork?

Yes. Fine dust settles into carvings and moldings and becomes harder to remove over time. Soft brushes and HEPA vacuums lift it without abrasion.

Do you clean exterior masonry on historic public buildings?

We clean light construction residue with low-pressure, gentle methods. Heavy restoration cleaning of historic masonry follows the preservation specification and its approved test panels.

What if a historic building has lead paint?

Crews avoid disturbing painted surfaces that may contain lead. Any lead-related work is handled by licensed technicians under the applicable rules, separate from general cleaning.

What else is government historic preservation project cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "historic government building cleaning" and "landmark restoration cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
