Dust re-clean after late-trade work

A dust re-clean after late-trade work re-cleans areas that were already cleaned but got dusty again when a trade came back for punch work, touch-ups, cutting or installations.

Why it matters

Late-trade work is almost guaranteed on every project. Without a planned re-clean, the building fails the owner walk even though the final clean was done.

How we do it

Crews re-clean only the affected areas, using HEPA vacuums and microfiber, and document which trade caused the re-clean so it can be back-charged if needed.

Our capture-first method

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Re-clean pricing in the contract
  • Areas affected and responsible trade
  • Documentation for back-charges
  • Timing before inspection

Watch-outs

  • Without agreed re-clean pricing, disputes are likely.
  • Late trades should protect clean areas, but often do not.

Common questions

Why do buildings need re-cleaning after the final clean?

Trades come back for punch work, adjustments and late installations, and they create new dust and debris.

Who pays for re-cleaning after late trades?

It depends on the contract. GCs often carry a re-clean allowance or back-charge the responsible trade.

How can re-cleans be reduced on a project?

By finishing punch work before the final clean and protecting cleaned areas from returning trades.

Is re-cleaning included in a final clean price?

Usually not; it should be priced separately or as an allowance.

What else is dust re-clean after late-trade work called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "re-dust", "after-trade re-clean", "re-clean after punch work" and "re-clean". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
