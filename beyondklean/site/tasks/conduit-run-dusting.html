Conduit run dusting

Conduit run dusting removes dust and overspray from exposed electrical conduit, boxes and supports on walls and ceilings, including clusters of parallel runs in open-ceiling spaces.

Also called conduit dusting

Why it matters

Conduit is often installed in bundles that create long dusty ledges. In exposed designs, painted conduit is a finish element, and dust on it is as visible as dust on the ceiling.

How we approach it

HEPA brush vacuuming along each run with damp microfiber follow-up on painted conduit. Junction boxes and covers are wiped, never opened.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Linear feet of exposed conduit and number of parallel runs
  • Painted or bare EMT and rigid conduit
  • Height and access
  • Junction boxes and fixture whips included

Watch-outs

  • Do not open electrical boxes or touch conductors.
  • Painted conduit may have fresh paint that should not be scrubbed.
Keep it clean after turnover. Overhead dust comes back. Recurring high dusting for this surface is handled through our partner program at AboveEyeLevelCleaning.com.

Questions about conduit run dusting

Do you need an electrician to clean conduit?

No. Cleaning the outside of conduit and box covers is cleaning work. Opening boxes or touching wiring is not.

Why is conduit dusty in open ceilings?

Like pipes and ducts, conduit runs are horizontal ledges that collect dust from every trade working overhead.

Can painted conduit be cleaned?

Yes, with damp microfiber and neutral cleaners once the paint has cured.

Is conduit cleaned at the same time as the ceiling?

Yes, it is cleaned in the same lift pass as the deck, joists and other MEP.

What else is conduit run dusting called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "conduit dusting". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
