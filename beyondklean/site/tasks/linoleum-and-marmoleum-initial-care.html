Linoleum and marmoleum initial care

Linoleum and marmoleum initial care cleans new linoleum flooring with neutral cleaners, following the manufacturer's procedures for this natural material.

Also called linoleum cleaning

Why it matters

Linoleum is made from natural materials and is sensitive to high-pH cleaners, which can damage it.

How we approach it

Crews use neutral cleaners and follow the manufacturer's initial maintenance, which may include specific finishes.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Area
  • Manufacturer guidance
  • Factory finish
  • Residues

Watch-outs

  • High-pH cleaners and strippers can damage linoleum.
  • Excess water at seams can affect the backing.

Questions about linoleum and marmoleum initial care

Is linoleum the same as vinyl flooring?

No. Linoleum is made from natural materials like linseed oil, wood flour and jute, while vinyl is a plastic.

What cleaner is safe for linoleum floors?

A pH-neutral cleaner recommended by the linoleum manufacturer. High-pH cleaners can damage the natural linseed binder.

Why avoid strong alkaline cleaners on linoleum?

High-pH products can damage the linseed binder and dull or discolor the surface.

Does new linoleum need a floor finish?

Follow the manufacturer; many products have factory finishes and specific initial maintenance steps.

What else is linoleum and marmoleum initial care called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "linoleum cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
