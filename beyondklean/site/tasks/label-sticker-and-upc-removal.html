Label, sticker and UPC removal

Label, sticker and UPC removal takes manufacturer labels, barcodes, shipping stickers and promotional decals off fixtures, doors, equipment, glass and appliances throughout a building, while leaving required safety and rating labels in place.

Why it matters

Labels are on almost everything delivered to a jobsite. Every one left behind looks unfinished and is likely to appear on the punch list.

How we do it

Crews peel labels and remove adhesive with surface-safe products, leaving required ratings, nameplates and warning labels in place.

Watch-outs

  • Fire rating labels, nameplates, warning labels and inspection tags must stay in place.
  • Razor blades scratch soft plastics, painted metal and coated surfaces.

Common questions

Which labels should not be removed during final cleaning?

Fire rating labels on doors and frames, equipment nameplates, electrical warning labels and inspection tags. Removing them can fail an inspection.

How is sticker residue removed without scratching?

Peel the label slowly, then use a surface-safe adhesive remover and a plastic scraper. Blades are only used on glass that allows it.

Why are leftover labels such a common punch item?

Almost every item delivered to a jobsite has labels, and every one left behind looks unfinished to the owner.

Who removes labels at the end of construction?

The cleaning crew at final clean, working room by room so nothing is missed.

What else is label, sticker and upc removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "sticker removal". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
