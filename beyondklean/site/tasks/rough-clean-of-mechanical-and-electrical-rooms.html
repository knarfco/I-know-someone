Rough clean of mechanical and electrical rooms

A rough clean of mechanical and electrical rooms removes debris, packaging and heavy dust from equipment rooms after major equipment and rough-in are installed.

Also called MEP room rough clean

Why it matters

Equipment rooms collect packaging, scraps and dust that can be drawn into new equipment. They are also inspected by fire officials and commissioning agents.

How we approach it

Crews remove debris and packaging, HEPA vacuum floors and accessible surfaces, and stay clear of energized and operating equipment.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Rooms and equipment installed
  • Access and lockout status
  • Debris volume
  • Commissioning schedule

Watch-outs

  • No cleaning inside energized equipment.
  • Coordinate access with the electrical and mechanical contractors.

Questions about rough clean of mechanical and electrical rooms

Why clean mechanical rooms early?

Dust and debris can be pulled into new equipment and filters.

Can cleaners work in electrical rooms?

Only on non-invasive cleaning with permission, never inside energized equipment.

What debris is common in mechanical rooms?

Packaging, pipe and duct scraps, insulation and dust.

Who allows access to equipment rooms?

The GC and the responsible trade.

What else is rough clean of mechanical and electrical rooms called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "MEP room rough clean". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
