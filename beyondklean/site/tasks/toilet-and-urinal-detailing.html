Toilet and urinal detailing

Toilet and urinal detailing cleans new water closets and urinals inside and out, including bases, bolts caps, flush valves, seats and the floor and wall around them.

Also called toilet cleaning; urinal cleaning

Why it matters

Restrooms are inspected more closely than any other room, and new fixtures usually carry labels, dust, grout and caulk residue.

How we approach it

Crews remove labels, clean with non-acidic products safe for vitreous china and chrome, and detail behind and under each fixture where inspectors look.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Fixture count
  • Labels and residue
  • Flush valves and chrome
  • Floor and wall around fixtures

Watch-outs

  • Acid bowl cleaners can damage chrome and grout.
  • Do not adjust flush valves.

Questions about toilet and urinal detailing

How are new toilets and urinals cleaned after construction?

Labels and residue are removed, then fixtures are cleaned inside and out with non-acidic products safe for vitreous china and chrome, including bases, bolt caps and flush valves.

Why avoid acid bowl cleaners in a new restroom?

Acids can damage chrome flush valves, etch some grouts and stone, and are rarely needed on new fixtures.

Do inspectors look behind toilets during an owner walk?

Many do. Behind and under fixtures is where dust, grout and caulk residue are often missed.

Who cleans new restroom fixtures before turnover?

The cleaning crew during the final clean, with a touch-up before the owner walk.

What else is toilet and urinal detailing called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "toilet cleaning" and "urinal cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
