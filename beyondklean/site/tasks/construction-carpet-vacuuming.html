Construction carpet vacuuming

Construction carpet vacuuming removes drywall dust, grit, threads and debris from newly installed carpet with commercial HEPA vacuums before turnover.

Also called carpet vacuum

Why it matters

New carpet traps fine construction dust deep in the pile. Ordinary vacuums blow it back into the air and leave the carpet looking gray and feeling gritty.

How we approach it

Crews vacuum in slow overlapping passes with HEPA uprights or backpacks, edge along walls with crevice tools and empty or change bags often, because drywall dust fills them fast.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Carpet area and type: broadloom or tile
  • Dust load from late trades
  • Edges, stairs and corners
  • Number of passes expected

Watch-outs

  • Drywall dust clogs vacuum filters quickly and reduces suction.
  • Debris like screws can damage vacuum brush rolls; pick it up first.

Questions about construction carpet vacuuming

Why does new carpet look dusty after vacuuming?

Fine drywall dust needs slow, repeated passes with a HEPA vacuum. A single fast pass leaves much of it in the pile.

What kind of vacuum is best for construction dust in carpet?

A commercial upright or backpack with sealed HEPA filtration, so fine dust is captured instead of exhausted.

Is vacuuming enough or does new carpet need extraction?

Vacuuming is the minimum. Extraction is needed when there are stains, mud or heavy residue.

When should new carpet be vacuumed?

After the last dusty trade finishes and after furniture installation, close to turnover.

What else is construction carpet vacuuming called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "carpet vacuum". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
