Stairwell and egress path clearing

Stairwell and egress path clearing keeps stairs, exits and corridors free of debris, materials and dust so workers can move and evacuate safely.

Also called egress cleanup; exit path clearing

Why it matters

Blocked egress is a serious safety and fire code issue. Stairwells also collect debris because every trade uses them.

How we approach it

Crews clear stairs top to bottom, remove stored materials from exit paths and keep landings and handrails clean.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Stairwells and exit routes
  • Frequency
  • Material storage rules
  • Coordination with trades

Watch-outs

  • Never store materials in egress paths.
  • Clean stairs top down to avoid re-soiling.

Questions about stairwell and egress path clearing

Why must egress paths stay clear on a construction site?

Workers need safe, unobstructed exits in an emergency, and fire codes require it.

How often should construction stairwells be cleaned?

Daily on active multistory projects, because every trade uses them.

Who keeps stairwells and exits clear?

All trades are responsible, with cleanup crews doing regular sweeps.

Can materials be stored in construction stairwells?

Generally no. Stored materials block egress and are a common safety finding.

What else is stairwell and egress path clearing called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "egress cleanup" and "exit path clearing". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
