Final clean of entire building

A final clean of the entire building is the complete, detailed cleaning of every space, inside and out, after construction is finished and before the Substantial Completion inspection and owner turnover.

Why it matters

Most specifications require final cleaning to be complete before the GC requests the Substantial Completion inspection. A building that is not inspection-clean can fail the walk and delay the certificate, occupancy and payment.

How we do it

Crews work top down and inside out, room by room against a checklist: overhead, fixtures, walls, glass, casework, restrooms and finally floors, using HEPA capture and finish-safe products.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Spec section requirements for final cleaning
  • Inspection and owner walk dates

Watch-outs

  • Trades returning for punch work will re-soil clean areas.
  • Overhead and exterior work are often missed unless written into the scope.

Common questions

What is included in a final construction clean?

Every surface in every room: overhead, lights, walls, doors, glass, casework, fixtures, restrooms, floors, plus exterior entries and walks.

When should the final clean happen?

After all trades finish, before the Substantial Completion inspection and owner walk.

How much does a final clean cost?

It is usually priced per square foot or by crew-day, based on size, finishes, heights and schedule.

What is the difference between final clean and touch-up?

The final clean is complete and detailed; touch-up is a quick pass to fix areas that got dirty again.

What else is final clean of entire building called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "final cleaning", "phase 3 clean", "sparkle clean", "pre-substantial completion clean" and "substantial completion clean". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
