Concrete efflorescence removal

Concrete efflorescence removal cleans white, powdery salt deposits from concrete slabs, walls and foundations using cleaners suited to the surface.

Also called efflorescence cleaning; white haze removal

Why it matters

Efflorescence looks like white haze or powder on new concrete and makes floors and walls look stained. It comes back if the moisture source is not addressed, and the wrong cleaner can etch or discolor the concrete.

How we approach it

Crews dry-brush and vacuum loose salts, then use efflorescence cleaners tested on the surface and rinse thoroughly.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Area and surface type
  • Moisture source
  • Cleaner testing
  • Rinse and disposal

Watch-outs

  • Acidic cleaners can etch or discolor concrete.
  • Efflorescence may return if moisture continues.

Questions about concrete efflorescence removal

What causes efflorescence on concrete?

Water moving through the concrete carries dissolved salts to the surface, where they remain as white deposits when the water evaporates.

Will efflorescence come back after cleaning?

It can if moisture keeps moving through the concrete. New slabs often stop once they dry out.

How is efflorescence removed from concrete floors?

Dry brushing and vacuuming first, then efflorescence cleaners tested on the surface if needed.

Is efflorescence harmful to concrete?

It is mostly cosmetic, but it signals moisture movement that can affect floor coverings.

What else is concrete efflorescence removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "efflorescence cleaning" and "white haze removal". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
