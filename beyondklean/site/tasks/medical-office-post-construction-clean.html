Medical office post-construction clean

A medical office post-construction clean prepares a new or renovated outpatient office, such as a primary care, specialty or imaging suite, for patient use, covering exam rooms, waiting areas, labs and restrooms.

Also called medical office cleaning

Why it matters

Medical offices see patients immediately after opening. Dust on exam room surfaces, cabinetry and equipment is unacceptable, and practices often move in quickly after turnover.

How we approach it

Crews clean top down with HEPA vacuums and damp wiping, detail casework, exam room surfaces and sinks, and leave disinfection to the practice's protocols or registered-product services.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Exam room and procedure room count
  • Medical equipment installed
  • Practice move-in date
  • Disinfection responsibility

Watch-outs

  • Do not clean inside medical equipment.
  • Disinfection claims require registered products used per label.

Questions about medical office post-construction clean

Is a medical office cleaned differently from a regular office?

Yes. Exam rooms, sinks, casework and waiting areas need detailed cleaning, and the practice usually follows with its own disinfection protocol.

Who disinfects a new medical office?

The practice's cleaning provider or staff, using registered disinfectants according to the label.

When should a medical office be cleaned?

After all trades and furniture installation, close to move-in.

Are exam room cabinets cleaned inside?

Yes, cabinets and drawers are vacuumed and wiped.

What else is medical office post-construction clean called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "medical office cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
