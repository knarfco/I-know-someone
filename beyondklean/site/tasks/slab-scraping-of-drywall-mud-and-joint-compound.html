Slab scraping of drywall mud and joint compound

Slab scraping of drywall mud and joint compound removes hardened mud drips and patches from concrete slabs after taping and finishing, so flooring can be installed on a flat surface.

Why it matters

Hardened joint compound under flooring creates bumps, telegraphs through vinyl and carpet, and prevents adhesive from bonding.

How we do it

Crews scrape with long-handled floor scrapers, HEPA vacuum the residue and spot-clean stubborn areas, keeping water to a minimum so mud does not smear.

Our capture-first method

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Floor areas receiving new flooring
  • Amount of mud on slabs
  • Flooring installer's slab requirements
  • Dust control

Watch-outs

  • Wet scrubbing mud can smear it into a film.
  • Scraping can damage sealed or polished concrete; adjust tools.

Common questions

Why scrape drywall mud off the slab before flooring?

Bumps of dried mud show through resilient flooring and carpet and keep adhesive from bonding.

When is slab scraping done?

After drywall taping and finishing and before flooring installation.

Can a floor scrubber remove dried drywall mud?

Scraping works better first; scrubbing dried mud often smears it into a film.

Who scrapes the slab before flooring?

The drywall contractor or the cleanup crew, depending on the contract.

What else is slab scraping of drywall mud and joint compound called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "mud scrape" and "slab scrape". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
