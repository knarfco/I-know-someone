Water damage cleanup

Water damage cleanup removes water, dries structures and contents, and removes damaged materials after leaks, floods or storms. It is restoration work assigned only to qualified restoration contractors.

Also called flood cleanup

Why it matters

Wet building materials can grow mold within days. Water damage on a jobsite or in a new building needs fast, documented drying to protect the structure and the warranty.

How we approach it

Requests are reviewed and assigned only to restoration contractors with appropriate training and insurance, who extract water, dry with monitored equipment and document moisture readings.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Source and extent of water
  • Category of water: clean, gray or black
  • Materials affected
  • Insurance and documentation needs

Watch-outs

  • Contaminated water requires specialized handling.
  • Wet drywall and insulation often must be removed, not dried.

Questions about water damage cleanup

What should be done first after water damage on a jobsite?

Stop the water source, make the area electrically safe and photograph the damage. Then get a qualified restoration contractor drying the structure quickly, because wet materials start growing mold within days.

How quickly can mold grow after water damage?

Mold can begin growing on wet drywall, wood and insulation within roughly 24 to 48 hours in warm conditions. That is why fast extraction and monitored drying matter so much.

Who handles water damage restoration on a construction project?

A restoration contractor trained in water damage drying. They extract water, set dehumidifiers and air movers, and track moisture readings until materials are dry.

Does water damage on a new building need insurance documentation?

Usually yes. Photos, moisture readings and drying logs support builder's risk or property insurance claims and protect the owner's warranty position.

What else is water damage cleanup called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "flood cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
