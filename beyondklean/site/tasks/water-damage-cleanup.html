Water damage cleanup

Water damage cleanup removes water, dries structures and contents, and removes damaged materials after leaks, floods or storms. It is restoration work routed only to qualified restoration contractors.

Also called flood cleanup

Why it matters

Wet building materials can grow mold within days. Water damage on a jobsite or in a new building needs fast, documented drying to protect the structure and the warranty.

How we approach it

Requests are reviewed and routed only to restoration contractors with appropriate training and insurance, who extract water, dry with monitored equipment and document moisture readings.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Source and extent of water
  • Category of water: clean, gray or black
  • Materials affected
  • Insurance and documentation needs

Watch-outs

  • Contaminated water requires specialized handling.
  • Wet drywall and insulation often must be removed, not dried.

Questions about water damage cleanup

What should be done first after water damage on a jobsite?

Stop the source, protect people and power, document the damage, and get a qualified restoration contractor started on drying quickly.

How quickly can mold grow after water damage?

Mold can begin growing within a few days on wet materials, which is why fast drying matters.

Who handles water damage restoration?

Restoration contractors with training in water damage drying and documentation.

Does water damage need insurance documentation?

Usually, including photos, moisture readings and drying records.

What else is water damage cleanup called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "flood cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
