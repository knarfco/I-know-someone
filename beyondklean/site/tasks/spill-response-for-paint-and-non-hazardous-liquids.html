Spill response for paint and non-hazardous liquids

Spill response for paint and non-hazardous liquids contains and cleans small spills of latex paint, primer, joint compound, water-based adhesives and similar materials before they damage finishes or reach drains.

Also called small spill cleanup

Why it matters

Small spills spread fast on new floors and are much harder to remove once dry. Paint and slurry entering drains can violate storm water rules.

How we approach it

Crews contain the spill with absorbents, remove the material without spreading it, clean the surface with suitable products and dispose of waste properly.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Materials present on site
  • Spill kits and absorbents
  • Disposal procedures
  • Reporting

Watch-outs

  • Hazardous material spills require trained responders.
  • Do not rinse paint or slurry into drains.

Questions about spill response for paint and non-hazardous liquids

How do you clean up a latex paint spill on a new floor?

Contain it, scoop and blot up as much as possible, then clean the remaining residue before it dries, testing products on the floor type.

Can paint be washed down a floor drain?

No. Paint should be absorbed, collected and disposed of according to site rules.

What is in a construction spill kit?

Absorbent pads and granules, bags, gloves, eye protection and basic tools for small spills.

What should be done if a spill is hazardous?

Stop, isolate the area and call trained hazardous material responders under the site's spill plan.

What else is spill response for paint and non-hazardous liquids called?
