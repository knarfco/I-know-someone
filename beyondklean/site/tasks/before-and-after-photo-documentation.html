Before-and-after photo documentation

Before-and-after photo documentation photographs areas before cleaning and after completion, organized by room or location, to prove completion, protect against damage claims and support closeout and LEED records.

Also called cleaning photo log

Why it matters

Disputes about what was dirty, what was damaged and what was finished are common at closeout. Dated, organized photos settle them quickly.

How we approach it

Crews photograph each area from consistent angles before and after work, note existing damage, and deliver photos organized by floor and room.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Areas to document
  • Photo format and naming
  • Existing damage notes
  • Delivery method

Watch-outs

  • Photos without dates or locations lose value.
  • Existing damage must be photographed before cleaning starts.

Questions about before-and-after photo documentation

Why take before-and-after photos of construction cleaning?

They prove the work was done and show existing damage, which prevents disputes at closeout.

How should cleaning photos be organized?

By floor and room, with dates and consistent angles, so anyone can find a specific space.

Who receives cleaning photo documentation?

The GC, and through them the owner, for closeout records.

Do LEED projects need cleaning photos?

Construction IAQ credits often require dated photos of protection and housekeeping measures.

What else is before-and-after photo documentation called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "cleaning photo log". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
