Public infrastructure project cleaning

Public infrastructure project cleaning covers construction cleanup on publicly funded works such as pump stations, treatment plant buildings, bridges, tunnels, roadside facilities and municipal buildings, removing debris and dust so the agency can inspect and accept the project.

Why it matters

Public agencies inspect closely and often hold final payment until the site is clean. Clean structures, drains and surrounding right-of-way also protect stormwater systems and public safety.

How we do it

Our crews follow the agency's site rules, traffic control plans and stormwater requirements, cleaning buildings, structures and right-of-way areas in the order the GC needs for inspection.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Facility or structure type
  • Traffic control and lane closures
  • Stormwater and inlet protection

Watch-outs

  • Work in road right-of-way requires the project's traffic control plan.
  • Storm inlets must be protected from sediment and wash water.
  • Confined spaces such as vaults and wet wells need permits and procedures.

Common questions

What kinds of public projects need construction cleaning?

Pump stations, treatment plant buildings, transit shelters, bridges, tunnels, rest areas, libraries, fire stations and other municipal or state facilities nearing acceptance.

Do public works cleaning jobs require prevailing wage?

Many do, depending on funding and location. The bid documents state the requirements, and we price and report payroll accordingly.

How do you clean near traffic on a road project?

Only within the work zone set by the project's traffic control plan. Crews wear high-visibility gear and do not work in open lanes.

Can your crews clean inside utility vaults or wet wells?

Only where the project has a confined space program and permits in place. Otherwise we clean surrounding areas and leave confined spaces to qualified entrants.

Why does the agency inspect cleanliness at closeout?

Final acceptance often includes a site walk. Debris in drains, dust in buildings or materials left in the right-of-way can hold up acceptance and final payment.

How do you protect storm drains during public project cleanup?

Inlet protection stays in place, crews sweep instead of washing where possible, and any wash water is recovered or directed to approved areas.

What else is public infrastructure project cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "public works construction cleaning" and "municipal infrastructure cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
