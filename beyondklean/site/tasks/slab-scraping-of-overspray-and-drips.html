Slab scraping of overspray and drips

Slab scraping of overspray and drips removes paint, primer, texture and coating drips and splatter from concrete slabs before floor finishes are installed or before polished concrete is exposed.

Also called floor scrape

Why it matters

Paint drips under flooring cause bumps and adhesion problems. On exposed or polished concrete, overspray is a visible defect.

How we approach it

Crews scrape with sharp floor scrapers and razor tools held flat, then treat remaining marks with removers matched to the floor and paint type.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Flooring plans and exposed concrete areas
  • Paint and coating types
  • Concrete finish: raw, sealed or polished
  • Testing of removers

Watch-outs

  • Aggressive scraping can gouge polished concrete.
  • Solvent removers can discolor sealers; test first.

Questions about slab scraping of overspray and drips

How do you remove paint drips from a concrete slab?

Scrape with a sharp floor scraper held low, then treat remaining marks with removers suited to the paint and floor.

Why remove overspray from a slab before flooring?

Overspray and drips create bumps and can stop flooring adhesive from bonding.

Is overspray hard to remove from polished concrete?

It needs gentle methods so the polish is not scratched or dulled.

Who pays for overspray removal on slabs?

Often the painting contractor, depending on the contract and protection requirements.

What else is slab scraping of overspray and drips called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "floor scrape". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
