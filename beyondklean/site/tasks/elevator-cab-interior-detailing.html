Elevator cab interior detailing

Elevator cab interior detailing cleans the walls, ceiling, lights, handrails, control panel, door faces and floor of passenger and service elevator cabs after protection pads come down.

Also called elevator cleaning

Why it matters

The elevator is used by every visitor, and its finishes are close to the eye. Fingerprints on stainless, scratches hidden under pads and dust on ceiling lights all show up immediately.

How we approach it

Crews work with the cab placed on independent service by the elevator contractor, clean ceiling to floor with finish-specific products, and wipe stainless with the grain.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Number of cabs
  • Wall and floor finishes
  • Elevator contractor coordination for independent service
  • Pad removal timing

Watch-outs

  • Do not spray liquids into control panels or door sensors.
  • Report scratches found under pads rather than trying to polish them out.

Questions about elevator cab interior detailing

How are elevator cabs cleaned after construction?

With the cab on independent service, crews clean ceiling, lights, walls, rails, control panel and floor using products matched to each finish.

Can cleaning crews operate elevators?

No. The elevator contractor or building staff places the cab on independent service for cleaning.

How is stainless in an elevator cleaned without streaks?

With a neutral cleaner, then a clean dry cloth wiped in the direction of the grain.

What if the cab is scratched under the pads?

The damage is documented and reported to the GC; scratches are repaired by finish specialists, not cleaners.

What else is elevator cab interior detailing called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "elevator cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
