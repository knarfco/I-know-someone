Gymnasium rafter cleaning

Gymnasium rafter cleaning removes dust, debris and lost balls from the joists, trusses, deck and equipment above a gym floor. It is done from lifts that are set up to protect the finished wood or sport floor.

Also called gym ceiling cleaning

Why it matters

Gym ceilings are high, hard to reach and directly above an expensive floor. Dust falling from rafters scratches the floor finish during play and lands on bleachers and equipment.

How we approach it

Crews work from lifts with non-marking tires and floor protection under every outrigger and wheel path, HEPA vacuuming rafters, deck and lights. Debris such as stuck balls is removed by hand rather than knocked down.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Gym size and clear height
  • Floor type and required protection: maple, rubber or synthetic
  • Basketball goals, divider curtains, scoreboards and lights to work around
  • Timing relative to floor sanding and finishing

Watch-outs

  • Lifts on a finished maple floor need approved protection; point loads can dent wood.
  • Retractable goals and divider curtains have motors and cables; crews must not use them as supports.
Keep it clean after turnover. Overhead dust comes back. Recurring high dusting for this surface is handled through our partner program at AboveEyeLevelCleaning.com.

Questions about gymnasium rafter cleaning

When should gym rafters be cleaned in a new school?

Before the final screen and recoat or finish coats on the gym floor if possible, and always before the final floor clean. Dust from above ruins fresh floor finish.

Can you clean rafters without damaging the gym floor?

Yes, with non-marking tires, floor protection boards under lifts and careful routing. The floor contractor's protection requirements should be in the plan.

How often should gym rafters be cleaned?

Many schools do it once a year during summer maintenance, before floors are recoated.

Do balls stuck in the rafters get removed?

Yes, it is a common part of the job. Crews retrieve them by hand from the lift instead of knocking them loose.

What else is gymnasium rafter cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "gym ceiling cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
