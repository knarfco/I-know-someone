Production line and machinery cleaning

Production line and machinery cleaning removes construction dust, packing material, protective film, grease smears and installation debris from newly installed conveyors, machines and line equipment so the line is ready for trial runs and owner acceptance.

Why it matters

New lines often sit for weeks while trades finish around them. Dust and metal shavings on guards, belts and controls cause jams, sensor faults and a poor first impression at acceptance.

How we do it

Crews coordinate with the equipment installer, confirm lockout, then vacuum, wipe and detail machine exteriors, frames, guards and floors beneath the line using cleaners approved for each surface.

Our capture-first method

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Line length and machine count
  • Installer lockout coordination
  • Approved cleaners per surface
  • Film and packaging removal
  • Under-machine floor cleaning

Watch-outs

  • Machines must be locked out by the authorized party before crews clean them.
  • Some coatings and food-contact surfaces need specific cleaners, so confirm before wiping.
  • Removing protective film too early can expose finishes to more construction damage.

Common questions

Who cleans new machinery after it is installed?

The installer typically handles internal components and calibration. A construction cleaning crew handles exteriors, frames, guards, floors and surrounding areas once the installer releases the machine.

Is machinery cleaning done while the line is running?

No. Our crews clean only equipment that is stopped and locked out by the authorized party, following the plant's energy control program.

What do you use on stainless steel conveyor frames?

Neutral cleaners and stainless polish wiped with the grain, unless the owner specifies a food-safe or validated product. We confirm chemicals before starting.

Can you remove metal shavings from around new equipment?

Yes. Crews use magnetic sweepers and HEPA vacuums to collect drilling and cutting shavings from frames, pans and the floor under the line.

When should line cleaning be scheduled?

After mechanical and electrical installation is complete and before trial runs or factory acceptance testing. A touch-up pass is often needed after final adjustments.

How is production line cleaning priced?

Usually per hour or per lot based on line length, machine density and access. Tight guarding and overhead conveyors take longer than open floor equipment.

What else is production line and machinery cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "machinery cleaning after install" and "production equipment cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
