Recycling stream separation

Recycling stream separation sorts construction waste into separate containers for metal, cardboard, clean wood, drywall, concrete and mixed debris to reduce landfill disposal.

Also called waste diversion sorting; construction recycling

Why it matters

Separated materials are often cheaper to haul than mixed debris and support LEED and local waste diversion requirements.

How we approach it

Crews set up and label containers, train trades on what goes where, and remove contamination before pickups.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Materials to separate
  • Containers and labels
  • Hauler requirements
  • Documentation needs

Watch-outs

  • Contaminated recycling loads may be rejected or charged as trash.
  • Space for multiple containers must be planned.

Questions about recycling stream separation

What materials can be recycled on a construction site?

Commonly metal, cardboard, clean wood, concrete, asphalt and sometimes drywall.

Does recycling save money on construction projects?

It often can, depending on local hauling rates and markets.

What is contamination in recycling?

Trash or other materials mixed into a recycling container.

Who manages recycling on a jobsite?

The GC sets the plan; cleanup crews help keep streams separate.

What else is recycling stream separation called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "waste diversion sorting" and "construction recycling". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
