HEPA vacuuming of work areas

HEPA vacuuming of work areas uses sealed, HEPA-filtered vacuums to capture fine construction dust from floors, ledges and surfaces during construction.

Also called HEPA vac cleanup

Why it matters

Fine dust from drywall, concrete and wood is easily inhaled and spreads through the building. HEPA vacuums capture it instead of exhausting it back into the air.

How we approach it

Crews use properly maintained HEPA vacuums with sealed housings, change filters and bags as required and work top to bottom.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Areas and frequency
  • Vacuum type and filter maintenance
  • Silica-producing work
  • Disposal of collected dust

Watch-outs

  • A HEPA filter only works in a sealed vacuum.
  • Empty and dispose of collected dust without creating a cloud.

Questions about hepa vacuuming of work areas

What makes a vacuum a HEPA vacuum?

A sealed system with a filter that captures at least 99.97 percent of particles at 0.3 microns.

Why use HEPA vacuums on construction sites?

They capture fine dust instead of blowing it back into the air.

Do OSHA silica rules mention HEPA vacuums?

Yes. HEPA vacuuming is one of the approved alternatives to dry sweeping.

How often should HEPA filters be changed?

According to the manufacturer and the amount of dust collected.

What else is hepa vacuuming of work areas called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "HEPA vac cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
