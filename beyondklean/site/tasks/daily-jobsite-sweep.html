Site, Debris & Waste Management · Jobsite housekeeping and safety

Daily jobsite sweep

A daily jobsite sweep is a routine cleaning pass that clears debris, sweeps or vacuums walking paths, empties trash points and keeps active work areas safe at the end of each day.

Also called daily cleanup; progress sweep

Why it matters

Daily housekeeping prevents injuries, keeps trades productive and helps sites pass safety walks. Small messes left overnight become big messes by Friday.

How we approach it

Crews follow a set route through active areas, using HEPA vacuums or wet sweeping where dust is fine and keeping egress paths clear.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Areas and route
  • Time of day
  • Debris disposal points
  • Dust control methods

Watch-outs

  • Dry sweeping fine dust can violate silica rules and spread dust.
  • Coordinate with trades so materials in use are not removed.

Questions about daily jobsite sweep

Why sweep a jobsite every day?

To keep floors safe, clear debris and prevent dust buildup.

Who does the daily sweep?

Trades or a cleanup crew hired by the GC.

Is daily sweeping required by OSHA?

OSHA requires good housekeeping; daily cleanup is a common way to meet it.

Should the daily sweep use a broom?

For fine dust, wet sweeping or HEPA vacuuming is better than dry sweeping.

What else is daily jobsite sweep called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "daily cleanup" and "progress sweep". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
