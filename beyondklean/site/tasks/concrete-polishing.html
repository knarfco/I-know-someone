Concrete polishing

Concrete polishing grinds and polishes a concrete slab with progressively finer diamond tooling and densifier to create a durable, glossy finish. It is a flooring specialty, not routine cleaning.

Also called concrete grinding and polishing

Why it matters

Polished concrete is a finish system with heavy equipment and silica dust control requirements. It must be done by qualified contractors.

How we approach it

Concrete polishing specialists are routed only after review, with dust control equipment and silica compliance in place.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Slab condition and area
  • Desired finish level
  • Dust control and silica compliance
  • Schedule before other trades

Watch-outs

  • Grinding creates silica dust; controls are required.
  • Slab defects affect the result.

Questions about concrete polishing

Is concrete polishing a cleaning service?

No. It is a flooring specialty that grinds and polishes the slab with heavy equipment. Cleaning contractors maintain polished concrete afterward.

What is concrete densifier?

A liquid chemical, often silicate-based, that reacts with concrete to harden the surface during polishing.

Does concrete polishing create silica dust?

Yes. Grinders need dust collection with HEPA filtration or wet methods to comply with OSHA's silica rules.

When is concrete polished during construction?

Often early, before walls go up, or late with heavy protection, depending on the project's sequencing.

What else is concrete polishing called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "concrete grinding and polishing". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
