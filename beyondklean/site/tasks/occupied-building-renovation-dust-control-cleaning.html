Occupied-building renovation dust control cleaning

Occupied-building renovation dust control cleaning keeps dust from a renovation out of the occupied spaces around it, cleaning both inside the work zone and along the paths people and materials travel.

Also called occupied renovation cleaning; ICRA-style cleaning

Why it matters

Tenants, patients, students or shoppers continue using the building during work. Dust escaping the work zone causes complaints, health concerns and lost business.

How we approach it

Crews clean transit routes, entry points and adjacent spaces frequently, maintain sticky mats and barriers, and HEPA vacuum and damp-wipe outside the work zone.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Work zone and adjacent occupied areas
  • Material and worker transit routes
  • Cleaning frequency
  • Occupant communication

Watch-outs

  • Dust complaints can stop work; respond quickly.
  • Healthcare work follows infection control requirements.

Questions about occupied-building renovation dust control cleaning

How is dust controlled during renovation of an occupied building?

With containment barriers, negative air machines, sticky mats at exits and frequent HEPA and damp cleaning outside the work zone.

Who cleans the occupied areas during a renovation?

A dedicated cleaning crew coordinated with the contractor and building management.

What are sticky mats?

Adhesive mats at containment exits that pull dust off shoes and wheels.

Is occupied renovation cleaning done at night?

Often, yes. Night and weekend cleaning avoids disrupting tenants, patients or customers while keeping occupied areas clean every morning.

What else is occupied-building renovation dust control cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "occupied renovation cleaning" and "ICRA-style cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
