Elevator shaft and hoistway cleanup

Elevator shaft and hoistway cleanup removes construction debris, dust, fasteners and trash from hoistway walls, sills, ledges and the pit, done under the elevator contractor's control so the elevator can pass inspection.

Why it matters

Debris in hoistways is a fire hazard and can damage elevator equipment. Elevator inspectors routinely fail shafts and pits that still hold construction trash.

How we do it

Our crews work only under the elevator contractor's lockout and access control, clean ledges and sills from the top down and remove pit debris last.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Number of hoistways and stops
  • Pit depth and any standing water
  • Elevator contractor escort and lockout fees
  • Sill and door track cleaning included
  • Inspection date

Watch-outs

  • No one should enter a hoistway without the elevator contractor's access control in place.
  • Pits may hold water, oil or debris that requires separate disposal.
  • Cleaning the pit before the shaft above is finished wastes the effort.

Common questions

Who is allowed to clean inside an elevator shaft?

Only crews working under the elevator contractor's direction, with the car locked out and access controlled. Our crews never enter a hoistway on their own.

Why does an elevator pit need cleaning before inspection?

Inspectors check pits for debris, water and trash because they create fire and equipment hazards. A dirty pit is a common reason elevators fail acceptance.

What is cleaned in a hoistway during construction cleanup?

Ledges, beams, hoistway door sills and tracks, wall surfaces within reach and the pit floor, removing dust, fasteners, drywall scraps and trash.

Do I have to pay the elevator company for access?

Usually. The elevator contractor typically charges for the mechanic who provides lockout and escort, and that cost is often separate from the cleaning price.

How long does it take to clean an elevator pit?

A single pit is often an hour or two once access is arranged, but water, oil or heavy debris add time and may need separate disposal.

Should hoistway cleaning happen before or after cab finishes?

Before. Shaft and pit cleanup should follow the last trade working in the hoistway and come before cab finishes and the elevator inspection.

What else is elevator shaft and hoistway cleanup called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "hoistway cleaning" and "elevator pit and shaft cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
