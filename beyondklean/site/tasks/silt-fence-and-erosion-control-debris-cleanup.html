Silt fence and erosion control debris cleanup

Silt fence and erosion control debris cleanup removes silt fence, wattles, stakes and trapped sediment at the end of a project, once the site is stabilized and the storm water permit allows removal.

Also called erosion control cleanup

Why it matters

Erosion controls protect waterways during construction, but leftover silt fence and stakes at turnover look unfinished and can be hazards for mowing and maintenance.

How we approach it

Crews remove trapped sediment first, pull fabric and stakes, dispose of materials properly and repair disturbed ground for the landscaper.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Length of silt fence and wattles
  • Permit closeout status
  • Sediment disposal
  • Ground repair

Watch-outs

  • Removing controls before stabilization can violate the permit.
  • Stakes left in the ground are a hazard for mowers.

Questions about silt fence and erosion control debris cleanup

When is silt fence removed from a construction site?

After vegetation or paving stabilizes the site and the storm water permit allows it.

Who removes silt fence?

The site contractor, GC or cleaning crew, depending on the contract.

What happens to sediment behind silt fence?

It is removed and spread or disposed of properly before the fence comes out.

Why remove erosion control stakes completely?

Stakes left behind are hazards for mowing and foot traffic.

What else is silt fence and erosion control debris cleanup called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "erosion control cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
