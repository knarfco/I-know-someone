Metal scrap and stud offcut collection

Metal scrap and stud offcut collection gathers steel stud and track cutoffs, sheet metal, duct scraps and other metal waste, usually for recycling.

Why it matters

Metal offcuts have sharp edges that cut boots and hands, and scrap steel has recycling value. Keeping it separate turns a hazard into diverted material.

How we do it

Crews collect metal into a dedicated container, handle it with cut-resistant gloves and keep it free of other debris so it qualifies for metal recycling.

Our capture-first method

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Metal stud and duct volume
  • Dedicated metal bin and pickup
  • Cut-protection PPE
  • Recycling documentation

Watch-outs

  • Sharp edges require cut-resistant gloves.
  • Mixing other debris in metal bins can reduce recycling value.

Common questions

Can steel studs and track be recycled?

Yes. Steel studs, track, sheet metal and duct scraps are widely recyclable as scrap metal.

Does scrap metal from a jobsite have value?

Often yes. Depending on local scrap prices, separated metal can offset hauling costs.

Why are metal stud offcuts dangerous?

Cut edges are razor sharp and cause cuts and punctures to hands and boots.

Who keeps the metal recycling bin clean?

Trades and the cleanup crew, with the GC making sure other debris stays out so the load is accepted.

What else is metal scrap and stud offcut collection called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "metal recycling pickup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
