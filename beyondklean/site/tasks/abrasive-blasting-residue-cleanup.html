Abrasive blasting residue cleanup

Abrasive blasting residue cleanup removes spent blast media, paint chips, rust scale and fine dust left after sandblasting or media blasting, cleaning floors, ledges, steel and surrounding areas so coatings can be applied or the space returned to use.

Why it matters

Spent media and dust contaminate fresh coatings, clog drains and spread through the building. Old coatings may contain lead or other metals, so residue must be handled carefully.

How we do it

Crews collect bulk media with shovels and vacuum loaders, HEPA vacuum steel and ledges, and bag or contain waste as the coating test results and site plan require.

Watch-outs

  • Silica-containing media and dust require wet or HEPA methods, never dry sweeping.
  • If the removed coating contains lead, cleanup follows the project's lead plan.
  • Blast residue must stay out of storm drains and adjacent occupied areas.

Common questions

How do you clean up after sandblasting?

Bulk media is shoveled or vacuum loaded first, then steel, ledges and floors are HEPA vacuumed. Waste is bagged and handled according to what the old coating contained.

Is blast media dust dangerous?

It can be. Silica sand and some coatings release harmful dust. Our crews use HEPA vacuums, wet methods and respiratory protection suited to the material.

What if the paint that was blasted contained lead?

Then cleanup follows the project's lead plan, including containment, worker protection and waste testing. The GC or owner provides the plan and testing results before work starts.

Can spent blast media be reused or recycled?

Some media such as steel grit and garnet can be recovered by the blasting contractor. We separate it when asked, otherwise it goes out as waste.

Does blast residue need to be removed before painting?

Yes. Coating manufacturers require surfaces free of dust and spent media. Even fine residue left on steel weakens adhesion of the new coating.

How long does blast cleanup take?

It depends on media volume and access. Open floors go quickly with vacuum loaders, while structural steel, ledges and enclosed areas take far longer per square foot.

What else is abrasive blasting residue cleanup called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "sandblast cleanup", "blast media cleanup" and "media blasting residue removal". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
