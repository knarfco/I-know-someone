Commercial kitchen post-construction clean

A commercial kitchen post-construction clean removes construction dust, debris, labels and residue from a new or remodeled restaurant or institutional kitchen so it is ready for equipment startup and health inspection.

Why it matters

A kitchen cannot open until it passes health inspection. Dust on equipment, debris in drains and residue on food-contact surfaces are inspection failures, not cosmetic issues.

How we do it

Crews clean top down, from ceiling and hood exteriors to walls, equipment and floors, using food-safe, residue-free cleaners, and leave sanitizing to a separate, label-directed step.

Our capture-first method

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Kitchen size and equipment list
  • Health inspection date
  • Specialty work by others: hood, ice machine, refrigeration
  • Food-safe product requirements

Watch-outs

  • Dust on equipment before startup can be drawn into fans and compressors.
  • Do not use solvent cleaners on food-contact surfaces.

Common questions

What does a restaurant post-construction clean include?

Ceilings, hood exteriors, walls, equipment exteriors, food-contact surfaces, floors, drains and restrooms, cleaned top down with food-safe products.

When should a new kitchen be cleaned?

After equipment is installed and before the health inspection and equipment startup.

Who cleans the kitchen hood in a new restaurant?

Hood exteriors are part of the clean; hood and duct interiors are cleaned by certified hood cleaning contractors.

Why does a new kitchen fail health inspection?

Common reasons include dust overhead, debris in drains, dirty equipment and missing sanitation steps.

What else is commercial kitchen post-construction clean called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "restaurant construction cleanup" and "kitchen turnover clean". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
