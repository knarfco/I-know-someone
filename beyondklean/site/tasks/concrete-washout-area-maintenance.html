Concrete washout area maintenance

Concrete washout area maintenance keeps the designated washout pit or container in working order, removes hardened material and prevents slurry from leaking into soil or drains.

Also called washout pit cleanup

Why it matters

Concrete washout water is highly alkaline and harmful to waterways. Storm water permits typically require a contained washout that is maintained.

How we approach it

Crews check liners and capacity, remove hardened concrete when needed and keep the area clean so trucks and pumps use it properly.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Washout type and location
  • Capacity and inspection schedule
  • Hardened material disposal
  • Storm water permit requirements

Watch-outs

  • Leaking liners can lead to storm water violations.
  • Washout water must not be discharged to drains or soil.

Questions about concrete washout area maintenance

What is a concrete washout area?

A lined pit or container where concrete trucks, pumps and tools are rinsed so the wash water is contained.

Why is concrete washout water a problem?

It is highly alkaline and full of fine solids that harm soil, plants and waterways.

How often should a concrete washout be cleaned out?

Before it fills up, commonly when it reaches about two-thirds to three-quarters of capacity.

Who maintains the concrete washout?

The GC, sometimes through the concrete contractor or cleanup crew.

What else is concrete washout area maintenance called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "washout pit cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
