Carpet extraction

Carpet extraction cleans carpet by injecting a cleaning solution and immediately vacuuming it back out, removing embedded soil, mud and residue that vacuuming cannot reach.

Also called hot water extraction; carpet shampoo

Why it matters

Construction leaves mud, drywall residue and spills in carpet. Extraction resets it before move-in, but over-wetting can cause odors, shrinkage and stains that come back.

How we approach it

Crews pre-vacuum, pre-treat spots, extract with low-moisture settings and neutral-pH rinse agents, and use air movers to speed drying.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Carpet area and fiber type
  • Manufacturer cleaning requirements
  • Drying time before occupancy
  • Water source and disposal

Watch-outs

  • Over-wetting can delaminate carpet backing or cause wicking.
  • Residue left in carpet attracts new soil quickly.

Questions about carpet extraction

What is carpet extraction?

Cleaning that injects a cleaning solution into the carpet and immediately vacuums it back out, often called hot water extraction or steam cleaning.

How long does carpet take to dry after extraction?

Usually a few hours with low-moisture methods and air movers, longer if humidity is high.

Can extraction damage new carpet?

Over-wetting or harsh chemicals can, which is why the carpet manufacturer's cleaning guidance is followed.

Why do stains come back after carpet extraction?

Soil or residue deep in the backing wicks back up to the surface as the carpet dries.

What else is carpet extraction called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "hot water extraction" and "carpet shampoo". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
