Insulation scrap removal

Insulation scrap removal collects batt, board and spray foam insulation offcuts, bags and wrapping from walls, ceilings and roofs.

Also called batt insulation cleanup

Why it matters

Loose insulation fibers irritate skin and lungs and spread across a floor quickly. Foam scraps blow around outside and end up in drains and neighboring properties.

How we approach it

Crews bag fiberglass and mineral wool scraps promptly, collect foam board and spray foam trimmings, and HEPA vacuum fibers instead of sweeping.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Insulation types and quantities
  • Bagging and disposal
  • PPE requirements
  • Exterior debris control

Watch-outs

  • Fiberglass requires gloves, long sleeves and eye protection.
  • Spray foam trimmings must be collected before they blow away.

Questions about insulation scrap removal

Is insulation dust harmful?

Fiberglass and mineral wool fibers can irritate skin, eyes and lungs, so crews use gloves, long sleeves, eye protection and avoid dry sweeping.

How should insulation scraps be cleaned up?

Pieces are bagged promptly and loose fibers are HEPA vacuumed instead of swept.

Can insulation scraps be recycled?

Some rigid foam boards can be reused or recycled; most batt and loose insulation scrap is disposed of.

Why does spray foam trimming need quick cleanup?

Light foam pieces blow around outside and end up in drains and on neighboring properties.

What else is insulation scrap removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "batt insulation cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
