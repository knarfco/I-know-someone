Spray-applied fireproofing area light dusting

Spray-applied fireproofing area light dusting is careful removal of loose dust from surfaces near or below sprayed fire-resistive material, without touching, scraping or wetting the fireproofing itself. It is only done with the GC's approval.

Why it matters

Spray-applied fireproofing is a life-safety system. Damaging it means repairs, inspection and possibly a code violation. Some older buildings also have fireproofing that may contain asbestos, which must not be disturbed.

How we do it

This is assigned only to qualified specialists under a defined procedure: HEPA vacuuming of adjacent surfaces with the hose kept off the fireproofing, no contact cleaning, and stop-work if any damage or suspect material is found.

Our capture-first method

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Confirmation of fireproofing type and that it is not asbestos-containing
  • Defined surfaces to be cleaned and surfaces to be left alone
  • Written GC approval and inspection status of the fireproofing
  • Procedure for reporting damage

Watch-outs

  • Never scrape, brush or wet spray-applied fireproofing; repairs must be made by the fireproofing applicator and inspected.
  • In renovations of older buildings, suspect fireproofing must be tested before any work near it.
Keep it clean after turnover. Overhead dust comes back. Recurring high dusting for this surface is handled through our sister brand, AboveEyeLevelCleaning.com.

Common questions

Can you clean spray-applied fireproofing?

No. The fireproofing itself should be left alone. Only nearby surfaces are cleaned, carefully, so the fire-resistive material is not damaged.

Why is fireproofing overhead work conditional?

Because damage affects fire safety and code compliance, and older materials may contain asbestos. It needs approval and a defined procedure.

What happens if fireproofing gets damaged during cleaning?

Work stops, the GC is notified and the fireproofing contractor repairs it, usually followed by inspection.

Does fireproofing make overhead dust worse?

It can shed fine particles if disturbed, which is one more reason to keep vacuum tools and lifts clear of it.

What else is spray-applied fireproofing area light dusting called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "SFRM area dusting". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
