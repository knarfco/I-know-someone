Joint and crack debris vacuuming

Joint and crack debris vacuuming removes dust and debris from control joints, saw cuts and cracks in concrete floors before sealing or final cleaning.

Also called control joint cleanout

Why it matters

Debris in control joints keeps releasing dust long after cleaning and prevents joint fillers and sealants from bonding properly.

How we approach it

Crews vacuum joints with crevice tools and HEPA vacuums, removing debris before joint filling or sealing.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Linear feet of joints
  • Joint filling plans
  • Debris
  • Timing

Watch-outs

  • Debris in joints affects filler adhesion.
  • Do not damage joint edges.

Questions about joint and crack debris vacuuming

Why clean concrete control joints?

Debris in joints keeps releasing dust and prevents joint fillers from bonding to the joint walls.

What is a concrete control joint?

A planned groove or cut in a slab that controls where cracks form as the concrete shrinks.

How are concrete joints cleaned?

With crevice tools and HEPA vacuums, sometimes after blowing debris loose in controlled conditions.

When is joint cleaning done?

Before joints are filled and before the final floor clean.

What else is joint and crack debris vacuuming called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "control joint cleanout". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
