Elevated signage and banner cleaning

Elevated signage and banner cleaning removes dust and fingerprints from high-mounted signs, wayfinding, department signs, banners and graphics in stores, airports, stadiums and warehouses.

Also called high sign cleaning

Why it matters

Overhead signs are meant to be read from a distance, and dust or smudges on them look neglected. They are also hung from structure where dust from above settles on them.

How we approach it

Crews clean signs from lifts with methods matched to the material: damp microfiber on acrylic and metal, dry brushing on fabric banners, and care not to shift hanging hardware.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Number, size and material of signs and banners
  • Mounting heights and suspension methods
  • Illuminated signs and electrical components
  • Who installed them and warranty concerns

Watch-outs

  • Do not lean on or pull suspended signs.
  • Illuminated signs should be off while cleaned.
Keep it clean after turnover. Overhead dust comes back. Recurring high dusting for this surface is handled through our partner program at AboveEyeLevelCleaning.com.

Questions about elevated signage and banner cleaning

Who cleans hanging store signs?

The overhead cleaning crew, usually during store opening cleaning or scheduled high dusting.

Can fabric banners be cleaned in place?

Light dust can be removed with a soft brush vacuum. Stains usually require removal and professional cleaning.

Do illuminated signs need special care?

Yes, they should be off, and liquids kept away from electrical parts.

How often should overhead signs be cleaned?

Usually with routine high dusting, often quarterly or semiannually.

What else is elevated signage and banner cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "high sign cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
