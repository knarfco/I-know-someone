Rough Clean · Rough clean (phase 1)

Interior wall stud bay vacuuming before close-in

Interior wall stud bay vacuuming before close-in removes debris, dust, scraps and trash from inside open stud walls before drywall is hung on the second side.

Also called stud cavity cleanout

Why it matters

Debris sealed inside walls stays there for the life of the building: it can harbor pests, rattle, block cable pulls and create odors. Once the wall is closed, it is too late.

How we approach it

Crews vacuum the bottom track and each stud cavity with HEPA crevice tools and pick out larger debris by hand, coordinating with insulation and inspection schedules.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Linear feet of walls to close
  • Close-in inspection schedule
  • Insulation installation timing
  • Areas with low-voltage or plumbing in walls

Watch-outs

  • Do not disturb insulation, wiring or plumbing in the cavity.
  • Food waste and trash in walls attracts pests.

Questions about interior wall stud bay vacuuming before close-in

Why clean inside walls before drywall is installed?

Debris sealed inside walls can attract pests, cause odors and get in the way of future cable pulls and repairs.

When is stud bay cleaning done?

After MEP rough-in and the in-wall inspection, before the second side of drywall is hung.

What is commonly found in open stud bays?

Drywall scraps, screws, food wrappers, bottles and wire clippings.

Who cleans stud bays before close-in?

Often the drywall contractor or a cleanup crew hired by the GC.

What else is interior wall stud bay vacuuming before close-in called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "stud cavity cleanout". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
