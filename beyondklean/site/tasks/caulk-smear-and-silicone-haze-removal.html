Caulk smear and silicone haze removal

Caulk smear and silicone haze removal takes thin films and smears of sealant off surfaces around joints at sinks, tubs, counters, windows and fixtures.

Also called caulk residue removal; Caulk and sealant smear removal; sealant removal

Why it matters

Silicone haze attracts dirt and looks smeary. It is hard to remove once cured, and the wrong method damages surfaces.

How we approach it

Crews use plastic blades and silicone removers suited to the surface, without cutting into the caulk joint.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Locations of smears
  • Surface types
  • Caulk type
  • Joint condition

Watch-outs

  • Cutting the joint can cause leaks.
  • Solvents can damage some surfaces.

Questions about caulk smear and silicone haze removal

What is silicone haze?

A thin film of silicone left around a caulk joint.

How is silicone haze removed?

With silicone removers and plastic tools.

Can caulk be removed without damaging the joint?

Yes, with care.

Who removes caulk smears?

The installer or cleaning crew.

What else is caulk smear and silicone haze removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "caulk residue removal", "Caulk and sealant smear removal" and "sealant removal". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
