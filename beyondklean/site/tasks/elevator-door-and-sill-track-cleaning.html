Elevator door and sill track cleaning

Elevator door and sill track cleaning vacuums the grooved door sills at every landing and in each cab and cleans the faces of hoistway and cab doors, removing grit, screws and debris that keep doors from closing properly.

Also called elevator sill cleaning

Why it matters

Grit, screws and debris in elevator sills keep doors from closing properly and cause service calls and failed inspections.

How we approach it

Crews vacuum each sill groove with crevice tools and pick out debris by hand, working floor by floor with the elevator contractor's coordination.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Number of landings and cabs
  • Debris in sills
  • Coordination with elevator contractor
  • Door finishes

Watch-outs

  • Never reach into the hoistway.
  • Do not wash debris into sill grooves.

Questions about elevator door and sill track cleaning

Why are elevator sills cleaned during construction?

Debris in the sill grooves stops elevator doors from closing smoothly, causing service calls, failed inspections and trapped-passenger complaints.

How often should elevator sills be cleaned on a jobsite?

Regularly during construction, because every delivery fills them with grit, and thoroughly before the elevator inspection and turnover.

Can elevator sills be washed with water?

They should be vacuumed, not washed. Water pushes debris deeper into the track and can cause corrosion.

Who adjusts elevator doors that do not close properly?

The elevator contractor. Cleaning crews only remove debris and report problems.

What else is elevator door and sill track cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "elevator sill cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
