Cardboard and packaging breakdown

Cardboard and packaging breakdown flattens boxes, removes foam, plastic and strapping, and stages packaging for recycling or disposal after fixtures, equipment and finishes are delivered.

Why it matters

Finish stages produce enormous volumes of packaging: light fixtures, plumbing fixtures, furniture and equipment. Unbroken boxes fill dumpsters with air and become a fire load.

How we do it

Crews break down boxes on site, separate cardboard from foam and plastic wrap, and bundle cardboard for recycling.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Expected deliveries and packaging volume
  • Cardboard recycling container
  • Foam and plastic disposal

Watch-outs

  • Box cutters cause cuts; use proper blades and technique.
  • Wet cardboard is often rejected by recyclers.

Common questions

Why break down boxes on a construction site?

Flattened cardboard takes far less dumpster space, which reduces hauling costs and fire load.

Is cardboard recycled on construction projects?

Often, especially on projects with waste diversion or LEED goals. Dry, clean cardboard is widely accepted.

When does a construction project produce the most packaging?

During finish installation, when fixtures, equipment, furniture and appliances arrive.

Who removes packaging from installed fixtures?

Installers are often responsible, but cleanup crews commonly break down and remove it.

What else is cardboard and packaging breakdown called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "packaging removal" and "box breakdown". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
