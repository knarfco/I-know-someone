Government infrastructure construction cleanup

Government infrastructure construction cleanup is jobsite and final cleaning on publicly funded projects such as pump stations, treatment plant buildings, maintenance yards and public works facilities, done to the agency's closeout requirements.

Why it matters

Public works projects are inspected by the agency before acceptance. Debris, mud and dust left in control rooms or around equipment can hold up final payment and system startup.

How we do it

Our crews handle progress cleanup and final cleaning, keep debris out of drains and equipment, follow the prime contractor's site safety plan and track work against the closeout checklist.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Site area, buildings and equipment rooms
  • Progress cleanup frequency
  • Final clean ahead of agency inspection
  • Drain inlet and stormwater protection
  • Wage rules and certified payroll where required

Watch-outs

  • Equipment rooms may have energized gear and lockout rules to follow.
  • Wash water and debris must be kept out of storm drains.
  • Confined spaces such as wet wells require permits and trained entrants.

Common questions

What is included in public works construction cleanup?

Ongoing debris pickup, dust control and housekeeping during construction, then a final clean of buildings, equipment rooms and site areas before the agency's inspection.

Do you clean pump stations and treatment plant buildings?

Yes, the buildings and accessible equipment areas. Crews follow lockout rules around equipment and do not enter permit-required confined spaces without the required permits and training.

How often should a government construction site be cleaned?

Weekly progress cleanup is common, with added visits before inspections. Busy sites with many trades may need cleanup several times a week.

Are prevailing wages required for infrastructure cleanup work?

On many public projects, yes. We pay cleaning labor under the wage determinations or local wage rules as the contract requires, with certified payroll where required.

How do you keep stormwater clean during site cleanup?

Crews protect drain inlets, sweep instead of hosing debris, and collect wash water so it does not reach storm drains, following the project's stormwater plan.

Can you work on a site with active traffic or equipment?

Yes, under the prime contractor's traffic control and site safety plan. Crews wear high-visibility gear and work only in released areas.

What else is government infrastructure construction cleanup called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "public infrastructure construction cleanup" and "government construction site cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
