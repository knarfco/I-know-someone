Door face cleaning

Door face cleaning removes dust, fingerprints, labels, pencil marks and smudges from both faces of wood, metal, laminate and glass doors.

Why it matters

Doors are touched by everyone and inspected closely. Factory labels, handling marks and overspray on door faces are frequent punch items.

How we do it

Crews remove labels, clean faces with finish-safe cleaners and microfiber, and check both sides of every door against a door schedule.

Our capture-first method

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Door count by type and finish
  • Labels and protective films
  • Overspray or adhesive
  • Door schedule for tracking

Watch-outs

  • Wood door finishes can be damaged by harsh cleaners.
  • Do not remove fire rating labels.

Common questions

How should new wood doors be cleaned after construction?

With mild, wood-safe cleaners and soft cloths, removing labels carefully so the factory finish is not damaged.

Why are door faces such a common punch list item?

Doors collect fingerprints, labels, pencil marks and overspray from every trade that passes through.

Should labels be removed from new doors?

Factory stickers and shipping labels should be removed; fire rating labels must stay in place.

Are both sides of every door cleaned?

Yes, both faces, the edges and the top of each door are cleaned at final clean.

What else is door face cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "door cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
