Ventilation commissioning cleaning

Ventilation commissioning cleaning prepares air handlers, fans, coils, plenums and diffusers for system startup and functional testing by removing construction dust and debris that would skew readings or foul new equipment.

Why it matters

Commissioning agents cannot test a system full of construction dust. Dirty coils and plenums reduce airflow, foul filters and can fail inspection on the first walk.

How we do it

Our crews clean to the commissioning agent's checklist, coordinate lockout with the mechanical contractor and document each unit before startup and test and balance.

Watch-outs

  • Equipment must be locked out by the mechanical contractor before any interior cleaning.
  • Cleaning coil fins with high pressure bends them and reduces airflow.
  • Startup before cleaning spreads construction dust through the whole building.

Common questions

What gets cleaned before HVAC commissioning?

Air handler interiors, fan sections, coils, drain pans, plenums, grilles and diffusers, plus mechanical rooms, so the equipment starts clean and test readings are accurate.

Who decides when the ventilation system is clean enough to start?

The commissioning agent and mechanical contractor set the criteria. Our crews clean to their checklist and provide photos so they can sign off each unit.

Do you clean air handler coils as part of commissioning prep?

Yes, with low-pressure rinsing or coil-safe cleaners where allowed, after lockout. High pressure is avoided because it bends fins and restricts airflow.

Is commissioning cleaning different from duct cleaning?

It overlaps. Commissioning cleaning focuses on equipment and terminal devices ready for startup, while duct cleaning addresses the duct runs; many projects specify both.

Can commissioning cleaning be phased by system?

Yes. Our crews follow the startup sequence, cleaning each air handler and its zone as the mechanical contractor releases it for commissioning.

Should filters be changed after commissioning cleaning?

Usually. Construction filters are swapped for final filters after cleaning and before test and balance, as directed by the mechanical contractor.

What else is ventilation commissioning cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "HVAC commissioning cleaning" and "air system startup cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
