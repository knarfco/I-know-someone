Pure-water fed-pole window cleaning

Pure-water fed-pole window cleaning uses deionized or reverse-osmosis water delivered through a brush on an extension pole to clean windows up to several stories without ladders or detergents.

Also called water fed pole cleaning

Why it matters

Pure water dries spot-free, so little or no chemical is needed, and pole work reduces ladder risk for low- and mid-rise windows.

How we approach it

Crews scrub and rinse glass and frames with pure water from the ground or a roof, using pole systems instead of ladders where possible.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Window heights
  • Water source and purification
  • Residue on glass
  • Access from ground

Watch-outs

  • Pure water does not remove paint, labels or stucco; detail work is needed first.
  • Water runoff should be controlled.

Questions about pure-water fed-pole window cleaning

What is pure-water window cleaning?

Cleaning with deionized or reverse-osmosis water through a brush on a water-fed pole, so glass dries spot-free without detergent.

Does pure-water window cleaning use soap?

Usually not. Purified water dissolves dirt and dries without leaving residue.

How high can water-fed poles reach?

Many systems reach several stories from the ground, depending on pole length and conditions.

Can pure water remove construction debris from glass?

No. Paint, labels and stucco must be removed by hand first.

What else is pure-water fed-pole window cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "water fed pole cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
