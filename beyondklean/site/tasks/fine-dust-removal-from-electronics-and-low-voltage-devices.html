Fine dust removal from electronics and low-voltage devices

Fine dust removal from electronics and low-voltage devices cleans dust from the exterior of displays, card readers, cameras, sensors, thermostats and other installed devices.

Also called device dusting

Why it matters

Fine dust can affect sensors, camera lenses and cooling vents. Devices also have sensitive surfaces that liquid cleaners can damage.

How we approach it

Crews use dry microfiber, soft brushes and low-suction HEPA vacuums on vents, never spraying liquids onto devices, and avoid pressing buttons or changing settings.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Device types and locations
  • Owner or integrator restrictions
  • Screens and lenses
  • Areas with active systems

Watch-outs

  • Do not spray liquids on devices.
  • Do not change settings or press buttons.

Questions about fine dust removal from electronics and low-voltage devices

How are electronic devices cleaned after construction?

With dry microfiber, soft brushes and gentle HEPA vacuuming of vents, never spraying liquid.

Can construction dust damage electronics?

Yes. Dust clogs cooling vents and can interfere with sensors and camera lenses.

Should touchscreens be cleaned with glass cleaner?

Only with the manufacturer's approved cleaner applied to a cloth.

Who cleans low-voltage devices after installation?

Cleaning crews clean device exteriors; integrators handle anything internal.

What else is fine dust removal from electronics and low-voltage devices called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "device dusting". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
