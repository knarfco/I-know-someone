Attic stock and spare material staging

Attic stock and spare material staging cleans, labels, inventories and stages spare finish materials, such as tile, ceiling panels, carpet tiles and paint, in the location the owner designates.

Also called attic stock staging

Why it matters

Specs often require attic stock for future repairs. If it is lost, damaged or thrown out during cleanup, the owner cannot match finishes later.

How we approach it

Crews gather spare materials, label them by type and location used, list them on an inventory and stage them in the owner's designated storage room.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Attic stock list from specs
  • Storage location
  • Labeling
  • Inventory sign-off

Watch-outs

  • Attic stock is easily thrown out during final haul-off.
  • Materials must be kept dry and protected.

Questions about attic stock and spare material staging

What is attic stock?

Spare finish materials, such as tile, carpet, ceiling panels and paint, left for the owner's future repairs.

Why is attic stock labeled?

So the owner knows what each material is and where it was used.

Where is attic stock stored?

In a storage room or location the owner designates.

Who is responsible for attic stock?

The GC, with the cleaning crew often handling staging and labeling.

What else is attic stock and spare material staging called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "attic stock staging". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
