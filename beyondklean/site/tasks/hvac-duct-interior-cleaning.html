HVAC duct interior cleaning

HVAC duct interior cleaning removes construction dust and debris from inside supply and return ductwork, usually following NADCA standards, when ducts were contaminated during construction.

Why it matters

Ducts that were left uncapped or ran without filters during construction carry dust into occupied rooms for months. Cleaning them is far easier before ceilings close.

How we do it

Requests go only to qualified duct cleaning contractors after review. Work follows NADCA methods with source-removal equipment and documentation.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Evidence of contamination
  • Duct types and access
  • NADCA or project standard

Watch-outs

  • Flexible and lined duct need different methods than sheet metal.
  • Cleaning without proper containment spreads dust into the building.

Common questions

Do new buildings need duct cleaning?

Only when ducts were contaminated during construction, for example when ends were left uncapped or the system ran without filters. Well-protected systems usually do not.

What is NADCA?

The National Air Duct Cleaners Association, which publishes standards for assessing, cleaning and restoring HVAC systems.

How can duct contamination be prevented during construction?

By capping open duct ends, storing ductwork clean and covered, and not running the HVAC without temporary filtration.

Who verifies that duct cleaning was done properly?

The duct contractor documents the work with photos, and some owners hire independent inspectors to verify cleanliness.

What else is hvac duct interior cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "duct cleaning" and "NADCA duct cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
