Grout sealing

Grout sealing applies a penetrating sealer to clean, dry cementitious grout joints so they resist staining and moisture and are easier to clean, typically after the tile's initial clean.

Also called grout sealer application

Why it matters

Unsealed grout absorbs spills, grease and dirt and darkens quickly, especially in restrooms, kitchens and entries.

How we approach it

Crews apply sealer after grout cures and is clean and dry, wiping excess from tile surfaces.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Grout type and cure time
  • Area
  • Sealer type
  • Tile compatibility

Watch-outs

  • Excess sealer on tile can leave haze.
  • Epoxy grout usually does not need sealing.

Questions about grout sealing

Should grout be sealed in commercial buildings?

Cementitious grout usually benefits from sealing, especially in restrooms, kitchens and entries.

When can new grout be sealed?

After it fully cures and is clean and dry, according to the grout and sealer manufacturers.

How long does grout sealer last?

It varies by product and traffic, often one to a few years before resealing.

Does epoxy grout need sealing?

Usually not, because epoxy grout is non-porous.

What else is grout sealing called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "grout sealer application". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
