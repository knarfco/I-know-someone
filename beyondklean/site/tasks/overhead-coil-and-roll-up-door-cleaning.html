Overhead coil and roll-up door cleaning

Overhead coil and roll-up door cleaning removes dust, overspray and debris from coiling doors, their curtains, guides, hoods and bottom bars.

Also called roll-up door cleaning

Why it matters

Coiling doors collect dust in their slats and guides, and debris in guides can affect operation. They are used constantly at docks and service areas.

How we approach it

Crews clean the curtain in sections as the door is operated by authorized staff, vacuum guides and wipe hoods without lubricating or adjusting anything.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Number and size of doors
  • Operation by authorized personnel
  • Guides and hoods
  • Fire doors and their requirements

Watch-outs

  • Do not operate doors without authorization.
  • Fire-rated coiling doors have release mechanisms that must not be disturbed.

Questions about overhead coil and roll-up door cleaning

How do you clean a roll-up door after construction?

The curtain is cleaned in sections while authorized staff move the door, and guides and the hood are vacuumed and wiped.

Why clean roll-up door guides?

Debris in the guides can make the door run roughly or jam.

Are fire-rated rolling doors cleaned the same way?

The curtain can be cleaned, but release mechanisms and fusible links must never be disturbed.

Who adjusts roll-up doors?

The door contractor; cleaning crews do not adjust or lubricate doors.

What else is overhead coil and roll-up door cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "roll-up door cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
