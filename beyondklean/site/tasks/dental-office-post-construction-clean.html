Dental office post-construction clean

A dental office post-construction clean prepares operatories, sterilization rooms, labs and reception areas after build-out, working around delivery units, chairs and cabinetry installed by dental equipment vendors.

Also called dental office cleaning

Why it matters

Dental offices have dense equipment, plumbing and cabinetry in every operatory. Construction dust in operatory cabinetry and equipment is unacceptable for patient care.

How we approach it

Crews coordinate with the dental equipment installer, clean surfaces and cabinetry with HEPA and damp methods, and leave equipment internals and lines to the vendor.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Operatory count
  • Dental equipment installer schedule
  • Sterilization room
  • Move-in date

Watch-outs

  • Do not clean inside dental equipment or waterlines.
  • Chairs and delivery units have manufacturer care requirements.

Questions about dental office post-construction clean

Who cleans dental equipment after installation?

The dental equipment vendor handles internal components and waterlines. Cleaning crews handle external surfaces, cabinetry and the room itself.

What is a dental operatory?

A treatment room with a dental chair, delivery unit, light and cabinetry. Most dental offices have several.

Are dental sterilization rooms cleaned at turnover?

Yes, surfaces, sinks and cabinetry are cleaned before the practice sets up its sterilization equipment and process.

Do dental offices require special cleaning products?

Practices often specify products compatible with their chairs and equipment, so crews confirm before using anything on equipment surfaces.

What else is dental office post-construction clean called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "dental office cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
