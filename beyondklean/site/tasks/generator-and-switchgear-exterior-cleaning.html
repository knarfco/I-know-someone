Generator and switchgear exterior cleaning

Generator and switchgear exterior cleaning cleans the outside of emergency generators, enclosures and switchgear lineups under the direction of the electrical contractor. It is conditional work.

Also called switchgear dusting

Why it matters

Generators and switchgear are critical life-safety and power equipment, often energized or ready to start automatically.

How we approach it

Requests are reviewed and assigned only with the electrical contractor's approval, using dry methods and respecting automatic start hazards.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Equipment and energized status
  • Electrical contractor approval
  • Automatic start lockout
  • Dry cleaning methods

Watch-outs

  • Generators can start automatically during a power event, so lockout is essential.
  • Arc-flash boundaries around switchgear must be respected at all times.

Questions about generator and switchgear exterior cleaning

Can a cleaning crew clean an emergency generator?

Only the exterior enclosure and surrounding area, with the responsible trade's approval and the generator locked out against automatic start.

Why is switchgear cleaning treated as conditional work?

Switchgear is energized, high-voltage equipment with arc-flash hazards, so only work approved by the electrical contractor is allowed.

Who cleans inside switchgear?

Qualified electrical workers during de-energized maintenance. It is never part of construction cleaning.

Why are generators dangerous to clean around?

They can start automatically when utility power drops, so lockout and coordination are required before anyone approaches.

What else is generator and switchgear exterior cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "switchgear dusting". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
