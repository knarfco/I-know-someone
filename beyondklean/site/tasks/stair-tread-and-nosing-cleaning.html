Stair tread and nosing cleaning

Stair tread and nosing cleaning cleans stair treads, risers and nosings, including rubber, stone and metal nosings, removing dirt and adhesive.

Why it matters

Stairs collect dirt and adhesive from construction, and nosings are safety features that must be visible.

How we do it

Crews clean from the top step down, remove adhesive and residue from treads and nosings with compatible products and scrub textured nosings so the contrast strip stays visible.

Our capture-first method

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Number of stairs
  • Tread material
  • Nosing type
  • Adhesive

Watch-outs

  • Wet stairs are slippery; clean in sections and keep a dry path.
  • Nosing contrast strips must stay clean and visible for safety.

Common questions

How are stairs cleaned after construction?

From the top step down, tread by tread, finishing with risers and landings.

What is a stair nosing?

The front edge of a stair tread, often with a contrasting or anti-slip strip.

Why are stair nosings important to clean?

They help people see the edge of each step, so contrast and grip must stay clear.

Are stairs part of the final clean?

Yes, stairs and landings are cleaned in every stair tower and feature stair.

What else is stair tread and nosing cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "stair cleaning", "stair nosing and tread detailing" and "stair nosing cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
