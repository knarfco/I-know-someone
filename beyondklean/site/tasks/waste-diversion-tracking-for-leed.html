Waste diversion tracking for LEED

Waste diversion tracking for LEED records the weight or volume of construction waste diverted from landfill, using hauler tickets and reports, to document green building credits.

Why it matters

LEED construction waste credits require documentation. Missing tickets at the end of a project can cost the credit.

How we do it

Crews and coordinators collect hauler tickets, log each pull by material type, and compile diversion reports for the project's LEED documentation.

Watch-outs

  • Tickets are easy to lose; collect them as each pull happens.
  • Some haulers report diversion differently; confirm methods early.

Common questions

What is construction waste diversion?

Keeping construction and demolition waste out of landfills by recycling, reusing or salvaging it.

How is waste diversion documented for LEED?

With hauler tickets and reports showing the weight or volume of each material and where it went.

Who tracks waste diversion on a project?

Usually the GC, often with help from the waste hauler and the cleanup contractor.

What waste diversion rate do projects target?

It depends on the rating system and local options; many projects target 50 to 75 percent or more.

What else is waste diversion tracking for leed called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "LEED waste log" and "diversion report". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
