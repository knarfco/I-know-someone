Dust containment barrier cleanup and removal

Dust containment barrier cleanup and removal takes down plastic sheeting, zipper doors and temporary walls used to contain dust, without releasing the dust collected on them.

Also called zip wall removal; poly barrier removal

Why it matters

Containment barriers hold dust on their surfaces. Pulling them down carelessly releases it into clean areas, undoing the protection.

How we approach it

Crews HEPA vacuum and mist barrier surfaces, fold sheeting inward to trap dust, bag it and then clean the floor and walls where the barrier stood.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Barrier locations and sizes
  • Dust conditions
  • Disposal
  • Cleaning of floor and wall after removal

Watch-outs

  • Pulling down dusty plastic releases dust into clean areas.
  • Tape residue from barriers needs cleaning.

Questions about dust containment barrier cleanup and removal

How do you remove dust barriers without spreading dust?

HEPA vacuum and lightly mist the plastic, fold it inward to trap dust, bag it, then clean the floor and walls where it stood.

What is a zip wall?

A temporary dust barrier system that uses spring-loaded poles and plastic sheeting, often with a zipper door.

Do dust barriers leave tape residue?

Often, on floors, walls and ceilings, and it is removed with surface-safe products.

Who removes containment barriers after renovation?

The trade that installed them or the cleanup crew, after the area is cleaned.

What else is dust containment barrier cleanup and removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "zip wall removal" and "poly barrier removal". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
