Lumber and wood scrap collection

Lumber and wood scrap collection removes framing offcuts, blocking, form boards and wood packaging from the jobsite, keeping clean wood separate when it can be recycled or reused.

Also called wood waste pickup

Why it matters

Wood scraps with nails are a puncture hazard, and piles of lumber are combustible. Clean wood can often be recycled or chipped instead of landfilled.

How we approach it

Crews pick up offcuts during and after framing, pull or bend exposed nails, and separate clean wood from treated, painted or composite wood.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Framing schedule and expected volume
  • Treated or engineered lumber present
  • Recycling or reuse options
  • Nail and fastener hazards

Watch-outs

  • Pressure-treated wood usually cannot go in clean wood recycling.
  • Exposed nails must be handled carefully to prevent puncture injuries.

Questions about lumber and wood scrap collection

Is construction wood recyclable?

Clean, untreated wood often is. Treated, painted or glued wood usually goes to landfill.

Why are wood scraps a fire hazard?

Piles of dry wood and packaging are fuel. OSHA requires combustible scrap to be removed regularly.

Can wood scraps be reused on site?

Sometimes, as blocking or temporary protection, if the GC approves.

Who removes framing scraps?

The framer or a cleanup crew, depending on the contract.

What else is lumber and wood scrap collection called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "wood waste pickup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
