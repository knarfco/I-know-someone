Landscape bed debris removal

Landscape bed debris removal picks construction debris, packaging, concrete chunks, screws and trash out of planting beds, tree wells and planters without damaging new plants or mulch.

Also called planter debris cleanup

Why it matters

Debris hidden in landscape beds is visible from walkways and can injure maintenance crews later. Landscapers often install plants while construction is still active.

How we approach it

Crews walk every bed by hand, pick out debris, rake mulch back into place and report damaged plants to the landscape contractor.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Bed areas and planters
  • Plant and mulch status
  • Debris type
  • Landscape contractor coordination

Watch-outs

  • Avoid stepping on new plants and irrigation heads.
  • Sharp debris like screws and glass requires gloves.

Questions about landscape bed debris removal

Why clean construction debris out of landscape beds?

It looks bad from walkways and can injure people doing landscape maintenance later.

Who removes debris from planting beds?

The cleaning crew or landscape contractor, depending on the contract.

Is mulch replaced after cleaning?

Disturbed mulch is raked back; replacement is usually the landscaper's job.

When should landscape beds be cleaned?

After exterior trades finish and before the owner walk.

What else is landscape bed debris removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "planter debris cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
