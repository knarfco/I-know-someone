Duct opening seal inspection and re-seal cleanup

Duct opening seal inspection and re-seal cleanup checks that open duct ends, registers and returns are capped during construction, reports torn or missing caps and cleans debris around them.

Also called duct capping

Why it matters

Uncapped ducts collect construction dust that later blows into finished rooms. Catching torn caps early prevents expensive duct cleaning.

How we approach it

Crews walk the building on a schedule, report open or torn duct protection to the mechanical contractor and clean debris around openings.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Inspection frequency

Watch-outs

  • Only the mechanical contractor reseals ductwork.
  • Do not remove caps to clean inside.

Questions about duct opening seal inspection and re-seal cleanup

Why are duct openings capped during construction?

To keep construction dust out of the HVAC system.

What happens if duct caps tear?

Dust can get into ducts, which may require duct cleaning later.

Who reseals duct openings?

The mechanical contractor.

Is duct protection part of LEED?

Yes, it is part of construction IAQ management.

What else is duct opening seal inspection and re-seal cleanup called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "duct capping". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
