Truck court and dock apron sweeping

Truck court and dock apron sweeping clears gravel, debris, mud and trash from the paved truck courts and dock aprons of warehouses and distribution centers with power sweepers.

Also called truck court sweeping

Why it matters

Debris in truck courts punctures tires, gets blown into dock doors and washes into storm drains. Tenants expect clean courts when they move in.

How we approach it

Crews power sweep in lanes with truck-mounted or ride-on sweepers, hand-sweep corners and dock pits and clear drain inlets.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Truck court area
  • Dock positions
  • Drain inlets
  • Traffic and timing

Watch-outs

  • Truck traffic must be controlled during sweeping.
  • Drain inlets must not receive swept debris.

Questions about truck court and dock apron sweeping

What is a truck court?

The paved area behind a warehouse where trucks maneuver and park at dock doors.

How is a truck court cleaned?

With truck-mounted or ride-on power sweepers, plus hand sweeping at corners and dock positions.

Why sweep truck courts before turnover?

Debris punctures tires, blows into docks and washes into storm drains.

When is the truck court swept?

Before tenant move-in, and then periodically during operation to keep debris away from tires and dock doors.

What else is truck court and dock apron sweeping called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "truck court sweeping". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
