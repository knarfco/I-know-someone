Exit sign and emergency light cleaning

Exit sign and emergency light cleaning removes dust, overspray and labels from exit signs, emergency light units and combination fixtures so they are clean and clearly visible at inspection.

Also called exit light cleaning

Why it matters

Exit signs and emergency lights are life-safety devices checked during fire inspections. They must be visible and legible, and overspray or labels on the face can be a problem.

How we approach it

Faces and housings are wiped with damp microfiber and appropriate cleaners. Test buttons and indicators are left untouched, and units are never disconnected.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Number of exit signs and emergency units
  • Mounting heights
  • Overspray or label removal needed
  • Inspection date

Watch-outs

  • Do not press test buttons repeatedly or disconnect units.
  • Painted-over exit signs may need replacement by the electrical contractor.
Keep it clean after turnover. Overhead dust comes back. Recurring high dusting for this surface is handled through our partner program at AboveEyeLevelCleaning.com.

Questions about exit sign and emergency light cleaning

Do exit signs need cleaning before inspection?

They should be clean and fully legible. Overspray or labels on the face can draw an inspector's attention.

Can paint overspray be removed from an exit sign?

Often, carefully, with a plastic blade and mild remover. Heavy paint may require replacement.

Who maintains emergency lights after cleaning?

The facility or electrical contractor tests and maintains them. Cleaners only clean the exterior.

Are exit signs part of final clean?

Yes, they are a standard final clean item.

What else is exit sign and emergency light cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "exit light cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
