Federal government building construction cleaning

Federal government building construction cleaning is the rough, final and touch-up cleaning of new or renovated federal office buildings, finished to the agency's closeout checklist and the prime contractor's site safety plan before acceptance.

Why it matters

Federal acceptance walks are formal and documented. Dust, labels or debris left behind can hold up substantial completion, delay occupancy and add punch-list rounds the prime contractor has to manage.

How we do it

Our crews work under the prime contractor's site safety plan, clean floor by floor with HEPA methods and log each area against the agency's closeout checklist before the government walk.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Gross square footage and floor count
  • Phase: rough, final or touch-up
  • Agency closeout checklist and acceptance walk dates
  • Badging and background checks as the facility requires
  • Wage determination and certified payroll where required

Watch-outs

  • Badging and background checks can take weeks, so start them early through the prime contractor.
  • Wage determinations in the contract apply to cleaning labor and must be priced in.
  • Secure or restricted rooms may need escorts, which slows production rates.

Common questions

What does a final clean on a federal building include?

Floors, glass, restrooms, fixtures, millwork, vents, doors and hardware, all cleaned and checked against the agency's closeout checklist so the acceptance walk focuses on construction items, not dust.

Do cleaning crews on federal projects need background checks?

Often, yes. Background checks and badging are handled as the facility requires and are arranged through the prime contractor, so we submit crew names well before the start date.

Does Davis-Bacon apply to construction cleaning on a federal job?

It can. When the contract carries Davis-Bacon or Service Contract Act wage determinations, we pay and report cleaning labor as the contract requires, including certified payroll where required.

How far ahead should we schedule cleaning for a federal turnover?

Plan three to six weeks out for a large building, since access paperwork, phased floor releases and the acceptance walk date all drive the crew schedule.

Can your crews follow the prime contractor's safety plan?

Yes. Our crews attend site orientation, follow the prime contractor's site safety plan and activity hazard analyses, and wear the protective equipment the plan calls for.

Who sets the standard for clean on a government project?

The contract and the agency's closeout requirements set it. We clean to that checklist and to the prime contractor's punch list, and document completed areas for the record.

What else is federal government building construction cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "federal building cleaning" and "GSA building construction cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
