Military base construction and renovation cleaning

Military base construction and renovation cleaning is the rough, final and touch-up cleaning of new and renovated buildings on military installations, performed under the prime contractor's schedule and the installation's access, safety and environmental rules.

Why it matters

Base buildings turn over on mission-driven dates and are inspected closely by the government. Dust, debris or missed areas can hold up acceptance and the units waiting to move in.

How we do it

Crews work under the prime contractor, complete whatever background checks, badging or escorts the installation requires, and clean to the project's closeout checklist with documented sign-off.

Our capture-first method

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Building type and square footage
  • Rough, final and touch-up phases
  • Installation access and badging lead time
  • Escort requirements for restricted areas
  • Waste handling under base environmental rules
  • Closeout checklist and photo documentation

Watch-outs

  • Installation access paperwork can take weeks, so start it early.
  • Escort requirements can cut daily production in restricted buildings.
  • Wash water and debris must follow the installation's environmental rules.
  • Mission schedules can close gates or buildings with little notice.

Common questions

Do you clean new buildings on Army installations?

Yes. Our crews clean barracks, motor pools, headquarters and training buildings on Army posts, with access arranged through the prime contractor and the installation's own access process.

Can your crews clean construction projects on Navy bases?

Yes. Navy work often includes pier-side buildings, shipyard support shops and warehouses, and our crews follow the marine environmental rules there. Access is arranged through the prime contractor and the base access process.

Do you clean new buildings on Air Force bases?

Yes. Typical Air Force projects include hangars, flightline support buildings and squadron offices, where we keep strict foreign object debris control. Access goes through the prime contractor and the installation's access process.

Do you handle cleaning on Marine Corps installations?

Yes. Our crews clean barracks, motor pools, armory support buildings and training facilities on Marine Corps installations, with access arranged through the prime contractor and the installation's access procedures.

Can you clean Space Force facilities after construction?

Yes. Space Force projects often mean operations centers, communications buildings and offices with sensitive equipment, so crews use low-dust methods and work escorted where required, with access arranged through the prime contractor and the installation.

Do you clean construction projects at Coast Guard stations?

Yes. Coast Guard work tends to be waterfront stations, boathouses, small piers and housing, where we protect the waterway from debris and wash water. Access is arranged through the prime contractor and the station's access process.

How do your crews get access to a military base?

Through the prime contractor. They sponsor our crews through the installation's access process, which can include background checks, badging and escorts, and we submit the paperwork as early as possible.

What screening do your crews complete for base work?

Our crews complete the background checks, badging or escorts each installation requires, arranged through the prime contractor, and restricted areas are cleaned only under the escort and rules the facility sets.

Who do you work for on a military construction project?

Usually the prime contractor or a subcontractor under them. We follow their schedule, safety plan and closeout list, and the government inspects the result.

How long does a final clean on a base building take?

It depends on size, phases and access rules. Escort requirements and gate times slow production, so we plan crew size around the installation's working hours.

Is environmental cleanup on base part of this service?

Construction cleaning is. Regulated waste or contamination is handled only by licensed, certified technicians under the installation's environmental program, as a separate scope.

What else is military base construction and renovation cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "military installation cleaning", "Army base cleaning", "Navy base cleaning", "Air Force base cleaning", "Marine Corps base cleaning", "Space Force facility cleaning", "Coast Guard station cleaning" and "DoD facility construction cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
