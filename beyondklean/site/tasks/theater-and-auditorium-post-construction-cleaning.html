Theater and auditorium post-construction cleaning

Theater and auditorium post-construction cleaning cleans seating, aisles, stages, orchestra pits, lobbies and control booths in performing arts venues and school auditoriums after construction.

Also called auditorium cleaning

Why it matters

Auditoriums have hundreds of seats, high ceilings and stage equipment. Construction dust settles on seats and lighting and is visible under stage lights.

How we approach it

Crews clean high areas first, clean seating row by row, clean stage floors with care for their finish and leave rigging and stage equipment to stage contractors.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Seat count and type
  • Stage floor finish
  • Ceiling height and lighting
  • Rigging and stage equipment

Watch-outs

  • Stage rigging and lighting are handled only by stage contractors.
  • Stage floors often have special finishes that cleaners must not damage.

Questions about theater and auditorium post-construction cleaning

Are theater stages cleaned after construction?

Stage floors are cleaned with methods suited to their finish; rigging and equipment are left to stage contractors.

Who handles stage rigging during cleanup?

Stage and rigging contractors. Cleaners do not touch rigging, curtains or lighting.

How are auditorium seats cleaned?

Row by row, vacuuming seats and floors and wiping armrests and tablet arms.

Is the auditorium lobby cleaned too?

Yes, lobbies, concessions and restrooms are part of the clean.

What else is theater and auditorium post-construction cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "auditorium cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
