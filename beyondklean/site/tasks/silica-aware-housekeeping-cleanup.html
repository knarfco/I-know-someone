Silica-aware housekeeping cleanup

Silica-aware housekeeping cleanup is construction cleaning done with methods that keep crystalline silica dust out of the air: wet sweeping, HEPA vacuuming and approved sweeping compounds instead of dry sweeping, brushing or compressed air.

Why it matters

OSHA's construction silica standard restricts dry sweeping and compressed-air cleaning where they could expose workers to silica. Concrete, masonry, stone and some joint compounds all contain it.

How we do it

Crews plan housekeeping around the work producing silica dust, use HEPA vacuums and wet methods, and bag collected dust carefully.

Common questions

What is silica-aware housekeeping?

Construction cleaning that keeps crystalline silica dust out of the air by using wet sweeping, HEPA vacuuming and approved compounds instead of dry sweeping or compressed air.

Is dry sweeping allowed on construction sites?

Not where it could contribute to silica exposure, unless wet sweeping, HEPA vacuuming or other safer methods are not feasible.

What construction materials contain crystalline silica?

Concrete, masonry, brick, stone, tile, mortar and some joint compounds.

Is HEPA vacuuming required for silica dust?

OSHA's construction silica standard names HEPA-filtered vacuuming as one of the accepted alternatives to dry sweeping.

What else is silica-aware housekeeping cleanup called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "silica dust cleanup", "crystalline silica housekeeping", "silica-aware wet sweeping", "wet sweep" and "dust suppression sweeping". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
