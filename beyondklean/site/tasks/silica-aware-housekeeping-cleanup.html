Silica-aware housekeeping cleanup

Silica-aware housekeeping cleanup is construction cleaning done with methods that keep crystalline silica dust out of the air: wet sweeping, HEPA vacuuming and approved sweeping compounds instead of dry sweeping, brushing or compressed air.

Also called silica dust cleanup; crystalline silica housekeeping; Silica-aware wet sweeping; wet sweep; dust suppression sweeping

Why it matters

OSHA's construction silica standard restricts dry sweeping and compressed-air cleaning where they could expose workers to silica. Concrete, masonry, stone and some joint compounds all contain it.

How we approach it

Crews plan housekeeping around the work producing silica dust, use HEPA vacuums and wet methods, and bag collected dust carefully.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Silica-producing work on site
  • Housekeeping methods
  • Respiratory protection if required
  • Waste disposal

Watch-outs

  • Dry sweeping and compressed air are restricted where they could expose workers to silica.
  • Collected dust must be handled without creating a cloud.

Questions about silica-aware housekeeping cleanup

What is silica-aware housekeeping?

Cleaning methods that avoid putting silica dust in the air.

Is dry sweeping allowed on construction sites?

Not where it could contribute to silica exposure, unless safer methods are not feasible.

What materials contain silica?

Concrete, masonry, stone, tile and some joint compounds.

Is HEPA vacuuming required for silica?

It is one of the methods the standard names as an alternative to dry sweeping.

What else is silica-aware housekeeping cleanup called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "silica dust cleanup", "crystalline silica housekeeping", "Silica-aware wet sweeping", "wet sweep" and "dust suppression sweeping". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
