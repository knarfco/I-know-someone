Track lighting cleaning

Track lighting cleaning removes dust from track rails, heads, lenses and power feeds in retail, gallery, restaurant and office spaces.

Also called track light dusting

Why it matters

Track heads are aimed at products, art or displays, so dust and fingerprints show in the light pattern. Cleaning must not change the aim the lighting designer set.

How we approach it

Crews wipe rails and heads with damp microfiber and lenses with appropriate cleaners, holding each head steady so its aim does not move.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Linear feet of track and number of heads
  • Lens types and accessories
  • Height and access over displays
  • Aiming that must not be disturbed

Watch-outs

  • Moving track heads changes the lighting design; hold heads steady.
  • Track heads may be hot if recently on.
Keep it clean after turnover. Overhead dust comes back. Recurring high dusting for this surface is handled through our partner program at AboveEyeLevelCleaning.com.

Questions about track lighting cleaning

Will cleaning track lights change where they are aimed?

It should not. Crews hold each head steady while cleaning so the lighting designer's aiming is preserved.

Why clean track lighting in a new store?

Dust on lenses dims the light and shows in the beam on merchandise.

Should track lights be off during cleaning?

Yes, it is safer to turn them off and let them cool before cleaning.

Do track rails need cleaning?

Yes, rails are horizontal ledges that collect dust like any other.

What else is track lighting cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "track light dusting". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
