Stainless steel protective film removal

Stainless steel protective film removal peels the blue or white PVC film off new stainless equipment, panels and appliances and removes the adhesive left behind.

Also called PVC film removal from stainless

Why it matters

Film left on stainless in a hot kitchen bakes on and leaves residue that is hard to remove and looks dirty.

How we approach it

Crews peel film slowly, remove adhesive with stainless-safe removers and clean and dry the surface with the grain.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Equipment with film
  • Age and heat exposure of film
  • Adhesive removers
  • Final wipe

Watch-outs

  • Film near heat sources bakes on quickly.
  • Blades scratch stainless.

Questions about stainless steel protective film removal

When should protective film be removed from kitchen equipment?

Before equipment is fired up and as close to installation as practical, before heat bakes it on.

How do you remove adhesive left by stainless film?

With a stainless-safe adhesive remover, then clean and dry the surface with the grain.

Why is old protective film hard to remove?

Heat and time harden the adhesive.

Who removes equipment film in a new kitchen?

The installer or the cleaning crew, depending on the contract.

What else is stainless steel protective film removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "PVC film removal from stainless". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
