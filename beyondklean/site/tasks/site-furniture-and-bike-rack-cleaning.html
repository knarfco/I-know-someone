Site furniture and bike rack cleaning

Site furniture and bike rack cleaning cleans benches, picnic tables, trash receptacles, planters, bike racks and bollards, removing labels, dust, mud and concrete splatter.

Also called site furnishing cleaning

Why it matters

Site furniture is used the day the building opens, and it often arrives with labels and gets splattered during landscape and paving work.

How we approach it

Crews remove labels and adhesive, wash surfaces with finish-safe cleaners and remove concrete splatter from bases and mounting plates.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Furniture items and finishes
  • Labels and adhesive
  • Concrete on mounting plates
  • Receptacle liners

Watch-outs

  • Powder-coated finishes scratch with abrasive pads.
  • Solvents can damage recycled plastic furniture.

Questions about site furniture and bike rack cleaning

Is site furniture cleaned before a building opens?

Yes, benches, tables, receptacles and bike racks are cleaned and labels removed.

How is concrete removed from bike rack bases?

With careful scraping and cementitious residue removers safe for the finish.

Do trash receptacles need liners at turnover?

Owners often want liners installed; confirm in the scope.

What cleaner is safe for powder-coated site furniture?

Mild detergent with water and soft brushes or cloths. Abrasive pads and harsh solvents damage powder-coated finishes.

What else is site furniture and bike rack cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "site furnishing cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
