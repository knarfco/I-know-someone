Trash room and chute room cleaning

Trash room and chute room cleaning cleans trash and recycling rooms, chute doors, compactor areas and floors in residential and mixed-use buildings before occupancy.

Also called trash room cleaning

Why it matters

Trash rooms collect construction debris and odors, and chute doors on every floor are seen by residents. A dirty trash room causes complaints from day one.

How we approach it

Crews remove debris, degrease floors and walls, clean chute doors floor by floor and leave compactor equipment to its service provider.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Rooms and floors with chute doors
  • Compactor equipment
  • Floor drains
  • Odor control

Watch-outs

  • Compactors must be locked out.
  • Chute interiors need specialist cleaning.

Questions about trash room and chute room cleaning

Are trash chutes cleaned in new buildings?

Chute doors and rooms are cleaned; chute interiors are cleaned by specialists if needed.

Why do trash rooms smell in new buildings?

Construction debris and food waste from workers can leave odors before residents arrive.

Is the compactor cleaned?

Exteriors around it are; the compactor itself is serviced by its provider.

When are trash rooms cleaned?

Before residents move in, as part of common-area turnover.

What else is trash room and chute room cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "trash room cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
