Ceiling tile scrap and carton removal

Ceiling tile scrap and carton removal collects cut acoustical ceiling tile pieces, dust and boxes after the ceiling installer finishes each area.

Also called ACT scrap removal

Why it matters

Mineral fiber tile crumbles into fine white dust that gets tracked everywhere. Tile boxes are bulky and arrive in large quantities.

How we approach it

Crews collect cut pieces and cartons as each area is finished, HEPA vacuum tile dust, and flag attic stock boxes to be kept rather than thrown out.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Ceiling area and installation sequence
  • Attic stock requirements
  • Carton recycling
  • Dust control

Watch-outs

  • Do not discard attic stock cartons the owner needs to keep.
  • Tile dust spreads easily when walked on.

Questions about ceiling tile scrap and carton removal

Why is ceiling tile dust so messy?

Mineral fiber tiles shed fine white dust when they are cut, and it tracks onto every floor and surface nearby.

Should spare ceiling tiles be thrown away?

No. Specifications usually require attic stock, so spare cartons are kept and staged for the owner.

Who cleans up after ceiling installers?

The ceiling contractor or the cleanup crew, depending on the contract.

Can ceiling tile scraps be recycled?

Some ceiling manufacturers run take-back programs for certain tile types.

What else is ceiling tile scrap and carton removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "ACT scrap removal". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
