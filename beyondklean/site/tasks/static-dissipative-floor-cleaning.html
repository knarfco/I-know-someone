Static-dissipative floor cleaning

Static-dissipative floor cleaning cleans ESD and static-control floors with cleaners that do not leave insulating residue, preserving the floor's electrical performance.

Also called ESD floor cleaning

Why it matters

Static-control floors protect sensitive electronics from static discharge. Wax and residue-forming cleaners can insulate the surface and stop the floor from working.

How we approach it

Crews use manufacturer-approved cleaners only, avoid finishes unless specified and may coordinate testing.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Floor type and area
  • Manufacturer cleaners
  • Testing requirements
  • Equipment in the space

Watch-outs

  • Wax and residue-leaving cleaners can block static dissipation.
  • Only manufacturer-approved cleaners should be used.

Questions about static-dissipative floor cleaning

What is a static-dissipative floor?

A floor designed to safely drain static electricity to ground, protecting sensitive electronics.

Can ESD floors be waxed?

Usually not, unless a special conductive or dissipative finish is specified by the manufacturer.

Where are static-dissipative floors used?

In electronics manufacturing, labs, data centers, hospital imaging rooms and control rooms.

How is static-dissipative floor performance checked?

With electrical resistance testing according to the manufacturer and project specifications.

What else is static-dissipative floor cleaning called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "ESD floor cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
