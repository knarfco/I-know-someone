Rough Clean · Rough clean (phase 1)

Rough clean of entire building

A rough clean of the entire building is the first construction cleaning phase, done after framing, drywall and MEP rough-in. It removes bulk debris, heavy dust and slab residue from every floor before finish trades begin.

Also called phase 1 clean; rough cleaning

Why it matters

If rough-stage dust and debris are left, they get sealed behind ceilings, ground into new flooring and pulled into ductwork. Every later phase costs more because of it.

How we approach it

Crews work top floor down, removing debris first, then HEPA vacuuming and scraping slabs, so dust from upper floors does not fall onto floors already cleaned.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Total square footage and floor count
  • Trades that must be complete before the rough clean
  • Debris removal route and container plan
  • Slab scraping and HEPA vacuuming requirements

Watch-outs

  • Starting before drywall finishing is done means repeating the work.
  • A rough clean is not detail cleaning; set expectations with the owner.

Questions about rough clean of entire building

What does a rough clean include?

Bulk debris removal, slab scraping, HEPA vacuuming of floors, sills and openings, and clearing mechanical rooms and shafts.

How is a rough clean priced?

Usually by square foot or by crew-day, based on debris volume, floors and access.

Is a rough clean required by specs?

Many specs call for progress cleaning that covers it. It is also a practical step before finishes.

Can the rough clean be skipped on small projects?

Sometimes, on small tenant spaces, if trades clean as they go. It usually makes the final clean harder.

What else is rough clean of entire building called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "phase 1 clean" and "rough cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
