Concrete dust removal after grinding or saw cutting

Concrete dust removal after grinding or saw cutting captures the fine, silica-containing dust and slurry left by concrete grinding, coring, saw cutting and chipping.

Also called concrete dust cleanup; slurry dust cleanup

Why it matters

Concrete dust contains crystalline silica, which is regulated by OSHA. It is also abrasive and spreads through the building if not captured.

How we approach it

Crews use HEPA vacuums and wet methods, never dry sweeping or compressed air, collect slurry for proper disposal and keep it out of drains.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Areas where concrete work occurred
  • Dust or slurry
  • Silica exposure controls
  • Disposal of slurry

Watch-outs

  • Dry sweeping or blowing concrete dust is restricted under OSHA's silica standard.
  • Slurry should not be washed into drains.

Questions about concrete dust removal after grinding or saw cutting

Is concrete dust hazardous?

Yes. It contains respirable crystalline silica, which OSHA regulates because it causes lung disease.

How do you clean up concrete dust safely?

With HEPA vacuums and wet methods, not dry sweeping or compressed air.

What is concrete slurry?

The wet mixture of water and fine concrete produced by wet cutting, coring or grinding.

Can concrete slurry go down the drain?

No. Slurry is collected, allowed to settle or solidify and disposed of properly.

What else is concrete dust removal after grinding or saw cutting called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "concrete dust cleanup" and "slurry dust cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
