Concrete stain and oil spot treatment

Concrete stain and oil spot treatment removes oil, grease, hydraulic fluid, food and other stains from concrete floors and slabs using absorbents, degreasers and poultices suited to the stain and floor finish.

Why it matters

Oil stains on concrete look neglected and can be slippery. Concrete absorbs oil, so stains get harder to remove over time.

How we do it

Crews absorb fresh spills, then treat stains with degreasers or poultices and scrub, testing on sealed floors.

Common questions

How are oil stains removed from concrete floors?

Fresh spills are absorbed first, then degreasers and poultices draw the oil out of the pores, followed by scrubbing and rinsing.

Can old oil stains be removed from concrete?

Often partly. Oil that has soaked in for months may only lighten, especially on unsealed concrete.

What is a poultice for concrete stains?

A paste of absorbent material and cleaner that pulls stains out of porous concrete as it dries.

Are oil stains on concrete a safety concern?

Yes. Oil and grease on concrete are slippery, especially when wet, so stains near walkways and docks are a safety concern as well as a cosmetic one.

What else is concrete stain and oil spot treatment called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "oil stain removal". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a bid request under any of them gets the same crew and the same line item.
