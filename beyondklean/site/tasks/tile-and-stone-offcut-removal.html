Tile and stone offcut removal

Tile and stone offcut removal collects ceramic, porcelain and stone scraps, broken pieces, mortar bags and cartons left by tile setters.

Also called masonry scrap pickup

Why it matters

Tile and stone scraps are heavy, sharp-edged and can scratch other finishes. Mortar and grout bags leave cementitious dust.

How we approach it

Crews collect scraps into heavy-duty containers, keep attic stock tile separate, and HEPA vacuum the cementitious dust from cutting and mixing.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Tile and stone areas
  • Attic stock requirements
  • Heavy debris handling
  • Dust from cutting and mixing

Watch-outs

  • Tile edges are sharp; use gloves.
  • Do not wet sweep cement dust into floor drains.

Questions about tile and stone offcut removal

Can tile scraps be recycled?

Some aggregate recyclers accept tile and stone; most is landfilled.

Why keep extra tile?

Spare tile from the same production lot is used for future repairs.

Is tile cutting dust hazardous?

Yes. Tile and stone dust can contain crystalline silica.

Who removes tile debris?

The tile setter or the cleanup crew.

What else is tile and stone offcut removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "masonry scrap pickup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
