Combustible waste removal

Combustible waste removal regularly clears paper, cardboard, wood, packaging, oily rags and other burnable debris from the building to reduce fire risk.

Also called fire load reduction cleanup

Why it matters

Construction fires often start in accumulated combustible waste, especially near hot work. OSHA requires regular removal of combustible scrap.

How we approach it

Crews remove combustible waste on a set schedule, keep it away from hot work areas and use approved containers for oily rags.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Combustible materials present
  • Hot work areas
  • Removal frequency
  • Oily rag containers

Watch-outs

  • Oily rags can self-heat and ignite; use approved closed containers.
  • Keep combustibles away from welding and cutting.

Questions about combustible waste removal

Why remove combustible waste on construction sites?

Accumulated waste is fuel for fires, especially near hot work.

How should oily rags be stored?

In approved, closed metal containers.

How often should combustible waste be removed?

Regularly, often daily on active sites.

What is hot work?

Welding, cutting, brazing and other work that produces sparks or flame.

What else is combustible waste removal called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "fire load reduction cleanup". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
