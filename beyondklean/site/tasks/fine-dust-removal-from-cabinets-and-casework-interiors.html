Fine dust removal from cabinets and casework interiors

Fine dust removal from cabinets and casework interiors vacuums and wipes the inside of cabinets, drawers, shelves and casework boxes after installation.

Also called cabinet interior dusting

Why it matters

Owners and inspectors open cabinets and drawers. Dust, screws and packaging inside them make a new building look unfinished.

How we approach it

Crews remove debris and packaging, HEPA vacuum corners and shelf pin holes, then wipe with damp microfiber suited to the finish.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Number of cabinets and drawers
  • Finishes
  • Hardware and shelf pins
  • Protective film on interiors

Watch-outs

  • Excess water damages particleboard edges.
  • Do not adjust hinges or slides.

Questions about fine dust removal from cabinets and casework interiors

Do cabinet interiors need cleaning in a new building?

Yes. They collect dust, sawdust, screws and packaging during construction.

How do you clean inside new cabinets?

Vacuum corners and shelf pin holes, then wipe with damp microfiber suited to the finish.

What is commonly found inside new cabinets?

Construction dust, sawdust, loose screws, shelf pins and installation packaging. Owners usually find them the first time they open a drawer.

Who cleans cabinet interiors at turnover?

The cleaning crew handles cabinet interiors during the final clean, opening every door and drawer.

What else is fine dust removal from cabinets and casework interiors called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "cabinet interior dusting". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
