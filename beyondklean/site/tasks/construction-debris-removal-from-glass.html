Construction debris removal from glass

Construction debris removal from glass takes off paint, stucco, plaster, tape, sealant and other construction residue from new glass using methods that protect the surface.

Also called paint and stucco removal from glass

Why it matters

Debris on glass is common on every project. Improper removal is a leading cause of scratched glass and disputes between GCs, glaziers and cleaners.

How we approach it

Crews inspect the glass type, soak debris, use fresh, lubricated blades only where appropriate and document existing damage before starting.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Glass types and coatings
  • Residues present
  • Existing damage documentation
  • Scratch waiver terms

Watch-outs

  • Dragging debris under a blade causes scratches.
  • Some coated glass must never be scraped.

Questions about construction debris removal from glass

What causes scratches during construction window cleaning?

Debris dragged under a blade, dull or dirty blades, dry scraping and scraping coated or tempered glass that should not be scraped.

What is a glass scratch waiver?

A contract clause, recommended by the window cleaning industry, that addresses liability for scratches that can occur when construction debris is removed.

Should existing glass damage be documented before cleaning?

Yes. Photos and notes before cleaning show which scratches and defects were already there.

Is removing construction debris from glass a specialty?

It takes training and judgment about glass types and coatings, so experienced crews are best.

What else is construction debris removal from glass called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "paint and stucco removal from glass". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
