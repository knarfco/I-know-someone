Equipment exterior wipe-down

Equipment exterior wipe-down cleans the outer housings of installed building equipment such as water heaters, pumps, compressors, panels and appliances, removing dust, labels, overspray and fingerprints.

Also called equipment cleaning

Why it matters

New equipment is part of what the owner accepts at turnover. Dusty housings look neglected, and dust on vents and grilles affects cooling.

How we approach it

Crews HEPA vacuum vents and grilles, wipe housings with damp microfiber and neutral cleaner, and leave nameplates and required labels in place.

Methods follow our capture-first, least-aggressive-chemistry standard.

Scope checklist for your bid

  • Equipment list by room
  • Required labels and nameplates to keep
  • Energized or hot equipment
  • Finishes: painted, stainless or plastic

Watch-outs

  • Do not remove nameplates, warning labels or inspection tags.
  • Hot surfaces must cool before cleaning.

Questions about equipment exterior wipe-down

Should labels be removed from new equipment?

Shipping and protective labels can come off, but nameplates, warning labels, and inspection tags must stay.

How is dust removed from equipment vents?

With a HEPA vacuum and brush attachment, so dust is captured instead of pushed inside.

Can equipment be wiped while it is running?

Only cool, safe exterior surfaces, and only where the responsible trade allows it. Many owners require equipment to be off.

Why does equipment need to be clean at turnover?

The owner accepts it as new, and clean housings make leaks, damage and missing labels easy to see.

What else is equipment exterior wipe-down called?

Contractors, specs and buyers also call it "equipment cleaning". On Beyond Klean, all of these names point to the same scope, so a request under any of them reaches the same qualified contractors.
