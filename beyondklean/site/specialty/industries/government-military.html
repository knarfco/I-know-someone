Specialty cleaning for government & military

Federal, state and local agencies and military installations buy specialty cleaning through formal solicitations. Work often takes place in secure areas and must follow contract specifications, wage rules and base access requirements.

Common needs

  • Hangar, motor pool and maintenance facility cleaning
  • Firing range lead cleanup and maintenance
  • Barracks and housing mold remediation
  • HVAC and duct cleaning in older buildings
  • Hazardous waste and spill cleanup
  • Post-construction cleaning for new or renovated facilities

Rules and standards

  • Active SAM.gov registration for federal contracts
  • Service Contract Act wage determinations on many federal service contracts
  • Installation access, background checks and, for some sites, security clearances
  • Contract specifications such as Unified Facilities Guide Specifications, plus OSHA and EPA rules

Questions

Where are government cleaning projects posted?

Federal opportunities over set thresholds are posted on SAM.gov. State and local agencies use their own procurement portals.

What makes firing range work different?

Ranges collect lead dust and debris. Cleanup typically involves OSHA lead standard compliance, HEPA equipment, air monitoring and waste testing before disposal.

Can contractors without clearances work on bases?

Many jobs require only base access credentials and background checks. Work in classified areas may require cleared personnel or escorts, which the solicitation will state.
