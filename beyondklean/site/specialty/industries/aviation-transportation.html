Specialty cleaning for aviation & transportation

Airports, airlines, rail yards and transit agencies clean aircraft, vehicles, terminals and the infrastructure that supports them. Schedules are tight, security is strict and some surfaces affect safety directly.

Common needs

  • Aircraft interior and exterior cleaning
  • Hangar floor and maintenance shop cleaning
  • Runway rubber removal
  • Terminal high-level and glass cleaning
  • Rail car, bus and transit vehicle deep cleaning
  • Tunnel, station and platform cleaning

Rules and standards

  • Airport security badging and background checks for airside access
  • FAA Advisory Circular 150/5320-12 on runway friction and rubber removal
  • Aircraft manufacturer and operator maintenance manual requirements for cleaning products
  • OSHA fall protection and walking-working surfaces rules for work on aircraft and vehicles

Questions

Why do aircraft cleaning products need approval?

Some chemicals can damage aluminum, composites, seals or acrylic windows. Operators usually require products that meet the airframe manufacturer's specifications.

How is runway rubber removal scheduled?

Airports schedule it from friction testing and traffic levels, usually during low-traffic hours or closures. It involves high-pressure water or other methods and coordination with airfield operations.

What slows transportation cleaning projects?

Badging, short work windows between trips or flights, and limited access to tracks or airside areas. Building those into the schedule avoids surprises.
