Specialty cleaning for ports & marine

Ports, terminals, shipyards and vessels need cleaning in tanks, holds, bilges and cargo areas. Much of this work happens in confined spaces near water, which brings environmental and safety rules that do not apply on land.

Common needs

  • Cargo hold and tank cleaning on vessels and barges
  • Bilge and oily water removal
  • Terminal warehouse and shed cleaning
  • Crane, conveyor and bulk handling equipment cleaning
  • Hull and dock area cleaning
  • Spill response on docks and waterways

Rules and standards

  • OSHA 29 CFR Part 1915 shipyard standards, including Subpart B for confined and enclosed spaces
  • NFPA 306 marine chemist certificates for gas-free conditions before entry or hot work
  • TWIC credentials for unescorted access to MTSA-regulated facilities and vessels
  • Clean Water Act and Coast Guard rules on oil discharge and wastewater

Questions

What is a marine chemist certificate?

It is a certificate issued under NFPA 306 stating whether a space on a vessel is safe for entry or hot work. Many tank and hold jobs need one before work starts.

Do contractor crews need TWIC cards?

Workers who need unescorted access to secure areas of regulated port facilities and vessels generally need a TWIC. Escort arrangements are possible but slow the work.

How is wash water handled?

Oily or contaminated water must be collected and disposed of properly, not discharged overboard or to storm drains. The scope should name the receiving facility.
