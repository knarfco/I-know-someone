Specialty cleaning for retail & hospitality

Stores, hotels, restaurants and venues need specialty work that happens overnight or between events without disturbing guests. Appearance matters, but so do fire safety and health code requirements.

Common needs

  • Kitchen exhaust hood and duct cleaning
  • Storefront, atrium and high-level glass cleaning
  • Biohazard and trauma cleanup in guest rooms or public areas
  • Mold and water damage remediation
  • High-ceiling and fixture dusting
  • Odor removal and post-fire smoke cleaning

Rules and standards

  • NFPA 96 inspection and cleaning frequencies for kitchen exhaust
  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens standard for staff and contractors exposed to blood
  • Local health department food code requirements
  • OSHA 1910.27 and IWCA I-14.1 for exterior window work on taller buildings

Questions

How often should a restaurant hood be cleaned?

NFPA 96 sets inspection frequency by cooking volume and type, from monthly for solid-fuel cooking to annually for low-volume operations. Systems are cleaned when inspection finds grease buildup.

Who should clean up blood or bodily fluids in a hotel?

Small incidents can be handled by trained staff under a bloodborne pathogens exposure control plan. Larger scenes typically go to a specialized biohazard cleanup company.

How can stores avoid disrupting customers?

Schedule work after hours, define staging and protection in the scope and confirm crews know the store's security and alarm procedures.
