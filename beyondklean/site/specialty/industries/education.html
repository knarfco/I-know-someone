Specialty cleaning for education

Schools, colleges and universities do most specialty work during summer and holiday breaks. Older buildings often contain asbestos or lead paint, and child-occupied areas carry additional rules.

Common needs

  • Summer deep cleaning and floor restoration
  • HVAC duct and unit ventilator cleaning
  • Mold remediation after leaks or humidity problems
  • Asbestos and lead work during renovations
  • Kitchen exhaust cleaning in cafeterias
  • Lab, science room and chemical storage cleanout

Rules and standards

  • AHERA asbestos rules for K-12 schools, 40 CFR Part 763 Subpart E, including management plans
  • EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting rule for pre-1978 child-occupied facilities, 40 CFR Part 745
  • OSHA standards for workers, plus state rules on cleaning products in schools in some states
  • NFPA 96 for cafeteria kitchen exhaust

Questions

Why does AHERA matter for cleaning projects?

K-12 schools must keep an asbestos management plan. Any work that could disturb asbestos-containing material must be checked against that plan and done by accredited personnel.

When should schools book summer projects?

Many schools start solicitations in winter or early spring. Contractors fill their summer schedules early, and some work needs testing or permits first.

What extra steps apply to child-occupied areas?

Lead-safe work practices under the RRP rule apply to many renovations in pre-1978 buildings. Products and re-occupancy timing should also be reviewed.
