Specialty cleaning for data centers

Data centers keep particles and corrosive gases away from servers and cooling equipment. Cleaning is done with tools and methods that will not cause static discharge, airflow disruption or accidental shutdowns.

Common needs

  • Subfloor plenum and raised floor cleaning
  • Rack exterior, cabinet top and cable tray cleaning
  • Post-construction cleaning before equipment goes live
  • Air handler and CRAC/CRAH unit coil cleaning
  • Particle counting and contamination testing
  • Overhead and structural dust removal

Rules and standards

  • ISO 14644-1 cleanliness classes, often used as a target for white-space air quality
  • ASHRAE TC 9.9 guidance on particulate and gaseous contamination
  • NFPA 75 for the protection of IT equipment
  • Site security, access control and change management procedures

Questions

Why use specialists instead of the janitorial team?

Data hall cleaning uses HEPA vacuums, anti-static tools and controlled methods near live equipment. Mistakes can trip fire suppression, disturb airflow or damage hardware.

How often is a data hall cleaned?

Many operators schedule it annually or semiannually, plus after construction, equipment moves or contamination events. Particle counts can help set the interval.

What should a contractor provide after cleaning?

A report with areas cleaned, methods used, before-and-after photos and, if included in the scope, particle count results against an agreed class.
