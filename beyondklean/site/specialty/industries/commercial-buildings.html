Specialty cleaning for commercial buildings

Office towers, mixed-use buildings and multi-tenant properties need periodic work that day porters and janitorial crews do not cover. Much of it involves height, building systems or regulated materials in older buildings.

Common needs

  • High-rise window and facade cleaning
  • HVAC duct and air handler cleaning
  • Kitchen exhaust cleaning for tenant restaurants
  • Mold remediation and water damage restoration
  • Asbestos or lead work during tenant improvements
  • Parking garage and loading dock cleaning

Rules and standards

  • OSHA 29 CFR 1910.27 for rope descent systems and certified anchorages
  • ANSI/IWCA I-14.1 window cleaning safety standard
  • EPA asbestos NESHAP, 40 CFR Part 61 Subpart M, before renovation or demolition
  • ASHRAE Standard 62.1 for ventilation and indoor air quality

Questions

Who is responsible for roof anchorages?

The building owner must provide the employer with written information that anchorages have been identified, tested, certified and maintained, as required by OSHA 1910.27. Contractors should ask for it before work starts.

Do older buildings need testing before renovation cleaning?

Often yes. Before materials are disturbed, an asbestos inspection is generally required for renovation and demolition under NESHAP, and painted surfaces in older buildings may contain lead.

How can multi-tenant buildings limit disruption?

Schedule work at night or on weekends, notify tenants early and define access, elevator use and protection of finishes in the scope.
