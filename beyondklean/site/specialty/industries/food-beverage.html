Specialty cleaning for food & beverage

Food plants, breweries, bakeries and commercial kitchens clean constantly, but some work goes beyond daily sanitation. Specialty contractors handle high-level surfaces, exhaust systems, deep equipment cleaning and contamination events.

Common needs

  • Overhead and structural cleaning above production areas
  • Kitchen exhaust hood and duct cleaning
  • Dry ice blasting of ovens, mixers and packaging lines
  • Spiral freezer, cooler and evaporator cleaning
  • Tank, silo and bin cleaning
  • Post-contamination or allergen changeover deep cleaning

Rules and standards

  • FDA preventive controls rules under FSMA, 21 CFR Part 117, including sanitation controls
  • USDA FSIS sanitation SOP requirements, 9 CFR Part 416, at meat and poultry plants
  • NFPA 96 for commercial kitchen exhaust systems
  • NFPA 660 where grain, flour, sugar or other combustible food dusts are present

Questions

How do outside contractors fit into a food safety plan?

Their work should follow the plant's sanitation procedures, chemical approvals and GMP rules. Ask contractors for their GMP training and how they prevent cross-contamination between sites.

Which chemicals can contractors use in food areas?

Only products the plant has approved for that use, with SDSs on file. Food-contact surfaces usually need rinse and sanitize steps after cleaning.

Why is dry ice blasting common in food plants?

It leaves no secondary residue or water and can clean some equipment in place. It still produces noise and CO2, so ventilation and hearing protection need planning.
