Specialty cleaning for manufacturing

Plants run equipment that collects oil, chips, process residue and fine dust, often in places that are hard to reach safely. Cleaning that cannot happen during production is usually planned around shutdowns, line changeovers or weekend windows.

Common needs

  • Machine tool, press and conveyor degreasing
  • High-level dust removal from beams, trusses, ducts and light fixtures
  • Dust collector, baghouse and ventilation ductwork cleaning
  • Paint booth, oven and finishing line cleaning
  • Pit, trench, sump and coolant tank cleanout
  • Dry ice or media blasting of molds, dies and production equipment

Rules and standards

  • NFPA 660 (2025), which consolidated the earlier combustible dust standards such as NFPA 652 and 654, and OSHA's Combustible Dust National Emphasis Program
  • OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 lockout/tagout before equipment is cleaned
  • OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146 permit-required confined spaces for pits, tanks and hoppers
  • OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1200 hazard communication for cleaning chemicals and process residues

Questions

Why does dust on overhead surfaces matter in a plant?

Many process dusts are combustible. A thin layer on beams and ducts can be lifted into the air by a small event and fuel a larger secondary explosion. Fire codes and OSHA inspectors look at accumulated dust, so many plants schedule high-level cleaning at set intervals.

Can cleaning happen while lines are running?

Some can, such as floors and overhead areas away from moving equipment. Machine cleaning usually requires lockout/tagout, so it is scheduled during shutdowns, changeovers or off shifts.

What should a plant send contractors before a site visit?

Send a list of areas and equipment, process materials and SDSs, shutdown windows, site safety rules and access limits. Photos and drawings help contractors give comparable bids.
