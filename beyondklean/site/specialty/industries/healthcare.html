Specialty cleaning for healthcare

Hospitals, clinics, labs and pharmacies need cleaning that protects patients and meets infection prevention requirements. Specialty work is often done next to occupied areas, so containment and coordination with infection prevention staff matter.

Common needs

  • Construction and renovation cleaning under an infection control risk assessment
  • Operating room and sterile processing terminal cleaning
  • Cleanroom and compounding pharmacy cleaning
  • HVAC duct and air handler cleaning
  • Biohazard and bodily fluid cleanup
  • Mold remediation in occupied buildings

Rules and standards

  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030
  • Infection control risk assessment (ICRA) practices referenced in the FGI Guidelines and by accreditation surveyors
  • USP <797> and <800> cleaning requirements for sterile and hazardous drug compounding areas
  • State rules on regulated medical waste handling and transport

Questions

What is an ICRA and why does it affect cleaning?

An infection control risk assessment rates the risk of construction or maintenance work to nearby patients and sets the required precautions. Cleaning contractors working in those areas must follow the containment, barrier and cleaning steps it sets.

Can regular housekeeping staff handle specialty cleaning?

Routine cleaning, yes. Work such as construction cleaning in sensitive areas, duct cleaning, mold remediation or large biohazard events usually goes to contractors with specific training and equipment.

What documentation do healthcare buyers usually request?

Training records, safety data sheets, product EPA registration numbers for disinfectants, proof of insurance and a written work plan. Accredited facilities often want records that can be shown to surveyors.
