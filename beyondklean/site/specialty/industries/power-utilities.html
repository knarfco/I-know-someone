Specialty cleaning for power generation & utilities

Power plants, substations and water and wastewater facilities need cleaning that fits outage schedules and strict site safety programs. Much of the work involves ash, coal dust, scale, sludge or electrical equipment that must stay de-energized.

Common needs

  • Boiler, ash hopper and precipitator cleaning during outages
  • Coal dust and fly ash removal from conveyors, galleries and structures
  • Cooling tower, condenser and heat exchanger cleaning
  • Insulator and substation equipment cleaning
  • Wastewater digester, clarifier and wet well cleanout
  • Turbine hall and control building housekeeping

Rules and standards

  • OSHA 29 CFR 1910.269 for work in electric power generation, transmission and distribution
  • OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146 confined spaces for boilers, tanks, digesters and vaults
  • NRC licensing and 10 CFR Part 20 radiation protection rules at nuclear plants
  • EPA and state rules on handling and disposal of ash, sludge and wash water

Questions

How is outage cleaning usually scheduled?

Plants plan it months ahead as part of the outage scope. Contractors are expected to staff around the critical path, work multiple shifts and finish inside a fixed window.

Do contractors need special training for utility sites?

Expect site-specific orientation, plant safety program training and, for electrical areas, training required under 1910.269. Nuclear sites add radiation worker training and access screening.

Who handles the waste?

The scope should say. Ash, sludge and wash water may need sampling and permitted disposal, and the generator of the waste usually keeps legal responsibility for it.
