Specialty cleaning for oil, gas & chemical

Refineries, chemical plants, terminals and pipelines generate some of the most hazardous cleaning work in industry. Tanks, vessels, exchangers and sewers often hold flammable, toxic or reactive residues and are cleaned during planned turnarounds.

Common needs

  • Storage tank cleaning and sludge removal
  • Heat exchanger bundle and tube hydroblasting
  • Vessel, reactor and column cleaning during turnarounds
  • Industrial vacuum and catalyst handling
  • Process sewer, sump and separator cleanout
  • Spill response and contaminated soil or debris removal

Rules and standards

  • OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119 process safety management, including management of change and pre-startup review
  • OSHA HAZWOPER, 29 CFR 1910.120, where hazardous substances or emergency response are involved
  • ANSI/API Standard 2015 for safe entry and cleaning of petroleum storage tanks
  • RCRA rules for hazardous waste generated during cleaning, and DOT hazmat rules for transport

Questions

What qualifications matter most for tank and vessel cleaning?

Look for a documented confined space program, gas testing and rescue capability, HAZWOPER-trained crews where required and experience with the specific product. Ask for safety statistics and references for similar work.

Who owns waste generated during cleaning?

The facility is typically the generator. The contract should say who characterizes, packages, manifests and transports the waste, and to which permitted facility.

Why do turnarounds need early contractor involvement?

Cleaning often sits on the critical path. Early planning settles equipment, water supply, waste handling and permits so the work does not delay restart.
