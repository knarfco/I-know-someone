Biohazard & Infectious Material Cleanup

This family covers cleanup of blood, body fluids, decomposition, sewage, animal waste, sharps and other infectious material. Buyers include property managers, hotels, employers, public agencies, healthcare facilities and families dealing with a difficult event.

Biohazard cleanup exposes workers to bloodborne and airborne pathogens and generates waste that many states regulate as medical waste. Specialists bring OSHA-compliant training, protective equipment and disposal contracts, and they can work discreetly in sensitive situations.

Questions about biohazard cleanup

Does insurance cover biohazard cleanup?

Many commercial and homeowners policies cover some trauma, death or sewage cleanup, but coverage varies. Contact your insurer early and ask the contractor whether they can provide documentation for a claim.

Is there a national license for biohazard cleanup?

No single federal license exists. Employers must follow OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens Standard, and some states, such as California, require registration for trauma scene cleanup. Requirements vary by state.

What happens to the waste?

Blood-soaked materials and sharps are usually managed as regulated medical waste under state rules and shipped under DOT rules to a treatment facility. Ask for disposal records.
