Commercial HVAC & Ventilation Cleaning

This family covers cleaning of air handling, exhaust and heat transfer systems in commercial and industrial buildings. Buyers include facility managers, property managers, restaurant operators, plant engineers and laboratory safety officers.

Dirty ductwork, coils and exhaust systems waste energy, spread contaminants and, in kitchens and dust collection, create fire and explosion hazards. Specialists bring the containment equipment, access methods and documentation that insurers, fire marshals and building codes expect.

Questions about hvac & ventilation

How do I know if a duct cleaning contractor follows a recognized standard?

Ask whether the work will be done to ACR, the NADCA Standard, and whether an Air Systems Cleaning Specialist (ASCS) will supervise the job. A contractor working to ACR should provide before-and-after documentation and a post-cleaning verification method.

Is kitchen exhaust cleaning required by code?

NFPA 96 requires commercial cooking exhaust systems to be inspected and cleaned at intervals based on cooking volume and fuel type. The local fire marshal or authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) enforces it, and some jurisdictions require cleaners to hold a specific certification.

Can HVAC cleaning be done while the building is occupied?

Often, yes. Contractors typically work at night or in zones, keep the section under negative pressure with HEPA-filtered collection equipment, and seal openings so dust does not enter occupied space.
