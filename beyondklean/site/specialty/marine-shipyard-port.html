Specialty

Marine, Shipyard & Port Cleaning

This family covers cleaning on vessels, in shipyards and dry docks, and across port, terminal and marina property. Buyers include ship owners and operators, shipyards, port authorities, terminal operators, barge fleets and marina managers.

Vessel spaces can hold flammable vapors, toxic residues and too little oxygen, and shipyard rules control who may declare a space safe to enter or weld in. Spills, wash water and blast debris near open water also bring Clean Water Act exposure, so buyers look for crews that know maritime safety rules and environmental controls.

Questions about marine & port

Which OSHA rules apply to cleaning work on ships?

Work on a vessel in a shipyard or at a pier falls under OSHA's shipyard employment standards in 29 CFR Part 1915, including Subpart B for confined and enclosed spaces. Cargo handling areas on land fall under the marine terminal standards in 29 CFR Part 1917. Ask the contractor which part they work under for your job.

What is a Marine Chemist?

A Marine Chemist is a professional certified under the program administered by the National Fire Protection Association. They test vessel spaces and issue certificates under NFPA 306 stating whether a space is safe for workers, safe for hot work, or neither. OSHA requires a Marine Chemist certificate before hot work in many spaces that held flammable liquids or gases.

Who handles the waste from marine cleaning?

Oily water, sludge, tank residues and spent blast media must be collected, characterized and sent to permitted receivers. The vessel or facility usually remains the generator of record, so ask the contractor for manifests, receipts and the name of each disposal or recycling facility.
