Transportation & Aviation Cleaning

This family covers cleaning for aircraft, airports, rail, transit fleets and tank trucks. Buyers include airlines, fixed-base operators, aircraft owners, airport authorities, railroads, transit agencies and trucking fleets.

Vehicles and aircraft have strict maker rules on what products and methods can touch them, and a mistake can damage parts or ground equipment. Work also happens inside secured airfields or rail yards, near live traffic, and often produces wash water that falls under permits.

Questions about transportation

Does aircraft cleaning require an FAA certificate?

Washing and surface cleaning are generally not maintenance. Removing panels, seats or parts, or applying certain treatments, is maintenance under 14 CFR Part 43 and must be done or supervised by appropriately certificated personnel. Ask how the contractor handles that line.

Who handles security access at airports?

Airports control access to secured areas through badging programs under TSA rules. Contractors usually need badged workers or escorts, so allow time for background checks.

What about wash water from fleets?

Wash water from vehicles, rail cars and aircraft often contains oil, metals and detergents. Most sites must send it to a sanitary sewer under a permit, recycle it, or haul it away.
