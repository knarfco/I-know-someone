Specialty

High-Rise, Facade & Exterior Cleaning

This family covers cleaning work on the outside of buildings and structures, much of it done at height from ropes, powered platforms or aerial lifts. Buyers include property managers, building owners, asset managers, parking operators and facilities teams at campuses and public buildings.

Exterior work at height puts workers, pedestrians and the building envelope at risk. A falling tool, a failed anchor or the wrong chemical on stone or coated glass can cause injury or permanent damage. Specialists bring certified access methods, insurance that covers work at height, and knowledge of how each facade material reacts to cleaning.

Questions about facade & exterior

Who is responsible for roof anchors used for window cleaning?

Under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.27(b), the building owner must tell the cleaning employer in writing that each anchorage has been identified, tested, certified and maintained. The contractor may not use an anchorage until it has that written information. Ask your building engineer for the current anchor certification before the job is scheduled.

Can one contractor handle windows, facade and roof work?

Some can, but the skills differ. Rope-access window crews are not always trained in masonry cleaning chemistry, and pressure-washing crews are not always qualified to work from suspended equipment. Ask for references on each specific task rather than assuming one firm covers all of them.

How is wastewater handled on exterior cleaning jobs?

Many cities prohibit discharging cleaning water, detergent or paint residue into storm drains. A qualified contractor will explain how runoff will be captured, filtered or sent to sanitary sewer with permission. Requirements vary by municipality, so ask the contractor which local rules apply to your site.
