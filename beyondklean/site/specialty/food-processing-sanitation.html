Food Processing Sanitation

This family covers contract sanitation for plants that make, pack or hold food and beverages. Buyers include plant managers, food safety and quality managers, and sanitation supervisors at FDA- and USDA-regulated facilities.

Sanitation failures lead to pathogen findings, allergen recalls and failed audits. Specialists bring trained crews, validated chemical programs and documentation that fit FSMA preventive controls, USDA FSIS sanitation rules and GFSI audit schemes.

Questions about food sanitation

Which rules apply to sanitation in my plant?

FDA-regulated facilities follow the FSMA Preventive Controls for Human Food rule in 21 CFR Part 117, including current good manufacturing practices. USDA-inspected meat, poultry and egg product plants follow FSIS rules, including Sanitation SOP requirements in 9 CFR Part 416.

Who is responsible if a contract crew misses something?

The plant remains responsible for its food safety system. The contractor works under the plant's written procedures, and the plant verifies results through inspection and environmental monitoring.

What should a sanitation contractor document?

Expect records of tasks completed, chemicals and concentrations used, pre-operational inspection results and corrective actions. These records support your food safety plan and audits.
