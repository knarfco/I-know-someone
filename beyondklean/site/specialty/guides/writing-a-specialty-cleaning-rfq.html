Specialty · Buyer's guide

What to put in a specialty cleaning RFQ or scope

How to write a request for quotes that gets comparable bids: scope, site conditions, safety, waste, schedule, deliverables and pricing format.

Describe the work and the result

Open with a short description of the facility and the reason for the work. Then list each area or item to be cleaned with quantities: square feet of surface, linear feet of duct, number of hoods, tank volumes or equipment counts. Describe what is being removed, such as grease, process dust, scale, mold growth or debris. Most important, state the result you will accept. That might be a visual standard, a NADCA surface test, a particle count, clearance sampling by a third party or a written inspection report. Without acceptance criteria, bidders price different levels of effort.

Give site conditions and access

Bidders need to know what they will find on site. Include working heights, ceiling types, floor loads, access routes, elevator use and staging areas. Note whether water, power and drainage are available and where. Identify known hazards, including chemicals in use, energized equipment, confined spaces and any test results for asbestos, lead or other materials. Attach drawings and photos. Say whether the building or line will be occupied or running during the work. Hold one site walkthrough and share every question and answer with all bidders.

Set safety, credential and insurance requirements

List the credentials required for the work, such as state asbestos licenses, HAZWOPER training or NADCA certification, and ask bidders to include copies. State your site safety rules, orientation requirements, PPE minimums and permit systems for confined space, hot work or lockout. Give insurance requirements with minimum limits and additional insured wording. If you prequalify contractors through a third-party safety platform, say so. Ask for a written work plan describing methods, equipment, containment and how the contractor will protect adjacent areas.

Address waste, schedule and reporting

Say who is responsible for waste: characterization, containers, transport, disposal and manifests. Name any disposal facilities you require. Give the start date, work windows, shutdown or outage dates and any penalties or constraints on overtime. Ask for a proposed schedule with crew size and shifts. Define daily reporting and the closeout package, such as photos, cleaning reports, waste records and test results. If the job may grow once surfaces are opened, explain how unit rates will apply to added work.

Ask for a pricing format you can compare

Provide a bid form with line items that match your scope. Ask for a lump sum for the defined work and unit rates for likely extras, such as additional square footage, extra hoods, standby time or added waste. Ask bidders to list exclusions and assumptions separately. Require pricing for mobilization, equipment, access gear and disposal so these are not hidden or missing. Set a deadline for questions and a deadline for bids, and say how you will evaluate them, such as price, schedule, experience and safety record.

Checklist

  • Facility description, reason for the work and areas with quantities
  • Clear acceptance criteria and who verifies them
  • Site conditions, hazards, test results, drawings and photos
  • Required credentials, safety rules, permits and insurance limits
  • Waste responsibilities and disposal requirements
  • Schedule, work windows and reporting requirements
  • Bid form with lump sum, unit rates, exclusions and assumptions
  • Question deadline, bid deadline and evaluation criteria
