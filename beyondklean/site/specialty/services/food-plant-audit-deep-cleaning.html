Pre-audit deep cleaning for food plants

Periodic deep cleaning of overheads, structures and hard-to-reach areas ahead of SQF, BRCGS or customer audits.

Also called: SQF audit cleaning, BRCGS audit prep cleaning, GFSI audit deep clean

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Pre-audit deep cleaning targets areas daily sanitation does not reach: overhead pipes and cable trays, ceiling structures, wall-floor junctions, behind and under equipment, motor housings and storage areas. Auditors for GFSI-benchmarked schemes such as SQF and BRCGS look for a working master sanitation schedule and a clean, maintained facility. Crews use lifts, low-moisture methods over open product zones, and food-safe chemicals, working during shutdowns so product is protected. The best results come from adding these areas to the plant's ongoing master sanitation schedule rather than treating the cleaning as a one-time event.

When you need it

  • Before a scheduled SQF, BRCGS, FSSC 22000 or customer audit
  • After an audit finding about overhead or structural cleanliness
  • Annual or semiannual shutdown
  • Takeover of a facility or line from another operator

Credentials to ask for

  • Familiarity with GFSI scheme requirements for cleaning and sanitation programs
  • Aerial lift training and fall protection under OSHA rules
  • GMP training consistent with 21 CFR Part 117 or 9 CFR Part 416
  • Food-safe chemical selection and documentation

What the scope includes

  • Walk-through with the food safety team to set priority areas
  • Product and equipment protection
  • Top-down cleaning of overheads, structures and walls
  • Cleaning behind and under fixed equipment
  • Final inspection and documentation for the audit file

How it is priced

Usually priced as a lump sum per shutdown or per labor hour with lift rental. Ceiling height, area size, equipment density and shutdown length drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How will you keep debris from falling on equipment or product zones?
  • Which chemicals will you use, and are they approved for our facility?
  • Will you provide records that fit our master sanitation schedule?
  • What lifts and access equipment do you bring?

Questions about pre-audit deep cleaning for food plants

Will a deep clean alone pass an audit?

No. Auditors look at the whole program, including procedures, schedules and records. A deep clean helps the facility condition score.

When should the cleaning happen?

Far enough before the audit to inspect results and fix gaps, usually during a planned shutdown.

What is a master sanitation schedule?

It is the plant's list of non-daily cleaning tasks with frequencies and responsibilities. Deep clean areas should be added to it.
