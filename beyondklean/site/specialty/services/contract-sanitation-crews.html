Contract sanitation crews for food plants

Outsourced nightly or between-shift sanitation crews that clean production lines under the plant's written sanitation procedures.

Also called: third-party sanitation, nightly sanitation services, outsourced sanitation, sanitation contractor

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Contract sanitation crews take over the daily or nightly cleaning of production equipment and rooms, usually during the third shift. The crew follows the plant's master sanitation schedule and sanitation standard operating procedures: dry pickup, pre-rinse, foam or chemical application, scrubbing, rinse, inspection and sanitizing. Supervisors check each line before handing it back to quality assurance for pre-operational inspection. The contractor supplies labor, training and supervision, and may also supply chemicals and equipment. In USDA-inspected plants the work must meet the establishment's Sanitation SOPs under 9 CFR Part 416; in FDA plants it supports the sanitation controls in 21 CFR Part 117.

When you need it

  • Labor shortages or turnover on the in-house sanitation team
  • Repeated pre-op failures or audit findings
  • Plant expansion or new lines without added staff
  • Need for bilingual supervision and structured training

Credentials to ask for

  • Training in the plant's SSOPs and GMPs under 21 CFR Part 117 or 9 CFR Part 416
  • Chemical safety training under OSHA hazard communication (29 CFR 1910.1200)
  • Lockout/tagout program under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147
  • Workers' compensation and liability coverage suited to wet, high-risk work

What the scope includes

  • Review and alignment with the master sanitation schedule
  • Nightly teardown, cleaning and sanitizing of assigned lines
  • Supervisor inspection and handoff to plant QA
  • Documentation of tasks, chemicals and deviations
  • Ongoing training and periodic performance reviews

How it is priced

Usually priced per labor hour or as a fixed monthly fee based on headcount and shifts. Number of lines, equipment complexity, hours available and who supplies chemicals drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How do you train new crew members, and how is training documented?
  • What is your lockout/tagout program, and how do you audit it?
  • How will you measure performance, such as pre-op pass rate or ATP results?
  • Who supplies chemicals and equipment, and who owns the SSOPs?

Questions about contract sanitation crews for food plants

Does the contractor write our SSOPs?

Some contractors help draft or update them, but the plant owns and signs its procedures. The crew works to those documents.

How is quality checked?

Plant QA usually performs pre-op inspection, often with ATP swabs and environmental monitoring. Results feed back to the contractor.

Why is lockout/tagout so important here?

Sanitation workers reach into conveyors, slicers and mixers. Many serious food industry injuries happen during cleaning when energy is not controlled.
