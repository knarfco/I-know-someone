Ship hull cleaning and biofouling removal

Removing slime, weed and barnacles from vessel hulls and niche areas in the water or in dry dock to restore speed and limit invasive species.

Also called: hull scrubbing, underwater hull cleaning, biofouling removal, propeller polishing

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Hull cleaning removes biofouling from the underwater hull, sea chests, rudders, thrusters and propellers. In the water, divers or remotely operated cleaning units use brushes or water jets, ideally with a system that captures removed material. In dry dock, crews pressure wash the hull as soon as it is exposed. The right method depends on the antifouling coating, since aggressive cleaning can strip biocide paint into the water. Federal vessel discharge standards under EPA's VIDA rules and some state and port rules limit when and how in-water cleaning may be done.

When you need it

  • Fuel consumption or speed shows rising hull resistance
  • A vessel is arriving from overseas into waters with biofouling rules
  • A vessel has been idle or in lay-up for weeks
  • Hull inspection before a charter, sale or survey
  • Propeller roughness is reducing efficiency

Credentials to ask for

  • Commercial diving credentials and a written dive safety program for in-water work
  • Knowledge of EPA's Vessel Incidental Discharge Act standards at 40 CFR Part 139 and any state or port permit rules
  • Capture and filtration equipment where local rules require capture of removed material
  • Protection and indemnity or marine liability insurance suitable for vessel work

What the scope includes

  • Review the coating type, age and maker's cleaning guidance
  • Confirm port or harbor approval for in-water cleaning where needed
  • Inspect and photograph or video fouling before cleaning
  • Clean hull, niche areas and propeller with methods matched to the coating
  • Capture and dispose of removed material where required
  • Deliver a report with before and after images

How it is priced

In-water work is usually priced per square meter or square foot of hull, or per dive day, with propeller polishing as a separate line. Cost depends on fouling level, niche areas included, capture requirements and vessel location.

Ask before you hire

  • Will you capture what you remove, and where does it go?
  • How do you avoid damaging our antifouling coating?
  • Do we need port or state approval for in-water cleaning here?
  • What will the inspection report include?

Questions about ship hull cleaning and biofouling removal

Is in-water hull cleaning allowed everywhere?

No. Some states and ports restrict or ban in-water cleaning without capture, and federal VIDA standards also address it. Ask the contractor to confirm what is allowed in your harbor before scheduling.

Can cleaning damage the hull coating?

Yes. Hard brushes on soft antifouling paint can remove coating and release biocide. A good contractor checks the coating maker's guidance and adjusts tools and pressure.

How often should a hull be cleaned?

It depends on water temperature, trade route, idle time and coating type. Many operators clean based on performance data or inspection results rather than a fixed calendar.
