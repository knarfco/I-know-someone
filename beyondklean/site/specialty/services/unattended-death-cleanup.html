Unattended death and decomposition cleanup

Cleanup and odor removal after a death that went undiscovered, including removal of saturated building materials.

Also called: decomposition cleanup, decomp cleanup, undiscovered death cleanup

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

When a death goes undiscovered for days or weeks, decomposition releases fluids and odors that soak deep into flooring, subfloors, mattresses and walls. Insects may also be present. A contractor starts after the deceased is removed. The team identifies the full extent of contamination, removes affected contents and building materials down to clean structure, and cleans and disinfects what remains. Odor control usually involves sealing treated surfaces with an odor-blocking primer and using air treatment equipment. Waste is managed as regulated medical waste where state rules apply. Rebuilding is a separate step that follows.

When you need it

  • Tenant or resident found deceased after several days
  • Hotel or senior housing room with decomposition odor
  • Death in a vehicle or enclosed space discovered late
  • Property sale or turnover after an unattended death

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard 29 CFR 1910.1030 compliance and respiratory protection under 1910.134
  • State trauma scene or medical waste registration where required; requirements vary by state
  • Trade certification from a bio-recovery association
  • Experience with structural removal and odor control

What the scope includes

  • Assess spread of fluids and odor
  • Remove contaminated contents and building materials
  • Clean, disinfect and treat structural surfaces
  • Seal surfaces and run odor control equipment
  • Package and dispose of waste
  • Final odor check and report

How it is priced

Usually priced per project after inspection, driven by time since death, extent of fluid spread, materials removed, odor treatment and waste volume.

Ask before you hire

  • How will you confirm all saturated material is removed?
  • What odor control methods do you use, and are any unsafe for occupants?
  • Do you handle contents that family members want to keep?
  • Can you coordinate with a rebuild contractor?

Questions about unattended death and decomposition cleanup

Why can't odor just be masked?

Decomposition odor comes from residue in materials. Unless that residue is removed or sealed, the odor returns, especially in warm, humid weather.

Do personal belongings have to be thrown away?

Porous items soaked with fluids usually cannot be safely cleaned. Hard items and items outside the affected area can often be cleaned and returned.

Is this covered by property insurance?

Some policies cover it. Ask your insurer and request documentation from the contractor.
