Hydro dam intake and trash rack cleaning

Removal of logs, debris and sediment from hydroelectric intakes, trash racks and screens to restore flow and protect turbines.

Also called: trash rack cleaning, intake screen cleaning, hydro intake debris removal

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Trash racks and intake screens at hydro plants and water intakes catch logs, vegetation, ice and trash. Blockage reduces flow and power output and can damage racks. Cleaning uses mechanical rakes, cranes with grapples, divers or remotely operated vehicles. Divers work under OSHA commercial diving rules in 29 CFR 1910 Subpart T, with lockout of units to prevent intake flow. Sediment may be dredged. Debris is hauled away or disposed of under state rules.

When you need it

  • Head loss across racks is increasing
  • After floods or storms
  • Seasonal debris loads
  • Before inspection or repair

Credentials to ask for

  • Commercial diving compliance under OSHA 29 CFR 1910 Subpart T
  • Lockout and flow isolation procedures
  • Crane and rigging certification
  • Environmental permits for sediment removal where required

What the scope includes

  • Plan with plant operations
  • Isolate flow and lock out units
  • Remove debris by rake, crane or divers
  • Inspect racks
  • Dispose of debris
  • Report condition

How it is priced

Typically priced by crew-day or per event. Debris volume, access, diving needs and disposal drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Will you use divers or remote methods?
  • How will you isolate flow?
  • Where will debris go?
  • Can you inspect rack condition?

Questions about hydro dam intake and trash rack cleaning

Why clean trash racks?

Debris blocks flow and raises head loss, which cuts power output or water supply. Heavy debris loads can also bend or collapse racks and let material reach turbines or pumps.

Is diving required?

Not always. Mechanical rakes and cranes remove most surface and near-surface debris. Divers or remotely operated vehicles are used for deep racks and for inspection below the waterline.

How often is cleaning needed?

It depends on the river and the season. Some sites rake daily during spring runoff or leaf fall, then switch to periodic cleaning the rest of the year.
