Concrete washout and slurry cleanup

Providing and servicing concrete washout containers and vacuuming saw-cut and grinding slurry so high-pH water stays out of storm drains.

Also called: concrete washout service, slurry vacuum cleanup, saw cutting slurry removal, washout bin service

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Concrete truck chutes, pumps and tools must be washed out, and saw cutting, coring and grinding create a gray slurry. Both are highly alkaline and can harm waterways. Washout services place lined containers or portable bins on site, then pump out water and remove hardened solids. Slurry cleanup uses wet vacuums to collect slurry at the source. Water may be settled, neutralized or hauled. EPA's Construction General Permit and state stormwater permits require concrete washout to be contained and kept out of storm drains.

When you need it

  • Concrete pours on permitted construction sites
  • Saw cutting, coring or grinding indoors or on paving
  • A stormwater inspection finds washout violations
  • Polished concrete floor projects

Credentials to ask for

  • Knowledge of the project's stormwater pollution prevention plan under the Construction General Permit or state equivalent
  • Licensed hauling of washout water and solids where required
  • Silica controls under OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1153 during slurry handling

What the scope includes

  • Place lined washout containers at agreed locations
  • Vacuum slurry at saw cutting and grinding points
  • Pump out water and remove solids
  • Neutralize or haul water as required
  • Provide service logs for stormwater inspections

How it is priced

Usually priced per container per month plus service visits, or per hour for slurry vacuum work. Cost depends on pour schedule, volume, haul distance and disposal rules.

Ask before you hire

  • How often will containers be serviced?
  • Where does washout water and solids go?
  • Will your logs satisfy our stormwater inspector?

Questions about concrete washout and slurry cleanup

Why is concrete washout a stormwater issue?

Washout water has high pH and fine solids. If it reaches a storm drain, it can harm fish and plants and lead to permit violations.

Can slurry go down the sanitary sewer?

Usually not without approval. Many cities ban it because solids settle and harden in pipes.

What happens to hardened washout?

Hardened washout is often broken up and recycled with other concrete debris. Liners and bags are disposed of separately.
