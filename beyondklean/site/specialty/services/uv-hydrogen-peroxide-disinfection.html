UV and hydrogen peroxide vapor disinfection

No-touch whole-room disinfection with UV-C devices or hydrogen peroxide vapor, used after manual cleaning in healthcare and labs.

Also called: no-touch disinfection, HPV room decontamination, UV-C disinfection, whole-room disinfection

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

No-touch disinfection uses either ultraviolet C light or vaporized hydrogen peroxide to treat a room after it has been manually cleaned. UV-C devices are placed in the room for timed cycles and work by line of sight, so shadowed surfaces get less dose. Hydrogen peroxide vapor fills a sealed room and reaches hidden surfaces, but the room must be sealed, monitored and aerated before reentry. Hydrogen peroxide products used this way must be EPA-registered and applied per label. UV devices are regulated by EPA as pesticide devices when sold with antimicrobial claims; they are not registered, so ask for efficacy data. Neither method replaces manual cleaning.

When you need it

  • Terminal cleaning of rooms after multidrug-resistant organism cases
  • Outbreak response in hospitals or long-term care
  • Lab or vivarium room decontamination between uses
  • Infection prevention programs adding an extra disinfection step

Credentials to ask for

  • EPA registration number and label for any hydrogen peroxide product used
  • Efficacy data for UV devices, since they are not EPA-registered
  • Training on room sealing, hydrogen peroxide monitoring and OSHA exposure limits
  • Coordination with infection prevention or biosafety staff

What the scope includes

  • Confirm manual cleaning is complete
  • Room prep: seal vents and doors for vapor, or position UV units
  • Run the cycle with chemical or biological indicators where used
  • Aeration and hydrogen peroxide level check before reentry
  • Cycle record with room, time, dose or concentration

How it is priced

Usually priced per room or per cycle, or as a device lease with consumables. Room volume, number of rooms, travel and indicator use drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • For hydrogen peroxide, what is the product's EPA registration number?
  • For UV, what independent efficacy data supports your device?
  • How do you verify each cycle worked?
  • How do you confirm the room is safe to reenter?

Questions about uv and hydrogen peroxide vapor disinfection

Which is better, UV or hydrogen peroxide vapor?

UV cycles are shorter but limited by line of sight. Vapor reaches hidden surfaces but needs sealing and longer cycles. The choice depends on room type and turnaround needs.

Can staff stay in the room during treatment?

No. Both UV-C and hydrogen peroxide vapor are hazardous to people. Rooms must be empty and secured.

Does this replace cleaning?

No. Soil shields organisms, so surfaces must be cleaned first.
