Construction silica dust cleanup

Cleaning concrete, masonry and stone dust with HEPA vacuums and wet methods that meet OSHA's construction silica standard.

Also called: crystalline silica cleanup, HEPA dust cleanup, concrete dust cleanup, silica-safe housekeeping

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Cutting, drilling, grinding and demolishing concrete, brick, block and stone releases respirable crystalline silica. Settled dust can be stirred back into the air by sweeping or blowing. OSHA's construction silica standard, 29 CFR 1926.1153, bars dry sweeping or brushing where it could contribute to exposure unless wet sweeping, HEPA vacuuming or other low-exposure methods are not feasible, and it bars compressed air for cleaning unless used with a ventilation system that captures the dust. Cleanup crews use HEPA vacuums, wet methods and respirators when required, then dispose of dust in sealed bags.

When you need it

  • After concrete cutting, coring or grinding
  • Masonry demolition inside buildings
  • Final cleaning before occupancy
  • Dust complaints from adjoining tenants

Credentials to ask for

  • Compliance with OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1153, including the employer's written exposure control plan
  • HEPA vacuums that meet the standard's definition
  • Respiratory protection program under 29 CFR 1910.134 when respirators are required
  • Worker training on silica hazards

What the scope includes

  • Review the project's exposure control plan
  • HEPA vacuum surfaces top to bottom
  • Wet wipe or wet sweep as needed
  • Bag and dispose of dust
  • Inspect for remaining dust

How it is priced

Usually priced per square foot or per crew shift. Cost depends on dust volume, height, surfaces and respirator needs.

Ask before you hire

  • How do your methods meet OSHA's silica housekeeping rules?
  • Do your HEPA vacuums meet the standard?
  • Will your crew need respirators, and do you have a respiratory program?

Questions about construction silica dust cleanup

Why is silica dust a concern?

Respirable crystalline silica can cause silicosis, lung cancer, COPD and kidney disease. Fine dust stays airborne and can be breathed in long after cutting stops.

Can we use leaf blowers to clear dust?

No. Blowers and compressed air throw dust back into the air. The standard restricts compressed air unless it is used with a system that captures the dust.

Do cleanup crews need respirators?

It depends on exposure levels. The employer's exposure assessment and written control plan decide when respirators are required.
