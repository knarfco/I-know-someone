Oil-water separator and stormwater structure cleaning

Pump-out and cleaning of oil-water separators, catch basins and stormwater treatment units, with waste characterization.

Also called: OWS cleaning, catch basin cleaning, stormwater interceptor pump-out

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Oil-water separators and stormwater units trap oil, sediment and debris before water leaves a site. Over time they fill with sludge and free oil and stop working. A contractor pumps out the unit with a vacuum truck, removes sludge, cleans coalescing media and baffles, and inspects for damage. Entry into vaults uses confined space procedures. Recovered oil may be managed as used oil, while sludge and water are tested to decide whether they are hazardous. Maintenance records support the site's stormwater permit, sewer discharge permit or stormwater pollution prevention plan.

When you need it

  • Scheduled maintenance required by a stormwater or sewer discharge permit
  • Oil sheen leaving the site or an inspector notes buildup
  • Vehicle wash bay, fleet shop or airport separator reaching capacity
  • Sediment buildup causing flooding or poor performance

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA permit-required confined space program under 29 CFR 1910.146 for vault entry
  • Knowledge of used oil rules at 40 CFR Part 279 and hazardous waste determinations
  • State waste hauler or liquid waste transporter permits where required; requirements vary by state
  • Familiarity with NPDES stormwater and local pretreatment requirements

What the scope includes

  • Inspect and measure oil and sludge levels
  • Vacuum out oil, water and sludge
  • Clean media, baffles and chambers
  • Inspect for damage and report repair needs
  • Characterize and dispose of waste
  • Provide maintenance records for permit files

How it is priced

Usually priced per unit or per service visit plus disposal per gallon or ton. Cost depends on unit size, sludge volume, waste classification, confined space entry and service frequency.

Ask before you hire

  • How will you classify the oil, water and sludge for disposal?
  • Will your records satisfy our permit and SWPPP?
  • Do you enter vaults, and under what confined space program?
  • Can you set up a recurring service schedule?

Questions about oil-water separator and stormwater structure cleaning

How often should an oil-water separator be cleaned?

It depends on use and design. Many permits or manufacturers call for cleaning when oil or sludge reaches a set depth. Regular inspections help set the right frequency.

Can separator waste go down the sanitary sewer?

Generally not without approval. Local sewer authorities set discharge limits, and sludge and oil need proper disposal.

Is separator sludge hazardous waste?

It can be, depending on what drains into the unit. Testing or generator knowledge supports the decision.
