Wastewater digester and clarifier cleaning

Removal of grit, sludge and rags from anaerobic digesters, clarifiers and treatment basins at water and wastewater plants.

Also called: anaerobic digester cleaning, clarifier cleaning, grit removal

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Digesters fill with grit, rags and struvite over time, reducing capacity and gas production. Clarifiers and basins collect grit and sludge that strain mechanisms. Cleaning involves taking the tank out of service, removing digester gas and purging, then pumping and vacuuming contents. Remaining grit is removed by hydraulic or mechanical methods, sometimes with remote equipment. Digester gas is mainly methane and may contain hydrogen sulfide, so entry is permit-required confined space work. NFPA 820 covers fire protection in wastewater treatment and collection facilities. Solids are dewatered and disposed of or beneficially reused.

When you need it

  • Reduced digester volume or gas production
  • Mechanism or mixer failures
  • Planned rehabilitation or inspection
  • Clarifier drive torque problems

Credentials to ask for

  • Permit-required confined space program per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146 or state-plan equivalent
  • Methane and hydrogen sulfide monitoring and ventilation
  • Awareness of NFPA 820 hazard classifications
  • Biosolids handling knowledge under EPA 40 CFR Part 503
  • Public works and prevailing wage compliance where applicable

What the scope includes

  • Plan with plant staff for isolation and bypass
  • Gas removal, purge and atmospheric testing
  • Pump down liquid contents
  • Remove grit, rags and solids
  • Dewater and haul solids
  • Clean tank and handover

How it is priced

Often priced per dry ton or cubic yard removed, or lump sum per tank. Tank size, grit volume, dewatering and disposal route drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How will you purge and verify the digester is safe?
  • Will you dewater on site?
  • Where will solids go?
  • Can you keep part of the plant online?

Questions about wastewater digester and clarifier cleaning

Why are digesters hazardous?

They contain methane, which can explode, and hydrogen sulfide, which is toxic. Purging and constant monitoring are required before and during entry.

Can digesters be cleaned without draining?

Some contractors use online cleaning systems that remove grit while the digester operates. Full cleaning still requires draining.

What happens to the solids?

Solids are dewatered and sent to a landfill, land application or another approved use. Grit and rags usually go to a landfill, while biosolids reuse must meet EPA 40 CFR Part 503 and state rules.
