Electronics and control room cleaning after a smoke event

Assessment and decontamination of electronics, control panels and rooms exposed to smoke or soot to limit corrosion and equipment loss.

Also called: smoke damaged electronics cleaning, control room smoke remediation, corrosion control cleaning, electronic equipment decontamination

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Smoke from fires, especially burning plastics and PVC cable, can leave acidic, chloride-bearing residues on electronics. Corrosion can begin within days, even when equipment looks fine. Response starts with stabilizing the environment: controlling humidity, isolating the area and preventing more contamination. Specialists sample surfaces to measure residues and decide which equipment can be cleaned and which should be replaced. Decontamination can include HEPA vacuuming, specialized wiping and cleaning of boards and components, coordinated with equipment makers and insurers. ANSI/IICRC S700, published in 2025, covers fire and smoke damage restoration in buildings and contents.

When you need it

  • A fire or smoke event in or near a control room, data center or telecom space
  • Soot or odor present on electronic equipment
  • Insurance claims involving electronics after a fire
  • Smoke from an electrical failure or battery event

Credentials to ask for

  • Familiarity with ANSI/IICRC S700 for fire and smoke damage restoration
  • Experience sampling and testing residues on electronics
  • Coordination with equipment manufacturers and insurance adjusters
  • ESD control training and static-safe tools

What the scope includes

  • Stabilization of humidity and isolation of affected areas
  • Surface sampling and residue assessment
  • Decision on clean versus replace for each equipment group
  • Decontamination of equipment and the room
  • Post-cleaning testing and documentation for the insurer

How it is priced

Usually priced on time and materials or by equipment item after assessment, often billed through an insurance claim. Extent of contamination, equipment count, testing and urgency drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How quickly can you stabilize the space?
  • How do you decide which equipment can be saved?
  • Have you worked with our equipment makers and our insurer before?
  • What testing will you do after cleaning?

Questions about electronics and control room cleaning after a smoke event

Why act quickly after smoke exposure?

Acidic residues and humidity can start corrosion within days. Controlling humidity and starting cleaning early can save more equipment.

Can smoke-damaged electronics really be saved?

Often yes, depending on the residue, exposure time and equipment. Testing helps decide what can be cleaned.

Will the insurer pay for cleaning?

Many policies cover it. Insurers may compare cleaning cost to replacement cost, so documentation is important.
