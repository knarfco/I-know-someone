Legionella and building water system remediation

Disinfection and corrective work on building water systems and cooling towers to control Legionella, guided by ASHRAE 188.

Also called: Legionella remediation, water system disinfection, cooling tower disinfection

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Legionella remediation brings a building water system back under control after positive tests or a case of Legionnaires' disease. Work is guided by the building's water management program, ANSI/ASHRAE Standard 188-2021, ASHRAE Guideline 12-2023 and CDC's water management toolkit. Typical steps include hyperchlorination or thermal disinfection of potable systems, flushing dead legs, cleaning and disinfecting cooling towers, and removing scale and biofilm. Fixtures and components may be replaced. Follow-up samples, analyzed by a qualified lab, confirm the result. Corrective actions are then written into the water management program to prevent recurrence.

When you need it

  • Positive Legionella results from routine sampling
  • A Legionnaires' disease case linked to a building
  • Building reopening after a long shutdown
  • Cooling tower or hot water system failing control limits

Credentials to ask for

  • Knowledge of ANSI/ASHRAE 188-2021, ASHRAE Guideline 12-2023 and CDC water management guidance
  • Water treatment and plumbing licenses where required; requirements vary by state
  • Use of laboratories in CDC's ELITE program for Legionella testing
  • Compliance with local cooling tower rules, such as New York State and New York City registration and maintenance rules
  • Healthcare buildings: support for CMS water management expectations under memo QSO-17-30

What the scope includes

  • Review water management plan and test results
  • Identify risk areas such as dead legs, low temperatures or stagnant tanks
  • Disinfect potable systems or cooling towers
  • Clean, flush and replace components as needed
  • Post-remediation sampling
  • Update the water management program with corrective actions

How it is priced

Usually priced per project or per system, with lab analysis per sample. Cost depends on building size, number of outlets, disinfection method, cooling tower cleaning and sampling rounds.

Ask before you hire

  • Which disinfection method do you recommend and why?
  • Which ELITE-certified lab will analyze samples?
  • How will the work fit into our water management program?
  • Can you help with health department communication if there is a case?

Questions about legionella and building water system remediation

Is ASHRAE 188 required by law?

ASHRAE 188 is a consensus standard. Some jurisdictions reference it, and CMS expects many healthcare facilities to have water management programs. Check local and state requirements.

Does CMS require Legionella testing?

CMS requires water management programs in many healthcare facilities but leaves testing protocols to the facility. Many follow CDC and ASHRAE guidance.

Is one disinfection enough?

Not if the conditions that let Legionella grow remain. Long-term control depends on temperature, flow, disinfectant levels and maintenance.
