Drum and container cleanout

Identification, sampling, overpacking and disposal of drums, totes and containers with known or unknown contents.

Also called: abandoned drum removal, unknown drum disposal, tote cleanout

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Drum and container cleanout deals with drums, totes and small containers left in storage yards, warehouses or vacant buildings. Many are unlabeled or leaking. A contractor inspects each container, opens unknowns with remote or spark-resistant tools when needed, and runs field hazard categorization tests to sort contents into compatible groups. Leaking or damaged containers go into overpack drums. Contents are profiled and shipped for disposal, and containers are emptied to RCRA empty-container standards, then recycled, reconditioned or disposed of. The owner gets an inventory and disposal records for each item.

When you need it

  • Acquiring or closing a property with leftover chemical drums
  • Abandoned drums found on land or in a building
  • Leaking or bulging containers in storage
  • Consolidating many partly full containers before disposal

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA HAZWOPER 29 CFR 1910.120 training, including drum handling procedures
  • Chemists or technicians experienced in hazard categorization of unknowns
  • DOT hazmat training and packaging knowledge for overpacks and shipments
  • State hazardous waste transporter permits where required; requirements vary by state

What the scope includes

  • Inventory, photograph and assess each container
  • Safely open and sample unknowns
  • Field hazard categorization and compatibility grouping
  • Overpack, bulk or lab pack contents
  • Ship to permitted facilities with manifests
  • Empty, clean or dispose of the containers

How it is priced

Typically priced per container or per drum by waste type, with added charges for unknown analysis, overpacks and labor. Cost depends on number of unknowns, container condition and disposal method.

Ask before you hire

  • How do you open and identify drums with unknown contents?
  • What happens if a drum contains something very reactive or shock sensitive?
  • Will we get an inventory that ties each container to a disposal record?
  • What makes a drum count as RCRA empty?

Questions about drum and container cleanout

What does RCRA empty mean?

Under 40 CFR 261.7, a container that held most hazardous wastes is empty when all wastes have been removed by common practices and no more than a set small residue remains. Acutely hazardous waste containers have stricter rules.

Can unknown drums be shipped without testing?

No. Contents must be identified well enough to classify them for DOT shipping and to meet the disposal facility's profile. Field testing is the usual first step.

Who is responsible for abandoned drums on land we bought?

Under environmental law, current owners may share liability for contamination even if they did not leave it. Talk to environmental counsel about your situation.
