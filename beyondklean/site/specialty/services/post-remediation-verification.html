Post-remediation verification (mold clearance)

Independent inspection and sampling after mold remediation to confirm the work met the protocol before rebuild.

Also called: mold clearance testing, PRV, post-remediation assessment

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Post-remediation verification is an independent check that mold remediation succeeded. Before containment comes down, an assessor inspects for visible growth and dust, checks that moisture readings are normal, and confirms that removal matches the written protocol. Air or surface samples are often collected and compared with outdoor and control areas. IICRC S520 describes this verification step. If the area fails, the remediator cleans again and the area is rechecked. A passing result lets repairs and rebuild proceed and gives the owner documentation for tenants, insurers and buyers. In some states, clearance must come from a licensed assessor.

When you need it

  • Mold remediation is complete and containment is still up
  • Owner or insurer requires documented clearance
  • Sensitive occupants such as patients or children return
  • Disputed remediation needing a neutral review

Credentials to ask for

  • State mold assessor license where required, such as Florida, Texas and New York; requirements vary by state
  • Professional credentials such as CIH or ACAC certifications
  • Independence from the remediation contractor
  • AIHA EMLAP accredited laboratory for sample analysis

What the scope includes

  • Visual inspection inside containment
  • Moisture readings on remaining materials
  • Air or surface sampling as the protocol requires
  • Compare results to clearance criteria
  • Issue a clearance report or list of items to redo

How it is priced

Usually priced per visit plus per sample, with added charges for repeat visits after a failed check. Cost depends on number of containment areas and samples.

Ask before you hire

  • Are you independent from the remediation company?
  • What clearance criteria will you use?
  • Will you test before containment is removed?
  • How quickly can you return if the area fails?

Questions about post-remediation verification (mold clearance)

Why use a different company for clearance?

An independent assessor removes the conflict of interest of a remediator grading its own work. Some states require that separation.

What does it mean to fail clearance?

Visible dust, growth, high moisture or elevated spore counts can mean the area needs more cleaning. The remediator re-cleans and the assessor re-tests.

Is air sampling always part of verification?

Not always. A careful visual and moisture check is the core. Sampling adds data where needed or where a protocol calls for it.
