Transit bus and fleet vehicle cleaning

Exterior washing and interior deep cleaning of buses, paratransit vans and fleet vehicles, including fluid cleanup and wash water control.

Also called: bus washing, fleet washing, transit vehicle deep cleaning, school bus cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Fleet cleaning keeps transit buses, paratransit vans, school buses and service vehicles clean for riders and inspectors. Exterior washing uses automatic washers or mobile crews with water recovery. Interior deep cleaning covers seats, floors, windows, stanchions and wheelchair securements. Crews may also clean engine bays and undercarriages before inspections. Bodily fluid cleanup follows bloodborne pathogen procedures. Electric buses need care around high voltage components and charging ports.

When you need it

  • Daily or weekly fleet cleaning programs
  • Before inspections or audits
  • After fluid incidents or vandalism
  • Seasonal salt or road grime buildup

Credentials to ask for

  • Bloodborne pathogens training under 29 CFR 1910.1030
  • Wash water handling that meets local sewer or stormwater rules
  • Training on high voltage safety for electric buses
  • Commercial auto and general liability insurance

What the scope includes

  • Wash exteriors with water recovery
  • Clean interiors, seats and floors
  • Detail windows, stanchions and wheelchair areas
  • Clean undercarriage or engine bays if requested
  • Report damage or defects found

How it is priced

Usually priced per vehicle or per wash, or as a monthly contract. Cost depends on fleet size, frequency, interior detail level and wash water rules.

Ask before you hire

  • How do you handle wash water at our yard?
  • What products do you use on seats and floors?
  • Are your crews trained for electric buses?

Questions about transit bus and fleet vehicle cleaning

Can mobile crews wash buses in our yard?

Yes, if wash water is captured or the yard has approved drains. Check local rules first.

How often should buses be deep cleaned?

Many agencies wash exteriors several times a week and deep clean interiors on a monthly or mileage-based cycle. Contract terms usually spell out the frequency.

How are fluid incidents handled?

Crews trained under OSHA's bloodborne pathogens standard contain, clean and disinfect the area with an EPA-registered disinfectant. Contaminated materials are bagged and disposed of under local rules.
