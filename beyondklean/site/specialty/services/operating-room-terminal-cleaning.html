Operating room terminal cleaning

End-of-day terminal cleaning and disinfection of operating rooms and procedure rooms following facility protocols and AORN guidance.

Also called: OR terminal clean, surgical suite cleaning, end-of-day OR cleaning

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Terminal cleaning is the thorough cleaning of an operating or procedure room after the last case of the day. Staff clean and disinfect all surfaces from high to low and clean to dirty: lights, booms, equipment, furniture, walls touched during cases, and the full floor, including under fixed equipment. EPA-registered hospital disinfectants are applied at their label contact times. Facilities generally follow AORN guidelines and CDC environmental infection control guidance, with the hospital's own policy as the rule. Contract crews must work within the facility's infection prevention program, including bloodborne pathogen precautions under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030, and their work is verified by fluorescent marking or ATP audits.

When you need it

  • Daily after the last case in each OR or procedure room
  • Opening a new surgical suite
  • Surgical site infection cluster or infection prevention concern
  • Staffing gaps in the environmental services department

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens training and exposure control plan (29 CFR 1910.1030)
  • Training on the facility's OR cleaning policy and AORN guidance
  • Correct use of EPA-registered hospital disinfectants per label
  • Environmental services credentials such as CHESP or certified technician programs

What the scope includes

  • Removal of waste, linen and sharps per facility policy
  • High-to-low cleaning and disinfection of all surfaces and equipment
  • Full floor cleaning, including under movable equipment
  • Restocking and readiness check
  • Verification by fluorescent marker or ATP audit

How it is priced

Usually priced per room per clean or as a monthly contract by number of rooms and shifts. Room count, case volume, equipment density and verification requirements drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How are your staff trained on OR cleaning, and how is competency checked?
  • Which disinfectants will you use, and how do you assure contact time?
  • How will you verify cleaning, and will we see the results?
  • How do you handle regulated medical waste and sharps?

Questions about operating room terminal cleaning

Is terminal cleaning different from between-case cleaning?

Yes. Between-case cleaning covers surfaces near the patient. Terminal cleaning is a complete top-to-bottom clean at the end of the day.

Who sets the cleaning procedure?

The hospital's infection prevention and perioperative policies set it, usually based on AORN and CDC guidance.

Can UV or vapor systems replace manual cleaning?

No. They can add to manual cleaning, but surfaces must still be cleaned to remove soil.
