Nuclear plant decontamination support (information only)

Overview of radiological decontamination support at nuclear plants, performed under the licensee's radiation protection program.

Also called: radiological decontamination, nuclear outage decon support, contamination control

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

At nuclear plants, decontamination support includes cleaning contaminated surfaces, tools and areas during outages under the plant's radiation protection program. All work involving radioactive materials is controlled by the licensee, which holds the license from the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission or an Agreement State. Workers must be trained per 10 CFR Part 19 and protected under the dose limits in 10 CFR Part 20. Contractors work under the licensee's radiation work permits and supervision. This page is information only and does not describe procedures for radiological work.

When you need it

  • Refueling and maintenance outages that need extra contamination control labor
  • Decommissioning projects with large areas to decontaminate
  • Unplanned spread of contamination that the licensee must recover from
  • Tool and equipment decontamination for reuse or release

Credentials to ask for

  • Work under the licensee's radiation protection program
  • Training required by 10 CFR Part 19
  • Dose limits and monitoring under 10 CFR Part 20
  • Site access authorization and fitness for duty programs

What the scope includes

  • Coordination with the licensee's radiation protection staff
  • Training and access authorization
  • Work under radiation work permits
  • Decontamination as directed
  • Waste handled by licensee procedures

How it is priced

Usually priced by labor hour or per outage under contracts with the licensee. Outage length, staffing and training requirements drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • What experience do your workers have at nuclear sites?
  • How do you support the licensee's radiation protection program?
  • What training and access records do you maintain?
  • How do you handle fitness for duty requirements?

Questions about nuclear plant decontamination support (information only)

Who controls radiological work?

The licensee controls all radiological work under its license and radiation protection program. Commercial power reactors are licensed by the NRC, while many other radioactive materials users are licensed by Agreement States.

Can a general cleaning contractor do this?

No. Only workers trained and authorized under the licensee's program can perform radiological work. Ordinary cleaning firms may support non-radiological areas of the site, but they cannot enter radiologically controlled areas without that authorization.

Is this page a guide to decontamination?

No. This page is information only and gives no procedures. All radiological work must follow the licensee's approved procedures and the direction of its radiation protection staff.
