Fire and smoke damage restoration

Assessing, cleaning and restoring a building after a fire, from board-up and debris removal to residue cleaning and final evaluation.

Also called: fire damage cleanup, smoke damage cleanup, fire restoration, post-fire cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Fire restoration starts with an assessment of how far residue and odor traveled, which materials can be cleaned and which must be removed. Crews board up openings, tarp roofs and remove charred debris. They then clean structure, finishes and building systems with methods matched to the residue, clean or isolate HVAC, and manage odor. ANSI/IICRC S700 covers assessment, source removal, HVAC, odor management, contents and post-restoration evaluation. In older buildings, damaged materials should be tested for asbestos and lead before they are torn out.

When you need it

  • After any structure fire, even a small one
  • Smoke migrated from a fire in an adjoining unit or nearby building
  • A furnace or boiler puffback spread soot through a building
  • An electrical or equipment fire in a plant, shop or data room

Credentials to ask for

  • IICRC Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician (FSRT) certification for supervisors
  • Work methods consistent with ANSI/IICRC S700
  • Asbestos inspection before demolition or renovation where EPA NESHAP (40 CFR 61 Subpart M) or state rules require it
  • General liability and contractors pollution liability insurance

What the scope includes

  • Assess residue spread, structural safety and utility status
  • Board up, tarp and secure the site
  • Remove debris and unsalvageable materials
  • Clean structure, finishes and HVAC systems
  • Manage odor after source removal
  • Evaluate results and hand over records for the claim

How it is priced

Usually priced as an itemized estimate by task and square foot, often written in the insurance estimating software the carrier uses. Cost drives include affected area, residue type, demolition, HVAC work and contents volume.

Ask before you hire

  • Do you follow ANSI/IICRC S700, and who on your crew is FSRT certified?
  • How will you decide what to clean versus replace?
  • Will you test suspect materials for asbestos and lead before removal?
  • What documentation will you send to my insurer?

Questions about fire and smoke damage restoration

Can a building with smoke damage be cleaned instead of rebuilt?

Often, yes. Hard surfaces and many finishes clean well, while porous materials that are heavily affected or burned usually need removal. A proper assessment decides which is which.

How long does fire restoration take?

Light smoke jobs can finish in days. Large losses with demolition, HVAC cleaning and rebuild often take weeks or months, depending on permits and claim approvals.

Why does the HVAC system matter?

Running air systems carry smoke and soot into ducts and other rooms. If ducts are not cleaned or isolated, odor and residue can return after the rooms are cleaned.
