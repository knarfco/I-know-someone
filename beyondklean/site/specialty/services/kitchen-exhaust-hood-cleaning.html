Kitchen exhaust hood and duct cleaning

Removal of grease from commercial kitchen hoods, filters, ducts and exhaust fans, performed to NFPA 96 and local fire code requirements.

Also called: hood cleaning, grease duct cleaning, restaurant exhaust cleaning, NFPA 96 cleaning

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Kitchen exhaust cleaning removes grease deposits from the hood, filters, horizontal and vertical ductwork, and the rooftop exhaust fan. Crews cover cooking equipment, scrape heavy buildup, apply a degreaser, and pressure wash or steam the system down to bare metal. Wastewater is captured and disposed of according to local rules. NFPA 96 sets inspection and cleaning frequency based on cooking volume and fuel type, and the local fire authority enforces it. Many jurisdictions also require a cleaning certificate or label on the hood. A good contractor reports areas that could not be reached and any damaged components.

When you need it

  • A scheduled cleaning interval under NFPA 96 based on cooking volume
  • A fire inspection or insurance audit noting grease buildup
  • After a kitchen fire or fire suppression system discharge
  • Taking over a restaurant space or reopening a kitchen
  • Visible grease dripping from the fan or hood seams

Credentials to ask for

  • Cleaning to NFPA 96 and any local fire code amendments
  • Certification programs vary by state and AHJ; IKECA certifications (CECT, CECS, CESI) are widely recognized
  • Proof of liability insurance that covers kitchen exhaust work
  • Any local license or registration your fire marshal requires

What the scope includes

  • Inspection of hood, filters, ductwork, access panels and fan
  • Protection of cooking line and floors with plastic and drip containment
  • Scraping, chemical degreasing and pressure washing to bare metal
  • Cleaning and hinge check of the rooftop exhaust fan
  • Wastewater capture and disposal per local rules
  • Service label on the hood and a written report with photos

How it is priced

Usually priced per cleaning visit for the whole system, sometimes per hood plus per foot of duct. Duct length, number of access panels, grease load, rooftop access and frequency drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Which certifications do your technicians hold, and are they accepted by our fire marshal?
  • Will you clean the full duct run, or only the hood and fan?
  • How do you handle and dispose of grease wastewater?
  • Will you note missing access panels or areas you could not reach?

Questions about kitchen exhaust hood and duct cleaning

How often does a kitchen exhaust system need cleaning?

NFPA 96 ties frequency to the type and volume of cooking. High-volume operations such as charbroiling may need monthly service, while low-volume kitchens may need it only semiannually or annually.

Does hood cleaning include the fire suppression system?

No. Suppression system inspection is separate work done by a licensed fire protection contractor, though the two are often scheduled together.

Why does the report mention inaccessible areas?

Ductwork without enough access panels cannot be fully cleaned. Reporting it lets the owner add panels and avoid a hidden grease fire hazard.
