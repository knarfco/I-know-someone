Demolition debris removal and haul-off

Loading, sorting and hauling demolition debris to permitted landfills and recyclers, with weight tickets and diversion records.

Also called: C&D debris removal, demo debris hauling, construction waste removal, roll-off debris cleanup

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Demolition debris removal clears concrete, masonry, wood, metal, drywall and mixed waste after a structure or interior is taken down. Crews load debris with skid steers, excavators or by hand into roll-offs or dump trucks. Material is sorted on site or at a transfer station so concrete, metal and clean wood can be recycled. Haulers deliver to permitted construction and demolition landfills or recyclers and return weight tickets. Asbestos-containing material must be identified and removed before demolition, and suspect items such as fluorescent lamps or PCB ballasts are pulled separately.

When you need it

  • After full or partial structure demolition
  • Site clearing before new construction
  • Green building projects that need diversion records
  • Storm-damaged structures being taken down

Credentials to ask for

  • Asbestos inspection completed before demolition under EPA NESHAP, 40 CFR 61.145
  • Familiarity with OSHA demolition rules in 29 CFR Part 1926 Subpart T
  • Hauler licensing or registration and permitted disposal sites; requirements vary by state and city
  • Commercial auto and general liability insurance

What the scope includes

  • Confirm hazardous materials have been surveyed and removed
  • Stage roll-offs and plan truck access
  • Load and sort debris by material
  • Haul to permitted landfills and recyclers
  • Sweep and grade the site as specified
  • Deliver weight tickets and diversion reports

How it is priced

Usually priced per load, per container pull or per ton, sometimes as a lump sum for the site. Cost depends on debris volume and weight, sorting, haul distance and disposal fees.

Ask before you hire

  • Where will each type of debris go, and will we get weight tickets?
  • Can you track recycling and diversion rates for our green building goals?
  • Has the building been surveyed for asbestos and other hazardous materials?

Questions about demolition debris removal and haul-off

Can concrete be recycled?

Yes. Clean concrete is often crushed for base material. Sorting it from other debris lowers disposal cost.

Who is responsible for proper disposal?

The generator, usually the owner or contractor, remains responsible if waste goes to an improper site. Weight tickets show where it went.

What items need separate handling?

Fluorescent lamps, PCB ballasts, mercury switches, refrigerants and asbestos need separate removal. Federal and state rules control how each is handled and where it goes.
