Combustible dust housekeeping and high-level cleaning

Removal of combustible dust from beams, rafters, ducts and equipment using approved vacuums and methods to reduce dust explosion risk.

Also called: combustible dust cleaning, rafter and beam dust cleaning, explosion hazard dust removal

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Fine dust from wood, food, plastics, metals, chemicals and grain can explode when suspended in air near an ignition source. Layers on beams, ledges and equipment can feed a secondary explosion. Combustible dust housekeeping removes these layers using lifts and vacuums designed for combustible dust, often with grounding and bonding. Blowing down with compressed air is generally avoided or tightly controlled because it puts dust into the air. Work is coordinated with the facility's dust hazard analysis, required under NFPA 660 (and earlier NFPA 652), and OSHA's Combustible Dust National Emphasis Program targets these hazards.

When you need it

  • Dust layers are visible on elevated surfaces
  • A dust hazard analysis or insurer survey recommended cleaning
  • Before an OSHA inspection or after a citation
  • After a process change that creates more dust

Credentials to ask for

  • Knowledge of NFPA 660 and the legacy NFPA 652, 654 and 484 requirements
  • Vacuums listed or designed for combustible dust and hazardous (classified) locations as required
  • Aerial lift and fall protection training
  • Familiarity with OSHA's Combustible Dust National Emphasis Program and the General Duty Clause

What the scope includes

  • Review of the facility's dust hazard analysis and hot work controls
  • Identification of surfaces and zones to clean, from high to low
  • Vacuum cleaning of beams, rafters, ledges, ducts and equipment tops
  • Controlled low-pressure methods only where vacuuming is impractical and the area is de-energized and isolated
  • Collection and disposal of dust
  • Before and after photos and documentation of cleaned areas

How it is priced

Usually priced by crew-day or per square foot of elevated surface. Height, lift access, dust type, and whether production must stop drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • What makes your vacuums suitable for our dust?
  • How do you avoid creating a dust cloud while cleaning?
  • Have you read our dust hazard analysis?
  • How will you document dust levels before and after?

Questions about combustible dust housekeeping and high-level cleaning

How much dust is too much?

NFPA guidance uses layer depth and area to judge accumulation, with a commonly cited threshold of 1/32 inch over a significant area for many dusts. The right threshold for your facility should come from your dust hazard analysis.

Can we use a shop vacuum?

Ordinary shop vacuums can create sparks and static. Combustible dust should be collected with vacuums designed for that purpose, and in classified areas the equipment must be suitable for the location.

Is housekeeping enough to prevent an explosion?

Housekeeping reduces fuel, but it is only one part of a broader program. Dust collection, ignition source control, explosion protection on equipment and worker training are also needed.
