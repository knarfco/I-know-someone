Graffiti removal and anti-graffiti coating

Removal of spray paint, marker and etching from masonry, metal, glass and signage, with optional sacrificial or permanent anti-graffiti coatings.

Also called: graffiti abatement, tag removal, anti-graffiti coating application

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Graffiti removal matches the remover to both the paint and the surface. Porous brick and stone may need poultices or gel strippers followed by a hot-water rinse. Painted metal and signage need solvents that lift the tag without softening factory coatings. Etched glass may need polishing or film replacement. Where removal would damage a surface, color-matched paint-out is an option. Many owners then apply an anti-graffiti coating. Sacrificial coatings come off with the next tag. Permanent coatings let future graffiti be wiped off with a mild solvent. Runoff and paint residue are captured where local rules require it.

When you need it

  • New tags on storefronts, walls, bridges, signs or fleet vehicles
  • Municipal notices requiring removal within a set number of days
  • Repeat vandalism that justifies a protective coating program
  • Ghosting or shadows left by earlier removal attempts

Credentials to ask for

  • Experience removing graffiti from the same substrate (brick, stone, metal, glass)
  • Training on the safe handling of solvents, with safety data sheets available on request
  • Knowledge of local stormwater rules for paint and solvent runoff
  • Coating applicator training from the anti-graffiti coating manufacturer where a coating is used

What the scope includes

  • Identification of the surface and the type of paint or marker
  • Small test removal to check for ghosting or damage
  • Removal using the chosen chemistry, heat and pressure
  • Capture and disposal of residue and rinse water as required
  • Optional application of a sacrificial or permanent anti-graffiti coating

How it is priced

Priced per square foot, per incident or through a monthly rapid-response contract. Cost depends on surface type, paint type, area, access and whether coating is included.

Ask before you hire

  • How quickly can you respond after a new tag?
  • Will removal leave a shadow on this surface?
  • Do you recommend a sacrificial or permanent coating here, and why?

Questions about graffiti removal and anti-graffiti coating

Why remove graffiti quickly?

Paint bonds more deeply into porous surfaces over time, which makes removal harder. Fast removal also tends to discourage repeat tagging.

Can graffiti be removed from brick without damage?

Usually yes, with the right chemistry and low pressure. Abrasive blasting can remove graffiti but often damages the brick face, so it is generally a last resort.

Do anti-graffiti coatings change how a wall looks?

Some add a slight sheen or darken porous materials. Ask for a sample area. Breathable coatings are usually preferred on masonry.
