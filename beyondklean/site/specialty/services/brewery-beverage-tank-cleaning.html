Brewery and beverage tank cleaning

Cleaning of fermenters, brite tanks, silos and process vessels, including manual cleaning that may require confined space entry.

Also called: fermenter cleaning, brite tank cleaning, winery tank cleaning, beverage vessel sanitation

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Beverage tank cleaning covers fermenters, brite tanks, wine tanks, syrup tanks and storage silos. Most vessels are cleaned in place with spray devices, but scale such as beerstone, tartrate deposits, failed spray coverage and damaged gaskets need periodic manual attention. Entering a tank is permit-required confined space work under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146: the vessel must be isolated, ventilated and tested, especially for carbon dioxide left from fermentation, with an attendant and rescue plan in place. Crews descale with acid cleaners, inspect welds and fittings, replace gaskets, and verify cleanliness before the tank returns to service.

When you need it

  • Beerstone or tartrate buildup that CIP does not remove
  • Off-flavors or microbial spoilage traced to a vessel
  • Annual inspection of tank interiors and fittings
  • Commissioning used or new tanks

Credentials to ask for

  • Permit-required confined space entry under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146
  • Atmospheric testing for oxygen and carbon dioxide
  • Lockout/tagout under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147
  • Chemical handling for acid and caustic cleaners

What the scope includes

  • Isolation of lines, CO2 purge and atmospheric testing
  • Confined space permit, attendant and rescue plan
  • Manual cleaning and descaling of tank interiors
  • Inspection of spray devices, welds, valves and gaskets
  • Rinse, sanitize and verification before return to service

How it is priced

Usually priced per tank or per day. Tank size, scale level, confined space entry needs and number of vessels drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Will anyone enter the tank, and who provides the attendant and rescue?
  • How will you test for carbon dioxide before and during entry?
  • What chemistry will you use on our tank material?
  • Will you inspect and report on spray devices and welds?

Questions about brewery and beverage tank cleaning

Why is CO2 a concern in tanks?

Fermentation produces carbon dioxide, which is heavier than air and displaces oxygen. It can stay in a tank after it is drained.

Can tanks be cleaned without entry?

Usually, yes, with CIP. Entry is reserved for inspection or deposits that CIP cannot remove.

What is beerstone?

It is a calcium oxalate deposit that builds up in brewing vessels and can shelter microorganisms. Acid cleaners remove it.
