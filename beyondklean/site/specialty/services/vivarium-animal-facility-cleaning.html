Vivarium and animal facility cleaning

Cleaning and sanitation of research animal facilities, including animal rooms, cage wash areas and support spaces.

Also called: animal research facility cleaning, lab animal facility sanitation, cage wash support

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Vivarium cleaning keeps research animal housing and support areas sanitary so animal health and research results are protected. Crews clean and sanitize animal rooms, corridors, procedure rooms and cage wash areas on schedules set by the facility, following gowning and traffic patterns that move from clean to dirty and from higher to lower biosecurity. The Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals sets sanitation expectations used by AAALAC International accreditation and PHS-funded institutions, and USDA enforces the Animal Welfare Act for covered species. Workers face animal allergens and, in some rooms, biohazards, so respiratory protection and occupational health enrollment are common.

When you need it

  • Routine room sanitation between animal cohorts
  • Sanitation ahead of AAALAC site visits or USDA inspections
  • Pathogen detection in a colony
  • Staffing gaps in the animal care program

Credentials to ask for

  • Training in facility SOPs and the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals
  • Participation in the institution's occupational health program, including allergy precautions
  • Bloodborne pathogens or biosafety training for biohazard rooms
  • AALAS technician certification is a useful indicator of experience

What the scope includes

  • Gowning and entry following facility traffic patterns
  • Cleaning and sanitizing floors, walls, ceilings and fixtures
  • Support of cage wash and rack sanitation
  • Sanitation verification, such as RODAC plates or ATP where used
  • Logs for each room and task

How it is priced

Usually priced per labor hour or as a monthly contract based on rooms and frequency. Facility size, biosecurity level, PPE needs and cage wash support drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Have your staff worked in AAALAC-accredited facilities?
  • How do you follow traffic patterns between biosecurity zones?
  • How do you protect staff from animal allergens?
  • How is sanitation verified and recorded?

Questions about vivarium and animal facility cleaning

Why do traffic patterns matter?

Moving from dirty to clean areas can carry pathogens into healthy colonies. Facilities set one-way patterns to prevent this.

Who sets cleaning frequency?

The institution's animal care program, guided by the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and its veterinarian.

Are contract cleaners covered by the occupational health program?

Many institutions require it because of allergen and zoonotic risks. Ask before work starts.
