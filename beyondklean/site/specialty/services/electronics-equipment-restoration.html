Electronics and equipment restoration after fire or flood

Cleaning soot, moisture and corrosive residue from electronics, controls and IT equipment so they can be tested and returned to service.

Also called: electronics decontamination, server and IT equipment cleaning, control panel restoration

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Electronics restoration removes contamination that causes shorts and corrosion. Technicians power down and tag equipment, assess what can be saved, then disassemble and clean boards and components with deionized water, specialty solutions or controlled dry methods. Parts are dried and inspected, then function tested before return. Residue from burning PVC and other plastics is often acidic, so speed matters. Work is usually coordinated with the equipment maker or service provider to protect warranties and service agreements.

When you need it

  • Smoke or soot reached servers, controls or office electronics
  • Water or fire suppression agents contacted equipment
  • Corrosion appears on connectors after an event
  • The insurer asks whether equipment can be restored rather than replaced

Credentials to ask for

  • Documented electronics restoration procedures and testing
  • Electrostatic discharge (ESD) controls
  • Coordination with equipment makers and service providers
  • Insurance covering equipment in the contractor's care

What the scope includes

  • Power down, tag and assess equipment
  • Stabilize humidity in the space
  • Disassemble and clean components
  • Dry and inspect
  • Function test and report

How it is priced

Usually priced per unit or per rack, or by technician hour for large systems. Cost depends on equipment type, contamination level, disassembly and testing.

Ask before you hire

  • How will cleaning affect our warranties or service contracts?
  • What tests will you run before returning equipment?
  • What do you do with equipment you cannot restore?

Questions about electronics and equipment restoration after fire or flood

Should we power equipment on to check it?

Usually not. Powering wet or contaminated equipment can cause shorts. Ask a restoration specialist first.

Why is speed important?

Acidic residues and moisture start corroding metal right away. Early cleaning improves the chance of saving equipment.

Is restoration cheaper than replacement?

Often for expensive equipment, but not always. Lead times, warranty status and data risk also matter.
