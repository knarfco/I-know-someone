Stamping press and hydraulic press cleaning

Cleaning of mechanical and hydraulic presses, bolsters, die areas and press pits to remove drawing compound, oil and slug build-up.

Also called: press line cleaning, punch press cleaning, die area cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Press cleaning removes drawing lubricants, hydraulic oil, metal slugs and scale from the press frame, bolster, ram face, slide gibs and the pit below. Presses carry gravity and stored hydraulic energy, so the slide must be blocked and the press locked out before anyone works in the die space. Crews hand scrape heavy deposits, vacuum slugs and oily sludge, and degrease with products that do not attack seals. Press pits are often permit-required confined spaces and are handled under that program. The work is usually scheduled with die changes or press rebuilds.

When you need it

  • Oil and lubricant build-up is creating slip hazards and housekeeping findings
  • Slugs and scrap are accumulating in the bolster or pit
  • Before a press rebuild, relocation or inspection
  • Annual plant shutdown

Credentials to ask for

  • Training in mechanical power press safety (OSHA 29 CFR 1910.217) and use of safety blocks
  • Lockout/tagout per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147, including bleeding hydraulic and pneumatic energy
  • Permit-required confined space training (OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146) if press pits are entered
  • Experience with oily waste and used oil handling

What the scope includes

  • Coordinate with maintenance to block the slide and lock out the press
  • Vacuum slugs, scrap and loose debris from the bolster and die area
  • Scrape and degrease frame, uprights and ram surfaces
  • Clean the press pit under the applicable entry procedure
  • Collect oily water and sludge into drums or a vacuum unit
  • Joint inspection before locks are removed

How it is priced

Usually priced per press or by crew-day. Press tonnage and size, pit depth, confined space requirements and volume of oily waste are the main cost drivers.

Ask before you hire

  • Who installs safety blocks, your crew or our maintenance staff?
  • Is your crew trained for confined space entry into press pits?
  • How will you separate used oil from other waste?
  • What degreasers will you use near hydraulic seals?

Questions about stamping press and hydraulic press cleaning

Why do presses need safety blocks before cleaning?

A press slide can drift down from gravity or residual hydraulic pressure even when power is off. Safety blocks physically prevent the slide from closing on someone working in the die space.

Is a press pit a confined space?

Many are. A pit that is large enough to enter, has limited entry and exit, and is not designed for continuous occupancy meets OSHA's confined space definition, and oil vapors or low oxygen can make it permit-required.

Can the oily waste go in the regular trash?

No. Used oil and oily sludge must be managed under EPA used oil rules (40 CFR Part 279) and any state requirements, typically by a licensed used oil collector.
