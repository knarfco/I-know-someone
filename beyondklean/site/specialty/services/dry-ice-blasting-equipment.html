Dry ice blasting of industrial equipment

Non-abrasive dry ice blast cleaning of molds, machinery and electrical-adjacent equipment with no secondary blast media left behind.

Also called: CO2 blasting, cryogenic cleaning, dry ice cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Dry ice blasting propels solid carbon dioxide pellets or shaved ice with compressed air. The impact and thermal shock loosen coatings, oil, release agents and residue, and the ice sublimates to gas, so only the removed contaminant is left to clean up. It is used on injection molds, food equipment, printing presses, foundry core boxes and machinery where water or grit would cause damage. Work is noisy and produces carbon dioxide gas, so ventilation and monitoring matter, especially indoors or in small rooms. Equipment is normally de-energized and locked out first.

When you need it

  • Equipment cannot tolerate water, solvents or abrasive media
  • Cleaning molds in place to avoid removing them from the press
  • Removing baked-on residue during short downtime windows
  • Cleaning after a fire to remove soot from machinery

Credentials to ask for

  • Operator training from the blast equipment manufacturer
  • Carbon dioxide monitoring and ventilation plan; the OSHA PEL for CO2 is 5,000 ppm as an 8-hour TWA
  • Hearing conservation awareness under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.95, since blasting is loud
  • Lockout/tagout training per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147

What the scope includes

  • Test patch to confirm the method removes the residue without harming the substrate
  • Lockout of equipment and setup of ventilation and CO2 monitors
  • Blast cleaning of equipment surfaces
  • Collection of dislodged residue by vacuum or sweeping
  • Post-clean inspection with plant staff

How it is priced

Usually priced by the hour or day, plus dry ice consumed. Run time, ice usage, access, and the difficulty of the residue drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Can you do a test area first?
  • How will you monitor CO2 levels in our space?
  • What compressed air supply do you need, and do you bring your own compressor?
  • How will you contain and collect the residue that comes off?

Questions about dry ice blasting of industrial equipment

Is dry ice blasting safe on electrical equipment?

It is often used on de-energized electrical components because it leaves no water. Equipment should still be locked out, and condensation from cooled surfaces should be allowed to clear before re-energizing.

Does dry ice blasting remove rust or paint?

It removes many coatings and light rust but is less aggressive than abrasive blasting. Some contractors add a small amount of fine abrasive for tougher jobs, which then leaves media to clean up.

Why is ventilation important?

As the ice sublimates it releases carbon dioxide, which can build up in enclosed or low areas. Monitoring and fresh air keep levels below exposure limits.
