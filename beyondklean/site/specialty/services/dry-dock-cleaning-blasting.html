Dry dock hull blasting and cleaning

Abrasive blasting or water jetting of hulls in dry dock, plus cleanup of spent media, paint debris and dock floors.

Also called: dry dock blasting, hull abrasive blasting, dock floor cleanup, UHP hull stripping

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

While a vessel is in dry dock, crews remove old coatings, rust and fouling from the hull by abrasive blasting or ultra-high-pressure water jetting. The surface is prepared to the standard the coating system requires, then tested for salts and profile. Spent abrasive and paint chips can contain lead, chromium, tin compounds or copper, so they must be contained and characterized. Dock floors and drainage must be cleaned before the dock is flooded so debris does not wash into the harbor.

When you need it

  • Scheduled dry docking and hull repainting
  • Coating failure or heavy corrosion
  • Steel renewal that requires bare metal
  • Before undocking, to clear the dock floor

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA shipyard rules for mechanical paint removers, 29 CFR 1915.34
  • Exposure controls for lead (29 CFR 1915.1025), hexavalent chromium (29 CFR 1915.1026) and respirable crystalline silica (29 CFR 1910.1053)
  • AMPP (formerly SSPC or NACE) coating inspector or contractor qualifications such as AMPP QP 1
  • Waste characterization and disposal under RCRA rules, 40 CFR Part 261

What the scope includes

  • Review coating specification and hazardous material survey
  • Set up containment and dust controls
  • Blast or water jet to the specified cleanliness standard
  • Test surface profile and soluble salts
  • Collect spent media and paint debris for testing and disposal
  • Clean dock floor and drains before flooding

How it is priced

Usually priced per square meter or square foot of surface prepared, with waste disposal billed per ton. Cost depends on coating thickness and hazards, the cleanliness standard required, and dock time limits.

Ask before you hire

  • What surface standard and test methods will you meet?
  • Has the old coating been tested for lead or chromium?
  • How will spent media be tested and where will it go?
  • How will you clean the dock floor before flooding?

Questions about dry dock hull blasting and cleaning

Abrasive blasting or water jetting?

Abrasive blasting creates a surface profile quickly but makes more dust and waste. Water jetting makes little dust and removes salts well but does not create a new profile. The coating system often decides.

Why test spent abrasive?

Old marine coatings can contain lead, chromium or other metals. Testing tells you whether the waste is hazardous under RCRA.

Who controls dock floor runoff?

Shipyards hold stormwater or wastewater permits for docks. Cleanup must meet those permits before the dock is flooded.
