Mold assessment and testing

Independent inspection, moisture mapping and sampling to define mold problems and write remediation protocols.

Also called: mold inspection, mold air sampling, microbial assessment

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Mold assessment identifies where mold is growing, why, and how much material is affected. An assessor inspects the building, uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find wet materials, and may collect air, surface or bulk samples for analysis at an accredited laboratory. Sampling is useful for specific questions, but it does not replace a good inspection. The result is a report with findings and, for remediation projects, a written protocol that describes containment, removal and clearance criteria. In several states, including Florida, Texas and New York, the assessor must be licensed and separate from the remediation company.

When you need it

  • Musty odors or health complaints with no visible mold
  • After water damage to decide what needs removal
  • Before remediation to write a protocol
  • Property transaction or tenant dispute needing documentation

Credentials to ask for

  • State mold assessor license where required; for example Florida, Texas and New York; requirements vary by state
  • Professional credentials such as Certified Industrial Hygienist or ACAC council-certified designations
  • Use of laboratories accredited under the AIHA Laboratory Accreditation Program EMLAP
  • Professional liability (errors and omissions) insurance

What the scope includes

  • Interview occupants and review water history
  • Visual inspection and moisture mapping
  • Targeted sampling where it answers a question
  • Laboratory analysis and data interpretation
  • Report with findings and remediation protocol

How it is priced

Usually priced per inspection plus per sample, or hourly for larger buildings. Cost depends on building size, number of samples, lab turnaround and report detail.

Ask before you hire

  • Are you licensed as a mold assessor in our state?
  • Do you or your company perform remediation?
  • Which accredited laboratory analyzes your samples?
  • Will the report include a written remediation protocol?

Questions about mold assessment and testing

Is mold testing always needed?

No. If mold is visible and the cause is known, EPA guidance says sampling is often unnecessary. Testing helps when growth is hidden or results are disputed.

Why should the assessor be independent?

An independent assessor has no stake in how much remediation is done. Some states, such as Florida, restrict an assessor's company from remediating the same property within a set period.

What do air sample results mean?

Results are usually compared with outdoor samples and control areas. There are no federal mold exposure limits, so interpretation requires professional judgment.
