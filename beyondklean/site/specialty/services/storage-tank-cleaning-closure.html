Underground and aboveground storage tank cleaning and closure

Cleaning, sludge removal, closure in place or removal of USTs and ASTs, with site assessment and state closure paperwork.

Also called: UST removal, AST cleaning, tank closure, tank decommissioning

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Tank cleaning removes product, water and sludge from underground or aboveground storage tanks for inspection, repair, change of product or closure. Crews vacuum out liquids, ventilate the tank to safe vapor levels and enter only under a permit-required confined space program. For underground tanks, federal rules at 40 CFR Part 280 Subpart G require advance notice to the implementing agency, cleaning, and a site assessment for releases before permanent closure is complete. The tank is then removed or filled in place with an inert solid. Most states run their own tank programs and set added licensing and inspection rules.

When you need it

  • Retiring a fuel UST at a fleet yard, gas station or emergency generator
  • Cleaning an AST before an internal inspection
  • Changing the product stored in a tank
  • Sludge buildup reducing tank capacity or fuel quality

Credentials to ask for

  • State UST closure, installer or remover license or certification where required; requirements vary by state
  • OSHA permit-required confined space program under 29 CFR 1910.146
  • Knowledge of API Standard 2015 for safe entry and cleaning of petroleum storage tanks
  • HAZWOPER training where the site involves contaminated soil or hazardous waste
  • Pollution and contractor liability insurance

What the scope includes

  • File closure notice with the state or local implementing agency
  • Remove liquids and sludge and ventilate or inert the tank
  • Confined space entry and interior cleaning
  • Remove the tank or fill it in place with inert material
  • Site assessment sampling for evidence of release
  • Closure report and disposal records for the tank, product and sludge

How it is priced

Often lump sum per tank, driven by tank size, contents, sludge volume, excavation needs and whether contamination is found. Disposal of sludge and soil is usually billed by gallon or ton.

Ask before you hire

  • Are you licensed for tank closure in our state?
  • Who files the closure notice and who takes the site assessment samples?
  • What is your plan if the samples show a release?
  • How will you handle confined space entry and vapor control?

Questions about underground and aboveground storage tank cleaning and closure

How far ahead must we notify the agency before closing a UST?

Federal rules require notice at least 30 days before beginning permanent closure, unless the agency sets another reasonable timeframe. Many states add their own forms and shorter notices before the actual removal date.

Is it better to remove a tank or close it in place?

Removal makes contamination easier to see and avoids future questions from buyers or lenders. Closure in place may be allowed when removal would undermine a building or other structure. State rules and site conditions decide.

What happens if contamination is found?

The owner usually must report the release and begin corrective action under state rules. That can involve soil removal, groundwater investigation and longer-term monitoring.
