Hazardous waste removal and disposal (RCRA)

Profiling, packaging, manifesting and transport of RCRA hazardous waste to permitted treatment, storage and disposal facilities.

Also called: RCRA waste disposal, hazardous waste pickup, lab pack service, waste profiling

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

This service moves hazardous waste from where it is generated to a permitted treatment, storage or disposal facility. A provider helps the generator determine whether each waste is hazardous, assigns waste codes, builds a waste profile and chooses a disposal method such as fuel blending, incineration, stabilization or recycling. Chemists may sort laboratory chemicals into lab packs by compatibility. Containers are packed, labeled and marked to DOT rules and shipped under the Uniform Hazardous Waste Manifest, now tracked in EPA's e-Manifest system. The generator gets the final signed manifest back as proof of receipt.

When you need it

  • Routine pickup of spent solvents, paint waste, corrosives or other process waste
  • Laboratory cleanout or chemical inventory reduction
  • Plant closure or line shutdown leaving unused chemicals
  • Change in generator status that requires better waste tracking

Credentials to ask for

  • EPA identification number and state hazardous waste transporter permits where required
  • DOT hazmat registration and employee training under 49 CFR Parts 107 and 172
  • Knowledge of generator requirements in 40 CFR Part 262 and e-Manifest procedures
  • Use of permitted TSDFs, with the provider able to name each facility
  • State hazardous waste rules may be stricter than federal; requirements vary by state

What the scope includes

  • Inventory and identify waste streams
  • Waste determination, codes and profile approval with the receiving facility
  • Packaging, lab packing, labeling and marking
  • Manifest preparation and loading
  • Transport to a permitted facility
  • Return of signed manifests and certificates of disposal

How it is priced

Typically priced per container (by drum size) or per pound or gallon, plus transport, profile and labor charges. Cost depends on waste type, treatment method, volume and frequency of pickup.

Ask before you hire

  • Which permitted facility will treat each waste stream, and by what method?
  • Who is responsible for the waste determination and profile accuracy?
  • Will your drivers and packers have current DOT hazmat training on site?
  • How do you handle the e-Manifest and exception reporting if a manifest is not returned?

Questions about hazardous waste removal and disposal (rcra)

What does generator status mean?

EPA classifies generators as very small, small or large quantity based on how much hazardous waste they produce each month. Status sets storage time limits, training and reporting duties. Some states define categories differently.

What is a lab pack?

A lab pack is a drum holding many small containers of compatible chemicals surrounded by absorbent. Chemists sort and pack them so incompatible materials stay apart.

Does hiring a disposal company end my liability?

No. Under RCRA the generator remains responsible for its waste from generation to final disposal. Choosing a reputable provider and keeping records is part of managing that risk.
