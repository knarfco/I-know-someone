Sharps and needle sweeps

Systematic search and safe removal of discarded needles and sharps from properties, parks, encampments and buildings.

Also called: needle pickup, syringe cleanup, encampment sharps removal

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Sharps sweeps find and remove discarded syringes and other sharps from parking lots, stairwells, restrooms, landscaping, vacant buildings and encampment sites. Technicians search in grid patterns, use tongs or grabbers rather than hands, and drop each item into a puncture-resistant sharps container. They wear puncture-resistant gloves and footwear. Containers are sealed and disposed of as regulated medical waste under state rules. Many property owners schedule recurring sweeps, and some add sharps disposal boxes or staff training. Logs often record counts and locations so owners can track hot spots over time.

When you need it

  • Needles found in parking areas, alleys or landscaping
  • Vacant building or lot used for drug use
  • Encampment cleanup ordered by a city or property owner
  • Restroom or stairwell with repeated needle finds

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard 29 CFR 1910.1030, including sharps handling and hepatitis B vaccination offered
  • Contract with a permitted medical waste transporter or treatment facility
  • State medical waste rules for sharps; requirements vary by state
  • Post-exposure plan for needlestick injuries

What the scope includes

  • Walk the site and map search zones
  • Grid search with tools and lighting
  • Collect sharps into approved containers
  • Log counts and locations
  • Dispose of containers as regulated medical waste

How it is priced

Usually priced per sweep or per hour, or as a recurring service agreement. Cost depends on area size, terrain, debris load, frequency and disposal volume.

Ask before you hire

  • What is your post-exposure process if a worker is stuck?
  • Will you provide counts and maps of where sharps were found?
  • How do you dispose of the full containers?
  • Can you install public sharps disposal boxes?

Questions about sharps and needle sweeps

Can sharps go in a regular dumpster?

Loose sharps should never go in regular trash. Rules for disposal vary by state and by whether the source is a business or household.

How often should a property be swept?

It depends on how often needles are found. Many owners start weekly and adjust based on sweep logs.

Can staff pick up needles themselves?

Only if trained under OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens Standard and given tools and sharps containers. Untrained staff should leave them for trained personnel.
