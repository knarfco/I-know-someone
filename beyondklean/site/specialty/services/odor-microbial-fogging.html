Antimicrobial fogging and odor treatment

Application of EPA-registered antimicrobials and odor control after cleaning, used as a supplement, not a substitute, for source removal.

Also called: microbial fogging, hydroxyl odor treatment, ozone treatment

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Antimicrobial fogging and odor treatment applies registered products or air treatment equipment after cleaning to address residual odors and microbes. Methods include ULV fogging, thermal fogging, hydroxyl generators and ozone generators. Each has limits. Fogged products must be EPA-registered and used exactly as the label allows. Ozone is a lung irritant and requires that people, pets and plants be out of the space. Industry standards, including IICRC S520, treat fogging as a supplement to physical removal, not a fix for active growth. A qualified contractor removes the odor source first, then treats what remains.

When you need it

  • Lingering odors after smoke, sewage or decomposition cleanup
  • Final step after mold or water damage work
  • Vehicle, hotel room or unit turnover with persistent odors
  • Facility requires documented odor control before reopening

Credentials to ask for

  • Use of EPA-registered antimicrobials under FIFRA, applied strictly per label
  • IICRC Odor Control Technician training
  • State pesticide applicator license where antimicrobial application requires it; requirements vary by state
  • Written safety procedures for ozone or other equipment

What the scope includes

  • Identify and remove odor sources
  • Select the treatment method for the space
  • Apply products or run equipment per label and safety rules
  • Ventilate and verify the space is safe to reenter
  • Follow-up check for returning odor

How it is priced

Usually priced per room, per square foot or per equipment day. Cost depends on space size, treatment method, number of treatments and how much source removal is needed first.

Ask before you hire

  • What is causing the odor, and will you remove the source?
  • Which product or equipment will you use, and is it EPA-registered for this use?
  • When can people safely reenter?
  • Is ozone safe for the materials in this space?

Questions about antimicrobial fogging and odor treatment

Does fogging kill mold?

Fogging does not remove mold growth, and dead mold can still cause reactions. Industry standards call for physical removal.

Is ozone safe?

Ozone can harm lungs and degrade rubber and some materials. It should only be used in unoccupied spaces with proper controls.

Why do odors come back?

Odors return when the source remains in materials or when humidity and heat release trapped compounds. Source removal is the lasting fix.
