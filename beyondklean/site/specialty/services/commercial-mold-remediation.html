Commercial mold remediation

Contained removal of mold growth and affected materials in commercial buildings, following IICRC S520 and EPA guidance and state rules.

Also called: mold removal, mold abatement, microbial remediation

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Commercial mold remediation removes mold growth and contaminated materials from offices, schools, hotels and other buildings. A contractor follows a written protocol, often prepared by an independent assessor, and the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard or EPA's guidance for schools and commercial buildings. Work areas are sealed with containment under negative pressure and HEPA filtration. Porous materials with growth are removed. Structural surfaces are HEPA vacuumed, cleaned and, where needed, sanded or blasted. The moisture source must be fixed, or the growth returns. Post-remediation verification confirms the area is ready for rebuild.

When you need it

  • Visible mold after a roof leak, pipe break or flood
  • Occupant complaints of musty odors or health symptoms
  • Inspection before a property sale or lease turnover
  • Moisture problem found behind finishes during renovation

Credentials to ask for

  • State mold remediation license where required, such as Florida, Texas, New York and Louisiana; requirements vary by state
  • IICRC Applied Microbial Remediation Technician certification and work to ANSI/IICRC S520
  • OSHA respiratory protection program under 29 CFR 1910.134
  • Contractor pollution liability insurance that does not exclude mold

What the scope includes

  • Review the assessor's protocol and locate moisture sources
  • Build containment with negative air and HEPA filtration
  • Remove affected porous materials
  • Clean structural surfaces by HEPA vacuum, wiping or abrasive methods
  • Final cleaning and pass post-remediation verification
  • Return the area for repairs and rebuild

How it is priced

Usually priced as a lump sum per project or per square foot of affected area. Cost depends on extent of growth, containment size, materials removed, access, occupancy and verification rounds.

Ask before you hire

  • Are you licensed for mold remediation in our state?
  • Will you follow an independent assessor's protocol?
  • How will you contain the area and protect occupied spaces?
  • What happens if post-remediation verification fails?

Questions about commercial mold remediation

Can mold just be sprayed and painted over?

No. IICRC S520 and EPA guidance call for physical removal of mold growth. Biocides and paints do not remove the material, and dead mold can still cause reactions.

How big an area needs a professional?

EPA guidance suggests small areas under about 10 square feet may be handled by trained staff in many cases. Larger areas, HVAC involvement or sensitive occupants call for professionals. Some states set license thresholds by area.

Do we need to move people out?

Not always. With proper containment, work can sometimes proceed in occupied buildings. Sensitive occupants may need to relocate.
