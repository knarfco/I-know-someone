Pharmacy compounding cleanroom cleaning (USP 797/800)

Cleaning, disinfecting and sporicidal treatment of sterile and hazardous drug compounding areas following USP 797 and USP 800.

Also called: USP 797 cleaning, USP 800 hazardous drug cleaning, sterile compounding cleaning, IV room cleaning

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Pharmacy cleanroom cleaning supports sterile compounding under USP General Chapter 797 and hazardous drug handling under USP 800. USP 797, revised effective November 2023, sets minimum frequencies for cleaning, disinfecting and applying a sporicidal agent in primary engineering controls, buffer rooms, ante rooms and segregated compounding areas. USP 800 adds deactivation, decontamination, cleaning and disinfection steps where hazardous drugs are handled, with chemotherapy-rated PPE. Crews use sterile or low-lint supplies, follow the pharmacy's SOPs, and log each task. State boards of pharmacy enforce these chapters to varying degrees, and accreditors review the records.

When you need it

  • Routine cleaning required by USP 797 frequencies
  • Environmental monitoring excursions in air or surface samples
  • Hazardous drug spill or contamination concern
  • Recertification or reopening after construction

Credentials to ask for

  • Training in USP 797 garbing, cleaning and documentation
  • USP 800 hazardous drug handling training and appropriate PPE
  • Use of EPA-registered disinfectants and sporicidal agents per label
  • Competency documentation the pharmacy can file; state board rules vary by state

What the scope includes

  • Garbing and hand hygiene per pharmacy SOP
  • Cleaning and disinfecting of PECs, surfaces, ceilings, walls and floors at required frequencies
  • Sporicidal application on the required schedule
  • Deactivation and decontamination in hazardous drug areas
  • Completed cleaning logs for each area and task

How it is priced

Usually priced per visit or monthly by number of rooms and frequency. Room count, hazardous drug areas, after-hours work and supplies drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How do you train and document competency in USP 797 garbing and cleaning?
  • Which disinfectant and sporicidal products do you use, and what are the contact times?
  • How do you handle cleaning in USP 800 hazardous drug rooms?
  • What logs will you complete, and who keeps them?

Questions about pharmacy compounding cleanroom cleaning (usp 797/800)

Who is responsible for USP 797 compliance?

The pharmacy and its designated person are responsible. A contractor performs tasks under the pharmacy's SOPs and records.

Why is a sporicidal agent required?

Spores resist routine disinfectants. USP 797 requires a sporicidal step at set intervals in classified areas.

Is cleaning the same as certification?

No. Room and hood certification is separate testing done by a qualified certifier.
