Glass restoration for hard water and mineral stains

Removal of hard water spots, mineral etching and construction residue from architectural glass using chemical treatment and polishing.

Also called: hard water stain removal, glass etching restoration, mineral deposit removal on glass, glass polishing

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Glass restoration removes deposits that normal window cleaning cannot. Hard water from sprinklers, concrete and masonry runoff, and silicone residue bond to glass and can etch the surface over time. The contractor first identifies the glass type, since tempered, low-e coated, reflective and laminated glass each have limits. Treatment may include mild acid-based cleaners, cerium oxide polishing or fine abrasive pads used with care. Coated surfaces and the heat-treated face of tempered glass are easy to damage, so test areas are standard. After restoration, a protective treatment may be applied and the source of the water addressed.

When you need it

  • White spotting from irrigation overspray
  • Streaks from concrete, mortar or masonry runoff
  • Cloudy glass that does not improve with routine cleaning
  • Before deciding whether glass must be replaced

Credentials to ask for

  • Experience identifying glass types and coatings before treatment
  • Familiarity with glass manufacturer and National Glass Association cleaning guidance
  • Access method training for any glass above ground level
  • Insurance that covers damage to glass and coatings

What the scope includes

  • Inspection to identify glass type, coatings and stain severity
  • Test area to confirm the stain can be removed safely
  • Chemical treatment and polishing in controlled passes
  • Final cleaning and optional protective treatment
  • Recommendations to stop the source, such as adjusting sprinklers

How it is priced

Usually priced per pane or per square foot, sometimes by the hour for test work. Severity of etching, glass type, access and protective treatment drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Can you tell whether the glass is coated or tempered before you start?
  • What will you do if the stain is etched rather than surface deposit?
  • How will you prevent scratching?

Questions about glass restoration for hard water and mineral stains

Can all hard water stains be removed?

Surface deposits usually can. Deep etching may only partly improve, and in some cases replacement is more practical. A test area will show what to expect.

Why is tempered glass a concern?

Tempered glass can carry small surface debris from manufacturing that scratches under a blade or abrasive pad. Contractors who know this use methods that reduce the risk.

How do we stop new stains?

Fix the water source first, often sprinkler heads or runoff paths. A protective treatment can make future cleaning easier.
