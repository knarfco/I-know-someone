Tenant decommissioning and clean-out

Clearing equipment, furniture, chemicals and waste from vacated spaces so they can be returned to the landlord or reused.

Also called: lease-end clean-out, space decommissioning, facility clean-out, lab decommissioning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Tenant decommissioning gets a vacated office, lab, warehouse or shop ready for handback. Crews inventory and remove furniture, equipment, records and leftover chemicals. Universal waste, such as lamps and batteries, and electronic waste are separated for recyclers. Labs may need decontamination following ANSI/ASSE Z9.11. Spaces are then cleaned to the lease standard. Documentation helps show the space was returned in the required condition.

When you need it

  • A lease is ending and the space must be returned broom clean or better
  • A lab or research space is closing
  • A warehouse or shop is consolidating
  • A building is being sold or transferred

Credentials to ask for

  • Universal waste handling under 40 CFR Part 273 or state equivalents
  • Laboratory decommissioning practice consistent with ANSI/ASSE Z9.11 when labs are involved
  • Certified data destruction for records and drives
  • General liability insurance

What the scope includes

  • Walk the space with the tenant and landlord to confirm handback terms
  • Inventory and remove furniture, equipment and records
  • Separate universal waste, electronics and leftover chemicals
  • Decontaminate lab benches, hoods and storage if present
  • Clean the space and deliver photos and disposal records

How it is priced

Usually priced as a lump sum per space, or by crew-day plus disposal. Cost depends on volume left behind, waste types, data destruction and any lab decontamination.

Ask before you hire

  • How will you handle chemicals and universal waste?
  • Will you destroy records and drives securely?
  • What documentation will you provide for the landlord?

Questions about tenant decommissioning and clean-out

What is universal waste?

Universal waste is a group of common hazardous wastes, such as lamps, batteries and mercury devices. It has simpler handling rules but must still go to proper recyclers.

Do labs need special handling?

Yes. Labs may have chemical, biological or radioactive residues. Decontamination and documentation should be finished before the space is released.

Can furniture be donated?

Often, yes. Many clean-out contractors work with donation and resale partners to reduce landfill waste.
