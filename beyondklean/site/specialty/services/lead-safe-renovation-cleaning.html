Post-renovation lead-safe cleaning and cleaning verification

Specialized cleaning after renovation in pre-1978 housing and child-occupied facilities, ending with EPA RRP cleaning verification.

Also called: RRP cleaning verification, lead-safe cleanup, post-renovation lead cleaning, lead dust cleanup after remodeling

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Renovation that disturbs paint in pre-1978 homes and child-occupied facilities is covered by EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule, 40 CFR Part 745 Subpart E. After work, crews mist and remove plastic sheeting, HEPA vacuum all surfaces, and wet wipe and mop with a two-bucket method. A certified renovator then performs cleaning verification, wiping floors and sills with wet disposable cloths and comparing them to the EPA verification card, recleaning until they pass. Some projects use dust wipe testing by an accredited lab instead.

When you need it

  • Renovations in homes or schools built before 1978
  • Window replacement or paint removal in older buildings
  • Daycares or kindergartens with renovation
  • Federally assisted housing projects

Credentials to ask for

  • EPA RRP Certified Firm and Certified Renovator, or a state program authorized by EPA
  • Knowledge of 40 CFR 745.85 cleaning and cleaning verification steps
  • HUD Lead Safe Housing Rule compliance for federally assisted housing
  • HEPA vacuums and lead-safe work practice equipment

What the scope includes

  • Mist and remove plastic sheeting
  • HEPA vacuum surfaces
  • Wet wipe and mop
  • Perform cleaning verification
  • Reclean until surfaces pass
  • Document results

How it is priced

Usually priced per room or per work area, or as part of the renovation contract. Cost depends on work area size, number of verification rounds and any lab testing.

Ask before you hire

  • Is your firm EPA RRP certified, and who is the Certified Renovator?
  • How will you perform and record cleaning verification?
  • Will you provide dust wipe testing if required?

Questions about post-renovation lead-safe cleaning and cleaning verification

What is cleaning verification?

A procedure where a certified renovator wipes surfaces with wet cloths and compares them to an EPA card. Surfaces must pass before the area is released.

Is cleaning verification the same as clearance testing?

No. Clearance testing uses lab-analyzed dust samples and is required for abatement and some housing programs.

Does this apply to commercial buildings?

The federal RRP rule applies to target housing and child-occupied facilities. Some states and cities add rules for other buildings.
