Infectious disease decontamination

Targeted cleaning and disinfection of facilities after confirmed or suspected outbreaks such as norovirus, C. diff or MRSA.

Also called: outbreak disinfection, pathogen decontamination, C. diff or norovirus disinfection

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Infectious disease decontamination cleans and disinfects spaces after a confirmed or suspected outbreak, such as norovirus in a cruise terminal, C. difficile in a nursing home or MRSA in a locker room. A contractor works from public health guidance and the facility's infection prevention team to choose disinfectants with EPA-registered claims against the specific organism. Some organisms, like C. diff spores, need sporicidal products. Technicians clean first, then disinfect high-touch and affected surfaces for the full contact time. Some jobs add electrostatic spraying or other application methods, but only as directed on the product label.

When you need it

  • Norovirus or gastrointestinal outbreak at a school, hotel or care facility
  • Clusters of C. diff or drug-resistant organisms in healthcare settings
  • Public health department recommends enhanced cleaning
  • Reopening after an outbreak closure

Credentials to ask for

  • Training in OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens 29 CFR 1910.1030 and respiratory protection 1910.134 where exposure is possible
  • Use of EPA-registered disinfectants with claims for the target organism, applied per label as FIFRA requires
  • Familiarity with CDC environmental infection control guidance
  • Trade certification such as GBAC or IICRC courses is useful but not legally required

What the scope includes

  • Review the organism, affected areas and facility protocols
  • Select products with matching EPA claims
  • Clean surfaces to remove soil before disinfection
  • Disinfect for full contact time, focusing on high-touch surfaces
  • Handle linens and waste per facility and state rules
  • Document products, areas and times

How it is priced

Typically priced per square foot or per room, or per crew hour for urgent work. Cost depends on organism, product type, facility size, after-hours scheduling and level of documentation.

Ask before you hire

  • Which product will you use and does its EPA label list this organism?
  • How do you make sure surfaces stay wet for the full contact time?
  • Will you coordinate with our infection preventionist?
  • What documentation will we receive?

Questions about infectious disease decontamination

Does fogging replace manual disinfection?

No. Most disinfectants need clean surfaces and the label contact time to work. Spraying or fogging is only valid when the product label allows it, and it does not remove soil.

What is a sporicidal disinfectant?

It is a product with an EPA claim against bacterial spores, such as C. difficile. Many common disinfectants do not kill spores.

Is this regulated work?

There is generally no specific license, but pesticide labeling law under FIFRA governs disinfectant use. Healthcare facilities also follow infection control requirements from regulators and accreditors.
