Selective and interior demolition cleanup

Cleaning up after interior and selective demolition in occupied or adjoining buildings, with dust control and protected paths.

Also called: interior demo cleanup, soft strip cleanup, gut-out cleanup, tenant demo debris removal

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Selective demolition removes ceilings, partitions, flooring, fixtures and finishes while the building structure stays. Cleanup in these jobs often happens next to occupied floors or tenants. Crews set dust barriers and negative air, protect elevators and corridors, and move debris through agreed routes and times. They sweep, HEPA vacuum and wipe down surfaces so the next trade or tenant can start. Dry sweeping of silica-containing debris is restricted, so wet methods or HEPA vacuums are used.

When you need it

  • Office or retail fit-out removal
  • Hospital or school renovation in an occupied building
  • Preparing a floor for a new tenant
  • Removing damaged finishes after a loss

Credentials to ask for

  • Dust control plan meeting OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1153 housekeeping limits
  • Infection control risk assessment procedures for healthcare projects
  • Building access and after-hours work coordination
  • General liability insurance

What the scope includes

  • Set barriers and negative air
  • Protect paths, elevators and finishes
  • Remove debris on set routes and schedules
  • HEPA vacuum and wet wipe
  • Remove barriers and final clean

How it is priced

Usually priced per square foot of floor area or per crew shift. Cost depends on occupancy, after-hours work, elevator access and debris volume.

Ask before you hire

  • How will you control dust near occupied areas?
  • When and how will debris move through the building?
  • Will you follow our infection control requirements?

Questions about selective and interior demolition cleanup

Why not just sweep?

Dry sweeping spreads fine dust, including silica. HEPA vacuums and wet methods keep dust down.

Can work happen during business hours?

Quiet tasks can. Loud work and debris moves are often scheduled after hours to limit disruption.

What is negative air?

Negative air uses fans with HEPA filters to pull air out of the work area. This keeps air flowing into the work zone so dust does not escape.
