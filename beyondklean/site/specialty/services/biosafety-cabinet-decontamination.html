Biosafety cabinet decontamination

Gaseous decontamination of biosafety cabinets before filter changes, repairs, moves or disposal, followed by NSF/ANSI 49 field certification.

Also called: BSC decontamination, biological safety cabinet decon, BSC gas decontamination

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Biosafety cabinet decontamination treats the entire cabinet, including plenums, fans and HEPA filters, so it can be safely opened, moved or disposed of. Surface wiping alone cannot reach these internal areas. Technicians seal the cabinet and introduce a decontaminant such as vaporized hydrogen peroxide or chlorine dioxide, sometimes formaldehyde, following the method described in NSF/ANSI 49 and the agent's label. Biological or chemical indicators confirm the cycle worked. The cabinet is then aerated before work. After filter replacement, repair or relocation, the cabinet must be field certified to NSF/ANSI 49, ideally by an NSF-accredited field certifier.

When you need it

  • Before HEPA filter replacement or internal repair
  • Before moving a cabinet to another room or building
  • Before surplus, sale or disposal
  • After a large spill of infectious material inside the cabinet

Credentials to ask for

  • NSF-accredited biosafety cabinet field certifier, or qualified technician under the institution's policy
  • Decontamination method per NSF/ANSI 49
  • Decontaminant registered with EPA for the use, applied per label
  • Coordination with the institution's biosafety officer

What the scope includes

  • Review of agents used in the cabinet with the biosafety officer
  • Sealing of the cabinet and setup of decontamination equipment
  • Decontamination cycle with biological or chemical indicators
  • Aeration and residual monitoring
  • Decontamination tag and report; certification after service

How it is priced

Usually priced per cabinet, with discounts for multiple cabinets per visit. Cabinet size, decontaminant method, indicators and add-on certification drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Are your technicians NSF-accredited field certifiers?
  • Which decontaminant do you use, and how do you verify the cycle?
  • How long until the room is safe to reenter?
  • Will you certify the cabinet after the filter change?

Questions about biosafety cabinet decontamination

Can we just wipe the cabinet with bleach?

Surface disinfection is routine, but it does not reach the plenum, fan or filters. Gaseous decontamination is needed before those areas are opened.

How often does a BSC need certification?

NSF/ANSI 49 calls for field certification at installation, after relocation or filter changes, and at least annually.

Does decontamination damage the cabinet?

Approved methods are designed for cabinet materials. Ask the contractor about compatibility with your model.
