Allergen changeover cleaning

Validated cleaning between products to remove allergen residues and prevent undeclared allergens in the next run.

Also called: allergen cleaning, allergen washdown, product changeover sanitation

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Allergen changeover cleaning removes residues of major food allergens from shared equipment before a product without that allergen runs. US law recognizes nine major food allergens, including sesame. Under 21 CFR Part 117, facilities that need allergen preventive controls must have procedures that prevent cross-contact. The changeover follows a written procedure: disassemble, remove visible soil, wash, rinse, inspect and verify, often with allergen-specific test kits. Dry operations may require dry methods because water spreads some powders. Crews must understand which parts trap residue and how to avoid recontaminating cleaned equipment.

When you need it

  • Product scheduling that runs allergen-containing items before allergen-free items
  • Adding a new allergen to an existing line
  • Validation or revalidation of a changeover procedure
  • After an undeclared allergen complaint or recall

Credentials to ask for

  • Training on the plant's allergen control program under 21 CFR Part 117
  • Experience with allergen-specific verification test kits
  • GMP and lockout/tagout training
  • Ability to follow wet and dry cleaning procedures

What the scope includes

  • Review of the changeover procedure and allergen matrix
  • Disassembly of equipment parts that can trap residue
  • Cleaning by validated wet or dry method
  • Visual inspection and allergen verification testing
  • Records for each changeover

How it is priced

Usually priced per changeover or per labor hour on recurring schedules, with separate project pricing for validation support. Equipment complexity, dry versus wet cleaning and testing scope drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Have you worked with allergen changeovers on equipment like ours?
  • How do you clean dry-product equipment without spreading powder?
  • Which verification tests will you run, and who interprets results?
  • How do you prevent cross-contact from tools and clothing?

Questions about allergen changeover cleaning

What are the major food allergens in the US?

Milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame.

Is visual inspection enough?

Visual inspection is required but often not sufficient. Many plants verify with allergen-specific tests, especially during validation.

Who validates the procedure?

The plant does, usually with data from repeated changeovers and testing. A contractor can carry out the cleaning and help collect samples.
