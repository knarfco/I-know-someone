Raised floor and subfloor plenum cleaning

Cleaning of the underfloor plenum beneath raised access floors, including slab, pedestals and cabling, one tile section at a time.

Also called: subfloor cleaning, underfloor cleaning, raised access floor cleaning, plenum cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

In many data centers the space under the raised floor is a supply air plenum. Dust, construction debris and loose material there can be blown straight into equipment. Subfloor cleaning lifts a limited number of tiles at a time, following the site's tile-lift rules to protect airflow and floor stability. Crews HEPA-vacuum the slab, pedestals and the outside of cable bundles, then damp-wipe where allowed. They do not pull or strain cables. Debris, abandoned materials and signs of water or pests are reported. Tiles go back in their original positions.

When you need it

  • Annual or biennial subfloor maintenance
  • After cabling work, floor changes or construction
  • Debris visible through perforated tiles
  • Before particle testing or commissioning

Credentials to ask for

  • Experience with tile lifting limits and airflow management
  • ESD-safe HEPA vacuum equipment
  • Training in working around live cabling and power under the floor
  • Adherence to site MOP and access procedures

What the scope includes

  • Agreement on tile-lift limits and sequence
  • Lifting of tiles in small sections
  • HEPA vacuuming of slab, pedestals and cable exteriors
  • Report of debris, water, pests or abandoned materials
  • Reinstallation of tiles in original positions

How it is priced

Usually priced per square foot of raised floor. Cable density, plenum depth, tile-lift limits and working hours drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How many tiles will you lift at once?
  • How do you avoid disturbing cables and power whips?
  • Will you report what you find under the floor?

Questions about raised floor and subfloor plenum cleaning

Why clean under the floor?

If the subfloor is an air plenum, dust there can be blown into equipment. Construction debris often collects under floors.

Does lifting tiles affect cooling?

Lifting too many tiles can change airflow. Contractors limit open tiles and work in small sections.

What about cables under the floor?

Crews clean around them carefully and do not pull or move them. Abandoned cables are reported to the owner.
