Trauma and crime scene cleanup

Removal of blood, tissue and contaminated materials after violent incidents or serious injuries, once police release the scene.

Also called: crime scene cleaning, trauma scene remediation, homicide or suicide cleanup, bio-recovery

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Trauma and crime scene cleanup removes blood, tissue and other body fluids after a homicide, suicide, assault or serious accident. It begins only after law enforcement releases the scene. Technicians in protective equipment locate all affected areas, including fluid that has soaked into flooring, subfloor or walls. They remove porous materials that cannot be cleaned, clean and disinfect hard surfaces with EPA-registered disinfectants, and package waste as regulated medical waste. The work aims to return the space to a safe, usable condition and is often done with discretion for families, employers and building owners.

When you need it

  • Police release a scene after a violent incident in a home, business or vehicle
  • Workplace accident causing serious injury
  • Suicide in a rental property, hotel or public building
  • Assault or shooting in a retail, transit or public space

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard 29 CFR 1910.1030 compliance: exposure control plan, training and hepatitis B vaccination offered
  • State registration where required; for example, California registers trauma scene waste management practitioners with its Department of Public Health
  • Trade certification such as the American Bio Recovery Association technician credential
  • Contract with a permitted medical waste transporter or treatment facility
  • Requirements vary by state; check your state health department

What the scope includes

  • Walk-through and hazard assessment after scene release
  • Set up controlled work areas and protect unaffected spaces
  • Remove contaminated porous materials and contents
  • Clean and disinfect hard surfaces
  • Package and transport regulated medical waste
  • Final inspection with documentation for the owner or insurer

How it is priced

Usually priced per hour per technician plus materials and waste disposal per container, or as a project estimate after inspection. Cost depends on how far fluids spread, materials removed and the amount of waste.

Ask before you hire

  • Is your company registered or licensed for this work in our state?
  • Can you show your bloodborne pathogens exposure control plan?
  • How do you confirm fluids have not soaked into subfloor or walls?
  • Will you work directly with our insurance carrier?

Questions about trauma and crime scene cleanup

Do police or the medical examiner clean the scene?

No. They collect evidence and remove the deceased, but cleanup is the property owner's responsibility. Owners usually hire a specialist.

Can a regular janitorial company handle this?

Most janitorial firms are not set up for this level of bloodborne pathogen exposure or medical waste disposal. In some states, such as California, a company must be registered to do this work commercially.

How long does trauma cleanup take?

Small scenes may take a few hours. Scenes with fluid in subfloors or many rooms can take several days, plus time for any rebuild.
