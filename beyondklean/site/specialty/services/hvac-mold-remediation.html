HVAC mold remediation

Cleaning or replacement of mold-contaminated air handlers, coils, drain pans, insulation and ductwork under NADCA ACR and IICRC S520.

Also called: air handler mold cleaning, duct mold removal, coil and drain pan cleaning

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

HVAC mold remediation removes microbial growth from air handling units, coils, drain pans, internal insulation and ductwork. Contaminated HVAC systems can spread spores through a building. A contractor isolates the system, sets up negative pressure on the duct sections being cleaned, and uses source removal methods such as agitation and HEPA-filtered collection. Porous internal duct liner with growth is usually removed and replaced rather than cleaned. Coils and pans are cleaned, and drainage problems are fixed. Work follows the NADCA ACR standard, now in its 2025 edition, and IICRC S520. Verification may use visual inspection, surface tests and assessor sampling.

When you need it

  • Growth found in an air handler or on internal duct insulation
  • Clogged condensate drain pan with standing water
  • Mold remediation in a building where HVAC served the affected area
  • Odor complaints traced to the air system

Credentials to ask for

  • NADCA Air Systems Cleaning Specialist (ASCS) on staff and work to NADCA ACR
  • Mold remediation training to ANSI/IICRC S520
  • State mold remediation or mechanical licenses where required; requirements vary by state
  • Use of only EPA-registered antimicrobials labeled for HVAC use, if any are applied

What the scope includes

  • Inspect system components and identify moisture causes
  • Isolate system and set negative pressure
  • Source removal cleaning of ducts, coils and pans
  • Remove and replace contaminated porous insulation
  • Restore drainage and system operation
  • Post-cleaning verification and photo report

How it is priced

Usually priced per air handler, per linear foot of duct or as a lump sum. Cost depends on system size, access, amount of liner replacement and need for after-hours work.

Ask before you hire

  • Do you have a NADCA ASCS on staff?
  • Will you replace contaminated duct liner or try to coat it?
  • How will you keep debris from spreading during cleaning?
  • How will cleanliness be verified?

Questions about hvac mold remediation

Can mold in fiberglass duct liner be cleaned?

Growth on porous liner is usually not reliably cleanable. Industry guidance generally calls for removal and replacement.

Do antimicrobial sprays fix HVAC mold?

No. They are not a substitute for physical cleaning and must be EPA-registered and labeled for use in HVAC systems if used at all.

Should HVAC be cleaned during building mold remediation?

If the HVAC served the affected area or shows growth, it should be inspected and cleaned as part of the project.
