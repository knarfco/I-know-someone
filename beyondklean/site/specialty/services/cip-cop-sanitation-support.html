CIP and COP sanitation support

Support for clean-in-place and clean-out-of-place systems, including cycle checks, manual cleaning of dead legs and COP part washing.

Also called: clean-in-place support, clean-out-of-place, CIP validation support, COP tank cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Clean-in-place systems circulate rinse, caustic, acid and sanitizer through tanks, pipes and fillers without disassembly; clean-out-of-place uses a recirculating wash tank for parts that are removed. A contractor supports these systems by verifying flow, temperature, concentration and time against the validated cycle, finding dead legs and spray ball problems, and manually cleaning parts that CIP cannot reach, such as gaskets, valves and pump heads. COP work includes loading, monitoring and inspecting parts. Hygienic design references such as 3-A Sanitary Standards are common in dairy and beverage. Findings are documented so the plant can correct cycles or equipment.

When you need it

  • Positive swabs or residue found after a CIP cycle
  • New product, line or equipment needing cycle confirmation
  • Audit or regulatory request to show CIP effectiveness
  • Periodic teardown of components CIP does not clean well

Credentials to ask for

  • Experience with CIP chemistry and the plant's validated cycle parameters
  • Familiarity with 3-A Sanitary Standards or hygienic design in dairy and beverage
  • Chemical handling training for caustic and acid solutions
  • Lockout/tagout and confined space awareness for tank work

What the scope includes

  • Review of CIP circuits, cycle records and chemical concentrations
  • Spray device and flow checks
  • Manual teardown and cleaning of valves, gaskets and dead legs
  • COP tank loading, wash monitoring and parts inspection
  • Swab or ATP verification and written findings

How it is priced

Usually priced per project or per day for assessment, and per labor hour for recurring teardown support. Number of circuits, complexity of equipment and testing scope drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How do you check that each cycle meets time, temperature, concentration and flow?
  • How do you find dead legs or shadowed areas in a tank?
  • What verification method will you use after cleaning?
  • Will your findings include recommended cycle or equipment changes?

Questions about cip and cop sanitation support

If CIP is automated, why is outside help needed?

Automated cycles drift as pumps wear, chemicals change or equipment is modified. Periodic checks and manual teardown catch what the cycle misses.

What is COP?

Clean-out-of-place means removing parts and washing them in a dedicated recirculating tank or parts washer.

Who validates the CIP cycle?

The plant is responsible for validation. A contractor can help gather data, but the food safety team approves it.
