Lead-based paint abatement

Permanent removal or control of lead-based paint hazards by certified abatement firms, separate from routine RRP renovation work.

Also called: lead abatement, lead paint removal, lead hazard control

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Lead abatement is work designed to permanently eliminate lead-based paint hazards, not just to renovate around them. Methods include component replacement, enclosure, encapsulation and paint removal by approved wet or HEPA-controlled techniques. Open-flame burning and uncontained dry sanding or blasting are restricted. In homes and child-occupied facilities, abatement is done by EPA- or state-certified firms and individuals, and a certified inspector or risk assessor performs clearance testing afterward. On industrial steel and commercial structures, the work is governed mainly by OSHA lead rules and often uses containment classes from coatings industry standards. Waste may need hazardous waste testing.

When you need it

  • A child with an elevated blood lead level triggers a health department abatement order
  • Housing authority or HUD-assisted property requires lead hazard control
  • Daycare or school in a pre-1978 building has deteriorated lead paint
  • Bridge, tank or steel structure repainting where existing coatings contain lead

Credentials to ask for

  • EPA or state-authorized lead abatement firm certification, with certified supervisors and workers under 40 CFR Part 745 Subpart L
  • OSHA lead in construction standard 29 CFR 1926.62 compliance, including exposure monitoring and medical surveillance
  • For industrial structures, AMPP (formerly SSPC) QP 2 certification for removal of hazardous coatings
  • State lead license where the state runs its own program; requirements vary by state
  • Note: EPA RRP certification covers renovation, not abatement; ask which applies

What the scope includes

  • Review the lead inspection or risk assessment and the abatement plan
  • Set up containment, warning signs and worker hygiene facilities
  • Abate components by replacement, enclosure, encapsulation or controlled removal
  • Clean with HEPA vacuums and wet wiping
  • Clearance dust-wipe testing by a certified inspector or risk assessor
  • Waste characterization and disposal documentation

How it is priced

Residential and facility jobs are usually priced per unit or lump sum; industrial coating removal is often priced per square foot. Cost depends on abatement method, containment class, access, occupancy and waste classification.

Ask before you hire

  • Is this job abatement or renovation, and which certification applies?
  • Who performs clearance testing, and is that person independent of your crew?
  • What containment class will you use on the steel structure, and why?
  • How will you handle and test the waste stream?

Questions about lead-based paint abatement

What is the difference between lead abatement and RRP work?

Abatement is designed to permanently eliminate lead hazards and needs abatement certification. RRP covers renovation that disturbs lead paint as a side effect and needs separate RRP certification. EPA treats them as two different programs.

Does lead paint waste count as hazardous waste?

Sometimes. Commercial and industrial lead paint debris may fail the toxicity test and become RCRA hazardous waste. Federal rules give household renovation waste different treatment, and some states are stricter.

Is encapsulation an acceptable abatement method?

Yes, when the product and surface are suitable and a long-term monitoring plan is in place. It is less suitable for friction or impact surfaces such as windows and doors.
