Turbine hall and turbine deck cleaning

Cleaning of turbine decks, generator enclosures, lube oil areas and high structures in turbine halls during and after outages.

Also called: turbine deck cleaning, turbine building cleaning, powerhouse cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Turbine halls collect oil mist, dust and debris on decks, generator enclosures, structural steel, crane rails and lighting. Oil around lube oil skids and bearing housings is a fire hazard, and dust can foul cooling air intakes. Cleaning is done during outages or around operating units under plant rules. Crews degrease decks, clean equipment exteriors, vacuum high steel from lifts, and remove outage debris. Open turbine casings are protected with covers and foreign material exclusion (FME) controls so nothing falls inside.

When you need it

  • After a turbine or generator outage
  • Oil leaks have contaminated decks and equipment
  • Before a site visit, audit or insurer inspection
  • Dust build-up on high structures

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA 29 CFR 1910.269 training for generation plant work
  • Foreign material exclusion training for work near open equipment
  • Aerial lift and fall protection training
  • Lockout/tagout per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147

What the scope includes

  • Plant orientation and FME plan
  • Removal of outage debris
  • Degreasing of decks and lube oil areas
  • Exterior cleaning of turbine and generator enclosures
  • High-level cleaning of steel, crane rails and lights
  • Final walk-down

How it is priced

Priced by crew-day or lump sum per outage. Hall size, oil contamination, height of structures and shift coverage drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • What FME controls will you use near open equipment?
  • Can you work around an operating unit?
  • How will oily waste be handled?
  • Will you supply lifts?

Questions about turbine hall and turbine deck cleaning

Why is oil cleanup important in turbine halls?

Oil-soaked insulation and decks can ignite from hot surfaces. Removing oil reduces fire risk.

What is foreign material exclusion?

FME is a set of controls that keep tools, rags, debris and parts out of open equipment. It usually includes covers, tool lanyards, logged entry of items into the zone and a close-out inspection, because one loose bolt can wreck a turbine.

Can cleaning be done while a unit runs?

Some areas can, under plant rules and with clear zones. Work near hot or rotating equipment is usually limited to outages.
