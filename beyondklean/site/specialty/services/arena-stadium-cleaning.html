Arena and stadium overhead and seating bowl cleaning

Deep cleaning of seating bowls, concourses, catwalks, scoreboards and roof structure in arenas and stadiums between events or seasons.

Also called: stadium cleaning, arena deep cleaning, seating bowl power washing, catwalk cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Arenas and stadiums need more than post-event trash pickup. Deep cleaning includes power washing seating bowls and concourses, degreasing concession areas, and cleaning roof trusses, catwalks, speaker clusters, ductwork and lighting above the seating. Overhead work uses catwalk access, boom lifts set on protected floors or rope access where lifts cannot reach. Dust and debris must not fall on finished floors, ice or turf. Work is scheduled around event calendars and rigging, and coordinated with venue engineering because of rigging points, sound and lighting systems.

When you need it

  • Off-season or between-season deep cleaning
  • Before high-profile events, playoffs or broadcasts
  • Dust or debris falling from the roof onto seats or the event floor
  • After concerts or events with pyrotechnics or haze

Credentials to ask for

  • MEWP operator training and, where needed, SPRAT or IRATA rope access certification
  • Fall protection for catwalk and roof structure work under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.140
  • Experience coordinating with venue rigging and engineering teams
  • Floor protection methods for courts, ice or turf

What the scope includes

  • Planning around the event calendar and rigging schedule
  • Protection of event floor, seating and electronics
  • Overhead cleaning of trusses, catwalks, speakers and lighting
  • Power washing of seating bowl and concourses
  • Final walk-through with venue operations

How it is priced

Usually a lump sum per deep clean or a seasonal contract. Venue size, seat count, roof height, access method and event schedule drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How will you protect the event floor during overhead work?
  • Have you worked with venue riggers before?
  • Can you complete the work within our event gap?

Questions about arena and stadium overhead and seating bowl cleaning

How often should an arena roof structure be cleaned?

Many venues clean overhead areas once or twice a year, often in the off-season. Venues with frequent haze or pyrotechnics may need more.

Can lifts drive on the event floor?

Only with floor protection and load checks. Some floors need special mats or must be covered first.

Is rope access used in arenas?

Yes, for areas lifts cannot reach, such as high roof structure over seating. It requires certified technicians and approved anchors.
