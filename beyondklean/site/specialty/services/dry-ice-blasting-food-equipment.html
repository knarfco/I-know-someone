Dry ice blasting of food equipment

Non-abrasive, waterless cleaning of food equipment with dry ice pellets, used where water would spread contamination or damage parts.

Also called: CO2 blasting, dry ice cleaning, cryogenic cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Dry ice blasting propels food-grade carbon dioxide pellets at a surface, where they lift soil through impact and rapid cooling and then turn to gas, leaving no blast media behind. It suits baked-on residue, dry ingredient lines, electrical components, mixers and conveyors where water causes problems. Equipment can often be cleaned in place with less disassembly. The process creates noise and carbon dioxide gas, so ventilation and CO2 monitoring are needed in enclosed rooms; OSHA sets an exposure limit for carbon dioxide. Loosened soil still falls and must be collected, and food-contact surfaces may still need sanitizing afterward.

When you need it

  • Baked-on residue on ovens, molds or bakery equipment
  • Dry facilities where water introduces microbial risk
  • Cleaning around motors, sensors and electrical panels
  • Reducing downtime by cleaning equipment in place

Credentials to ask for

  • Training on the blasting equipment and its safety controls
  • Carbon dioxide hazard awareness and monitoring; OSHA limits in 29 CFR 1910.1000
  • Hearing protection program under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.95
  • Food-grade dry ice supply with documentation

What the scope includes

  • Site review of ventilation, electrical protection and debris capture
  • Lockout of equipment and setup of CO2 monitoring
  • Blasting of targeted surfaces
  • Collection of dislodged soil
  • Sanitizing of food-contact surfaces where required

How it is priced

Usually priced per day or per shift, plus dry ice consumed. Area size, soil type, enclosed space ventilation and access drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Is your dry ice food grade, and can you provide documentation?
  • How will you monitor CO2 levels in enclosed rooms?
  • How will you collect the soil you remove?
  • Is dry ice blasting safe for our equipment's materials and seals?

Questions about dry ice blasting of food equipment

Does dry ice blasting sanitize?

It cleans, but it is not a registered sanitizer. Plants typically sanitize food-contact surfaces after blasting.

Is it safe in a closed room?

Only with ventilation and CO2 monitoring. The pellets turn into carbon dioxide gas, which can build up.

Will it damage stainless steel?

Dry ice is non-abrasive and generally does not damage stainless steel. Delicate parts should still be tested first.
