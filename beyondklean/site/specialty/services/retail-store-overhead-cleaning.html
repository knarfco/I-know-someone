Overhead Cleaning · Specialty

Retail store overhead cleaning

After-hours cleaning of ceilings, ducts, signs and top-of-fixture surfaces in big box and specialty stores across single sites or portfolios.

Also called: store high dusting, big box ceiling cleaning, retail fixture high cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Retail stores with open ceilings, high shelving and hanging signs collect dust that shoppers notice and that can fall on merchandise. Overhead cleaning covers exposed structure, ducts, diffusers, hanging signs, light fixtures and the tops of tall fixtures and racks. Crews work after closing, covering merchandise and using HEPA vacuums from lifts. Multi-site retailers often contract for route-based service so stores are cleaned to the same standard on a set schedule. Work is coordinated with store managers, overnight stocking crews and alarm systems.

When you need it

  • Dust visible on signs, ducts or fixture tops
  • Before store remodels, grand reopenings or holiday seasons
  • Brand standards inspections
  • Portfolio-wide maintenance programs

Credentials to ask for

  • MEWP operator training per ANSI/SAIA A92.24
  • Experience with multi-site scheduling and reporting
  • Insurance that meets the retailer's vendor requirements
  • Background checks if the retailer requires them for after-hours access

What the scope includes

  • Scheduling with store management and alarm codes
  • Covering merchandise and fixtures below
  • HEPA vacuuming of structure, ducts, signs and fixture tops
  • Floor cleanup and removal of covers
  • Photo report by store

How it is priced

Usually priced per store or per square foot, with route pricing for portfolios. Store size, ceiling height and merchandise protection needs drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Can you cover all our stores in the region?
  • How do you protect merchandise?
  • What reporting will we receive after each store?

Questions about retail store overhead cleaning

How often should stores clean overhead?

Many stores clean once or twice a year, often before holiday season or after remodels.

Do stores need to close?

Usually not. Work is done after hours.

Can contractors work with overnight stocking crews?

Yes, with coordination. Lift paths and aisles are scheduled so both crews can work safely.
