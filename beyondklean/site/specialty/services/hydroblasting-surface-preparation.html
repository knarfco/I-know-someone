High-pressure water jetting for surface preparation

Using high and ultra-high pressure water to strip coatings, rust and contaminants from steel and concrete before recoating or repair.

Also called: hydroblasting, UHP water jetting, waterjet surface prep, coating removal by water jetting

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Water jetting removes coatings, rust, scale and soluble salts using water at high or ultra-high pressure, often with robotic or hand-held tools. It produces little dust and leaves no spent abrasive, though it does not create a new surface profile. Cleanliness grades follow joint AMPP standards for waterjet cleaning, often cited as SSPC-SP WJ-1 through WJ-4 and NACE WJ-1 through WJ-4. Crews follow WJTA-IMCA recommended practices for safe operation. Water and debris must be captured and may need testing.

When you need it

  • Coating removal on steel tanks, piping or structures
  • Concrete surface preparation before overlays or coatings
  • Removing soluble salts before recoating
  • Work in operating plants where abrasive dust is not allowed

Credentials to ask for

  • WJTA-IMCA recommended practices for waterjet safety
  • Familiarity with SSPC-SP WJ and NACE WJ cleanliness standards
  • Coating inspector support where specified
  • Water capture and waste handling plan

What the scope includes

  • Review coating type and any hazardous content
  • Set up water and debris capture
  • Jet surfaces to the specified cleanliness grade
  • Test cleanliness and soluble salts
  • Test, filter and dispose of water and debris

How it is priced

Usually priced per square foot of surface prepared or per shift with a crew and pump. Cost depends on coating thickness and type, cleanliness grade, access and water capture and treatment.

Ask before you hire

  • What cleanliness grade will you meet?
  • How will you capture water?
  • Do your crews follow WJTA-IMCA practices?

Questions about high-pressure water jetting for surface preparation

Does water jetting create a profile?

No. Water jetting removes coatings and rust but does not cut a new profile. It exposes the profile that was already there.

Is it safer than blasting?

It produces far less dust but brings its own hazards, including injection injuries. Trained operators and safety equipment are essential.

Can it remove lead paint?

Yes, with water and debris capture. Waste must be tested and handled as lead-containing material if results require it.
