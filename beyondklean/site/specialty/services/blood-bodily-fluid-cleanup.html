Blood and bodily fluid cleanup

Cleanup and disinfection of blood, vomit, urine and feces in workplaces, vehicles and public spaces under OSHA bloodborne pathogen rules.

Also called: body fluid spill cleanup, OPIM cleanup, vomit and blood cleanup

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Blood and bodily fluid cleanup handles smaller incidents than a full trauma scene: an injury in a warehouse, a fight in a bar, vomit on a bus or blood in a restroom. Technicians wear gloves, eye protection and fluid-resistant clothing, absorb the material, then clean and disinfect with an EPA-registered product suitable for bloodborne pathogens, following the label contact time. Contaminated items are bagged and labeled. Some materials qualify as regulated medical waste and others as ordinary trash, depending on saturation and state rules. Fast response limits staining and odor and reduces how long areas stay closed.

When you need it

  • Workplace injury leaving blood on floors or equipment
  • Bodily fluid incidents in transit vehicles, stadiums or restrooms
  • Fights or medical emergencies in hospitality venues
  • Your in-house team lacks bloodborne pathogen training for the size of the spill

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard 29 CFR 1910.1030 training, exposure control plan and hepatitis B vaccination offered
  • Use of EPA-registered disinfectants with label claims for the relevant pathogens
  • Medical waste handling under state rules; requirements vary by state
  • General liability insurance

What the scope includes

  • Isolate the area and assess the spill
  • Absorb and remove gross contamination
  • Clean, then disinfect for the full label contact time
  • Bag, label and dispose of waste
  • Restore the area to service with a short report

How it is priced

Often priced per call-out with a minimum charge, plus hourly labor and disposal. Cost depends on response time, spill size, surface type and after-hours service.

Ask before you hire

  • How fast can you respond during operating hours and after hours?
  • Which disinfectant do you use and what is its contact time?
  • How do you decide whether waste is regulated medical waste?
  • Can you train our staff for small incidents?

Questions about blood and bodily fluid cleanup

Can our employees clean up small blood spills?

Yes, if they are trained, have protective equipment and are covered by your exposure control plan under OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens Standard. Larger incidents often go to a contractor.

Is bleach acceptable?

Diluted bleach is commonly used, but any disinfectant should be EPA-registered for the use and applied at the label's concentration and contact time. Bleach can damage some surfaces.

Are all blood-stained items medical waste?

Not always. Rules generally focus on items saturated or dripping with blood. State definitions differ.
