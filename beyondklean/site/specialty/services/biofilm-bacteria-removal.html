Bacteria and biofilm removal in facilities

Deep cleaning of drains, equipment and surfaces where bacterial biofilms harbor pathogens such as Listeria in food and healthcare facilities.

Also called: biofilm remediation, Listeria harborage cleaning, drain biofilm removal

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Biofilms are layers of bacteria protected by a slimy matrix that resists routine sanitizers. They form in drains, conveyors, hollow equipment parts, cooling units and water lines, and can harbor pathogens such as Listeria. Removal uses mechanical scrubbing, foaming detergents and enzyme or chemical cleaners to break the matrix, followed by EPA-registered sanitizers or disinfectants applied per label. Food plants may require products cleared for food-contact surfaces. Environmental swabbing before and after confirms results. Contractors often work during shutdowns and alongside the facility's sanitation and food safety teams.

When you need it

  • Positive Listeria or pathogen swabs in a food plant
  • Persistent odors or slime in drains and equipment
  • Facility audit or customer finding on sanitation
  • Healthcare water or drain systems tied to infection concerns

Credentials to ask for

  • Experience with FDA FSMA preventive controls rules (21 CFR Part 117) and sanitation programs in food facilities
  • Use of EPA-registered sanitizers and disinfectants, applied per label under FIFRA
  • OSHA hazard communication 29 CFR 1910.1200 and lockout/tagout 1910.147 training for equipment work
  • Food safety training such as preventive controls coursework is helpful

What the scope includes

  • Review swab data and identify harborage sites
  • Lock out and disassemble equipment where needed
  • Mechanical and chemical cleaning to remove biofilm
  • Sanitize or disinfect per label
  • Post-cleaning swabbing and report

How it is priced

Usually priced per shift, per crew hour or per area during planned shutdowns. Cost depends on equipment disassembly, access, chemical program and verification testing.

Ask before you hire

  • How do you remove biofilm, not just sanitize the surface?
  • Are your chemicals approved for our food-contact surfaces?
  • How will you verify results?
  • Can you work within our sanitation and lockout procedures?

Questions about bacteria and biofilm removal in facilities

Why don't normal sanitizers remove biofilm?

Biofilm's protective layer keeps sanitizers from reaching the bacteria. Mechanical action and detergents must break it down first.

Where do biofilms usually form?

In wet, hard-to-clean spots: drains, hollow rollers, seals, gaskets and cooling units.

How do we know biofilm is gone?

Environmental swabbing, including ATP tests and pathogen sampling, gives evidence. Trends over time matter more than one result.
