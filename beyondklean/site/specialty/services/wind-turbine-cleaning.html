Wind turbine tower and nacelle cleaning

Cleaning of wind turbine towers, nacelles and blades to remove oil leaks, grime and contamination, using rope access or platforms.

Also called: wind turbine cleaning, nacelle cleaning, turbine tower washing

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Wind turbines leak gear and hydraulic oil that coats nacelle interiors and runs down towers and blades. Dirt and bugs on blades also affect performance. Cleaning crews use rope access, platforms or cranes to reach towers and blades, and clean nacelle interiors with absorbents and degreasers. Turbines are stopped and locked out before work. Rope access technicians commonly hold SPRAT or IRATA certification, and wind sites often require Global Wind Organisation (GWO) Basic Safety Training. Oily waste is collected for disposal.

When you need it

  • Oil leaks visible on towers or blades
  • Before inspection or repair work
  • Blade contamination reducing output
  • End of warranty inspections

Credentials to ask for

  • GWO Basic Safety Training including Working at Heights
  • SPRAT or IRATA rope access certification
  • Lockout/tagout and turbine safety procedures
  • Fall protection per OSHA and owner rules

What the scope includes

  • Turbine shutdown and lockout
  • Access setup with ropes or platforms
  • Clean nacelle interior and remove oil
  • Clean tower and blades as specified
  • Collect waste
  • Report leaks and damage

How it is priced

Typically priced per turbine or by crew-day. Turbine height, oil contamination, access method and weather delays drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Do your technicians hold GWO and rope access certification?
  • How do you handle weather delays?
  • Will you report leak sources?
  • How will oily waste be removed from the site?

Questions about wind turbine tower and nacelle cleaning

Why clean wind turbines?

Oil and dirt hide leaks, create fire and slip hazards, and can reduce blade performance. Cleaning also prepares surfaces for inspection.

What access methods are used?

Rope access is common for towers and blades because it needs little equipment and sets up quickly. Suspended platforms and crane baskets are used for longer blade work or where the owner prefers them.

Can cleaning happen in any weather?

No. Wind and lightning limits apply. Contractors stop work when conditions exceed safe limits.
