Hydro excavation

Non-destructive digging with pressurized water and vacuum to expose buried utilities, clear trenches and dig around plant piping.

Also called: hydrovac excavation, vacuum excavation, daylighting utilities

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Hydro excavation loosens soil with pressurized water and removes the slurry with a truck-mounted vacuum. It is used to expose underground utilities, dig potholes for verification, clean out trenches and work near plant piping where a backhoe could cause a strike. Before digging, the work still requires a call to 811 and locates under state one-call law. Pressure and wand tip are matched to soil and pipe type. Slurry is hauled to an approved dump site. Excavations deeper than five feet must meet OSHA's excavation standard in 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P.

When you need it

  • Digging near buried gas, power or fiber lines
  • Daylighting utilities to confirm depth and location before design or construction
  • Excavating in congested plant areas during turnarounds
  • Cleaning out valve boxes, trenches and sumps

Credentials to ask for

  • Compliance with state one-call (811) laws and locate procedures
  • Excavation competent person per OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P
  • Commercial driver's licenses for truck operators (FMCSA rules)
  • Site PSM and owner safety training where applicable

What the scope includes

  • Locate request and review of drawings
  • Setup of truck and barricades
  • Excavation by water and vacuum to the agreed depth
  • Protection or support of exposed utilities
  • Backfill if included
  • Transport and disposal of slurry

How it is priced

Usually priced by the hour or day per truck, plus dump fees. Soil type, distance to dump sites, depth and the number of holes drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Do you include the 811 locate request or should we file it?
  • Where will you dispose of slurry, and is it included?
  • Do you heat water for frozen ground?
  • Do you provide backfill and compaction?

Questions about hydro excavation

Is hydro excavation safer than mechanical digging?

It greatly reduces the risk of damaging a buried line because water and vacuum cut soil rather than force a bucket through it. It does not replace locating.

Can hydro excavation work in winter?

Many trucks heat their water, which allows digging in frozen ground. Cold weather slows the work and can raise cost.

Where does the excavated soil go?

It is hauled as slurry to an approved disposal or dewatering site. If the soil may be contaminated, it needs testing first.
