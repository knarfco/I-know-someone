Machine pit and trench cleaning

Removal of sludge, chips, oil and debris from machine pits, floor trenches and drainage channels, including confined space entry when required.

Also called: floor trench cleaning, process pit cleaning, chip trench cleaning

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Plants have pits under presses, lathes and conveyors, and trenches that carry chips, coolant and wash water to a central system. Over time they fill with sludge, oil and debris. The contractor removes grating, vacuums material with a portable or truck-mounted vacuum, scrapes walls, washes the pit and reinstalls covers. Deep pits often meet OSHA's permit-required confined space definition because of oil vapors, low oxygen or engulfment risk. In that case the crew uses atmospheric testing, an attendant and a rescue plan under 29 CFR 1910.146. Waste is sent for recycling or disposal.

When you need it

  • Trenches overflow or drain slowly
  • Oily sludge in pits creates odor or fire risk
  • Before installing new equipment over an old pit
  • During annual shutdown housekeeping

Credentials to ask for

  • Permit-required confined space entry training and written program per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146
  • Calibrated multi-gas meters and documented rescue arrangements
  • Lockout of conveyors and pumps per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147
  • Waste transport credentials and disposal facility approvals

What the scope includes

  • Survey pits and trenches and classify any confined spaces
  • Remove grating and set up barricades
  • Vacuum sludge, chips and liquids
  • Scrape and wash walls and floors
  • Reinstall grating and remove barricades
  • Dispose of waste and provide manifests or bills of lading

How it is priced

Priced per pit, per linear foot of trench or by crew-day. Depth, sludge volume, confined space requirements and waste disposal drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Which of our pits do you consider permit-required confined spaces?
  • Who provides the rescue service for entries?
  • What vacuum equipment will you bring?
  • Where will the waste go?

Questions about machine pit and trench cleaning

How do we know if a pit is a permit-required confined space?

OSHA's definition looks at whether a space is large enough to enter, has limited entry or exit, and is not designed for continuous occupancy. If it also has a hazardous atmosphere, engulfment risk or other serious hazards, it is permit-required.

Can pits be cleaned without entry?

Shallow trenches and many pits can be vacuumed from above using extension tools. Entry is needed when walls must be scraped or debris is out of reach.

How often should trenches be cleaned?

It depends on chip load and process. Many plants clean main trenches yearly and high-volume areas more often.
