Heat exchanger hydroblasting

High-pressure water jetting of heat exchanger tubes and shells to remove scale, coke and fouling during shutdowns.

Also called: tube bundle cleaning, hydrojetting, exchanger waterblasting

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Hydroblasting uses high-pressure water, often from 10,000 to 40,000 psi, to remove fouling from exchanger tubes and shell sides. Bundles are cleaned in place or pulled and moved to a wash pad. Automated lances and rotating tools are preferred for tube interiors because they keep operators farther from the jet. Shell-side cleaning uses lances or robotic units between tubes. Recovered water is collected and treated or disposed of. The WJTA-IMCA Industry Best Practices for the Use of High Pressure Waterjetting Equipment is the common reference for safe practice.

When you need it

  • Exchanger pressure drop is high or heat transfer has fallen
  • During turnarounds when bundles are pulled for inspection
  • Before eddy current or other tube testing
  • After a process upset that fouled the unit

Credentials to ask for

  • Operator training aligned with WJTA-IMCA Industry Best Practices for high pressure waterjetting
  • Use of automated or mechanized lancing where practical
  • Site PSM contractor training per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119 at covered sites
  • Hearing and eye protection programs and documented equipment inspections

What the scope includes

  • Receive bundle or set up at exchanger in place
  • Barricade the blast zone
  • Clean tube interiors with automated lances
  • Clean shell side and tube sheets
  • Inspect tubes for blockage and report plugged tubes
  • Collect water and debris for disposal

How it is priced

Priced per bundle, per tube or by unit-hour. Bundle size, fouling hardness, tube count and access drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Will you use automated lancing for tube interiors?
  • What pressures and tools do you plan to use for our fouling?
  • How will you report tubes you could not clear?
  • How will blast water be collected and handled?

Questions about heat exchanger hydroblasting

Why is automated lancing preferred?

Hand-held lances put the operator close to a very high-pressure jet. Automated equipment keeps the operator back and reduces the chance of injection injuries.

Can hydroblasting damage exchanger tubes?

Excessive pressure or holding a jet in one spot can erode thin tubes. Experienced operators choose pressure, flow and tools to match the fouling and tube material.

What if hydroblasting does not remove the deposit?

Hard scale or coke may need chemical cleaning first, or mechanical tools such as drills. The contractor should report problem tubes so the plant can decide.
