Food plant drain cleaning and sanitation

Scheduled cleaning and sanitizing of floor and trench drains, which are common harborage points in wet food plants.

Also called: floor drain sanitation, trench drain cleaning, drain Listeria control

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Drains in food plants collect product, water and organic matter and are a known reservoir for Listeria and other organisms. Drain sanitation removes baskets and covers, scrubs drain bodies and trenches with dedicated color-coded brushes, applies detergent and an approved sanitizer or foaming drain product, and rinses without spraying contaminated water onto equipment. Drain lines may be jetted to clear buildup, done at times when nearby product and equipment are protected. Tools used in drains stay in drains. The contractor should report cracked drains, poor floor slope and standing water, since those cause harborage.

When you need it

  • Routine drain cleaning on the master sanitation schedule
  • Positive swabs in or near drains
  • Slow or backed-up drains with odor
  • After floor or drain construction

Credentials to ask for

  • Training on drain hygiene and tool segregation
  • Use of EPA-registered sanitizers per label
  • Jetting equipment experience if lines are cleared
  • GMP training for working in production areas

What the scope includes

  • Protection of nearby equipment and product
  • Removal and cleaning of baskets, covers and grates
  • Scrubbing, detergent and sanitizer application
  • Line jetting when included and scheduled away from production
  • Report of damaged drains and standing water

How it is priced

Usually priced per drain or per visit on a recurring schedule. Drain count, trench length, line jetting and access hours drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How do you keep drain tools separate from equipment tools?
  • When will you jet drain lines, and how will you protect equipment?
  • What products do you use, and are they compatible with our drain materials?
  • Will you report drain repairs needed?

Questions about food plant drain cleaning and sanitation

Why focus on drains?

Drains are wet, nutrient-rich and hard to clean, which makes them a common Listeria site in food plants.

Can drains be cleaned during production?

Generally no. Cleaning can create splash and aerosols, so it is scheduled when production is stopped.

Is jetting the same as cleaning?

Jetting clears lines. The drain body, basket and cover still need scrubbing and sanitizing.
