Tank barge and hopper barge cleaning

Stripping, washing and gas-freeing tank barges, and washing out dry cargo hopper barges, for cargo changes, repairs and inspections.

Also called: barge cleaning, barge stripping, barge gas-freeing, hopper barge washout

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Barge cleaning prepares inland and coastal barges for a new cargo, a shipyard visit or a Coast Guard inspection. Tank barges are stripped of heel, washed with hot water or steam, ventilated and tested so a Marine Chemist can certify them. Hopper barges that carry grain, coal or aggregates are swept and washed out. Barge cleaning facilities generate wastewater covered by EPA's Transportation Equipment Cleaning effluent guidelines in 40 CFR Part 442 when they discharge it. Residues and slops must go to permitted handlers.

When you need it

  • Changing from one liquid cargo grade to another
  • Before shipyard repairs or hot work
  • Before a Coast Guard inspection or internal survey
  • Hopper barges with caked cargo residue or contamination

Credentials to ask for

  • Marine Chemist certificate under NFPA 306 for tank barges before hot work
  • Confined space training under 29 CFR Part 1915 Subpart B for tank entry
  • Wastewater discharge permit or pretreatment approval, including 40 CFR Part 442 Subpart C where it applies
  • HAZWOPER training where hazardous residues are handled

What the scope includes

  • Review prior cargoes and safety data sheets
  • Strip heel and transfer slops to licensed receivers
  • Wash, steam or ventilate tanks, or wash out hoppers
  • Test atmosphere and obtain certificates as needed
  • Hand-clean remaining residue after safe entry
  • Issue cleaning certificate and waste records

How it is priced

Usually priced per barge, with surcharges for heavy heel, difficult cargoes or extra certificate visits. Cost depends on barge size, cargo type and waste volume.

Ask before you hire

  • What prior cargoes can you clean, and which are excluded?
  • Do you hold the discharge permits for your wash water?
  • Who provides the Marine Chemist certificate?
  • What does your cleaning certificate state?

Questions about tank barge and hopper barge cleaning

Do hopper barges need a Marine Chemist?

Usually not, since dry cargo holds normally do not contain flammable vapors. A competent person may still need to test before hot work, under 29 CFR 1915.14(b).

What happens to wash water?

Barge cleaning facilities treat it on site and discharge under a permit, or send it to a treatment plant. EPA effluent guidelines for transportation equipment cleaning apply to many of these discharges.

How long does a barge clean take?

Simple hopper washouts may take hours. Tank barges with heavy residue and gas-freeing can take days.
