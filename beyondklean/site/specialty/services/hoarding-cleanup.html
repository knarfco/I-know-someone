Hoarding and severe clutter cleanup

Sorting, removal and sanitizing of severely cluttered or unsanitary units, often with animal waste, pests and spoiled food.

Also called: hoarder house cleanup, gross filth cleanup, extreme cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Hoarding cleanup removes large volumes of accumulated items, trash and spoiled food from homes or units, then cleans and sanitizes what remains. Many jobs involve animal or human waste, pests, mold and blocked exits. A contractor assesses hazards first, including structural damage, sharps and ammonia from animal urine. Crews sort items with the resident or owner when possible, set aside valuables and documents, and remove the rest for disposal or donation. After removal, surfaces are cleaned and disinfected, and odor control is applied. Landlords and code enforcement often have deadlines that shape the schedule.

When you need it

  • Code enforcement or health department order on a property
  • Landlord turnover of a unit after eviction or death
  • Family member needs help after a crisis or move to care
  • Animal hoarding case with heavy waste and odor

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens 29 CFR 1910.1030 training where human waste or sharps are present
  • Respiratory protection program under 29 CFR 1910.134 for ammonia, mold or dust
  • Training in working respectfully with residents; some firms follow the Institute for Challenging Disorganization clutter-hoarding scale
  • Licensed haulers and disposal sites for the waste

What the scope includes

  • Initial assessment of hazards, volume and goals
  • Sorting with the resident or owner and protection of valuables
  • Removal, hauling and disposal or donation
  • Cleaning, disinfection and odor control
  • Referral for pest control, mold or repairs as needed

How it is priced

Usually priced per project or per day with crew size, plus dumpsters or hauling by load. Cost depends on volume, how much sorting is required, hazards present and access.

Ask before you hire

  • How do you work with a resident who wants to keep items?
  • How will you protect important documents and valuables?
  • Can you handle animal waste and pest issues?
  • Can you meet a code enforcement deadline?

Questions about hoarding and severe clutter cleanup

Is hoarding cleanup a biohazard job?

Often, yes. Animal and human waste, sharps and rotting food can expose workers to pathogens, so trained crews use protective equipment.

Can everything just be thrown out?

Not without the owner's or legal representative's permission. A careful contractor sorts for valuables, documents and items of meaning.

Should a therapist be involved?

For an occupied home, many professionals recommend mental health support. Cleanup alone often does not prevent the problem from returning.
