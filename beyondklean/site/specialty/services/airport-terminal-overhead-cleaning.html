Overhead Cleaning · Specialty

Airport terminal overhead cleaning

Night-shift cleaning of terminal ceilings, skylights, signage, baggage areas and structure in secured airport environments.

Also called: terminal high cleaning, airport ceiling cleaning, concourse overhead cleaning

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Airport terminals have tall ceilings, glass walls, skylights, wayfinding signs, flight information displays, art installations and baggage system structure that all collect dust. Work is usually done overnight in narrow windows when concourses are quiet. Because many areas are inside the secured area, workers must hold airport-issued identification and pass the fingerprint-based background checks required under TSA rules (49 CFR Part 1542). Lifts must be escorted or approved for use on terminal floors and moving walkways. Debris must be kept away from passengers and baggage systems.

When you need it

  • Visible dust on skylights, signs and ceiling features
  • Before terminal openings, renovations or major events
  • Baggage system overhead structure dropping debris
  • As part of a recurring terminal maintenance contract

Credentials to ask for

  • Airport-issued badges with security threat assessment and fingerprint-based criminal history checks for secured areas, as required by the airport operator under 49 CFR Part 1542
  • MEWP operator training per ANSI/SAIA A92.24
  • Experience with airport tool control and escort rules
  • Insurance that meets the airport authority's requirements

What the scope includes

  • Badging and security coordination before work begins
  • Night-shift planning by concourse and zone
  • Overhead vacuuming and wipe-down of signs, ledges and structure
  • Interior skylight and high glass cleaning
  • Tool and equipment accountability at end of shift

How it is priced

Usually priced per shift or per zone, often through a multi-year contract. Badging time, overnight windows, ceiling height and escort needs drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Are your workers already badged at our airport?
  • How do you track tools in secured areas?
  • How many square feet can you complete per night?

Questions about airport terminal overhead cleaning

Why does badging matter?

Workers in secured areas must hold airport identification and pass background checks. Badging can take weeks, so ask early.

Can overhead cleaning happen during flights?

Most work happens overnight in closed zones. Some landside areas may allow limited daytime work.

Are flight displays cleaned too?

Often, but screens need specific methods and power coordination. Confirm in the scope.
