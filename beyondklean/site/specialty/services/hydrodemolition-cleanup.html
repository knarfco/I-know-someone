Hydrodemolition cleanup and slurry management

Vacuuming, settling and treating high-pH water and concrete debris from hydrodemolition on bridges, garages and dams.

Also called: hydrodemolition water treatment, hydrodemo cleanup, bridge deck hydrodemolition cleanup

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Hydrodemolition uses very high-pressure water to remove deteriorated concrete while leaving rebar intact. It makes large volumes of water mixed with concrete fines, debris and high pH. Cleanup crews vacuum slurry and rubble, pump water to settling tanks, and adjust pH before discharge or hauling. Debris is removed for recycling or disposal. Discharge must meet project permits, and parking garages and bridges need drains protected. Crews coordinate with the hydrodemolition operator so cleanup keeps pace with removal.

When you need it

  • Bridge deck rehabilitation
  • Parking garage repair
  • Dam and water structure repair
  • Industrial floor or tank repair

Credentials to ask for

  • Water treatment equipment for settling and pH adjustment
  • Discharge permits or approvals for treated water
  • Training under WJTA-IMCA recommended practices for work near high-pressure waterjets
  • Traffic control certification for roadway work where required

What the scope includes

  • Protect drains and set containment
  • Vacuum slurry and debris
  • Settle and adjust pH
  • Discharge or haul water
  • Remove debris and clean surfaces

How it is priced

Usually priced per day or per square foot of concrete removed, with water treatment as a separate item. Cost depends on water volume, discharge options and debris volume.

Ask before you hire

  • How will you treat and discharge water?
  • What pH and solids limits will you meet?
  • How will cleanup keep pace with the demolition crew?

Questions about hydrodemolition cleanup and slurry management

Why is hydrodemolition water high in pH?

Concrete contains calcium hydroxide that dissolves in water. That raises pH to levels harmful to waterways.

Can water be reused?

Some systems treat and recycle water back to the hydrodemolition unit. It depends on equipment, water quality needs and project specs.

What happens to debris?

Concrete rubble is often recycled as aggregate. Debris mixed with coatings or contaminants may need testing first.
