Food plant overhead cleaning (sanitation-safe)

Overhead cleaning of structure, piping, cable trays and fixtures above food production lines, using methods compatible with food safety plans.

Also called: food processing high cleaning, overhead sanitation cleaning, food plant structure cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Dust, condensation, flaking paint and residue overhead can fall into exposed food. FDA's Current Good Manufacturing Practice rules (21 CFR Part 117) require plants to keep fixtures, ducts and pipes from contaminating food and food-contact surfaces. Overhead cleaning must fit the plant's sanitation and allergen programs. Crews use HEPA vacuums, food-safe cleaners and color-coded tools, cover lines and food-contact surfaces, and follow the plant's GMPs for hair, jewelry and hygiene. Work is scheduled during sanitation shifts or shutdowns. Afterward, the plant's sanitation team cleans and inspects food-contact surfaces before production resumes.

When you need it

  • Audit findings on overhead condensation, dust or peeling paint
  • Before third-party food safety audits
  • Planned shutdowns and sanitation windows
  • Product changeovers involving allergens

Credentials to ask for

  • Training in the plant's GMPs and food safety plan under 21 CFR Part 117
  • Use of cleaners approved for use in food plants, with safety data sheets
  • MEWP operator training and lift cleanliness procedures
  • Experience with third-party audit schemes your plant uses

What the scope includes

  • Pre-job meeting with sanitation and quality teams
  • Covering of lines and food-contact surfaces
  • HEPA vacuuming and wiping of structure, piping, trays and fixtures
  • Lift and tool cleaning before entering production areas
  • Handoff to plant sanitation for final cleaning and pre-op inspection

How it is priced

Usually priced per shift or per zone during shutdowns. Ceiling height, equipment density, shutdown windows and hygiene requirements drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How do you keep lifts and tools from bringing contamination into production?
  • What cleaners will you use, and are they approved for our plant?
  • Will your crew follow our GMP and allergen rules?

Questions about food plant overhead cleaning (sanitation-safe)

Why is overhead cleaning a food safety issue?

Material from overhead can fall into food. Auditors often look for dust, condensation and flaking paint above lines.

Can overhead cleaning use water?

Sometimes, but water overhead can drip onto lines and spread contamination. Dry methods with HEPA vacuums are often preferred.

Who signs off before production resumes?

The plant's quality or sanitation team, after their own pre-op inspection.
