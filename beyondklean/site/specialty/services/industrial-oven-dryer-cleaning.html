Industrial oven and dryer cleaning

Cleaning of curing ovens, bake ovens and industrial dryers to remove condensed residue, char and dust that cause fires and quality defects.

Also called: curing oven cleaning, bake oven cleaning, industrial dryer cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Industrial ovens and dryers collect condensed resins, oils, char and product fines on walls, conveyors, burner housings, recirculation fans and exhaust ducts. These deposits can ignite and can flake onto product. A contractor cleans the oven after it has cooled and the fuel and electrical systems are locked out. Methods include scraping, vacuuming, dry ice blasting and chemical cleaning suited to the insulation and metal. NFPA 86 covers oven and furnace safety, including inspection and maintenance, so the plant usually involves its combustion or controls staff for restart checks.

When you need it

  • Flaking residue is causing defects on painted or baked product
  • Exhaust ducts are coated with condensate or grease
  • After an oven fire or a near miss
  • As part of annual safety inspection under NFPA 86

Credentials to ask for

  • Awareness of NFPA 86 (Standard for Ovens and Furnaces)
  • Lockout of burners, fuel trains and fans per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147
  • Confined space training (OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146) for walk-in ovens and large ducts
  • Heat stress awareness and suitable PPE

What the scope includes

  • Confirm cool-down, lockout and gas isolation
  • Remove loose debris and fines by vacuum
  • Scrape or blast condensed residue from walls, ceilings and conveyors
  • Clean recirculation fans, plenums and accessible exhaust ducts
  • Collect and dispose of residue
  • Report damage, such as cracked panels or failing insulation

How it is priced

Usually priced per oven or by crew-day. Oven length, residue type, duct access and confined space needs drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Will you clean the exhaust ducts and fans, or only the oven chamber?
  • How will you protect the insulation and burner components?
  • Do your crews hold confined space training for walk-in ovens?
  • Who handles restart and combustion safety checks?

Questions about industrial oven and dryer cleaning

Why do oven deposits create a fire risk?

Condensed resins and oils are fuel. At oven temperatures, thick deposits can smolder or ignite and spread into ducts, which are harder to reach and control.

Can a contractor clean the burner?

Burner and fuel train service is usually done by combustion technicians, not cleaning crews. The cleaning contractor typically works around those parts and leaves restart to qualified staff.

How long does an oven need to cool before cleaning?

Large ovens can take many hours to cool to safe temperatures. Plan the shutdown so the oven is cool when the crew arrives.
