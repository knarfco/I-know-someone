Vessel bilge cleaning

Pumping oily water and sludge from vessel bilges, degreasing the bilge structure, and sending waste to a licensed receiver.

Also called: bilge pumping, engine room bilge cleaning, oily bilge water removal

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Bilge cleaning removes the oily water, sludge, debris and grease that collect in the lowest spaces of a vessel, mainly under engine rooms. Crews pump liquids to a vacuum truck or tank, then degrease frames, plating and piping with low-pressure washing and absorbents. Oily water must go to a licensed receiver, not overboard. Bilges also count as spaces where OSHA shipyard rules call for a competent person to test before hot work. Clean bilges make leaks easier to find and support inspections.

When you need it

  • Before an engine room overhaul or hot work below the floor plates
  • Bilge oil levels are building faster than the oily water separator can handle
  • Before a Coast Guard, class or buyer inspection
  • After a fuel, lube oil or hydraulic leak

Credentials to ask for

  • Confined and enclosed space training under 29 CFR Part 1915 Subpart B
  • A Shipyard Competent Person to test bilges before hot work, as described in 29 CFR 1915.14(b)
  • Licensed used-oil or oily-waste transporter; requirements vary by state
  • Pollution liability insurance

What the scope includes

  • Pump oily water and sludge to a vacuum truck or holding tank
  • Remove debris and spent absorbents
  • Degrease structure and piping with approved cleaners
  • Rinse and recover all wash water
  • Provide waste receipts and a photo record

How it is priced

Typically priced per hour or per day plus disposal by the gallon or ton. Cost depends on bilge size and access, how much oil and sludge is present, and the receiver's rates.

Ask before you hire

  • Who will receive the oily water, and will we get a receipt for the oil record book?
  • Which cleaners do you use, and will they upset our oily water separator?
  • Will you test the space before work, and who signs off?

Questions about vessel bilge cleaning

Can bilge water just go through the separator?

Routine bilge water may go through an approved oily water separator under the vessel's procedures. Heavy sludge, debris and wash water from a deep clean usually must go ashore to a licensed receiver.

Do cleaners affect the separator?

Some detergents form stable emulsions that a separator cannot break. Ask for products that are compatible with your separator, or plan to send wash water ashore.

How long does a bilge clean take?

Small vessels may take a day. Large engine rooms with heavy sludge can take several days, especially when floor plates must be lifted.
