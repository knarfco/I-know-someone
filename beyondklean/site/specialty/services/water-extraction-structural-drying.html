Water extraction and structural drying

Removing water and drying buildings to documented goals with dehumidifiers and air movers under IICRC S500 practices.

Also called: water damage mitigation, water removal, structural drying, commercial dehumidification

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Water mitigation begins by stopping the source and extracting standing water with truck-mounted or portable extractors and pumps. Technicians map wet areas with moisture meters and thermal cameras, remove materials that cannot be dried, and set dehumidifiers and air movers. Wall cavities and floors may need injection or mat drying. Readings are logged daily until materials reach the drying goal. ANSI/IICRC S500 describes water categories, drying classes and documentation. Fast drying limits mold growth and structural damage.

When you need it

  • A pipe, sprinkler or water heater failure
  • Roof or window leaks during heavy rain
  • Rising groundwater or minor surface flooding
  • Fire suppression water left after a fire

Credentials to ask for

  • IICRC Water Damage Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) certifications
  • Work methods consistent with ANSI/IICRC S500
  • Calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging
  • Large-loss capacity, including generators, for commercial jobs

What the scope includes

  • Stop the source and classify water category
  • Extract standing water
  • Map moisture and remove non-dryable materials
  • Place and adjust drying equipment
  • Log moisture readings daily
  • Confirm drying goals and remove equipment

How it is priced

Commonly billed per equipment unit per day plus labor, extraction and materials removed. Cost depends on affected area, water category, building materials and drying days.

Ask before you hire

  • What category and class did you assign, and why?
  • How will you show materials are dry before rebuilding?
  • What will you remove, and what will you try to save?

Questions about water extraction and structural drying

How soon should drying start?

As soon as possible. Mold can begin to grow on wet materials within a few days.

Can wet drywall be saved?

Drywall wet with clean water and dried quickly can often stay. Drywall soaked with contaminated water is usually removed.

Why keep a moisture log?

The log shows the insurer and the rebuild contractor that materials reached a dry standard before being covered. It also helps defend against later mold claims.
