Industrial sludge removal and dewatering

Removal and dewatering of industrial sludge from tanks, basins, ponds and lagoons, with transport and disposal at approved facilities.

Also called: sludge cleanout, lagoon cleaning, pond sludge removal

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Sludge accumulates in process tanks, settling basins, equalization tanks, ponds and lagoons. Removal methods include vacuum trucks, submersible pumps, dredges and excavators, depending on volume and access. To reduce disposal weight, sludge is often dewatered on site with centrifuges, filter presses or geotextile tubes, and the water is returned to treatment. Sampling and waste characterization decide whether sludge is hazardous under RCRA or can go to a landfill or beneficial use. Hauling follows DOT rules, and some states require permits for land application or disposal.

When you need it

  • Basin or tank capacity has dropped from sludge build-up
  • Treatment performance is falling
  • Pond or lagoon closure
  • Change of process or permit requirements

Credentials to ask for

  • Waste characterization and RCRA knowledge (40 CFR Parts 261-262)
  • DOT hazardous materials and waste transporter credentials as needed
  • HAZWOPER training per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.120 for hazardous sites
  • Confined space training per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146 for tank entries
  • State permits for disposal or land application; requirements vary by state

What the scope includes

  • Sampling and waste characterization
  • Selection of removal and dewatering method
  • Mobilization of pumps, dredge or vacuum equipment
  • Dewatering and return of filtrate
  • Loading, transport and disposal
  • Disposal records and volume report

How it is priced

Often priced per dry ton or wet ton removed, or per cubic yard, with mobilization as a separate item. Sludge volume, solids content, disposal classification and haul distance drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Will you dewater on site, and what solids content will you reach?
  • Where will the sludge go?
  • How will you measure the volume removed for billing?
  • Who handles sampling and waste profiles?

Questions about industrial sludge removal and dewatering

Why dewater sludge?

Disposal is often charged by weight, and much of sludge's weight is water. Dewatering can cut hauling and disposal costs significantly.

Is industrial sludge hazardous waste?

Some is and some is not. It depends on the process and test results, such as the toxicity characteristic leaching procedure.

Can the plant keep running during removal?

Often yes, if the basin can be bypassed or the work is done in sections. The contractor and operations should agree on a plan.
