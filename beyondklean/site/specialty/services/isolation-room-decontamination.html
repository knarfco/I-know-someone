Isolation room decontamination

Terminal cleaning of patient rooms after discharge from isolation precautions, using disinfectants matched to the organism.

Also called: isolation room terminal clean, contact precautions room cleaning, AIIR cleaning, C. diff room cleaning

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Isolation room decontamination is the terminal cleaning of a patient room after a patient on contact, droplet or airborne precautions is discharged or moved. Crews wear the PPE the precaution type requires, remove linens and waste, clean all surfaces and equipment, replace privacy curtains, and disinfect using a product with claims for the organism. For Clostridioides difficile that means a product on EPA List K; for Candida auris, List P. Airborne infection isolation rooms must sit unoccupied long enough for air changes to clear the room before entry without respiratory protection. Many hospitals add UV or hydrogen peroxide vapor after manual cleaning.

When you need it

  • Discharge or transfer of a patient on isolation precautions
  • Outbreaks of C. difficile, MRSA, Candida auris or other organisms
  • Infection prevention request for enhanced cleaning
  • Surge capacity during respiratory disease outbreaks

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens training (29 CFR 1910.1030)
  • Respiratory protection program with fit testing (29 CFR 1910.134) for airborne precautions
  • Use of EPA-registered disinfectants from the correct EPA list per label
  • Training on CDC isolation precautions and the hospital's policies

What the scope includes

  • Check precaution type and required wait time before entry
  • PPE donning and removal of waste and linens
  • Cleaning and disinfection of all surfaces and equipment
  • Curtain change and restocking
  • Optional no-touch disinfection and verification audit

How it is priced

Usually priced per room clean or as part of a monthly environmental services contract. Room count, organism, PPE level, curtain changes and add-on no-touch disinfection drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Which disinfectants will you use for C. difficile and Candida auris?
  • How do your staff know the air-clearance wait time for airborne rooms?
  • How do you train and fit test staff for respirators?
  • How is the cleaning verified?

Questions about isolation room decontamination

Why do some organisms need special disinfectants?

Spores and some fungi resist common disinfectants. EPA lists products tested and labeled for those organisms.

How long should an airborne isolation room sit empty?

It depends on the room's air changes per hour. CDC guidance gives clearance times based on air changes, and the hospital sets its policy.

Is a no-touch system enough on its own?

No. Manual cleaning must come first to remove soil. UV or vapor systems are an added step.
