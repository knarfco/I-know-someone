24/7 emergency spill response (HAZWOPER)

Around-the-clock response to uncontrolled releases of hazardous materials by HAZWOPER-trained teams under an incident command system.

Also called: hazmat emergency response, emergency response contractor, on-call spill response

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Emergency spill response deals with releases that are still active or uncontrolled, such as a ruptured tank, a highway hazmat spill or a leaking railcar. Responders arrive on call, often within a contracted response time, and work under the incident command system with fire departments and agencies. They identify the material, set hot, warm and cold zones, stop or contain the release, and decontaminate people and equipment. Once the scene is stable, the job usually moves into cleanup and disposal. Many facilities, carriers and municipalities hold response contracts or retainers so a team is available without delay.

When you need it

  • Transportation accident releasing fuel or chemicals
  • Uncontrolled leak at a plant, terminal or warehouse
  • Fire department requests private resources for containment and cleanup
  • Release that threatens drains, waterways or nearby people

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA HAZWOPER emergency response training under 29 CFR 1910.120(q), at technician, specialist and incident commander levels
  • Respiratory protection program and fit testing, including supplied-air equipment where needed
  • DOT hazmat training and registration for transport of recovered material
  • State spill contractor approvals where they exist; requirements vary by state
  • Pollution liability and auto insurance suited to emergency work

What the scope includes

  • Respond on call and coordinate with incident command
  • Identify hazards and set control zones
  • Stop, contain and recover the release
  • Decontaminate responders and equipment
  • Transition to cleanup, waste handling and disposal
  • Incident report with timeline, quantities and disposal records

How it is priced

Usually billed at published emergency time-and-materials rates for crews, equipment and supplies, with minimum call-out charges. Retainer or contract agreements can set rates and response times in advance.

Ask before you hire

  • What response time can you commit to for our sites?
  • How many technician-level responders do you have available at night?
  • Can you sign a response agreement before an incident?
  • Do you carry equipment for both land and water spills?

Questions about 24/7 emergency spill response (hazwoper)

What is the difference between HAZWOPER levels?

OSHA's emergency response training runs from awareness and operations levels to technician, specialist and incident commander. Each level has defined duties and training hours.

Why sign a response contract before an incident?

A pre-arranged agreement sets rates, contacts and response times. It avoids searching for a contractor during an emergency.

Who pays for emergency response on a highway spill?

Usually the carrier or shipper responsible for the release, often through insurance. Agencies may bill responsible parties for their costs.
