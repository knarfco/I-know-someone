Laboratory decontamination and decommissioning

Decontamination of laboratory spaces and equipment before renovation, relocation, sale or lease end, with documented clearance.

Also called: lab decommissioning, lab closeout cleaning, laboratory decon

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Laboratory decontamination removes chemical, biological and sometimes radiological contamination from benches, hoods, sinks, floors and equipment so a lab can be renovated, reassigned or released. The process starts with a hazard review of past use, chemical inventories and permits. Crews remove remaining chemicals and waste, clean and decontaminate surfaces with methods suited to each hazard, and may sample surfaces for clearance. ANSI/ASSP Z9.11 covers laboratory decommissioning. Areas that used radioactive material need survey and release under the institution's NRC or Agreement State license, coordinated with the radiation safety officer. A closeout report documents the condition left behind.

When you need it

  • Lab relocation, renovation or demolition
  • End of a lease or sale of a lab building
  • Equipment transfer, sale or disposal
  • Spill or contamination event

Credentials to ask for

  • OSHA HAZWOPER training (29 CFR 1910.120) for hazardous material handling
  • Bloodborne pathogens training where biological agents were used
  • Familiarity with ANSI/ASSP Z9.11 laboratory decommissioning
  • RCRA and DOT capability for chemical waste
  • Radiation safety coordination under NRC or Agreement State license where applicable

What the scope includes

  • Review of lab history, inventories and hazards
  • Removal of chemicals, biological materials and waste
  • Decontamination of hoods, benches, sinks and equipment
  • Clearance sampling or surveys as required
  • Closeout report and equipment decontamination tags

How it is priced

Usually priced as a lump sum per lab or suite after a site review. Hazards present, waste volume, sampling, radiological surveys and equipment count drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • How will you assess what was used in the lab before starting?
  • How will you dispose of chemical, biological and radioactive waste?
  • What clearance sampling or surveys will you do?
  • What will the closeout report include?

Questions about laboratory decontamination and decommissioning

Why is lab decommissioning more than cleaning?

Labs may hold residues that are toxic, infectious or radioactive. Each needs its own handling, disposal and clearance.

Who signs off on radioactive areas?

The licensee's radiation safety program, under its NRC or Agreement State license. The contractor supports the survey and cleanup.

Can equipment be sold after decontamination?

Often, yes, with a decontamination certificate. Buyers and movers usually require one.
