Energized insulator washing (live-line)

Specialist washing of contaminated insulators on energized lines and substations to prevent flashover, done only by qualified live-line crews.

Also called: live-line insulator washing, hot washing, insulator cleaning

Licensed, regulated work. Only properly licensed and certified specialists may perform it. Requirements vary by state and agency, so confirm credentials before hiring.

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Salt, industrial dust and agricultural residues on insulators can cause leakage current and flashover, especially in fog or light rain. Live-line washing sprays high-resistivity deionized water at energized insulators using nozzles, pressures and distances designed to prevent a conductive path back to the operator. Dry methods using abrasives or dry ice are also used. This is highly specialized work done only by trained crews following utility procedures. IEEE 957 (Guide for Monitoring, Managing, and Cleaning of Contaminated Insulators) provides industry guidance, and OSHA 1910.269 governs the work.

When you need it

  • Flashovers or tracking on insulators in contaminated areas
  • Coastal salt or industrial pollution build-up
  • Before seasons with fog and light rain
  • When outages for de-energized cleaning are not possible

Credentials to ask for

  • Qualified employees for energized work under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.269
  • Procedures consistent with IEEE 957 guidance
  • Water resistivity testing equipment and records
  • Utility-approved live-line work procedures and insulated equipment

What the scope includes

  • Review of contamination and line voltages
  • Water resistivity test before washing
  • Setup with minimum approach distances
  • Washing in the sequence set by procedure
  • Inspection for damaged insulators
  • Report of work and readings

How it is priced

Usually priced by crew-day or per structure or substation. Voltage, access, number of insulators and contamination drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • What is your live-line washing experience at our voltage?
  • How do you test and record water resistivity?
  • What procedures do you follow, and are they approved by our utility?
  • Do you offer dry cleaning methods where washing is not suitable?

Questions about energized insulator washing (live-line)

Why must the water be high resistivity?

Pure water conducts poorly. High-resistivity water keeps leakage current through the stream very low, which protects the crew.

Can any pressure washing company do this?

No. Live-line washing requires qualified electrical workers, special equipment and utility procedures. It is not general pressure washing.

Are there alternatives to washing?

Yes. Options include de-energized cleaning during an outage, dry ice or crushed corncob blasting, and room-temperature-vulcanized silicone coatings. Some utilities replace porcelain insulators with polymer types in heavy contamination zones.
