Rodent dropping and animal waste cleanup

Wet-method removal of rodent droppings, nests and bird or bat guano with precautions for hantavirus, histoplasmosis and other diseases.

Also called: hantavirus cleanup, bird droppings removal, guano cleanup

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Animal waste cleanup removes rodent droppings, urine, nests and bird or bat guano from attics, crawlspaces, warehouses and mechanical rooms. Dry droppings can carry hantavirus, histoplasmosis spores and other pathogens, so CDC guidance warns against sweeping or vacuuming them dry. Instead, technicians ventilate the space, wet the material with a disinfectant, let it soak, and remove it with disposable materials. Heavy accumulations call for respirators and full protective clothing. Contaminated insulation is often removed. Pest control to stop new activity should come first, and exclusion work keeps animals from returning.

When you need it

  • Rodent infestation in a warehouse, cabin, barn or vacant building
  • Pigeon or bat roost in an attic, bell tower or structure
  • Before maintenance crews enter a contaminated crawlspace
  • Food facility found rodent activity during an inspection

Credentials to ask for

  • Respiratory protection program under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.134 for heavy contamination
  • Training on CDC hantavirus cleanup guidance and NIOSH histoplasmosis precautions
  • Use of EPA-registered disinfectants per label
  • State pest control license if the firm also traps or treats; requirements vary by state

What the scope includes

  • Confirm pest control and exclusion are in place
  • Ventilate the space before cleanup
  • Wet waste with disinfectant and allow contact time
  • Remove droppings, nests and contaminated insulation
  • Disinfect surfaces and bag waste for disposal
  • Recommend sealing or exclusion repairs

How it is priced

Usually priced per square foot or per project, driven by contamination depth, insulation removal, access, protective equipment and disposal.

Ask before you hire

  • How will you avoid making droppings airborne?
  • What respiratory protection will your crew wear?
  • Will you remove and replace contaminated insulation?
  • Do you coordinate with pest control and exclusion work?

Questions about rodent dropping and animal waste cleanup

Why can't droppings be vacuumed?

Sweeping or regular vacuuming can put virus particles or fungal spores into the air. CDC guidance calls for wetting with disinfectant first, then wiping up.

Is bird guano dangerous?

Accumulated bird and bat droppings can support the fungus that causes histoplasmosis. Disturbing large amounts without protection can cause infection.

Should cleanup happen before or after pest control?

After. Cleaning before the animals are gone means the area becomes contaminated again.
