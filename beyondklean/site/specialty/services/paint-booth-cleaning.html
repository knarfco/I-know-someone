Paint booth and spray booth cleaning

Removal of overspray from spray booth walls, floors, grates, exhaust plenums and ductwork, plus filter changes and booth coating.

Also called: spray booth cleaning, finishing booth cleaning, paint line cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Paint booth cleaning removes built-up overspray from walls, ceilings, floors, light fixtures, grates and the exhaust plenum and ducts. Overspray deposits are combustible, so NFPA 33 sets housekeeping expectations, and many fire marshals and insurers check booth condition. Crews scrape and strip deposits, change intake and exhaust filters, clean water-wash sections if present and often apply a strippable booth coating to make the next clean easier. Light fixtures and electrical items in the booth are rated for the location and must be handled carefully. Waste paint and solvent-soaked material may be hazardous waste.

When you need it

  • Overspray is visibly built up on walls, grates or fan blades
  • Airflow has dropped or finish quality is suffering from dirt
  • An insurer or fire inspector cited booth housekeeping
  • Changing to a new coating type or color system

Credentials to ask for

  • Familiarity with NFPA 33 (Spray Application Using Flammable or Combustible Materials)
  • Hazardous waste handling knowledge under RCRA (40 CFR Parts 260-273) for paint and solvent waste
  • Respiratory protection program per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.134
  • Lockout of booth fans and conveyors per OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147

What the scope includes

  • Lock out exhaust fans, conveyors and spray equipment
  • Remove and bag used filters
  • Scrape and strip overspray from walls, ceilings, grates and floors
  • Clean exhaust plenum, fan housing and accessible ductwork
  • Install new filters and apply strippable coating if specified
  • Package waste for characterization and disposal

How it is priced

Typically priced per booth or by crew-day, with filters and coating as materials. Booth size, depth of overspray, duct length and waste disposal requirements drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Will you clean the exhaust duct and fan, or only the booth interior?
  • Who characterizes and disposes of the paint waste?
  • Do you supply the filters and booth coating?
  • What respirators and PPE will your crew wear?

Questions about paint booth and spray booth cleaning

How often should a paint booth be cleaned?

It depends on production volume and coating type. NFPA 33 calls for regular cleaning so residue does not build up, and many shops schedule deep cleans quarterly or semiannually with filter changes in between.

Is paint booth waste hazardous?

It can be, depending on the paint and solvent. Waste should be characterized under RCRA, and many facilities treat solvent-based residue and soaked filters as hazardous waste until shown otherwise.

What is a strippable booth coating?

It is a peelable film applied to booth walls and floors. Overspray lands on the coating, and at the next clean the film is peeled off with the overspray, saving scraping time.
