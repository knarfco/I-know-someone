Air handling unit and coil cleaning

Deep cleaning of air handler interiors, coils, drain pans and blower assemblies to restore airflow, heat transfer and hygiene.

Also called: AHU cleaning, evaporator coil cleaning, cooling coil cleaning, condenser coil cleaning

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Air handling unit cleaning targets the components where dirt does the most harm: coils, drain pans, blower wheels, plenums and internal insulation. Coils are cleaned with low-pressure water or steam and a coil cleaner, working from the leaving-air side so debris is pushed out rather than deeper into the fins. Fins can be combed straight where bent. Drain pans are scrubbed and drains cleared to stop standing water. The contractor locks out the unit, protects motors and controls, and collects runoff. Static pressure or temperature readings before and after show whether heat transfer and airflow improved.

When you need it

  • Rising pressure drop across coils or falling cooling capacity
  • Microbial growth or odor in drain pans or on coils
  • Clogged condensate drains or water carryover into ductwork
  • As part of a duct cleaning project, since ACR includes the air handler
  • Annual preventive maintenance in hospitals, labs or schools

Credentials to ask for

  • ACR, the NADCA Standard, for air handler hygiene work
  • HVAC technician experience with the unit type; state mechanical license where required
  • Lockout/tagout training under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147
  • Use of coil cleaners according to their labels

What the scope includes

  • Lockout of the unit and inspection with photos
  • Vacuuming of plenums, fan section and accessible insulation
  • Coil cleaning with low-pressure water or steam and an appropriate cleaner
  • Drain pan scrubbing and condensate drain flushing
  • Blower wheel and housing cleaning
  • Before-and-after pressure drop or airflow readings

How it is priced

Usually priced per unit or per coil, with a lump sum for large central systems. Unit size, coil depth, fouling level, access and whether the coil must be removed drive cost.

Ask before you hire

  • Will you measure pressure drop or airflow before and after cleaning?
  • How will you protect motors, electrical components and insulation from water?
  • What cleaner will you use, and is it safe for the coil metal?
  • Can you clean deep coils fully, or only the face?

Questions about air handling unit and coil cleaning

Why not just pressure wash the coil?

High pressure bends fins and drives debris deeper into the coil. Controlled low-pressure methods work from the leaving-air side to push dirt out.

Is a coil cleaning worth it for energy savings?

A fouled coil raises fan energy and lowers heat transfer. Before-and-after readings let you see the gain on your own equipment.

Do I need to clean ducts at the same time?

Not always. If inspection shows ducts are clean, cleaning only the air handler may be enough.
