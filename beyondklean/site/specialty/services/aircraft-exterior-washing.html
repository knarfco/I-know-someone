Aircraft exterior washing

Wet or dry washing of aircraft exteriors with maker-approved products to remove exhaust soot, grime and corrosive deposits.

Also called: airplane washing, aircraft wet wash, dry wash aircraft, aircraft brightwork polishing

Get quotes See specialists

What it is

Aircraft exterior washing removes exhaust residue, insects, dirt and salt that add drag and promote corrosion. Crews follow the aircraft maintenance manual for approved cleaners, dwell times and areas to mask, such as pitot tubes, static ports and sensors. Wet washing uses water and approved detergent at a wash rack. Dry washing uses spray-on, wipe-off products where water is limited. Landing gear and wheel wells need extra care to avoid washing grease from bearings. Work is done from lifts or stands with fall protection and soft pads to avoid scratching paint.

When you need it

  • Routine wash intervals in the operator's maintenance program
  • After operating near salt water, volcanic ash or de-icing fluids
  • Before inspections, painting or aircraft delivery
  • Heavy exhaust staining on the fuselage and engines

Credentials to ask for

  • Use of cleaners approved in the aircraft maker's manual, such as products tested to SAE AMS 1526 for water-based exterior cleaners
  • Training on aircraft masking, fall protection and ground handling
  • Airport badging or escorts for work on the ramp
  • Aviation liability insurance

What the scope includes

  • Mask sensors, pitot tubes, static ports and vents
  • Apply approved cleaner and agitate with soft tools
  • Rinse or wipe per the method used
  • Clean landing gear areas without stripping lubricant
  • Remove masking and inspect for damage
  • Record products used for the aircraft logbook if requested

How it is priced

Usually priced per aircraft by type and size, or per linear foot of length. Cost depends on wet or dry method, soil level, brightwork polishing and access equipment.

Ask before you hire

  • Which cleaning products do you use, and are they approved for our aircraft?
  • How do you protect sensors and ports during washing?
  • Can you wash on our ramp, or do we need a wash rack?
  • What insurance do you carry for aircraft damage?

Questions about aircraft exterior washing

Wet wash or dry wash?

Wet washing removes heavy soil best but needs a wash rack and water capture. Dry washing works on the ramp with little runoff and suits lighter soil.

Can washing harm an aircraft?

Yes. Wrong chemicals can attack aluminum, composites or sealants, and water forced into ports can affect instruments. That is why maker approvals matter.

How often should an aircraft be washed?

The operator's program sets intervals based on environment and use. Aircraft near salt water often need more frequent washing.
