Questions about demolition & reconstruction

Who cleans up after a demolition contractor finishes?

The demolition contractor normally removes bulk material, and the GC hires a cleanup crew for what remains: small debris, dust on slabs and structure, and the edges of retained areas. Our crews handle that scope so rebuild trades start on a clean shell.

How is demolition dust different from regular construction dust?

It is heavier, spread wider and often contains silica from concrete, masonry and plaster. It settles on structure and ledges well above the floor, so it has to be captured with HEPA vacuums and wet methods from the top down rather than swept.

Can you clean a demolition area while the rest of the building is occupied?

Yes. Our crews work inside the contractor's barriers and negative air, schedule dusty steps around occupants when needed and clean the boundary with occupied areas before barriers are opened, so dust stays in the work zone.

Do you handle asbestos or lead removal during demolition?

No. Abatement is done by licensed abatement contractors. After an area passes clearance and is released, post-abatement cleaning is assigned only to licensed, certified technicians where required and follows the abatement plan and owner protocol.

What does reconstruction cleanup after a fire or flood include?

Clearing debris, sediment and residue from structure once drying is confirmed, cleaning ahead of each rebuild phase, documenting areas with photos for the owner and insurer, and a full final clean before people move back in.

Is demolition debris recycled?

Where local haulers accept it, yes. Our crews separate metal, clean wood, cardboard and concrete as directed by the GC's waste plan, which usually lowers disposal costs and supports waste-diversion goals on green building projects.

How is post-demolition cleanup priced?

Most interior work is priced per square foot, adjusted for debris left behind, ceiling height, dust type and protection needs. Heavy debris clearing after storms is often priced by crew-hour or by load, and hauling may be billed separately.

How soon after demolition should cleanup be scheduled?

As soon as the demolition crew releases an area and before layout, framing or new mechanical work begins. Prompt cleanup keeps dust out of new materials and ductwork and avoids paying to clean around new work later.
