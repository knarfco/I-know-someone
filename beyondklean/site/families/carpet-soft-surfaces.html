Carpet & Soft Surfaces

New carpet is the softest target on a jobsite. It traps drywall dust, catches paint and mud, and shows every stain at turnover. This family covers construction vacuuming, extraction, spotting and upholstery, using low-moisture methods that dry fast and do not leave residue that resoils.

Questions about carpet & soft surfaces

Does new carpet need to be cleaned before move-in?

Yes. Even when carpet is installed late, it collects drywall dust, footprints and debris during punch and furniture installation. A thorough HEPA vacuum is the minimum; extraction is common.

Can drywall mud be removed from carpet?

Usually, if it is handled properly. Let it dry, break it up and vacuum it, then treat the remaining residue. Wiping wet mud spreads it into the fibers.

How long does carpet take to dry after extraction?

Usually a few hours with low-moisture methods and good airflow. Over-wetting can cause odors, delamination and wicking stains.

What causes stains to reappear after carpet cleaning?

Wicking. Soil or residue deep in the backing rises back to the fiber tips as the carpet dries. Proper extraction and rinsing reduce it.

Should carpet tile be cleaned differently than broadloom?

Similar methods apply, but carpet tile can be lifted and replaced, so badly damaged tiles are often swapped rather than cleaned.

Do construction cleaners clean office furniture?

Many do vacuuming and wipe-down after furniture installation. Upholstery extraction is usually a separate line item.

What vacuum should be used on new carpet?

A commercial HEPA-filtered upright or backpack vacuum. Ordinary filters pass fine drywall dust back into the air.

Is carpet protection film safe to leave on?

Only for a short time. Adhesive film left too long, especially in heat or sun, can leave residue and damage pile.
