Exterior, Hardscape & Building Envelope

The exterior is the first thing an owner, tenant or inspector sees. Mud on sidewalks, mortar on brick and debris in landscape beds make a finished building look unfinished. This family covers flatwork, facades, masonry, parking areas and site cleanup, with wash water kept out of storm drains.

Exterior surfaces

Why it matters. Curb appeal, accessibility routes and storm-water rules all apply at closeout; exterior mud and debris are the first things an owner sees.

Our method. Water reclamation, biodegradable detergents where verified, and storm-drain protection keep wash water out of drains.

Questions about exterior & hardscape

Can wash water from pressure washing go into the storm drain?

Generally no. Wash water often carries soil, concrete fines, oils and detergents that storm water rules prohibit. Crews use recovery equipment or block and vacuum the drains.

How do you clean mortar off new brick?

With a masonry cleaner suited to the brick type, pre-wetting the wall and rinsing thoroughly. The wrong acid can stain light-colored brick or burn mortar joints.

When is the exterior cleaned on a construction project?

After landscaping, striping and exterior trades finish, and before the certificate of occupancy inspection and owner walk.

What is soft washing?

Cleaning facades and roofs with low pressure and cleaning solutions instead of high pressure, which can damage stucco, EIFS, paint and sealants.

Is parking lot sweeping part of a construction cleanup?

Often yes, especially before striping and before the final walk. Power sweepers handle large lots quickly.

Can graffiti be removed from new construction?

Usually, with removers matched to the surface. Fresh graffiti is much easier to remove, and anti-graffiti coatings can protect high-risk walls.

Who cleans the gutters at the end of a project?

The cleaning contractor or roofer. Gutters and roof drains collect construction debris that can cause backups in the first heavy rain.

Should solar panels be cleaned after construction?

If construction dust coated them, yes, but only by crews following the panel manufacturer's guidance and electrical safety rules.
