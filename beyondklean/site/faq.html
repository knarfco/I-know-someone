About Beyond Klean

What is Beyond Klean?

Beyond Klean is a specialty cleaning procurement platform for construction and commercial buildings. We help builders and owners define the cleaning scope and connect them with independent, verified contractors who quote and perform the work.

Does Beyond Klean do the cleaning itself?

No. Independent, verified contractors perform the work. Beyond Klean handles scoping, matching, coordination and documentation, and the contractor that quotes the job performs, insures and warrants it.

How is Beyond Klean different from a lead-selling directory?

We do not sell your request to a list of companies. Each request is scoped and qualified, then matched to contractors verified for that specific work, with capacity confirmed before an introduction.

What areas does Beyond Klean serve?

The service library is national. Contractor coverage is being built market by market, starting in Florida, and we tell you plainly whether a verified contractor covers your location.

What kinds of buildings does Beyond Klean cover?

Commercial, retail, grocery, restaurant, warehouse, industrial, office, healthcare, education, hospitality and multifamily projects, from tenant improvements to ground-up construction.

How many cleaning services does Beyond Klean list?

438 distinct services across 20 service families, searchable under nearly a thousand names that contractors, specs and buyers use for them.

Is there a cost to request a quote?

No. Requesting a scope review and quote is free for project owners and general contractors.

What is the relationship with Above Eye Level Cleaning?

Above Eye Level Cleaning is our partner program for recurring overhead and high-dusting work. Beyond Klean covers construction turnover, and AELC keeps overhead surfaces clean after the building opens.

For general contractors

How do I get a cleaning sub for my project?

Request a quote or send your bid package. We review the scope, phases, schedule and site details, then introduce a verified contractor who prices the work directly with you.

Can you price from my drawings and specs?

Yes. Send drawings, the Division 01 cleaning section and any finish schedules. The matched contractor prices the scope; we help make sure nothing is missing, such as overhead cleaning or glass.

Can one request cover rough, final and touch-up cleaning?

Yes. Most construction cleaning is bid by phase. One request can cover every phase, with each priced separately.

What if I need a cleaning crew tomorrow?

Tell us the deadline. We only promise a response after a contractor confirms capacity, and we will tell you plainly if we cannot cover it in time.

Do your contractors carry insurance?

Contractors must provide current insurance certificates before they are routed work, and expired documents pause routing automatically. Project-specific requirements such as additional insured status are confirmed by the contractor.

Who do I pay?

You contract with and pay the cleaning contractor that performs the work, under the terms you agree with them.

Can you help with multi-site rollouts?

Yes. National and regional programs can use one scope and checklist across many sites, with local verified contractors where coverage exists.

What documentation do I get at closeout?

Contractors can provide photo documentation, room checklists and product data sheets for the cleaners used, which help with closeout packages and LEED or IAQ requirements.

For owners and facility managers

When should cleaning be planned on a construction project?

Early. Overhead cleaning, glass, floors and kitchen equipment are often missed in scopes. Writing them in during bidding prevents change orders at the end.

Why is my new building still dusty after the final clean?

Usually because overhead surfaces were not in scope, or dust was moved instead of captured. HEPA capture and overhead cleaning fix the cause, not just the symptom.

Can Beyond Klean help after we open?

Yes. Recurring overhead and high dusting goes through Above Eye Level Cleaning, and floor, glass and specialty services can be arranged as needed.

Is construction cleaning the same as janitorial service?

No. Construction cleaning removes construction residue from new finishes: dust, labels, adhesive, grout haze and debris. Janitorial service maintains an occupied building.

How do I know the cleaning was done right?

Ask for room checklists and before-and-after photos, and walk the space with the punch list. We track complaints so performance is visible.

Can cleaning be scheduled around our operations?

Yes. Remodels in operating stores, offices and healthcare facilities are commonly cleaned overnight or in phases.

What should I check at the owner walk?

Look up at ceilings and fixtures, open drawers, check glass in daylight, look behind toilets and under sinks, and check floor edges and corners.

Who is responsible if something is damaged during cleaning?

The contractor performing the work is responsible under its contract and insurance. That is one reason we verify insurance before routing.

Green and safer cleaning

What makes Beyond Klean's approach greener?

Methods first: capture dust with HEPA instead of blowing it around, use microfiber and water before chemicals, pick pH-neutral and low-VOC products matched to the surface, and recover wash water instead of sending it to storm drains.

Do you use non-toxic cleaning products?

We avoid blanket claims like non-toxic. We describe the specific methods and products used and provide their data sheets, because every product has handling requirements.

What is capture-first cleaning?

Removing dust and soil from the building, with HEPA vacuums and microfiber, instead of moving it into the air or onto other surfaces.

Why does least-aggressive chemistry matter on new buildings?

New finishes are at their most vulnerable. Harsh acids and solvents can etch stone, dull coatings, damage grout and void warranties.

Are green cleaning products less effective?

Not when they are matched to the job. Many construction residues come off with mechanical methods and neutral products; stronger chemistry is reserved for when it is truly needed.

Do you follow green building requirements?

Contractors can follow project IAQ plans, document products and methods, and support LEED documentation for cleaning-related credits.

What certifications matter for green cleaning?

EPA Safer Choice certifies products, and Green Seal GS-42 certifies cleaning service providers. Project specs may name others.

How is wash water handled?

Exterior washing uses recovery equipment or drain protection so dirty water does not enter storm drains.

Pricing and scheduling

How is construction cleaning priced?

By square foot, by phase, by crew-day or by line item, depending on scope. Overhead work, glass and floor care are often priced separately.

What affects the price most?

Square footage, ceiling height and access, number of phases, finish types, the amount of residue, schedule pressure and night or weekend work.

Why do you not publish prices?

Prices depend on local labor, access and scope. We provide pricing factors, and the contractor prices after reviewing the actual project.

How far in advance should cleaning be booked?

As early as the bid phase. Final cleaning is scheduled around inspection dates, and crews book up near quarter-end and holiday openings.

Is night work more expensive?

Usually, because of labor premiums. It is often worth it for operating stores and occupied buildings.

Are re-cleans extra?

That depends on the contract. Clear re-clean terms in the scope avoid disputes at the end of the job.

How long does a final clean take?

From one day for a small tenant space to several weeks for large buildings cleaned in phases.

Can cleaning be split between phases of occupancy?

Yes. Each area can be final-cleaned and protected as it is handed over.

Site, Debris & Waste Management

Who is responsible for jobsite debris on a commercial project?

The general contractor is responsible overall, and most subcontracts make each trade clean up its own waste. In practice trades leave scraps behind, so GCs often hire a dedicated cleanup crew to sweep, haul and sort daily or weekly.

How often should a construction site be cleaned?

Busy interiors usually need a daily sweep of walking paths and a deeper weekly clean. Fast-track jobs with many trades on site sometimes need two passes a day near egress routes and stairwells.

What is the difference between debris removal and a rough clean?

Debris removal is ongoing: it clears waste as it is created. A rough clean is a defined phase after framing, drywall and rough-in that removes bulk debris and dust so finish trades can start.

Can construction waste be recycled?

Yes. Metal, clean wood, cardboard and gypsum drywall are commonly recycled where local haulers accept them. Separating streams at the source usually lowers haul costs and supports LEED waste-diversion credits.

Does Beyond Klean provide dumpsters?

No. The roll-off is usually arranged by the GC or the hauler. The cleanup contractors we match load, sort and manage the debris that goes into them.

What does OSHA require for jobsite housekeeping?

OSHA's construction rules require walking and working surfaces to be kept free of scrap, debris and protruding nails, and combustible waste to be removed regularly. Where silica dust is present, dry sweeping is restricted in favor of wet or HEPA methods.

Can a cleanup crew work while other trades are on site?

Yes, and most progress cleaning happens exactly that way. Crews coordinate with the superintendent's look-ahead schedule so they clear areas between trades without blocking work.

How is jobsite cleanup priced?

It is usually priced per visit, per crew-day or as a monthly allowance, based on square footage, number of floors, debris volume and whether hauling is included. Contractors confirm pricing after reviewing the site and schedule.

How often should debris be removed from each floor?

On active floors, usually daily or every other day. Slower areas may need only a weekly sweep. The superintendent's schedule drives it.

Rough Clean

When does the rough clean happen?

After framing, drywall hanging and taping, and mechanical, electrical and plumbing rough-in are complete, and before flooring, ceilings and finish carpentry start. On large buildings it moves floor by floor.

What is included in a rough clean?

Removing bulk debris, scraping drywall mud and overspray off slabs, vacuuming stud bays and openings, and clearing mechanical rooms and shafts. Fine detailing waits for the final clean.

Why not skip the rough clean and do one big final clean?

Dust and debris left at rough stage get trapped behind ceilings, inside ductwork and under new flooring. A rough clean protects finishes and makes the final clean faster and cheaper.

Is a rough clean the same as a construction clean?

It is one phase of it. Construction cleaning usually runs in three phases: rough clean, light or detail clean, and final clean, with touch-up before the owner walk.

How long does a rough clean take?

It depends on square footage, debris volume and access. A small tenant space may take a day; a multistory building is usually phased over several days as floors become ready.

Should the slab be scraped before flooring is installed?

Yes. Hardened joint compound, paint drips and overspray under resilient flooring or carpet create bumps and adhesion problems. Flooring installers expect a clean, scraped slab.

Who sets the rough clean schedule?

The general contractor's superintendent, based on when each area is released by the drywall and MEP trades. Good cleanup contractors work from the GC's three-week look-ahead.

Does a rough clean require HEPA equipment?

On most commercial jobs it should. Drywall and concrete dust are fine enough to pass through ordinary vacuums, and silica rules restrict dry sweeping where concrete dust is present.

What does a rough clean include?

Bulk debris removal, slab scraping, HEPA vacuuming of floors, sills and openings, and clearing mechanical rooms and shafts.

Fine Dust, Drywall Dust & Controlled-Dust Cleaning

Why does drywall dust keep coming back after cleaning?

Because it was moved, not removed. Dry dusting and sweeping lift fine particles into the air, where they settle again hours later. HEPA vacuuming and damp microfiber capture it so it stays gone.

Is drywall dust harmful?

Drywall dust can irritate eyes, skin and lungs, and some joint compounds contain crystalline silica. Concrete and masonry dust carry more silica. Capture methods and proper respiratory protection reduce exposure.

What is HEPA vacuuming?

Vacuuming with a sealed machine whose high-efficiency particulate air filter captures at least 99.97 percent of particles at 0.3 microns. It keeps fine construction dust from passing straight through the vacuum back into the room.

Can you use a leaf blower or compressed air to clean construction dust?

It is a poor practice indoors and restricted under OSHA's silica standard. Blowing dust spreads it into finished spaces and HVAC systems and increases worker exposure.

How is dust controlled during renovation of an occupied building?

With containment barriers, negative air machines, sticky mats at exits, HEPA vacuuming and frequent damp cleaning outside the work zone. Healthcare projects follow stricter infection-control protocols.

When should fine dust removal happen?

Last, after all dust-producing trades are finished in that area. Dusting before ceiling, electrical or millwork touch-ups finish usually means doing it twice.

Does fine dust removal include inside cabinets and drawers?

It should. Owners and inspectors open drawers and cabinet doors, and dust collects inside them during construction. Confirm it is in the scope.

What is silica-aware housekeeping?

Cleaning methods that keep crystalline silica dust out of the air: wet sweeping, HEPA vacuuming and approved sweeping compounds instead of dry sweeping, brushing or compressed air.

Why does drywall dust keep showing up after cleaning?

It was moved instead of captured. HEPA vacuuming and damp microfiber remove it; dry dusting puts it back in the air.

What is high dusting?

Cleaning surfaces that are out of normal reach, usually above eight to ten feet: open ceiling decks, beams, joists, ductwork, pipes, cable trays, light fixtures and the tops of racking and walls.

Why do new buildings need overhead cleaning?

Construction dust rises and settles on every horizontal surface overhead. In open-ceiling stores, warehouses and restaurants it is visible, falls onto finished surfaces and can fail food-safety and owner inspections.

How do crews clean ceilings 20 or 30 feet up?

With scissor lifts or boom lifts, extension poles and HEPA vacuum attachments that capture dust at the surface. Dust should be captured, not knocked down onto the floor.

Is overhead cleaning included in a normal final clean?

Often it is not. Many final-clean scopes stop at reachable surfaces. If the building has an open ceiling or exposed structure, overhead cleaning should be written into the bid as its own line item.

How often should high dusting be done after the building opens?

Retail and warehouse spaces typically schedule it quarterly or semiannually. Food facilities often tie it to audit schedules. Heavy-dust environments may need it more often.

Can high dusting be done while a store is open?

Yes, usually overnight or before opening, with merchandise covered or areas cordoned off. Lift work over shoppers is avoided.

Is high dusting safe for sprinkler heads?

Sprinkler heads need careful, light dusting only. Crews should never hang anything from them, paint them or use pressure on them. Damaged or painted heads must be replaced by a fire protection contractor.

Glass, Windows & Glazing

Why does new glass get scratched during construction cleaning?

Scratches come from dragging debris under a blade, from fabricating debris fused to tempered glass, and from using dull or dirty scrapers. Proper lubrication, fresh blades and the right technique on the right glass type reduce the risk.

Can razor blades be used on new windows?

On uncoated glass, a clean blade used flat with lubrication is a standard method. Some coated, tempered and low-E surfaces should not be scraped at all. The glass manufacturer's guidance controls.

What is a glass scratch waiver?

A contract clause, recommended by the window cleaning industry, that addresses liability for scratches that may result from removing construction debris from glass. Many construction window cleaners require one.

When should construction windows be cleaned?

After exterior finishes like stucco, painting and sealants are complete and the scaffolding is down, then touched up after punch work. Cleaning earlier usually means cleaning twice.

How do you remove concrete splatter from glass?

Carefully, starting with soaking and gentle mechanical removal. Acidic concrete removers can etch glass and damage metal frames, so they are a last resort and must be tested first.

Who cleans high-rise windows after construction?

Specialty contractors using rope access, swing stages or building maintenance units. It requires trained, insured crews and fall-protection plans, so it is routed only to qualified providers.

What is pure-water window cleaning?

Cleaning with deionized or reverse-osmosis water through a water-fed pole. With minerals removed, glass dries without spots, and little or no detergent is needed.

Should the stickers on new windows be removed?

Yes, at final clean, unless the inspector needs to see energy labels first. Ask the GC whether the code inspection requires labels in place.

When should interior windows be cleaned on a construction project?

Near the end, after painting and caulking are complete and before the owner walk.

What causes stains to reappear after carpet cleaning?

Wicking. Soil or residue deep in the backing rises back to the fiber tips as the carpet dries. Proper extraction and rinsing reduce it.

Mechanical, Electrical & Construction IAQ

Can a cleaning crew clean an electrical room?

Only non-invasive cleaning of floors, walls and enclosure exteriors, and only where the electrical contractor or facility allows it. Nothing inside energized equipment, and no liquids near live gear.

What is construction indoor air quality management?

A plan, often required on LEED projects, to keep dust and contaminants out of the HVAC system and building during construction: protecting ducts, filtering returns, housekeeping and scheduling.

Should HVAC filters be changed before occupancy?

Yes. Filters that ran during construction are loaded with dust. Green building standards call for new filtration media before occupancy.

What is a building flush-out?

Running the HVAC system with outdoor air for a set period after construction, before or during early occupancy, to dilute contaminants from new finishes. A thorough final clean should come first.

Is duct cleaning part of a construction cleanup?

Duct exteriors are. Duct interior cleaning is a specialty, often done to NADCA standards, and is only needed when ducts were contaminated during construction.

Why were the ducts capped during construction?

To keep construction dust out of them. If caps were missed or torn, dust gets in, and duct cleaning may be needed before turnover.

Who cleans the rooftop units?

The exterior of rooftop units and curbs can be cleaned by the cleaning contractor. Coils and internal components belong to the mechanical contractor.

Is post-construction cleaning in a hospital different?

Yes. Healthcare construction follows infection-control risk assessments, and final cleaning before patients return is protocol-based, often coordinated with the hospital's environmental services department.

What is cleanroom cleaning?

Cleaning to a defined particle standard using specific garments, low-lint wipes, approved cleaning agents and a set sequence. It is required before cleanroom certification.

Who cleans a data center after construction?

Specialty contractors trained for critical environments. They clean subfloors, raised floor tiles, cabinets and surfaces with antistatic methods and HEPA equipment.

Schools usually open on fixed dates, have many identical rooms and often require background-checked crews. Low-odor products matter because students arrive soon after.

What is a hotel PIP?

A property improvement plan, a brand-required renovation. PIP cleaning turns renovated rooms back to inventory quickly, often floor by floor.

Can any cleaning company clean a lab?

Not safely. Labs may have chemical, biological or equipment hazards. Cleaning is limited to what the lab's safety officer approves.

By checking training, protocols, references for that facility type, and insurance that covers the work. Regulated scopes are approved case by case.

How do you clean mortar off new brick?

With a masonry cleaner suited to the brick type, pre-wetting the wall and rinsing thoroughly. The wrong acid can stain light-colored brick or burn mortar joints.

When is the exterior cleaned on a construction project?

After landscaping, striping and exterior trades finish, and before the certificate of occupancy inspection and owner walk.

What is soft washing?

Cleaning facades and roofs with low pressure and cleaning solutions instead of high pressure, which can damage stucco, EIFS, paint and sealants.

Can graffiti be removed from new construction?

The cleaning contractor or roofer. Gutters and roof drains collect construction debris that can cause backups in the first heavy rain.

Residue, Stain & Protective-Material Removal

What does least-aggressive-first mean?

Start with the gentlest method that might work: dry removal, then water, then neutral cleaners, then targeted solvents, and test each one in a hidden spot. Stop at the first method that works.

How do you remove paint overspray from new surfaces?

It depends on the surface and paint type. Plastic blades and mild removers work on many hard surfaces; glass may take a blade; textured and porous surfaces need specialty products and testing.

What removes adhesive residue safely?

Citrus or soy-based removers and mineral oils work on many adhesives without harsh solvents. Some substrates, including certain plastics and finishes, react to solvents, so test first.

Why does protective film need to come off promptly?

Heat and UV make film adhesive bond harder over time. Film left on for months can leave residue or pull finish when removed.

Who removes floor protection at the end of a job?

Usually the cleaning contractor during final clean, unless the flooring contractor or GC handles it. The dust trapped under it must be captured, not swept around.

Is grout haze a cleaning or tile problem?

Both. The tile setter is expected to clean grout as it is installed, but light haze is common and is removed at final clean. Heavy haze goes back to the installer.

Can paint drips on concrete be removed?

Usually, by scraping and with removers suited to the paint and the floor finish. Polished concrete needs gentler methods than raw slab.

What is the most common residue on construction punch lists?

Labels and stickers, paint overspray, tape residue and caulk smears appear on nearly every punch list.

Turnover, Punch & Closeout Support

What is the difference between a final clean and a touch-up clean?

The final clean is a complete, detailed clean of the space before inspection. A touch-up clean is a quick pass right before the owner walk, or after punch work, to fix areas that got dirty again.

What does Substantial Completion have to do with cleaning?

Most construction specs require final cleaning to be complete before the GC requests the Substantial Completion inspection. A dirty building can fail that walk and delay the certificate and payment.

What is a sparkle clean?

An informal name for the last detailed touch-up before an owner walk or grand opening. It focuses on glass, fixtures, floors and anything visible.

How many phases does construction cleaning have?

Usually three: rough clean, light or detail clean, and final clean, followed by touch-ups. Not every project needs every phase.

Who pays for re-cleaning after punch work?

That depends on the contract. Many GCs carry a re-clean allowance, or back-charge the trade that made the mess. Cleaning contracts should define how re-cleans are priced.

What cleaning documentation do GCs need for closeout?

Often photo documentation, signed room checklists, product data sheets for cleaning chemicals and records supporting LEED or IAQ requirements.

What is attic stock?

Spare materials left for the owner, such as extra tiles, ceiling panels and paint. It should be cleaned, labeled and staged where the owner wants it.

Can a building be cleaned and turned over in phases?

Yes. Phased occupancy is common in large projects; each area is final-cleaned and protected when it is handed over.

Restoration & Regulated Work (Conditional)

Can a regular cleaning company remove mold?

Small, surface-level mold cleanup may be allowed in some places, but remediation of larger areas typically involves licensed or certified professionals, containment and clearance testing, depending on state rules.

Who handles asbestos found during construction?

Work must stop in the area, and a licensed asbestos abatement contractor handles removal under federal and state rules. Cleaning crews must not disturb suspect materials.

What happens if lead paint is disturbed during renovation?

Lead-safe work practices and cleanup are required under EPA and OSHA rules, and clearance testing may be needed. Certified renovators and abatement contractors handle this.

What is post-storm cleanup?

Removing debris, water, mud and damaged materials after hurricanes, floods or wind events, often followed by drying and mold prevention. Florida projects plan for it.

Can construction cleaners disinfect?

Only with EPA-registered disinfectants used according to the label, and only when the claim is accurate. Cleaning and disinfecting are different services.

How does Beyond Klean handle regulated scopes?

They are never routed automatically. Each request is reviewed, and only providers with the required licenses, training and insurance are introduced.

What is a biohazard on a jobsite?

Blood, bodily fluids, sharps or animal waste. Cleanup requires trained personnel, protective equipment and proper disposal.

Is fire and smoke damage cleaning a restoration job?

Yes. Smoke residue cleaning, odor control and contents cleaning are restoration specialties, often handled through insurance claims.
