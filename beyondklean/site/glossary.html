Glossary

Construction cleaning terms

The words that show up in specs, bid packages and punch lists, defined the way crews and superintendents use them.

71 terms shown

ACT
Acoustical ceiling tile. Lay-in ceiling panels supported by a metal grid.
AHU
Air handling unit. The equipment that moves and conditions air through ductwork.
Attic stock
Spare finish materials left for the owner for future repairs.
Auto-scrubber
A floor machine that applies cleaning solution, scrubs and vacuums it back up in one pass.
Back-charge
A cost the GC charges to a subcontractor for work, such as cleanup, that the sub was responsible for.
Bar joist
An open-web steel joist that supports roof or floor decks.
Burnishing
High-speed polishing of floor finish to restore gloss.
Cable tray
An open metal support system for electrical and data cables, usually overhead.
Certificate of occupancy
The document issued by the local building authority allowing a building or space to be occupied. Often abbreviated CO.
Containment
Temporary barriers, often plastic sheeting with zipper doors, that isolate a dusty work area.
Crystalline silica
A mineral in concrete, masonry, stone and some joint compounds. Its respirable dust is regulated by OSHA.
CSI MasterFormat
The standard numbering system for construction specifications. Cleaning is usually in Division 01.
Curtain wall
A non-structural exterior glass and metal wall system on larger buildings.
Disinfecting
Killing specified microorganisms on surfaces with an EPA-registered disinfectant used according to its label.
Efflorescence
White, powdery salt deposits left when water moves through concrete, masonry or grout and evaporates.
EIFS
Exterior insulation and finish system. A synthetic stucco cladding that can be damaged by high-pressure washing.
Extraction
Carpet cleaning that injects a cleaning solution and vacuums it out, often called hot water extraction.
Final clean
The complete, detailed cleaning of a space before the Substantial Completion inspection and owner turnover.
Floor finish
The polymer coating applied to resilient floors such as VCT. Often called wax.
Floor protection
Temporary board or film laid over finished floors during construction.
Flush-out
Running a building's HVAC with outdoor air after construction to dilute contaminants before or during early occupancy.
Food-contact surface
A surface that food normally touches, which must be cleaned and then sanitized.
FRP
Fiberglass reinforced plastic. Textured, washable wall panels used in kitchens, restrooms and food areas.
Glass scratch waiver
A contract clause addressing liability for scratches that can occur when construction debris is removed from glass.
Gondola
Freestanding retail shelving unit used in aisles.
Green Seal GS-42
A certification standard for commercial and institutional cleaning services covering procedures, purchasing and training.
Grout haze
A thin film of cement-based grout left on the face of tile after grouting.
HEPA
High-efficiency particulate air. A HEPA filter captures at least 99.97 percent of particles at 0.3 microns, so fine construction dust is held instead of exhausted.
High dusting
Cleaning surfaces out of normal reach, such as decks, beams, ducts, pipes and fixtures.
HVLS fan
High-volume, low-speed ceiling fan used in warehouses, gyms and large spaces.
IAQ
Indoor air quality. Construction IAQ management keeps dust and contaminants out of the HVAC system and occupied space.
IEQ
Indoor environmental quality. A broader measure of indoor air, lighting, acoustics and comfort used in green building standards.
LEED
Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design. A green building rating system that includes construction IAQ and waste diversion credits.
Light clean
The middle phase of construction cleaning, also called the detail clean. Cleans surfaces, fixtures and glass after most finishes are installed.
Low-E coating
A thin metallic coating on glass that controls heat transfer. Some coated surfaces cannot be scraped.
LVT
Luxury vinyl tile. A resilient floor with a factory wear layer, usually maintained without floor finish according to the manufacturer's guide.
Mastic
A thick adhesive used for flooring, tile and panels.
MEP
Mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems.
Microfiber
Synthetic cleaning cloth with very fine fibers that trap dust and soil mechanically, reducing the need for chemicals.
NADCA
National Air Duct Cleaners Association. Publishes standards for HVAC system cleaning.
Negative air machine
A HEPA-filtered fan unit that pulls air out of a contained work area so dust does not escape into occupied space.
NFPA 96
The fire code standard for ventilation control and fire protection of commercial cooking operations, including hood and duct cleaning.
Open ceiling
A design with no finished ceiling, leaving the deck, structure and MEP exposed.
Overspray
Paint, coating or texture that lands outside the intended surface.
pH-neutral cleaner
A cleaner with a pH near 7. Gentler on new finishes, stone, grout and floor coatings than acids or strong alkalis.
Progress cleaning
Ongoing cleaning during construction to keep the site safe and orderly. Often specified under section 01 74 13.
Punch list
The list of incomplete or defective items recorded during the pre-completion walk. Cleaning items such as smudges, labels and residue are common entries.
Pure-water cleaning
Window cleaning with deionized or reverse-osmosis water that dries spot-free without detergent.
Raised access floor
Removable floor panels over a plenum, common in data centers.
Rough clean
The first phase of construction cleaning, after framing, drywall and MEP rough-in. Removes bulk debris and heavy dust before finish trades start.
RTU
Rooftop unit. A packaged heating and cooling unit installed on the roof.
Safer Choice
An EPA program that certifies cleaning products meeting criteria for human health and environmental safety.
Sanitizing
Reducing microorganisms on a surface to safe levels with a registered sanitizer used at label concentration.
Scrub and recoat
Deep-scrubbing the top layers of floor finish and applying fresh coats without a full strip.
SDS
Safety Data Sheet. The manufacturer's document describing a chemical's hazards, handling and first aid.
Silica-aware housekeeping
Cleaning that avoids dry sweeping, dry brushing and compressed air where silica dust is present, using wet methods and HEPA vacuums instead.
Soft washing
Exterior cleaning with low pressure and cleaning solutions instead of high pressure.
Sparkle clean
An informal name for the last detailed touch-up before an owner walk or grand opening.
Static-dissipative floor
A floor designed to safely drain static electricity, used around electronics. Needs specific cleaners.
Storefront
Ground-level aluminum and glass framing system used for retail and building entries.
Strip and refinish
Removing old floor finish chemically and applying new coats.
Substantial Completion
The point when the work is complete enough for the owner to occupy and use it. Most specs require final cleaning before the inspection for it.
Sweeping compound
A granular material spread on floors before sweeping to hold dust down.
TDS
Technical Data Sheet. The manufacturer's document describing how a product performs and how to use it.
Tenant improvement
Interior build-out of a leased space for a specific tenant. Often abbreviated TI.
Touch-up clean
A quick pass after punch work or right before an owner walk to re-clean areas that got dirty again.
VCT
Vinyl composition tile. A resilient floor tile that needs initial preparation and floor finish after installation.
VOC
Volatile organic compound. Chemicals that evaporate from products and finishes and affect indoor air. Low-VOC cleaners release less.
Walk-off mat
Entrance matting that captures soil and moisture before it is tracked inside.
Wet sweeping
Sweeping with water or a damp compound to keep dust from becoming airborne.
Wicking
When soil or residue deep in carpet rises to the surface as it dries, making stains reappear.
