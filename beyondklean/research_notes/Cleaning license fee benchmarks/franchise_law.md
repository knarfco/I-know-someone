## FTC Franchise Rule (16 CFR Part 436): definitional elements and minimum-payment exemption

### Takeaway
A federal "franchise" has three elements: (1) a trademark, (2) significant control or assistance, and (3) a required payment. Since July 12, 2024, the minimum-payment exemption covers only deals where required payments to the franchisor or its affiliate total **less than $735** from any time before opening through the **first six months of operation**. A $368.71 weekly fee starting on day one passes that threshold in week 2, so the exemption is not available as the deal is structured.

### Cited Findings
- The FTC Rule's three components are a required payment, a license of a trademark, and significant assistance or control over the method of operation. All three must be present. — [Ontario Bar Assn., "The Danger of the Accidental Franchise"](https://oba.org/Sections/Franchise-Law/Articles/Articles-2018/January-2018/The-Danger-of-the-Accidental-Franchise); [Fox Rothschild, "What's an Accidental Franchise"](https://www.foxrothschild.com/publications/whats-an-accidental-franchise-and-could-i-be-one)
- The "required payment" element is read very broadly. It can capture any fee paid to the franchisor, including fees for advertising, assistance, training, equipment and supplies. — [Ontario Bar Assn.](https://oba.org/Sections/Franchise-Law/Articles/Articles-2018/January-2018/The-Danger-of-the-Accidental-Franchise)
- **Current threshold:** the FTC raised the minimum-payment exemption from **$615 to $735**, published at **89 FR 57078 (July 12, 2024)** and **effective July 12, 2024**. — [Federal Register / govinfo, FR Doc. 2024-14933](https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-07-12/pdf/2024-14933.pdf); [FTC announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/node/85862); [McDonald Hopkins](https://mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/ftc-publishes-new-monetary-thresholds-for-three-exemptions-to-the-franchise-rule); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/franchising/1493250/ftc-publishes-new-monetary-thresholds-for-three-exemptions-to-the-franchise-rule)
- **Threshold history:** the original threshold was $500. It was later $615, which practitioner posts cited before 2024. — [LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/?p=1730125); [FTC Informal Staff Advisory Opinion 99-1](https://ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/advisory-opinions/informal-staff-advisory-opinion-99-1)
- **How it is measured (16 CFR 436.8(a)(1)):** the test counts required payments, and commitments to make a required payment, to the franchisor or an affiliate. The window runs from any time before the franchisee's business starts operating until six months after it starts. Practitioners date the start as the day the franchisee first makes its goods or services available for sale. — [eCFR 436.8 (as summarized in search results)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-436/subpart-E/section-436.8); [McDonald Hopkins](https://mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/ftc-publishes-new-monetary-thresholds-for-three-exemptions-to-the-franchise-rule)
- **Adjustment cycle:** the FTC must adjust the thresholds every fourth year based on CPI-U. The next adjustment is expected around 2028. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/85862); [mychesco summary of FTC release](https://www.mychesco.com/?p=565146)
- **Expected payments count:** in a 1999 staff opinion, the FTC said that if a typical franchisee in the industry earns enough to owe at least the threshold in the first six months, it is reasonable to assume the parties expect the minimum payment to be met. — [FTC Informal Staff Advisory Opinion 99-1](https://ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/advisory-opinions/informal-staff-advisory-opinion-99-1)
- *Safe Step Walk In Tub v. CKH Industries* (S.D.N.Y. 2017): a "license" was held to be an accidental franchise. The court relied on minimum sales requirements, a marketing plan, training and reporting requirements, and a $5,000 initial fee. — [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/bathtub-manufacturers-licensing-arrangement-created-an-accidental-franchise-but-court-finds-limited-remedies)

### Inferences
- **Trademark element:** met. The brand name is licensed.
- **Required-payment element:** met. The weekly fee is a required payment, and the commission on brand-generated jobs very likely counts too. The total passes $735 by week 2.
- **Control/assistance element:** this is the only element still open. Website, marketing and lead support are "assistance." Whether it is "significant" depends on the facts. Brand standards, required uniforms or methods, territory rules, reporting, or training would push it toward "significant." A deal described as "national exclusive" with brand marketing support looks a lot like franchise-type assistance.
- **Conclusion:** as described, the deal has a substantial risk of being a franchise under the FTC Rule. It would then need an FDD delivered at least 14 calendar days before signing or payment. The 14-day timing comes from training knowledge and is not confirmed by a fetched source this session.

## Other FTC exemptions (fractional, large investment, large franchisee, etc.)

### Takeaway
None of the other exemptions realistically fits a small cleaning-business licensee. They require either a very large investment (at least $1,469,600) or net worth (at least $7,348,000), or a licensee with more than two years of experience in the same business for whom the new line is no more than 20% of sales.

### Cited Findings
- **Large franchise investment exemption:** rose from $1,233,000 to **$1,469,600**, effective July 12, 2024. The figure excludes unimproved land and any franchisor or affiliate financing. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/85862); [McDonald Hopkins](https://mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/ftc-publishes-new-monetary-thresholds-for-three-exemptions-to-the-franchise-rule)
- **Large franchisee (sophisticated entity) exemption:** net worth threshold rose from $6,165,000 to **$7,348,000**. The entity must also have been in business at least five years. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/85862); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/franchising/1493250/ftc-publishes-new-monetary-thresholds-for-three-exemptions-to-the-franchise-rule)
- **Fractional franchise exemption:** the franchisee or its principals must have more than two years of experience in the same or a similar business, and sales from the franchise must reasonably be expected to stay within 20% of total dollar volume in the first year. — [A Franchise Coach glossary](https://afranchisecoach.com/glossaries/fractional-franchise/); [Aaron Hall](https://aaronhall.com/fractional-franchise/)
- The 2007 amended Rule lets the experience of current directors or officers of a parent or affiliate count toward the fractional exemption, if it is in the same line of business. — [Pattishall (Fall 2007)](https://www.pattishall.com/pdf/Fall%202007.pdf)
- States may not recognize the federal exemptions and may have their own. — [LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/?p=1730125)

### Inferences
- **Fractional exemption:** this is the only one that could plausibly apply, and only if the licensee is an existing cleaning company with more than two years of experience, adding the brand as a minor sideline (20% or less of sales). It would not fit a startup licensee or a licensee whose whole business runs under the brand.
- **Other exemptions:** the rule also has exemptions for oral agreements, leased departments, fractional deals, and investments that need not be made within six months. These come from training knowledge (436.8(a)(2)-(7)), were not verified this session, and don't appear to fit.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve verbatim exemption text. The "oral agreement" and "leased department" exemptions are not source-verified here.

## FTC Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR Part 437)

### Takeaway
The Business Opportunity Rule has no dollar threshold, and its "business assistance" definition includes providing outlets, accounts or customers. Lead or customer provision could therefore pull the deal in. However, a franchise covered by Part 436 is generally excluded. Part 437 applies to a franchise only if it escapes Part 436 through the minimum-payment exemption or because no written document describes material terms. Either way, the deal falls under one of the two federal rules. Structuring under $735 to avoid the Franchise Rule can trigger the Business Opportunity Rule's one-page disclosure instead.

### Cited Findings
- Part 437 has three elements: (1) the seller solicits a prospect to start a new business, (2) the prospect makes a required payment, and (3) the seller represents that it or a designee will provide locations for equipment, provide outlets, accounts or customers, or buy back the purchaser's goods or services. — [Offit Kurman, "What's a Biz Op"](https://www.offitkurman.com/offit-kurman-blogs/whats-a-biz-op); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ftc-expands-business-opportunity-to-in-90468/)
- Unlike the Franchise Rule, the Business Opportunity Rule has **no minimum payment threshold**. — [Offit Kurman](https://www.offitkurman.com/offit-kurman-blogs/whats-a-biz-op)
- Reasonable amounts of inventory bought at bona fide wholesale prices are not "required payments." — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ftc-expands-business-opportunity-to-in-90468/)
- **16 CFR 437.8:** the rule applies to franchises exempt from Part 436 because required payments fall below the threshold (the 437.8 text still says $500) or because there is no written document describing material terms. — [GPO CFR 2014, sec. 437.8](https://www.gpo.gov/fdsys/pkg/CFR-2014-title16-vol1/pdf/CFR-2014-title16-vol1-sec437-8.pdf); [federal-regs.com 437.8](https://www.federal-regs.com/title/16/part-437/437.8/)

### Inferences
- **Lead support:** the licensor's "lead support / brand-generated jobs" is close to "providing customers." If the deal avoided the Franchise Rule by dropping payments below the threshold, it would likely land in the Business Opportunity Rule. That rule requires a one-page disclosure document (including earnings-claim, litigation and cancellation/refund information) delivered 7 days before signing or payment. The 7-day timing is training knowledge, not verified here.
- **No escape through the threshold:** the gap between the $500 figure in 437.8 and the $735 figure in 436.8 means a deal with first-six-month payments between $500 and $735 is in a gray area. Neither rule's minimum-payment language lines up neatly in that range. This needs counsel.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether 437.8 has been updated to track the inflation-adjusted Part 436 figure.

## Florida: Franchise Act (Fla. Stat. 817.416) and Sale of Business Opportunities Act (Fla. Stat. 559.80-559.815)

### Takeaway
Florida does not register franchises. A franchisor that meets the FTC franchise definition and complies with the FTC Rule must file an annual **exemption notice** with the Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS) on Form 10500 and pay a **$100** fee. If it doesn't, it risks being treated as a "business opportunity" under the Florida Sale of Business Opportunities Act (initial fee over $500 plus certain seller representations), which brings disclosure, filing and possible bond duties. Separately, the Florida Franchise Act (817.416) makes intentional misrepresentation to franchisees actionable, with return of all money invested plus attorney's fees.

### Cited Findings
- **559.801 definition:** a "business opportunity" is a sale or lease of products, equipment, supplies or services that requires the buyer to pay the seller an initial fee **over $500** to start a business, where the seller makes one of several listed representations. These include a refund or buy-back guarantee, and a sales or marketing program to help the buyer earn income. The sales/marketing-program representation is **excepted when offered in conjunction with the licensing of a registered trademark or service mark**. (Text current as of Jan. 1, 2025.) — [FindLaw, Fla. Stat. 559.801](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xxxiii-regulation-of-trade-commerce-investments-and-solicitations/fl-st-sect-559-801/)
- A 2009 Senate staff analysis describes another representation: the seller providing, or helping the buyer find, locations for business equipment. — [Fla. Senate analysis, SB 1262 (2009)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2009/1262/Analyses/20091262SCM_2009s1262.cm.pdf)
- **Exclusions:** among others, sales of an ongoing business where the owner sells no more than five, and not-for-profit sales of demonstration materials or sales training costing $500 or less. — [FindLaw 559.801](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xxxiii-regulation-of-trade-commerce-investments-and-solicitations/fl-st-sect-559-801/); [flsenate.gov 2013](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2013/559.801)
- **559.802 franchise exemption:** a franchise that meets the 16 CFR 436 franchise definition is exempt if the franchisor files a notice with the Department before selling in Florida, stating it substantially complies with the FTC Rule. The fee is capped at $100. The exemption lasts one year and is renewed annually with another notice and fee of up to $100. The Department may ask only for the name, franchise name, trade name, principal address and FEIN. — [flrules.org, 559.802](https://flrules.org/gateway/statute.asp?id=559.802); [FindLaw 559.802](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xxxiii-regulation-of-trade-commerce-investments-and-solicitations/fl-st-sect-559-802/)
- **Rule 5J-10.002:** the filing uses **DACS Form 10500** with a nonrefundable **$100** fee, renewed annually on the same form and fee. The program is administered by FDACS. — [Fla. Admin. Code 5J-10.002 (Justia)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/florida/5/5j/chapter-5j-10/section-5j-10-002); [state-rules.com](https://state-rules.com/florida/fac/5J-10.002/); [The Law Dept.](https://thelawdept.com/franchise-laws-by-state/florida-franchise-law/)
- **817.416 Florida Franchise Act, definition:** a "franchise or distributorship" is a contract, express or implied, for a commercial relationship of definite or continuing indefinite duration. The franchisee is granted the right to offer or sell goods or services supplied by another, and is substantially reliant on the franchisor for the basic supply of goods. — [florida.public.law 817.416](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._817.416); [myfloridahouse.gov](https://www.myfloridahouse.gov/Statutes/2017/0817.416/)
- **817.416 prohibited conduct:** intentionally misrepresenting the prospects or chances for success, the known required total investment (including by failing to disclose it), or the efforts to sell more franchises than the market can reasonably bear. — [florida.public.law](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._817.416); [FindLaw, "The Florida Franchise Act"](https://corporate.findlaw.com/business-operations/the-florida-franchise-act.html)
- **817.416 remedies:** return of all moneys invested plus reasonable attorney's fees. The Department of Legal Affairs may seek injunctions, and a knowing violation is a second-degree misdemeanor. — [FindLaw](https://corporate.findlaw.com/business-operations/the-florida-franchise-act.html); [syfert.com 817.416](https://www.syfert.com/florida/statutes/0817.416.html)

### Inferences
- **Does 817.416 reach this deal?** Its definition hinges on the franchisee being "substantially reliant on the franchisor for the basic supply of goods." A service-only cleaning brand license may not fit. Even so, misstating the success prospects or total investment of a license is risky under Florida's general Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (FDUTPA). That point is training knowledge, not sourced here.
- **Is it a Florida "business opportunity"?** The $368.71 weekly fee is arguably not an "initial fee." But the first several payments, or a deposit, could be argued to be one. If the licensor's mark is **federally registered**, the trademark carve-out reduces the risk under the marketing-program representation. If the mark is **unregistered**, the risk is higher.
- **Practical answer for Florida:** if the deal is an FTC franchise, the clean path is to comply with the FTC Rule and file the annual $100 FDACS exemption notice before offering in Florida.

## Georgia and Alabama: franchise and business-opportunity statutes

### Takeaway
Neither Georgia nor Alabama has a franchise registration or state disclosure law, so federal FTC Rule compliance governs. Georgia does have a **Sale of Business Opportunities Act (O.C.G.A. 10-1-410 et seq.)** with a **$500** initial-payment test. Its sales/marketing-program prong does not apply when the program is offered together with licensing a **registered** trademark or service mark. I found no Alabama business-opportunity statute. Alabama relies on its Deceptive Trade Practices Act (Ala. Code 8-19-1 et seq.).

### Cited Findings
- **Georgia definition (10-1-410):** a business opportunity includes an agreement where the seller offers a sales or marketing program and requires a total initial payment **over $500**. That subparagraph does **not** apply to a sales or marketing program offered with the licensing of a registered trademark or service mark. — [Onecle, O.C.G.A. 10-1-410](https://law.onecle.com/georgia/title-10/10-1-410.html); [Justia 2006](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/2006/10/10-1-410.html)
- Practitioners say franchisors with **federal USPTO registration** of their marks fall outside Georgia's business-opportunity definition. Franchisors without federal registration may fall within it. — [Internicola Law, Georgia](https://www.franchiselawsolutions.com/franchising/georgia-franchise-registration); [Georgia State Univ. Law Review](https://www.gsulawreview.org/latest/the-georgia-sale-of-business-opportunities-act/)
- **Georgia 10-1-412:** sellers that promise refunds or buy-backs need a surety bond or trust account of at least **$75,000**. — [Onecle, 10-1-412](https://law.onecle.com/georgia/title-10/10-1-412.html)
- The Georgia Department of Law, Consumer Protection Division, oversees multilevel distribution and sale-of-business-opportunity matters. — [consumer.georgia.gov](https://consumer.georgia.gov/node/1041)
- **Alabama:** no franchise registration or state-mandated disclosure. Franchisors must still comply with the federal Franchise Rule. — [Internicola Law, Alabama](https://franchiselawsolutions.com/franchising/alabama-franchise-registration)
- **Alabama DTPA (Ala. Code 8-19-1 et seq.):** covers pyramid schemes and has a catch-all for unconscionable, false, misleading or deceptive acts in trade. The Attorney General or a district attorney may enforce it, and private damages are available. 8-19-14 sets a one-year discovery-based limitations period, capped at four years from the transaction (2009 code version). — [USLegal, Alabama DTPA](https://businesslaw.uslegal.com/deceptive-trade-practices-laws/alabama-deceptive-trade-practices-laws/); [Justia, Ala. Code 8-19-14 (2009)](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/2009/Title8/Chapter19/8-19-14.html)

### Inferences
- **Georgia:** whether the licensor's brand is a **federally registered** mark matters a great deal. If it is registered, the Georgia business-opportunity risk from the marketing program drops sharply. If it is unregistered (or only state-registered, whose treatment is unclear), a deal with more than $500 up front plus a marketing/lead program could fall under the Georgia Act.
- **Alabama:** the main exposure is the FTC Rule plus general deceptive-practices law.

- I found no reliable source confirming that Alabama has no business-opportunity statute at all. The absence comes from search results, not from a source that says so explicitly.
- I did not confirm whether Georgia's carve-out requires federal registration or also accepts state registration.

## Trademark license vs. franchise; consequences of an unintended franchise; common structuring approaches

### Takeaway
A pure trademark license (a brand name plus quality control only, with no operating assistance or system) is generally not a franchise. Adding fees, marketing or lead programs, and operating controls is what turns it into one. The labels on the contract don't matter. Consequences include FTC enforcement (civil penalties up to **$53,088 per violation** as of the January 2025 adjustment), state-law claims for refunds or rescission, and deceptive-practices claims. There is no federal private right of action. Common structuring approaches are: keep required payments under **$735** through month 6 (for example, by starting fees after six months); sell leads on a pure per-lead or referral basis with no trademark license or controls; or comply with the FTC Rule and issue an FDD.

### Cited Findings
- **No federal private right of action:** the FTC Rule gives franchisees no private right of action. In *Safe Step*, the accidental-franchise finding produced only limited remedies. — [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/bathtub-manufacturers-licensing-arrangement-created-an-accidental-franchise-but-court-finds-limited-remedies); [Rep. Schakowsky, Franchisee Freedom Act one-pager](https://schakowsky.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/schakowsky.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/franchisee-freedom-act-one-pager.pdf)
- **FTC civil penalties:** the maximum for violating Sections 5(l) and 5(m)(1)(A)-(B) of the FTC Act (the provisions used for rule violations) rose from $51,744 to **$53,088 per violation**, effective on publication January 17, 2025. — [FTC civil penalty adjustment](https://www.ftc.gov/node/87476); [Federal Register public inspection 2025-01361](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2025-01361.pdf)
- **Rescission abroad, as an illustration:** under statutes like Ontario's Arthur Wishart Act, missing disclosure lets a franchisee rescind and recover fees paid. In *Vallee*, the court ordered a $40,000 fee refund. — [Ontario Bar Assn.](https://oba.org/Sections/Franchise-Law/Articles/Articles-2018/January-2018/The-Danger-of-the-Accidental-Franchise)
- **Deferred fees:** FTC staff have said a deferred payment evidenced by a non-negotiable promissory note maturing after six months may qualify for the minimum-payment exemption. A financing arrangement with an acceleration clause will not. — [LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/?p=1730125); [FTC Staff Advisory Opinion 96-5](https://search.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/advisory-opinions/informal-staff-advisory-opinion-96-5)
- **Fees after six months:** in a 1998 opinion, FTC staff said fees payable more than six months after the business starts operating appeared to fall within the exemption. Staff also warned that payment devices that could frustrate or circumvent the Rule's protections would not be acceptable. — [FTC Staff Advisory Opinion 98-3](https://search.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/advisory-opinions/informal-staff-advisory-opinion-98-3); [LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/?p=1730125)
- **Grace period:** a 1995 staff opinion approved a structure where the sub-agent owed nothing during its first six months of operation, giving it time to build working capital. — [FTC staff opinion (ftc.gov/node/33858)](https://www.ftc.gov/node/33858)
- **Expected payments:** fees that are expected to exceed the threshold within six months, such as profit shares in an industry where typical revenue makes them likely, are presumed to meet the minimum payment. — [FTC Staff Advisory Opinion 99-1](https://ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/advisory-opinions/informal-staff-advisory-opinion-99-1)
- Regulators look at economic substance over form, so a trademark license plus fees is commonly treated as a franchise. — [LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/?p=1730125); [Fox Rothschild](https://www.foxrothschild.com/publications/whats-an-accidental-franchise-and-could-i-be-one)

### Inferences
- **Option 1: Defer all fees and commissions until month 7.** This is the classic minimum-payment structure: no fees, deposits or commissions until after six months of operation, with no acceleration clause. **Caveat:** under FTC staff opinion 99-1, a commission on brand-generated jobs during months 1-6 would likely count, so it must also be deferred or waived. If the deal then avoids Part 436, check whether Part 437 (Business Opportunity Rule) applies because of the lead and customer provision.
- **Option 2: Pure lead-sale or referral deal.** No trademark license (the licensee operates under its own name), no operating controls, and per-lead or per-referral fees at market rates. Removing the trademark element defeats the franchise definition. But providing customers or leads for a payment can still trigger the Business Opportunity Rule.
- **Option 3: Bare trademark license.** Brand plus quality control only, with no marketing system, training or operating standards. This reduces the "significant control or assistance" element, but website, marketing and lead support work against it.
- **Option 4: Fractional franchise.** License only to established cleaning companies with more than two years of experience, for whom the brand is 20% or less of sales.
- **Option 5: Comply.** Prepare an FDD, deliver it 14 days before signing (timing from training knowledge), and file Florida's annual $100 FDACS exemption notice. Federally registering the mark also helps with the Florida and Georgia business-opportunity carve-outs.
- **Bottom line for the current terms:** a $368.71 weekly fee from day one, plus brand use, plus marketing and lead support, satisfies two elements outright and plausibly the third. The minimum-payment exemption is lost in week 2. Without restructuring, the deal carries **high** risk of being an unregistered and undisclosed franchise under the FTC Rule. A franchise attorney should review it before any licensee is signed.
