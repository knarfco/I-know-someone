# Referral Fees, Lead-Gen Website Rental, Cleaning Brokers and Cleaning Sales Commissions (U.S. benchmarks)

Purpose: test a proposed deal where a brand/lead source receives a sliding commission on construction-cleaning jobs (10% under $25k / 7% for $25k–$100k / 4% over $100k) plus a weekly fee of about $369 (about $1,599/month, $19,188/year).

Method note: WebFetch could not resolve most hosts in this environment, so the findings below come from search-result extracts of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Figures should be spot-checked before anyone quotes them externally. Source strength is labeled: [STRONG] = regulator, trade body, or primary platform terms; [MEDIUM] = industry publication, consultant, or employer posting; [WEAK] = promoter blog, forum, or aggregator.

## 1. Construction and contractor referral / finder's fee norms

### Takeaway
Referral fees paid to a third party on construction contracts usually run about 0.75%–5% of contract value, and sometimes combine a flat fee with a percentage. The biggest platform benchmark is BuildZoom at 2.5% of contract value. A 10% referral fee on construction work is well above these norms; 4% on jobs over $100k sits at the top of the normal range. I found no published sliding-scale standard by job size; tiered schedules are custom-negotiated.

### Cited Findings
- BuildZoom (contractor-matching platform) charges the contractor a referral fee of 2.5% of the total agreed contract price. It is due after the contract is signed, and nothing is owed if the contractor doesn't win the job. Optional advertising packages reportedly start at $99/month. No cap or job-size tiering appeared in the results. [STRONG for the 2.5% rate, which is BuildZoom's own answer page] — [BuildZoom Answers](https://www.buildzoom.com/answers/t/who-pays-the-referral-fee-to-buildzoom/2480); [BuildZoom Answers: cost](https://www.buildzoom.com/answers/t/is-there-a-cost-to-having-your-business-on-buildzoom/1198); [HigherVisibility BuildZoom review](https://www.highervisibility.com/industries/contractors/learn/buildzoom-review/)
- A construction business owner writing in Construction Business Owner says his firm usually pays 0.75% of contract value as a finder's/referral fee to outside parties, and that such fees are legal provided the recipient pays tax on them. [MEDIUM, a single practitioner in a trade publication] — [Construction Business Owner](https://www.constructionbusinessowner.com/article/should-your-company-pay-finders-fee/)
- An NSPE (National Society of Professional Engineers) ethics case describes a sample offer of a flat $500 plus 3% of total contract price for each contract signed through the referral. This is an example raised as an ethics question, not a recommended rate. [MEDIUM] — [NSPE](https://www.nspe.org/node/9569)
- Referral-agreement templates list typical structures as a percentage of total contract price (e.g., 2–5%), a percentage of profit, or a flat fee. They advise defining a "qualified lead", the fee basis, and when the fee is earned. [WEAK, template sites] — [Construction referral fee agreement template](https://itctel.com/kkostas/aeromates/docs/construction_referral_fee_agreement.html)
- Legal constraint, California: Bus. & Prof. Code §7157 makes it a misdemeanor and grounds for license discipline for contractors to pay or accept referral or "kick-back" fees to or from other contractors or unlicensed salespersons to secure construction work. The CSLB clarifies that the rule targets inducements between contractors, not payments to non-contractors. Referral services are lawful if they don't present themselves as able to do construction work and the customer contracts with and pays the licensed contractor directly. [STRONG, state regulator] — [CSLB Newsletter 2015-16, referral entities](https://cslb.ca.gov/Newsletter/2015-16%20winter/referral_entities.asp); [CSLB Newsletter 2011](https://www2.cslb.ca.gov/Newsletter/2011-Summer/page8.htm)
- Law-firm commentary covers referral-fee restrictions in contracting. Rules vary by state, and in one Florida county commissions are allowed if the salesperson registers with the contractor board. [MEDIUM for the law firm; WEAK for the forum claim] — [AGR Law, Referral Fees](https://www.agrlaw.com/law-talk-3-1-10); [AGR Law, Referral Fees Revisited](https://agrlaw.com/law-talk-3-5-10); [ContractorTalk forum](https://www.contractortalk.com/posts/7950499)

### Inferences
- Measured against construction referral norms (0.75%–5%, with 2.5% as the platform benchmark), the proposed 10% tier is about 2–4 times market, 7% is above market, and 4% is within the upper range.
- The weekly fee pushes the effective take higher still. $19,188/year in fixed fees equals 10% commission on about $192k of jobs. A contractor doing $300k/year of sub-$25k jobs through the brand would pay about $30k in commission plus $19.2k in fees, about $49.2k or 16.4% effective.
- Construction finder's fees are usually one-time, success-only, and carry no recurring fixed fee. The proposed deal's fee-plus-commission structure resembles a franchise or license more than a referral arrangement.
- Documentation norm: a written agreement that defines qualified leads, the fee basis (contract price vs. collected revenue), when the fee is earned (signing vs. payment), and tax reporting (Form 1099). Where state law applies, both parties should check contractor-licensing restrictions on paying commissions.

### Gaps
- No authoritative survey of construction finder's fees, and no published sliding-scale standard by job size. The tier breakpoints in the proposal can't be benchmarked directly.
- Couldn't retrieve full text of the Construction Business Owner or NSPE pages to confirm context.
- Angi, Thumbtack and Houzz Pro per-lead costs for post-construction cleaning were not retrieved.

## 2. Rank-and-rent / lead-generation website rental

### Takeaway
Practitioner sources consistently describe three pricing models: flat monthly rent (commonly $500–$2,000/month for trade niches, up to $3,000 for premium trades), pay-per-lead (about $40–$230/lead depending on trade), and revenue share (about 4–15% of closed-job value, often 10–15%, with 5–10% for lead-to-close commissions). Almost every source sells rank-and-rent training or services. Rent is usually one model or another; charging rent and a revenue share together is not described as standard.

### Cited Findings
- Revenue share: one guide puts the "typical rev share rate" at 10–15% depending on whether it is figured on gross or net revenue. A review of Rank and Rent Club describes taking 5–10% commission for each lead that becomes a closed job. [WEAK, promoter content] — [The Lead Guy, Rank and Rent Guide](https://theleadguy.online/rank-and-rent-guide/); [Ippei, Rank and Rent Club review (2026)](https://ippei.com/rank-and-rent-club/)
- A BlackHatWorld forum thread describes an offer of 4% for roofing/remodeling and 15% for HVAC, chimney cleaning and garage doors. Commenters called 4–15% low and suggested a test phase to measure job values and close rates first. Higher-ticket trades get a lower percentage. [WEAK, forum] — [BlackHatWorld](https://www.blackhatworld.com/seo/rank-and-rent-revenue-share-model-suggestion.1791255/)
- Revenue share is described as a percentage of jobs closed from the site's enquiries. It is typically used for higher-ticket work where both sides trust each other's reporting; flat rent and pay-per-lead are the alternatives. [WEAK] — [LocusPilot, What is rank and rent](https://locuspilot.com/blog/what-is-rank-and-rent)
- Monthly rent ranges by trade:
  - Roofing $750–$1,500/month, plumbing $500–$1,000. Lead values: roofing $80–$150/lead, plumbing $40–$80/lead. [WEAK] — [LocusPilot, Best niches](https://locuspilot.com/blog/best-rank-and-rent-niches)
  - Roofing $500–$1,800 (15–45 leads/month), plumbing $550–$1,600 (18–40 leads/month). [WEAK] — [LocusPilot, Examples](https://locuspilot.com/blog/rank-and-rent-website-examples)
  - Premium trades (water damage, roofing) $1,000–$3,000/month; mid-tier (HVAC, plumbing) $500–$1,500/month. [WEAK] — [Masum AK, How much to charge](https://masumak.com/how-much-to-charge-for-rank-and-rent/)
- Rent tracks lead volume. In one practitioner's tracked launches, sites producing 10–14 qualified leads in their first month rented for $300–$550/month, and sites with 31–45 leads rented for $1,100–$1,900/month. Running costs (hosting, domain, call tracking) are about $30–$50/month. [WEAK] — [LocusPilot, Complete guide](https://locuspilot.com/blog/rank-and-rent-complete-guide); [LocusPilot, Examples](https://locuspilot.com/blog/rank-and-rent-website-examples)
- Side Hustle Nation frames the model as roughly "$1,000 a month from simple local websites". Another promoter headlines $1,500/month. [WEAK] — [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/rank-and-rent-websites/); [Medium/mitaboost](https://mitaboost.medium.com/how-to-build-rank-and-rent-websites-for-1-500-month-312875edd33b)
- Pay-per-lead has friction. One operator (James Dooley interview) reports that about 30% of enquiries were disputed as fake or unreachable. [WEAK, podcast anecdote] — [Indexsy podcast via Metacast](https://metacast.app/podcast/james-dooley-podcast/wFMrmnPb/indexsy-interviews-james-dooley-entrepreneur-the-indexsy-podcast/Vg5A6AlO)
- Pricing structures are fixed monthly, per-lead, or percentage of job value. Per-lead requires reliable tracking, and pricing should be negotiated on actual lead volume and average job value. [WEAK, Dutch-language blog] — [Alejandro Rioja](https://alejandrorioja.com/nl/rank-and-rent/)

### Inferences
- The proposed weekly fee (about $1,599/month) is at the upper-middle of reported rank-and-rent rents. It is comparable to what promoters say a well-ranked site in a premium trade commands, and justified only if the site actually delivers a steady flow of qualified leads (roughly 30–45+/month by these sources' examples).
- The proposed 10%/7%/4% commission sits inside reported rank-and-rent revenue-share ranges (4–15%), and the declining-by-size shape matches the BlackHatWorld example of lower percentages on higher-ticket trades. But rank-and-rent sources present revenue share as an alternative to rent, not an addition. Charging both is roughly double-dipping relative to these norms, unless the fixed fee buys extra services (brand license, insurance, software, back office).
- A common compromise in this market is a base rent plus a performance component with a lower percentage, or rent credited against commission. This is an inference; I found no source documenting it as a norm.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-promoter) survey of rank-and-rent pricing exists. All figures are self-reported by people selling courses or services.
- No rank-and-rent data specific to post-construction cleaning or commercial cleaning niches was found.

## 3. Commercial cleaning brokers, franchisors and facility-services aggregators: margin retained

### Takeaway
No authoritative survey of broker or aggregator markup exists. The best-documented analog is janitorial franchising, where the franchisor that sells and bills the accounts keeps about 10–15% of monthly billings (royalty plus management/billing fee). Franchisors also charge account "finder's fees" or account-package fees: one source reports three times the monthly invoice, and California's regulator says a package fee is often about half a year's gross. Subcontractor markup norms in contractor forums are about 15–20%+. The proposed 10%/7%/4% falls at or below what janitorial franchisors keep on recurring work.

### Cited Findings
- California DFPI (regulator) guidance on janitorial franchises: franchisees typically pay the franchisor a fee for a "package" of cleaning accounts, and that fee usually is about half the gross income the accounts are expected to generate in a year. It also warns about the franchisor retaining royalty and management fees from billings. [STRONG, regulator; document dated circa 2019] — [DFPI, Buying a Janitorial Services Franchise](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2019/02/janitorial.pdf)
- Coverall: about a 5% royalty on gross billings plus a support or management/billing fee of 10% of gross dollar volume, for about 15% total retained. One listing shows "Coverall North America" at a 15% royalty, which conflicts with or merges these figures. [WEAK, aggregator directories; verify against the FDD, Item 6] — [Franzy, Coverall](https://franzy.com/franchises/coverall); [FranchiseOverview, Coverall NGT](https://franchiseoverview.com/company/coverall-ngt); [PulseRevOps, Coverall](https://pulserevops.com/franchises/fr1061)
- Jan-Pro: 10% royalty plus 0.5% ad fund (10.5%) per directory. One Jan-Pro regional listing shows a 10% royalty on gross billings. Jani-King regional franchisors range from 10% (Buffalo) to 11% of monthly gross (Milwaukee). [WEAK, aggregators] — [FranchiseInvestorData, Jan-Pro](https://franchiseinvestordata.com/franchise/jan-pro); [FranchiseOverview, Royal/Jani-King](https://franchiseoverview.com/company/royal); [FranchiseOverview, cleaning industry](https://franchiseoverview.com/industries/cleaning-restoration)
- An unnamed "major janitorial franchise" charges additional finder's fees of three times the monthly value of the customer's invoice. [WEAK, competitor/advocacy blog, single source] — [Secure Clean Building Services](https://www.securecleanbsi.com/what-we-need-to-know-about-cleaning-franchises/)
- Subcontracting markup, contractor forum: "typical is 20%", covering about 10% profit and 10% overhead. Others start at 15–20%, and some say about 20% just covers the cost of running billing through your company, with more if you manage the sub. [WEAK, forum] — [The Cleaning Standard forum, Subcontractor markup](https://thecleaningstandard.com/forum/threads/subcontractor-markup.295337/latest)
- Aggregator models: U.S. Maintenance serves 20,500+ retail locations through 3,500+ contractors. Symbiot uses 600+ contractors who pay a fee to become business partners, so it earns from contractor fees as well as markup. No markup percentage was disclosed. [MEDIUM, ForConstructionPros] — [ForConstructionPros, The Trade-off](https://www.forconstructionpros.com/pavement-maintenance/maintenance/sweeping/article/10306846/the-tradeoff)
- Hidden cost of aggregator work: national facility-services firms often pay subcontractors in 60–90 days, and factoring advances typically run 80–85% for a fee of 2.5–3.5% per 30 days. [WEAK, factoring vendor marketing] — [Bay Street Lending](https://www.baystreetlending.com/capital-solutions/invoice-factoring/janitorial)
- A BizQuest listing describes a "subcontractor-model cleaning business" with $80k revenue and 30% margins: a broker that subcontracts all labor and keeps about 30%. [WEAK, single sale listing] — [BizQuest](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/subcontractor-model-cleaning-business-w-80k-revenue-and-30-margins/BW2454306)

### Inferences
- A janitorial franchisor that finds, contracts and bills the customer, and carries brand, insurance and collections, keeps about 10–15% of recurring revenue. A pure broker or general-contractor intermediary marks up subcontracted cleaning about 15–30%. On that scale, 10%/7%/4% is moderate for a party that does all of that, and high for a party that only supplies leads (compare section 1's 0.75–5%).
- The proposed weekly fee resembles franchise fixed charges (account packages, finder's fees, ad funds). Combined with 10% commission it approaches franchise-level take, so the brand should be delivering franchise-like value (brand, sales, billing, insurance, training).
- Post-construction cleaning is mostly one-time project work, not recurring billing. Franchise and broker percentages are quoted on recurring monthly billings, so they compound over a contract's life, while a construction-cleaning percentage applies once per job.

### Gaps
- No verified FDD Item 6 figures were retrieved. Aggregator directories conflict (Coverall 5% + 10% vs. 15%).
- No published markup for national facility-services aggregators (e.g., ABM subcontracting, Kellermeyer Bergensons, SMS Assist/Lessen). No BSCAI benchmark was found.

## 4. Commercial cleaning salesperson commission norms (including recurring contracts)

### Takeaway
Janitorial sales commissions are usually expressed as a share of the first month's billing (about 15–100%, with 50–60% common in current postings), sometimes plus a small residual (5–10% of monthly billing for 12–24 months). Some firms instead pay a flat 15% of monthly billing for the life of the account. A consultant alternative is 2–2.5 times the account's monthly markup, paid over 4–7 months. Converted to share of first-year contract value, most plans pay about 4–15%.

### Cited Findings
- Choice Facility Services (Houston, 2025–26 posting) pays 50% of the first month's billing on each new contract, plus a 10% residual for 12 months, then 5% for another 12 months. [MEDIUM, employer posting] — [Gusto job posting](https://jobs.gusto.com/postings/choice-facility-services-llc-sales-representative-60430cf4-5ac5-4384-9ca1-b7a6ad36aa93)
- Anago of SWFL (franchise) pays a $3,500/month base plus 60% commission on first-month sales, with $500/month for gas and phone. [MEDIUM, posting via aggregator] — [Salesfolks](https://salesfolks.com/job/salesperson-business-development-commercial-cleaning)
- Lunexa Cleaning (NY) pays 100% of the first full month's recurring invoice once collected, then 5% of collected recurring revenue from month 2 onward. [MEDIUM, posting] — [Workable](https://jobs.workable.com/jobs/4b5fda72-d665-4102-9301-2231afc8e492.md)
- CAM Resources (1099, commission-only) pays 15% of the customer's monthly contract amount every month the contract is active, starting when the account goes live and the first payment arrives. [MEDIUM, posting] — [CareerPlug](https://cam-resources-inc.careerplug.com/jobs/3133240)
- City Wide Facility Solutions pays a $60k base plus uncapped commission, with on-target earnings of $120k+. Percentage not stated. [MEDIUM] — [Workable](https://jobs.workable.com/jobs/d0fbfe48-b9a6-4cf7-a8ae-05be3e4deaa1.md)
- The Janitorial Store (industry training vendor) describes paying reps a percentage of the first month's billing, paid 30–45 days after the contract starts, ranging from 15% to 40%. [MEDIUM] — [The Janitorial Store, How Should I Pay My Sales People](https://www.thejanitorialstore.com/public/How-Should-I-Pay-My-Sales-People.cfm)
- Elite BSC (janitorial consultant) recommends commission tied to markup, not gross. Its firm pays 2–2.5 times the account's monthly markup over cost, spread over about 4–7 months, with the first check tied to the customer's first payment. [MEDIUM] — [Elite BSC, Sales Compensation Plans](https://elitebsc.com/sales-compensation-plans-for-the-janitorial-industry/)
- Cleaning-business owners on Alignable report paying 10% on referred work, with suggestions of 10–15% for cleaning contracts. [WEAK, forum] — [Alignable forum](https://www.alignable.com/forum/to-all-commercial-cleaning-services-especially-office-cleaning)
- ZipRecruiter cleaning-sales listings often show base plus about 15% commission on new commercial work, with posted pay of $53k–$60k. [WEAK, aggregator] — [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Cleaning-Sales-Commission)
- General B2B context: new-logo commissions are about 8–14% of annual contract value and renewals about 4–5%. Residuals are commonly capped at 12–24 months because lifetime residuals are expensive (example: 5% of monthly recurring revenue for 36 months). [MEDIUM, sales-comp vendors, SaaS-heavy] — [SalesCookie, rates by industry 2026](https://blog.salescookie.com/2026/05/01/sales-commission-rates-by-industry-2026/); [Commissionly, Residual commission](https://www.commissionly.io/residual-commission/)

### Inferences
- Converted to first-year contract value: 50% of month one is about 4.2% of year-one billing, and with Choice's 10% residual for 11 more months it is about 13%. 100% of month one plus 5% is about 13%. A flat 15% residual is 15% every year, for life. Janitorial reps therefore earn roughly 4–15% of first-year value, with lifetime-residual plans the most expensive.
- On one-time construction-cleaning jobs, the closest analog is a one-off sale, where reps and referrers commonly earn about 10% (Alignable; UK deep-clean example). The proposed 10% on small jobs equals a typical rep or referrer commission. Adding a fixed weekly fee on top is not something salespeople receive.
- The declining 10/7/4 shape mirrors common sales practice (lower rates on large deals, lower rates on renewals or residuals).

### Gaps
- No ISSA, BSCAI or CleanLink survey of janitorial sales compensation was found. The evidence is postings and consultants.
- Commission norms specific to post-construction cleaning sales were not found.

## 5. Net and gross profit margins in commercial and post-construction cleaning

### Takeaway
U.S. janitorial services average about 11.9% profit margin (IBISWorld, reported in 2026), with wages at about 43% of revenue. Blog and calculator sources cite 10–20% net for commercial cleaning (lower on large accounts) and 30–50% gross for post-construction work. A 10% commission plus about $19k/year in fixed fees can consume most or all of the net margin on smaller jobs.

### Cited Findings
- IBISWorld U.S. Janitorial Services (NAICS 56172): profit margin averages 11.9%, wages consume 43.2% of revenue, industry revenue is $112.0B in 2026 (+1.8%). Margins are squeezed by rising labor costs and aggressive buyer negotiation. [STRONG-MEDIUM, IBISWorld via CMM Online, Feb 2026] — [CMM Online](https://cmmonline.com/news/u-s-janitorial-services-market-projected-to-grow-nearly-2-in-2026); [IBISWorld report page](https://img.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/janitorial-services/1496/)
- Commercial cleaning net margins are about 10–20% (calculator) or 15–20%, and 8–30% across commercial cleaning broadly. [WEAK, vendor content] — [ZenMaid calculator](https://get.zenmaid.com/tools/profit-margin-calculator); [Contractor+](https://www.contractorplus.app/blog/is-cleaning-business-profitable); [CleanLink article](https://www.cleanlink.com/cp/article.aspx?id=32024)
- Larger accounts carry thinner profit, about 3–8%, versus 10–20% for medium accounts. [WEAK, unsourced Q&A] — [eleccircuit Q&A](https://mail.eleccircuit.com/how-are-janitorial-contracts-calculated.html)
- Main Street Index (listed-business data) puts owner-earnings margins across cleaning types from 24.1% (janitorial) to 46.8% (carpet). This is seller's discretionary earnings, not net margin. [WEAK-MEDIUM] — [BigIdeasDB, Main Street Index, Oct 2026](https://bigideasdb.com/is-a-cleaning-business-profitable)
- Post-construction cleaning: gross margins often exceed 30–50% before overhead, and one source says they regularly clear 40%. Operating margins for well-run cleaning firms are 15–35%. Owner-operators earn 30–45%, firms with employees 15–25%, and a planning target is a minimum of 15–20% net. Some financialmodelslab pages show impossible margins (730%, 830%) and should be disregarded. [WEAK, blogs and calculators] — [Convertlabs (2026)](https://blog.convertlabs.io/how-profitable-is-a-cleaning-business-in-2026); [Connecteam](https://connecteam.com/e-is-a-cleaning-business-profitable/); [Harvest calculator](https://www.getharvest.com/calculators/profit-margin-calculator-for-cleaning-business); [FinancialModel.net, post-construction](https://financialmodel.net/blogs/owner-makes/post-construction-cleaning); [FinancialModelsLab](https://financialmodelslab.com/products/post-construction-cleaning-profitability)

### Inferences
- If a contractor nets about 12–20% on post-construction jobs, a 10% commission takes half or more of the profit on sub-$25k jobs; 7% takes about 35–60%, and 4% takes about 20–33%. Against 30–50% gross margins the percentages look affordable, but the contractor's overhead still has to come out of the remainder.
- The fixed fee is the bigger risk at low volume. At $19,188/year, a contractor with $150k/year of brand-sourced jobs pays 12.8% in fees alone before commission. Total take of about 22.8% likely exceeds the contractor's entire net margin. Breakeven requires substantial volume: at $500k/year of small jobs the total is about $69k, or 13.8%.
- Overall: the 10/7/4 percentages alone are comparable to janitorial franchise royalties (10–15%) and rank-and-rent revenue shares (4–15%). They are higher than construction referral norms (0.75–5%). Combined with a weekly fee of about $369, the deal is closer to a franchise or license package than a referral arrangement. It is reasonable only if the brand supplies substantial value beyond leads, or guarantees enough job volume.

### Gaps
- No rigorous (survey or audited) margin data specific to post-construction cleaning. IBISWorld's 11.9% covers janitorial broadly, and the full IBISWorld page could not be read directly.
- Couldn't confirm Cleanfax, ISSA or Swept benchmark figures; no results surfaced from them on margins.
