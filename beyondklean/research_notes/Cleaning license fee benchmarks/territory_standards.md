# Exclusive Territory Protection and Performance Conditions (Franchise, Licensing, Dealer, Master-License Agreements)

Method note: These findings come from about 12 web searches. Several attempts to fetch full pages failed with DNS errors (franchimp.com, fulcruminquiry.com, beancount.io, franchiselawsolutions.com), so many of the findings below rely on search-result extracts and were not read in full context. The sources are mostly practitioner commentary, clause databases (Law Insider and similar) and individual SEC-filed (EDGAR) agreements, not statutes or surveys. They show common practice, not a binding standard. Treat specific numbers as examples, not market averages.

## 1. Typical performance and minimum standards attached to exclusive territories

### Takeaway
Exclusivity is usually conditional. Franchise systems tie it to annual performance standards or minimum sales. Area-developer and master agreements tie it to a development schedule (units opened by set dates). Distributor agreements tie it to minimum purchase obligations. I found no contract-level norm for lead response time, bid counts or customer-satisfaction thresholds tied to territory; these exist only as operational benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- **Conditional exclusivity is standard.** Franchisors frequently impose performance requirements when granting an exclusive territory, and "conditional exclusivity" means protection is earned by meeting performance or operational criteria — [Franchimp](https://franchimp.com/blog/article/exclusive-territory-franchise-disclosure-explained); [Heritage Law, FDD Item 12](https://www.heritagelawwi.com/franchise-disclosure-document-item-12)
- **Minimum sales tied to exclusivity.** A franchisee may have to hit a minimum sales level. If it does not, exclusivity can be cut back or removed — [Sprintlaw NZ](https://sprintlaw.co.nz/articles/what-is-an-exclusive-territory-clause/); [Sprintlaw UK](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/what-do-you-need-to-know-about-a-franchise-s-exclusive-territory-clause/)
- **Example (as reported, not verified in an FDD).** Orangetheory is reported to tie continued territorial protection to annual performance standards — [Franchimp](https://franchimp.com/blog/article/exclusive-territory-franchise-disclosure-explained)
- **Calibration benchmark (UK).** British Franchise Association practice reportedly limits minimum targets to no more than 70% of average franchisee performance. Franchisors without performance data often impose no targets for the first three years — [Franchise World UK](https://www.franchiseworld.co.uk/setting-targets-for-franchisees/); see also [Elite Franchise](https://elitefranchisemagazine.co.uk/legal/item/ensure-maximum-effort-with-minimum-performance-clauses), [LegalVision AU](https://legalvision.com.au/minimum-performance-provisions-franchise-agreement)
- **Area development schedules.** Every area development agreement includes a schedule stating how many units must open and by when, e.g., first unit within 12 months, second within 24 months — [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/what-is-an-area-development-franchise-agreement/); [ZoomRoom](https://franchise.zoomroom.com/learn/what-is-an-area-development-agreement)
- **Exclusivity depends on staying on schedule.** The franchisor will not sell another franchise or open a corporate unit in the area *while the developer meets its schedule* — [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/what-is-an-area-development-franchise-agreement/); [Sprintlaw NZ, area developers](https://sprintlaw.co.nz/articles/area-developer-agreements-in-new-zealand-how-they-work-in-franchising/)
- **"Time of the essence."** A Friendly's development agreement makes time of the essence and requires the developer to meet the minimum number of units under contract before it qualifies for any cure period — [Law Insider, Development Agreement](https://www.lawinsider.com/contracts/1oiFpkr2DlU)
- **Master franchise targets.** Master franchise agreements set a development schedule with built-in annual and cumulative performance targets — [Franchising.com, "Franchising Abroad"](https://franchisors.franchising.com/articles/franchising_abroad_the_two_most_common_international_franchise_deals.html); [Perez-Llorca/Lexology](https://perezllorca.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Sub-franchising-Master-Franchising-and-Development-Agents-Lexology.pdf)
- **Distributor minimums.** Distributor and dealer agreements condition exclusivity on a minimum purchase obligation (MPO) — [Law Insider, Minimum Purchase Requirements](https://lawinsider.com/clause/minimum-purchase-requirements); [Business-in-a-Box template](https://www.business-in-a-box.com/template/exclusive-distribution-agreement-D1240)
- **Technology license diligence.** Exclusive technology licenses use diligence milestones. One filing treats each milestone as "a separate and independent condition" and requires an annual compliance report certified by an executive officer — [SEC EDGAR, HDS International exh10-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001454742/000100201412000226/exh10-1.htm); [SEC EDGAR, Innovest Global](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1735012/000118518520000495/ex_181739.htm)
- **Lead response time (vendor data only, not contract terms).**
  - FranFunnel's Q1 2025 study of 500+ franchise brands found that 35% never responded to an inquiry, only 26% responded within 5 minutes, and average email response time was 8.8 hours — [FranFunnel](https://franfunnel.com/speed-to-lead)
  - FranConnect's 2018 Sales Index found that 39% of closed leads had been contacted within 4 hours, versus 18% of those contacted between 4 and 24 hours — [FranConnect](https://www.franconnect.com/en/?p=280240)
  - Both studies cover franchise-sales leads, not customer service leads, and both vendors sell lead-response software.

### Inferences
- For a national cleaning operator, the closest analogs are:
  - an area-developer-style schedule: coverage milestones by state or metro over time;
  - a distributor-style annual minimum: a minimum commission or revenue floor.
- Operational SLAs (lead acknowledgment within X hours, quote within Y days, satisfaction score) would have to be written fresh. I found no market-standard numbers in contracts.
- The UK "70% of average" approach and "no targets until data exists" are useful calibration ideas. A brand with no performance history could set ramp-up minimums that rise over years 1 to 3.

### Gaps
- No U.S. survey data on how often franchise territories carry minimum-sales conditions, or at what levels.
- No contract clause found that sets a lead response time, a minimum number of bids or jobs, or customer-satisfaction (e.g., NPS) thresholds as conditions of exclusivity. Searches surfaced only vendor benchmarks.
- The Orangetheory standard was not verified against its actual FDD.

## 2. What happens when standards are missed: remedies and cure periods

### Takeaway
The usual remedies form a ladder: notice and cure, then conversion to non-exclusive or territory reduction, then termination. Practitioners call conversion to non-exclusive the "workhorse" middle remedy. Drafting guides stress that the remedy must be written down; otherwise exclusivity may survive a missed target.

### Cited Findings
- **Franchise remedy ladder.** Reported remedies for missed annual standards include:
  - reducing the territory's geographic scope;
  - ending territorial rights;
  - terminating the agreement (one cited example triggers these after two consecutive years of misses).
  — [Franchimp](https://franchimp.com/blog/article/exclusive-territory-franchise-disclosure-explained)
- **Other reported outcomes.** Extra training, a reduced territory, or termination — [Sprintlaw UK](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/what-do-you-need-to-know-about-a-franchise-s-exclusive-territory-clause/)
- **Termination as last resort.** UK commentary calls termination the worst case and says a reasonable chance to improve should come first — [Franchise World UK](https://www.franchiseworld.co.uk/setting-targets-for-franchisees/)
- **Area development defaults.**
  - Most ADAs include cure periods, but repeated delays can cost the developer its exclusive territory rights or the agreement — [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/what-is-an-area-development-franchise-agreement/)
  - Consequences range from losing rights to the undeveloped portion of the territory, to losing exclusivity, to outright default — [ZoomRoom](https://franchise.zoomroom.com/learn/what-is-an-area-development-agreement)
- **Cross-default.** Terminating an ADA can give the franchisor the right to terminate the developer's unit franchise agreements as well — [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/what-is-an-area-development-franchise-agreement/)
- **Master franchise remedies.**
  - Missing the schedule can lead to loss of exclusivity or termination, and some agreements add monetary penalties — [Perez-Llorca/Lexology](https://perezllorca.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Sub-franchising-Master-Franchising-and-Development-Agents-Lexology.pdf)
  - Some agreements instead require the master to pay what the franchisor would have received had the targets been met (a "shortfall payment") — same source
  - Global Franchise suggests resetting interim schedules every few years to reflect territory realities — [Global Franchise](https://global-franchise.com/?p=11974)
- **Distributor remedies.**
  - A template clause: failure to meet the MPO in any year entitles the supplier, at its sole discretion, to (a) convert the appointment to non-exclusive, (b) reduce the exclusive territory, or (c) terminate — [Business-in-a-Box](https://www.business-in-a-box.com/template/exclusive-distribution-agreement-D1240)
  - A Law Insider sample makes conversion available after written notice and a cure period, as the supplier's sole remedy for the shortfall, with termination as an alternative. It also includes a conversion fee payable *to* the distributor — [Law Insider](https://lawinsider.com/clause/minimum-purchase-requirements)
- **Drafting risk.**
  - Without an express provision, exclusivity may remain even when targets are missed — [Contracko clause library](https://contracko.com/clause-library/exclusivity/index.html.md)
  - A minimum with no stated consequence can leave the supplier only able to sue for damages — [Business-in-a-Box](https://www.business-in-a-box.com/template/exclusive-distribution-agreement-D1240)
- **Carry-forward.** Some agreements let purchases above the minimum count toward the next year's requirement. If the agreement is silent, assume they do not — reported from [Beancount 2026 distribution guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/10/13/distribution-agreements-exclusive-territory-quotas-map-trademark-termination-guide) (search extract only; page could not be fetched)
- **Technology licenses.**
  - Licensor may terminate or convert the exclusive license to non-exclusive if diligence obligations are missed — [SEC EDGAR, HDS International](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001454742/000100201412000226/exh10-1.htm)
  - Another draft lets the licensor terminate or narrow exclusivity by field or jurisdiction where the licensee has not commercialized, beginning 10 years in — [SEC EDGAR, Innovest Global](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1735012/000118518520000495/ex_181743.htm)
  - One agreement lets the licensee buy milestone extensions: $100,000 for the first year and $50,000 for each later year, up to 3 years — search extract from the EDGAR filings above
- **Cure periods (statutory reference point).**
  - California's Franchise Relations Act previously used a 30-day cure window — [CA AB 525 materials](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/15-16/bill/asm/ab_0501-0550/ab_525_bill_20150903_enrolled.htm); [CLA Business Law](https://calawyers.org/business-law/new-franchise-legislation-passes-in-assembly/)
  - The enrolled AB 525 (2015) text sets a cure period of at least 60 days and no more than 75 days. My searches did not confirm whether that was the final operative law; check Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §20020.

### Inferences
- A brand granting national exclusivity should spell out a graduated remedy, for example:
  - Missing a state or regional standard removes that state or region from exclusivity, or makes it non-exclusive there.
  - Missing the national minimum converts the whole grant to non-exclusive.
  - Repeated misses or non-payment allow termination.
- Notice and cure should come before each step: commonly 30 days for monetary defaults and 30 to 60 days for performance.
- A shortfall payment ("top-up to the minimum") is an established alternative that keeps exclusivity if the operator pays.
- The "sole remedy" and "conversion fee to the distributor" language in some samples favors the operator. A brand should avoid conceding these.

### Gaps
- No source gave a statistically typical cure-period length for performance defaults outside California law. The 30/60-day figures come from the statute and practice, not from a survey.
- I could not confirm the final enacted content of California AB 525.

## 3. Lead-sharing, declined leads and unserviced areas

### Takeaway
The clearest U.S. precedent comes from janitorial franchising. The franchisor offers accounts. If the franchisee cannot or will not serve an account, or another franchisee accepts first, the franchisor's obligation is treated as satisfied and the account goes elsewhere. Regulators warn that this policy is often not disclosed. FDD Item 12 also requires franchisors to disclose reserved channels and whether the franchisee is paid for territory sales made through them.

### Cited Findings
- **Declined accounts.** In janitorial franchises, the franchisor usually selects the accounts. A guide from the California regulator (now DFPI, then DBO) says the franchisor may not have to replace an account the franchisee rejects — [California DBO janitorial guide](https://dbo.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2019/02/janitorial.pdf)
- **Accounts that go to others.** The Maryland Attorney General / FTC advisory says that if the franchisee cannot serve a location, or another franchisee accepts the account first, the franchisor may have met its obligation to offer accounts. It warns buyers not to count on all promised revenue — [Maryland AG janitorial advisory](https://www.marylandattorneygeneral.gov/Securities%20Documents/janitorial.pdf)
- **Cancelled accounts.** Most janitorial franchise agreements say the franchisor need not replace an account the customer cancels — [California DBO](https://dbo.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2019/02/janitorial.pdf)
- **National account programs.** Coverall, ServiceMaster Clean and Jan-Pro run national-account programs: multi-location contracts serviced through their franchise networks. Their public pages do not describe allocation rules — [Coverall National Accounts](https://www.coverall.com/national-accounts/); [ServiceMaster Clean](https://servicemasterclean.com/national-accounts/national-account-benefits); [Jan-Pro](https://jan-pro.com/cleaning/national-accounts/)
- **Item 12 disclosure rules.** Under 16 C.F.R. §436.5(l), the FDD must disclose:
  - rights the franchisor reserves (other channels, other brands, company units);
  - whether the franchisee is compensated for sales the franchisor makes in the territory through those channels.
  Non-exclusive territories must carry a prescribed competition warning — [Franchise.law, Item 12](https://franchise.law/franchise-disclosure-document/item-12/); [Accurate Franchising](https://www.accuratefranchising.com/resources/learning/franchise-disclosure-document/how-are-franchise-territories-defined-in-an-fdd/)
- **Channel carve-outs.** Exclusivity may cover only physical sites, leaving online or other channels open to the franchisor — [Sprintlaw UK](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/what-do-you-need-to-know-about-a-franchise-s-exclusive-territory-clause/); [Fortman Law](https://www.fortmanlaw.com/franchise-territory/)

### Inferences
- A "declined/unserviced lead" clause for the brand could provide:
  - Every lead goes first to the exclusive operator.
  - If the operator declines, fails to acknowledge within X hours, or fails to quote within Y days, the brand may route the lead to a third party.
  - The brand may provide service through other means in any ZIP code or state the operator does not cover. That area may be treated as "unserviced" and removed from exclusivity after a set number of declined leads.
- The brand should also reserve national/multi-site accounts, which janitorial franchisors commonly keep at the franchisor level.
- The agreement should state whether the operator receives any override or commission on leads routed elsewhere. Item 12 practice makes this a recognized disclosure point.

### Gaps
- No franchise or license agreement text was found with an explicit "unable or unwilling to service" or right-of-first-refusal lead clause. Specific response windows (24 or 48 hours) are not sourced. Pulling actual FDDs (e.g., from state FDD databases in WI, MN or CA) would be needed.

## 4. Fee escalation, review cadence, reporting and audit rights

### Takeaway
Escalators are usually CPI-linked with a fixed floor or cap. The difference between "greater of" and "lesser of" decides whether the fixed percentage is a floor or a ceiling. Royalty and commission verification relies on periodic (commonly monthly) reports plus audit rights. Audit costs shift to the payer if underpayment exceeds 2% (common in franchise agreements) or 5% (common in IP licenses). Interest on underpayments typically runs at 1 to 1.5% per month or 10 to 18% per year, capped at the legal maximum.

### Cited Findings
- **Escalators: "greater of" means a floor.**
  - A Pruco/Prudential schedule allows increases up to the greater of CPI or 3%.
  - A Heap/Contentsquare MSA labeled a "cap" in fact allows up to the greater of 3% or CPI.
  — [Zuva inflation-adjustment examples](https://zuva.ai/contract-central/inflation-adjustment/); [ConductAtlas provision record](https://conductatlas.com/platform/heap/contentsquare-master-service-agreement/provision/CA-P-073052/annual-fee-increase-cap-of-three-percent-or-cpi/)
- **Escalators: "lesser of" means a true cap.** One Prudential clause allows the lesser of CPI or 5%. A software support clause allows CPI change but "in any case not more than 3%." Another sample sets the CPI factor *or a minimum of 3%* — [Law Insider, Fee Escalation](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/fee-escalation); [Law Insider, Annual Escalation](https://lawinsider.com/clause/annual-escalation); [Law Insider, CPI Factor Adjustment](https://lawinsider.com/clause/cpi-factor-adjustment)
- **Franchise audit cost-shifting at 2%.** The franchisee pays audit costs if reported sales are understated by more than 2% in any period, or if reports are late or incomplete. Costs include CPA fees, attorneys' fees, legal costs and travel — [SEC EDGAR, Planet Beach franchise agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001081899/000095012310031818/h69192exv10w4.htm); [SEC EDGAR, World Moto](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001492151/000106299314001353/exhibit10-1.htm)
- **Higher franchise thresholds.** Some franchise agreements use 5%, e.g., an understatement of 5% or more of quarterly gross revenue — [SEC EDGAR, Capital Alliance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001123870/000106299300000123/0001.txt); [SEC EDGAR, Rocky Mountain Chocolate Factory](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000785815/000095013407020570/d50173exv10w1.htm)
- **Interest on underpayments.** Rates found:
  - 1.5% per month, or the legal maximum if lower (Rocky Mountain Chocolate Factory, above);
  - 18% per year (Coffee People) — [SEC EDGAR, Coffee People](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000925908/000095015098001550/0000950150-98-001550.txt/seq-3)
- **IP license audit terms.**
  - Licensee reimburses audit costs if underpayment is 5% or more in any quarter, and pays 12% per year interest — [SEC EDGAR, Caladrius](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000320017/000032001716000119/exhibit103a.htm)
  - 1% per month interest on overdue amounts — [SEC EDGAR, Augme/Modavox](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001137204/000143209310000377/ex10-1.htm)
  - Other samples: 1.5% per month; the lesser of 110% of prime or the legal maximum; audits capped at once per calendar year — [Law Insider, Audits by Licensor](https://lawinsider.com/clause/audits-by-licensor); [Law Insider, Audit](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/audit/_4)
  - Some thresholds are the lesser of 5% or $5,000 — search extract of the same clause sets
- **Inspection access.** Franchisors commonly reserve the right to inspect records during business hours, often without prior notice — [SEC EDGAR, Planet Beach](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001081899/000095012310031818/h69192exv10w1.htm)
- **Audit guidance.** CASRAI has a general guide to royalty audit clauses — [CASRAI royalty audit rights](https://casrai.org/guides/royalty-audit-rights)

### Inferences
- For a weekly fee plus commissions, a brand-protective package would include:
  - weekly or monthly commission reports with job-level detail (customer, ZIP code, invoice amount);
  - read access to the operator's CRM, invoicing or POS;
  - an annual audit right with a 3-year lookback and 3-year record retention;
  - audit costs shifting to the operator above a 2% understatement (the franchise norm; 5% is the more licensee-friendly IP norm);
  - interest at 1 to 1.5% per month or the legal maximum, plus a late fee on the weekly fee;
  - repeated understatement (e.g., two audits over the threshold) as a termination ground.
- Escalation: "the lesser of CPI or X%" protects the operator. "The greater of CPI or X%" protects the brand, since X% becomes a guaranteed minimum raise. Adjustments typically happen annually on the anniversary date.

### Gaps
- No source surveyed how often weekly flat license fees escalate, or by how much, in service-business licenses.
- No reliable source on typical lookback or record-retention periods. The 3-year figures above are drafting suggestions, not sourced norms.
- The Fulcrum Inquiry audit-provision article could not be fetched.

## 5. Term length, renewal and termination rights

### Takeaway
Franchise agreements typically run fixed terms of about 5 to 10 years with conditional renewal. Master franchise and development agreements often run 10 to 20 years, with renewal conditioned on meeting annual and cumulative targets. Termination for cause after notice and cure is universal. Some states restrict franchisor termination and non-renewal without "good cause."

### Cited Findings
- **Franchise terms and renewal.** Franchise agreements are fixed-term (5 or 10 years cited as examples). Renewal usually requires:
  - premises upgrades;
  - a renewal fee;
  - strict compliance;
  - written notice within a set window before expiry.
  — [CFA Franchise Canada](https://cfa.ca/franchisecanada/q-why-is-the-start-of-my-franchise-relationship-the-best-time-to-think-about-the-end-of-it); [Heritage Law, termination vs. non-renewal](https://www.heritagelawwi.com/franchise-termination-vs-non-renewal-differences)
- **Good-cause states.** Some states bar franchisors from refusing renewal without "good cause" — [Heritage Law](https://www.heritagelawwi.com/franchise-termination-vs-non-renewal-differences)
- **Master franchise terms.** Master agreements are likely to run 10 to 20 years, often with renewals subject to annual and cumulative targets — [Franchising.com](https://franchisors.franchising.com/articles/franchising_abroad_the_two_most_common_international_franchise_deals.html)
- **California cure period.** A 30-day cure period applied under prior California law. AB 525 (2015) enrolled text: 60 to 75 days — [leginfo.ca.gov](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/15-16/bill/asm/ab_0501-0550/ab_525_bill_20150903_enrolled.htm)
- **IFA training material.** The IFA published a 2026 "Handling Defaults and Terminations" basics deck. Its contents could only partly be seen — [IFA](https://www.franchise.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Basics_Track-Handling_Defaults_and_Terminations-PPT.pdf)
- **Technology licenses.** Exclusivity sometimes becomes reviewable after a fixed period, e.g., narrowing allowed starting at year 10 — [SEC EDGAR, Innovest Global](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1735012/000118518520000495/ex_181743.htm)

### Inferences
- For a national exclusive cleaning license, a shorter initial term is the prudent pattern: 1 to 3 years for exclusivity, possibly inside a longer overall license. Renewal of exclusivity would depend on meeting coverage and revenue standards in the prior period.
- The brand could also seek termination for convenience on 60 to 180 days' notice, though that is uncommon in franchise agreements. A licensee would normally want a mirror right, or a longer protected period.
- Whether the arrangement is a "franchise" under the FTC Rule and state laws is a threshold issue. A weekly fee (over the FTC minimum-payment threshold) plus use of the brand's mark plus brand control or assistance can make it one. That would trigger FDD disclosure and state relationship laws, including good-cause termination limits. Confirm with franchise counsel.

### Gaps
- No source found on franchisee or licensee rights to terminate for convenience, or on typical notice periods for supplier termination for convenience in U.S. distribution agreements.
- I did not research the FTC Franchise Rule minimum-payment exemption threshold (adjusted periodically).

## 6. Is a single national exclusive grant to a small operator common? Risks flagged

### Takeaway
I found no source calling a single nationwide exclusive grant to a small service operator a common practice. Market practice favors time-limited, territory-by-territory exclusivity with performance milestones and a reversion path. Commentators repeatedly flag three risks: underperformance or "shelving," channel and coverage gaps, and ill-defined remedies. Competition law can also void exclusivity that is too broad or too long.

### Cited Findings
- **Small licensees.** Small licensees are usually given time-limited, field- or territory-specific exclusivity with conversion or termination rights. Indefinite broad grants were not found; the closest examples are EDGAR license agreements with territory-by-territory narrowing — [SEC EDGAR, HDS International](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001454742/000100201412000226/exh10-1.htm); [SEC EDGAR, Innovest Global](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1735012/000118518520000495/ex_181743.htm)
- **Shelving risk.** A licensee may shelve the licensed product so the licensor never sees revenue. Licensees resist absolute diligence obligations — [Taylor Wessing, "Licensing out: try not to get benched"](https://www.taylorwessing.com/fr/insights-and-events/insights/2020/07/licensing-out-try-not-to-get-benched); [National Law Review, "Five Traps for the Unwary Licensee"](https://natlawreview.com/node/10667/printable/print)
- **Milestone design.** Milestones should correlate with events that reduce risk and increase value — search extract from [THSH case study](https://www.thsh.com/?p=1901)
- **Competition law.** Exclusivity that is too broad, too long, or held by a party with market power can be void. The EU VBER safe harbor covers shares under 30%; this is EU, not U.S., law — [Contracko](https://contracko.com/clause-library/exclusivity/index.html.md)
- **Revenue risk in janitorial systems.** Regulators warn janitorial franchise buyers that the promised revenue may not materialize when accounts go to others. This shows the operator-side risk when coverage depends on capacity — [Maryland AG](https://www.marylandattorneygeneral.gov/Securities%20Documents/janitorial.pdf)
- **Master franchise schedules.** Master franchise commentary says exclusivity should end if the schedule is missed, and schedules should be reset every few years — [Global Franchise](https://global-franchise.com/?p=11974)

### Inferences
- The main risk for the brand is a single small operator holding all 50 states with physical capacity in only a few metros. That blocks the brand from monetizing everywhere else, and customers who get slow or no service damage the brand.
- Standard mitigations drawn from the analogs above:
  1. Grant exclusivity state by state or metro by metro, earned by coverage milestones (area-developer model).
  2. Set an annual minimum commission or revenue floor with a shortfall top-up option.
  3. Use a lead SLA with automatic release of unserviced leads and ZIP codes.
  4. Reserve national accounts and online channels for the brand.
  5. Use a short initial exclusivity period (12 to 24 months) with performance-conditioned renewal.
  6. Include conversion to non-exclusive as the primary remedy and termination for repeated or monetary defaults.
  7. Require monthly reporting and audit rights.
  8. Require the operator to keep the insurance, licensing and bonding needed for multi-state operation (not researched here).
- The arrangement may be a "franchise" or "business opportunity" under federal and state law. This is a significant legal risk that counsel should assess before signing.

### Gaps
- No commentator source found that directly discusses national exclusivity granted to a single small *service* operator, or that measures how common it is.
- Multi-state contractor licensing, insurance and bonding requirements for cleaning services were not researched.
