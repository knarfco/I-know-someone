## 1. Brand-by-brand ongoing fees (royalty, management/service fee, ad fee, finder's/account fees)

### Takeaway
"Unit franchise" janitorial brands, where the franchisor or master sells accounts to the franchisee (Coverall, Jani-King, Jan-Pro, Anago, Vanguard, CleanNet), usually take about 10% to 15%+ of every billed dollar. That total is a royalty plus a management, support or accounting fee, and it applies to all account revenue, including accounts the franchisee finds. On top of that comes a separate one-time finder's fee for each additional account the franchisor supplies. Owner-operator/"self-sell" models (Stratus masters, Office Pride) charge less: about 3% to 9% royalty plus about 1% brand fund.

### Cited Findings
**Coverall (Coverall North America)**
- 2025 FDD summary: a "Special Services Finder's Fee" of **20%**, plus a "Sales and Marketing Fee for Additional Business" of up to **5x monthly billing**, currently **4x monthly billing**. — [FDD Exchange, Coverall 2025 FDD](https://fddexchange.com/fdd/coverall-2025/) (from a search summary; page could not be opened)
- Royalty of **5% of Gross Dollar Volume** (billed customer amounts) plus a separate **10% "support fee"** covering billing, collections and customer relations. That is about **15% combined**, deducted from customer payments. — [franchiseoverview: Coverall North America](https://franchiseoverview.com/company/coverall-north-america); [franchiseoverview: Coverall NGT](https://franchiseoverview.com/company/coverall-ngt); [franzy: Coverall](https://franzy.com/franchises/coverall)
- Other sources call the 10% a "management fee" ("approximately 10% of gross monthly billings") or "management/billing fee ~10%+", and say combined royalty and management can be "15% or more of billings." — [franchisepayback: Coverall](https://www.franchisepayback.com/franchise/coverall); [pulserevops: Coverall](https://pulserevops.com/reviews/fr1061) (lower reliability)
- One listing shows a 0% marketing fee for Coverall. — [franzy: Coverall](https://franzy.com/franchises/coverall)

### Inferences
- For unit franchises, the "ongoing take" on franchisor-provided accounts has two layers. The first is a recurring **10–18% of billings** (royalty plus management/admin/accounting fee), charged for as long as the account is served and on all revenue. The second is a **one-time acquisition charge** of about 3–6x monthly billing. Coverall currently charges 4x and reserves up to 5x.
- The proposed deal is 10%/7%/4% commission **only on brand-generated jobs**, with nothing on the licensee's own work. That is structurally lighter than unit-franchise models, which charge their 10–15% on every dollar, including self-sourced accounts. Even the first-tier 10% matches or undercuts a single royalty line at Jani-King, Jan-Pro, CleanNet or Anago, before their extra admin, accounting and tech fees.

- No figures found for System4. Office Pride and Stratus customer-acquisition charges are unknown.

## 2. How finder's fees work, and analogues for one-time (e.g., construction clean) jobs

### Takeaway
Finder's fees are typically priced as a **multiple of the account's monthly billing** (about 3x to 5x). They are paid upfront, worked off by cleaning free for that many months, or financed. Coverall's "Special Services Finder's Fee" of **20%** is the closest disclosed analogue for one-time/special jobs.

### Cited Findings
- Coverall's 2025 FDD charges up to **5x monthly billing** (currently **4x**) as a "Sales and Marketing Fee for Additional Business," plus a **20% "Special Services Finder's Fee"** that applies to special (non-recurring) services. — [FDD Exchange: Coverall 2025](https://fddexchange.com/fdd/coverall-2025/)
- An industry article (about 6 years old, franchisor unnamed) describes "Finder's fees, which are three times the monthly value of the customer's invoice. They can pay the three months finder's fee upfront or clean the account for three months for free." — [Secure Clean Building Services](https://www.securecleanbsi.com/what-we-need-to-know-about-cleaning-franchises/)
- Jani-King's finder's fee is "optional" and "varies by formula" (2026 FDD). — [ClearlyFDD](https://clearlyfdd.com/franchise/jani-king/2025-vs-2026)
- A low-reliability source puts the Jani-King finder's fee at "3 to 6 months of the new account's gross billings." — [pulserevops](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/q14746)
- Coverall's "accounts receivable financing" payment approach was held unlawful under the Massachusetts wage law in Awuah (SJC 2011). This shows that financing fees by deducting them from customer receipts is legally risky. — [Fox Rothschild franchise blog](https://franchiselaw.foxrothschild.com/2011/08/articles/legal-decisions/coverall-update-mass-supreme-court-rules-against-accounts-receivable-financing-system/)
- Anago masters earn "commissions on additional cleaning contracts sold to unit franchisees." — [Anago](https://anagocleaning.com/master-franchising/about-master-franchising/how-do-i-make-money/)

### Inferences
- A finder's fee of 3–5x monthly billing equals **25–42% of the first year's revenue** of a recurring account. (Arithmetic: 3/12 = 25%, 5/12 = 42%.)
- A 10% commission on a brand-generated recurring job equals **1.2x monthly billing per year**, so it would take about 3–4 years to match a typical finder's fee.
- For one-time construction cleans, Coverall's 20% special-services finder's fee is the best benchmark, so a 10% first-tier commission is half that rate.

### Gaps
- No sources found specifying finder's fee interest rates, down-payment percentages, or construction-cleaning-specific fees.

## 3. General franchise royalty and ad-fund norms

### Takeaway
Across industries, royalties average about **6% of gross** (business services about 10%). Ad funds are typically **1–2%** (median about 2%), with ranges of 2–5% cited.

### Cited Findings
- FRANdata study of 2,425 brands: average royalty **about 6%**, stable 2012–2018. Business services had the highest category average at **10%**, and photographic services the lowest at **4.3%**. — [FRANdata: Average Royalty Fees 2012–2018](https://frandata.com/?p=8387); [FRANdata Avg Royalty Report PDF](https://www.frandata.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Avg-Royalty-Report.pdf)
- Royalties "often land between 4% and 8%"; ad fund contributions "commonly range from 2% to 5%." — [1851 Franchise: royalties and ad funds 2026](https://1851franchise.com/franchise-royalties-ad-funds-2026-2731694)
- A 2026 FDD analysis cited by a blog: median royalty **6.0%**, median ad fund **2.0%** of revenue (underlying dataset not traced). — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/franchising-statistics)
- FranConnect study: **72%** of franchise systems charge national advertising fees, mostly as a % of revenue; local marketing requirements average **2%** of monthly revenue. — [FranConnect](https://www.franconnect.com/en/?p=283817)

### Inferences
- Commercial cleaning unit franchises (10–15%+ combined) run at roughly 2x the cross-industry royalty mean. That is consistent with FRANdata's 10% for business services, plus the separate management fees.

### Gaps
- No current (2023–2026) FRANdata or IFA median published figure found. The latest primary FRANdata average is 2018.

## 4. Controversies/litigation over cleaning franchise fee levels

### Takeaway
The "unit franchise" janitorial model has drawn repeated misclassification litigation. Courts have treated the deduction of royalties, management fees, finder/additional-business fees and insurance from franchisees' receipts as unlawful wage deductions. One federal judge likened the model to a "modified Ponzi scheme."

### Cited Findings
- Awuah v. Coverall (D. Mass. 2010, 707 F. Supp. 2d 80): the court found the franchisees were misclassified employees, and the judge compared the franchise to a "modified Ponzi scheme." The court noted that "for each cleaning service provided, Coverall receives management and royalty fees." — [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/massachusetts-supreme-court-issues-damages-ruling-in-awuah/); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=84814ff7-d917-4900-aa0b-59113a5eebb0)
- Massachusetts SJC (Aug 31, 2011): Coverall violated the wage law in charging insurance-related and franchise fees, and its accounts-receivable payment approach was unlawful. A press statement (not a primary source) says the ruling required repayment of franchise fees, promissory note payments, **additional business fees** and insurance payments. — [Lathrop GPM](https://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/massachusetts-supreme-court-issues-damages-ruling-in-awuah/); [Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/massachusetts-employment-labor-report-october-2011.html); [complaintsboard repost](https://www.complaintsboard.com/jan-pro-lawsuits-c241605) (low reliability)
- Vazquez/Roman v. Jan-Pro (N.D. Cal. No. 3:16-cv-05961): the Ninth Circuit applied the Dynamex ABC test. In 2022 the court granted summary judgment on unlawful deductions of management fees and sales and marketing fees. Class counsel later received 30% of a settlement fund; the amount was not found. — [Buchalter](https://www.buchalter.com/insights/roman-v-jan-pro-franchising-international-inc-court-clarifies-employee-misclassification-and-abc-test-application); [Justia fee order PDF](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2016cv05961/304877/533/0.pdf)
- New Jersey DOL v. Coverall: the agency treated the deducted finance charges, royalties and management fees as part of an employment relationship. — [NJ DOL decision PDF](https://www.nj.gov/labor/assets/PDFs/Legal%20Notices/Final%20Decisions/Coverall%20North%20America%20Inc.%20v.%20NJDOL%20(OAL%20Dkt.%20No.%20LID%2007278-2019).pdf)

### Inferences
- The legal problems center on **control plus deductions from customer receipts**: the franchisor bills the customer, takes its fees, and remits the remainder. The fee percentage alone was not the issue.
- A licensing deal where the licensee bills its own customers, and pays a weekly fee plus commission only on brand-sourced jobs, avoids the pattern courts criticized. This is analysis, not legal advice.

### Gaps
- Settlement amounts for Jan-Pro and Jani-King cases were not found.
- No found source states a specific fee level that regulators deem "excessive"; criticism focuses on structure.
