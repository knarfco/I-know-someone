His story Draft until interview

Good floors build great lives.

Bob Miller laid his first floor as a teenager, on his knees next to his father, learning that a floor is only as good as what’s underneath it. More than sixty years later, he still checks the subfloor first.

In between, he has done it all. Linoleum kitchens in the ’60s, shag carpet in the ’70s, parquet and terrazzo, marble foyers, Pergo when laminate was brand new, and today’s waterproof vinyl. Homes, restaurants, churches, offices and more staircases than he can count.

“Anybody can make a floor look good on day one. I want it to look good when your grandkids are running on it.”

That’s why families call Bob back a generation later. It’s why business owners trust him to work overnight in their space. And it’s why, today, he splits his year between the sunshine of South Florida and the hills of Pittsburgh — two places where people know him by name.

Bob still walks every job. He still shows up when he says he will. And he still cares, maybe more than ever, how it looks and how long it lasts.

The road so far

Every era had its floor. Bob laid them all.

  1. 1960s

    The first floor

    A teenager with a borrowed hammer and his father’s patience. One room of oak, nailed by hand — then a lot of linoleum kitchens.

  2. 1970s

    Shag, parquet & terrazzo

    Every trend came through, and Bob learned to install each one properly — and how to rescue them later.

  3. 1980s

    Word gets around

    No ads. Just happy families telling neighbors. The schedule filled up and the first crew was hired.

  4. 1990s

    Marble, tile & business floors

    Marble foyers, porcelain tile and restaurants — done overnight so the doors stayed open.

  5. 2000s

    Laminate arrives

    Pergo and engineered wood changed the game. Bob learned which ones last and which ones don’t.

  6. 2010s

    Two home bases

    Family ties brought Bob to Pittsburgh, where century-old hardwood needed exactly his kind of patience.

  7. Today

    Still setting the standard

    Waterproof vinyl, natural linoleum’s comeback, and the same old rule: flat, dry and done right underneath.

Decade details are placeholders until Bob’s interview.
