His story

Good floors build great lives.

Bob Miller laid his first floor as a teenager, on his knees next to his father, learning that a floor is only as good as what's underneath it. More than sixty years later, he still checks the subfloor first.

In between, he has done it all: kitchens and ballrooms, beach condos and brick row houses, restaurants, churches, offices and more staircases than he can count. Wood, tile, stone, vinyl — if it goes under your feet, Bob has installed it, fixed it, or brought it back to life.

“Anybody can make a floor look good on day one. I want it to look good when your grandkids are running on it.”

That's why families call Bob back a generation later. It's why business owners trust him to work overnight in their space. And it's why, today, he splits his year between the sunshine of South Florida and the hills of Pittsburgh — two places where people know him by name.

Bob still walks every job. He still shows up when he says he will. And he still cares, maybe more than ever, how it looks and how long it lasts.

The road so far

Six decades, one floor at a time.

  1. The 1960s

    The first floor

    A teenager with a borrowed hammer and his father's patience. One room of oak, nailed by hand.

  2. The 1970s

    Learning by doing

    Parquet, terrazzo, linoleum, shag — every trend came through, and Bob learned to install them all properly.

  3. The 1980s

    Word gets around

    No ads. Just happy families telling neighbors. The schedule filled up and the first crew was hired.

  4. The 1990s

    Homes and businesses

    Restaurants, offices and storefronts joined the list — work done overnight so doors stayed open.

  5. The 2000s

    Thousands of floors

    Hardwood, tile and natural stone across South Florida, built to stand up to heat, humidity and hurricanes.

  6. The 2010s

    Two home bases

    Family ties brought Bob to Pittsburgh, where century-old hardwood needed exactly his kind of patience.

  7. Today

    Still setting the standard

    Sixty-plus years in, Bob still walks every job — with a hand-picked crew carrying his standard forward.
